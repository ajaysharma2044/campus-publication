A motorcycle, scooter or moped at Cornell needs the M permit, which costs $120.01 a year, and its plate must be registered in MyParking ([Cornell Transportation](https://fcs.cornell.edu/departments/transportation-delivery-services/parking-campus/parking-students)). A bike needs no permit. Lock it to a designated rack.

```glance
- The M permit covers motorcycles, scooters and mopeds and prorates monthly.
- Register motorcycle and scooter plates in MyParking, even for short-term parking.
- Park bikes at designated racks. Locking to railings, trees or poles can lead to impound.
- Do not charge e-bike batteries in residence rooms, offices or academic buildings.
```

## What does motorcycle, scooter and moped parking cost?

The M permit costs $120.01 and is prorated monthly. It is nonrefundable ([Cornell Transportation](https://fcs.cornell.edu/departments/transportation-delivery-services/parking-campus/parking-students)). Another Cornell page lists it at [$119.94](https://fcs.cornell.edu/node/1456); go by the student page.

Cornell does not say on the student page which lots the M permit works in, so ask Transportation and Delivery Services (TDS) at 607-255-4600 before you buy. Students must [register cars, motorcycles and scooters](https://fcs.cornell.edu/departments/transportation-delivery-services/parking-campus) in MyParking, including for short-term parking.

The permit page says "scooters" without defining them. Cornell's cycling page defines an e-scooter as under 100 pounds with a top speed of 20 mph. If yours is heavier or faster, ask TDS which rules apply. Our [permit guide](/cornell/getting-around/cornell-student-parking-permit-cost-rules/) lists every permit and the dates.

```reader "If you ride a motorcycle"
Buy the M permit, not a car permit. Because it prorates by the month, a mid-year purchase costs less. Do not expect a refund if you sell the bike.
```

## Where can I park a bike at Cornell?

Park only at designated bike racks. Locking a bike to railings, signposts, trees or poles can lead to impound, and so can blocking a path or leaving a bike abandoned. Cornell's cycling page lists no fine amounts. Indoor storage in campus buildings needs written permission from the building coordinator ([Cornell Transportation](https://fcs.cornell.edu/departments/transportation-delivery-services/alternative-transportation-options/cycling-campus)). Lock the frame and a wheel.

Cornell's assistant director of transportation wrote in the Sun that campus has more than 1,000 bike parking spots, about 7 miles of bike lanes and 2 miles of shared paths ([Cornell Daily Sun](https://cornellsun.com/article/2025/11/guest-room-from-bikes-to-buses-how-to-sustainably-navigate-campus-this-fall)).

```reader "If you live in a residence hall"
University housing requires external racks or sprinklered bike rooms ([Student and Campus Life](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/housing-details/rates-amenities-licenses/bicycle-registration-storage)). Do not keep a bike in your room, and do not charge its battery there.
```

## Do I need to register a bike?

Registration is free, and Cornell urges you to register bikes, e-bikes and scooters with Cornell Commuter and Parking Services and with the local municipality. The page does not say it is mandatory. It matters for theft: campus police reported 51 bike and scooter thefts in fall 2024, worth at least $41,177 ([Cornell Daily Sun](https://cornellsun.com/2024/11/14/this-happens-all-the-time-campus-police-report-more-than-40000-in-bike-and-scooter-thefts-this-semester-so-far)). Cornell advises registering and using a U-lock. Our guide to [bike, e-bike and scooter rules and theft](/cornell/getting-around/cornell-bike-ebike-scooter-rules-theft/) covers the full rules.

## What are the e-bike and charging rules?

Cornell defines an e-bike as a motor of no more than 750 watts, no wider than 36 inches, with working pedals ([Cornell Transportation](https://fcs.cornell.edu/departments/transportation-delivery-services/sustainable-transportation-options/cycling-campus)). Do not charge e-bike or e-scooter batteries in residential rooms, offices or academic buildings, according to a [Cornell guidance document](https://fcs.cornell.edu/sites/default/files/2025-02/Micro-Mobility%20E-Bikes_Litium-Ion%20Batteries%20Guidance%20Document%20v.2%20final%2002.09.2024.pdf).

## Where do Bikeshare bikes go?

Cornell requires Ithaca Bikeshare bikes to be parked at racks. Returning one to a Bikeshare hub waives the next start-up fee, as the [Cornell Daily Sun](https://cornellsun.com/2023/01/24/ithaca-bikeshare-a-comprehensive-guide-to-ithacas-newest-transportation-option/) explained at launch in 2023. Current prices and rules are in our [Bikeshare guide](/cornell/getting-around/ithaca-bikeshare-cost-cornell-students/).

## What about winter?

Cornell's cycling page says nothing about winter storage or riding. Campus snow removal covers roads, lots, steps and sidewalks, and it does not mention bike lanes or bike paths ([Cornell Facilities](https://fcs.cornell.edu/services/repair-maintenance/snow-removal)). Our [winter biking guide](/cornell/getting-around/biking-in-ithaca-winter-cornell/) covers riding in the snow.

## What about parking off campus?

Collegetown and downtown streets follow city rules. This page has no city rule for motorcycles, so call the City Clerk or see our [Collegetown parking guide](/cornell/getting-around/parking-collegetown-downtown-ithaca-garages-permits/).

## What to do next

Register your plate or bike, buy a lock, and find the rack nearest your building before the semester's first week.
