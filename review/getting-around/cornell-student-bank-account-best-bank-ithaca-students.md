Cornell does not recommend one bank ([Cornell Global Learning](https://international.globallearning.cornell.edu/getting-started/your-first-week-0)), but Alternatives Federal Credit Union and Tompkins Bank & Trust's student checking are the easiest starting points because both publish low fees. You do not need a Social Security number to open an account, though each bank handles that differently.

```glance
- Alternatives charges a $2 monthly fee that goes away if you choose eStatements.
- Tompkins student checking has no monthly fee, for full or part-time students up to age 25.
- Cornell says you need no SSN to open an account, but rules differ by bank.
- Set up direct deposit within 24 hours of a refund being generated, or you get a check.
```

## Do you need a local bank account?

Not required, but useful. Cornell's Bursar says a local account lets you write checks to off-campus businesses that don't always take out-of-town checks, and bank representatives visit campus during orientation ([Cornell Bursar](https://bursar.cornell.edu/students-parents/student-resources/local-banking-services)). The Bursar also lists banks with branches outside Ithaca, so you can keep the account after you leave.

The one thing you do need is a U.S. checking or savings account if you want refunds by direct deposit. You have 24 hours after a refund is generated to set up direct deposit, or a check is issued. Direct deposit arrives in 2 to 3 business days, and a check takes 7 to 10 business days after printing ([Cornell Bursar](https://bursar.cornell.edu/students-parents/deposit-refunds)). Set it up before your first refund.

## How do Ithaca banks compare?

The Bursar lists nine institutions with phone numbers but no fees, addresses or SSN rules. This table uses each bank's own page for fees and Cornell's international page for SSN rules.

| Bank | Published fees | SSN rule |
|---|---|---|
| Alternatives FCU | $2 one-time enrollment, $5 kept on deposit, no checking minimum, $2 monthly fee waived with eStatements ([Alternatives](https://www.alternatives.org/accounts/checking/)) | Needs only a lease with a local address |
| Tompkins Bank & Trust | No monthly fee, $3.50 for paper statements, $0 to open in a branch and $25 online ([Tompkins](https://www.tompkinsbank.com/student-checking-101)) | Student account without an SSN, for students up to age 25 |
| CFCU | Not on its student page | Debit card and online banking without an SSN or ITIN |
| Ithaca Community Bank | Check its fee page | Accounts, debit cards and online banking without an SSN |
| M&T | Check its fee page | Debit cards and online banking without an SSN |
| Bank of America | Check its fee page | Requires a driver's license and passport, in person |

Source: SSN rules from [Cornell Global Learning](https://international.globallearning.cornell.edu/getting-started/your-first-week-0); fees from each bank's page.

## Which bank fits which student?

- **You want the cheapest, simplest account:** Alternatives. Its ID list covers a state driver's license, learner's permit, non-driver ID or U.S. passport, plus a paycheck stub, utility bill or signed lease as proof of address ([Alternatives](https://www.alternatives.org/accounts/open-an-account/)). Its Ithaca branch is at 125 N. Fulton Street, and ATMs are surcharge-free at over 30,000 locations through the CO-OP Network.
- **You are 25 or younger and a student:** Tompkins student checking. It charges no fee for using non-Tompkins ATMs, though the ATM's owner can still charge its own. At 26 the account moves to Direct Checking terms.
- **You want a bigger ATM network:** Beginnings, which lists more than 85,000 surcharge-free ATMs ([Beginnings](https://www.beginnings.com/personal/deposit-accounts/banking-for-college-students)). You can join if you live, work, worship, attend school or own a business in its service area and keep $5 in a share account ([Beginnings](https://www.beginnings.com/about-us/about-beginnings-credit-union/who-can-become-a-member)).
- **You will leave the area:** the Bursar lists Bank of America, M&T, Key and Citizens, but Cornell's page gives Syracuse as the nearest Bank of America. Confirm an Ithaca branch before you rely on one.

```reader "If you're an international student"
Use Alternatives, CFCU, Ithaca Community Bank or M&T from the table, and bring your passport and lease. International students generally need authorized employment to be eligible for an SSN, with exceptions such as J-2 dependents with a work card ([Cornell Global Learning](https://international.globallearning.cornell.edu/employment-and-taxes/apply-ssn)). ## How do you open an account in your first week?
```

```figure

  - label: Open the account in person with ID
```

Open in person if you can, since Bank of America requires it. Then add the account for refunds: log in to Nelnet Business Services with your NetID, choose Direct Deposit and enter your routing and account numbers ([Cornell Bursar](https://bursar.cornell.edu/students-parents/deposit-refunds)).

## What mistakes should you avoid?

- Waiting for your first refund before setting up direct deposit.
- Assuming "no SSN needed" means every service works. Ask whether online banking and a debit card are included.
- Ignoring the Tompkins age cap if you are a graduate student.
- Picking by branch near campus alone. Compare ATM networks and what happens after you graduate.

## FAQ

**Can I open an account before I arrive?** Cornell's pages do not say. Alternatives asks for proof of address, which many students have only after signing a lease, so ask each bank whether it accepts an application before you arrive.

**Is a credit union better than a bank?** Cornell does not rank them. Compare published fees and ATM access.

**Does Cornell have a partner bank?** No. The Bursar describes bank representatives on campus at orientation but states no partnership.
