Wear a down coat, mittens, a hat that covers your ears and insulated boots with thick, treaded rubber soles. Cornell's International Services office calls [down the warmest coat fabric](https://international.globallearning.cornell.edu/life-cornell/student-resources) and says January lows can dip below 0°F. Add layers underneath that you can take off indoors.

```glance
- A down coat is warmest, wool is next, and leather alone is not warm enough.
- Mittens are warmer than gloves; cover your ears and hands.
- Boots need insulation and thick treaded rubber soles; waterproof them if they are leather.
- Cornell's Access Fund can pay up to $100 for boots or $150 for a coat, hat and gloves.
```

## What does an Ithaca winter feel like?

Cold, windy and snowy from December through March. The average January temperature is 22.8°F, and the average January snowfall is 16.6 inches ([Northeast Regional Climate Center](https://www.nrcc.cornell.edu/wxstation/ithaca/normal.html)). A year's normal snowfall is 62.9 inches.

| Month | Average temperature | Average snowfall |
|---|---|---|
| November | 38.6°F | 4.6 in. |
| December | 29.1°F | 12.2 in. |
| January | 22.8°F | 16.6 in. |
| February | 24.1°F | 14.5 in. |
| March | 31.3°F | 12.0 in. |
| April | 43.7°F | 2.8 in. |

Source: Northeast Regional Climate Center, Ithaca normals. The first inch of snow normally arrives around Nov. 25, according to [The Cornell Daily Sun](https://cornellsun.com/2021/11/17/delayed-first-snowfall-in-ithaca-reflects-changing-climate-patterns/), which reported the date moved from Nov. 20 with the newest 30-year averages.

Averages hide the worst weeks. Winter 2025-26 ran 3.1°F below normal, with about 19 days that never rose above freezing from Jan. 23 to Feb. 11 and a low of -7°F on Jan. 30 ([The Cornell Daily Sun](https://cornellsun.com/article/2026/03/5z6poblqwoea)). Wind makes it worse: at 0°F with a 15 mph wind, the wind chill is -19°F, and exposed skin can freeze in 30 minutes ([National Weather Service](https://www.weather.gov/safety/cold-wind-chill-chart)).

## What should I wear on my body?

Dress in layers, with a water-resistant jacket on top, according to [Cornell Health](https://health.cornell.edu/about/news/cold-weather-safety). A layered outfit lets you shed a sweater in a warm lecture hall and add it back for the walk across the Arts Quad.

**Packing checklist**

- A down coat, or a wool coat if you have no down one
- Layers to wear under it, so you can adjust indoors
- A hat that covers your ears
- Mittens, which are warmer than gloves
- Insulated boots with thick treaded rubber soles
- Silicone waterproofing spray for all-leather boots

## What boots should I buy?

Choose insulated hiking boots with thick treaded rubber soles, or waterproof all-leather boots treated with silicone spray, according to Cornell International Services. Treads matter on campus walks, because Cornell's snow removal page says grounds crews clear roads, lots, steps and sidewalks, but response times vary with the snowfall. Cornell's page urges people to walk like a penguin on slippery ground ([Cornell Facilities](https://fcs.cornell.edu/services/repair-maintenance/snow-removal)).

Salt helps less as it gets colder. A pound of salt melts 46 pounds of ice at 30°F but barely 5 pounds at 10°F, according to [Cornellians magazine](https://alumni.cornell.edu/cornellians/cold-comforts-of-a-big-red-winter), so expect slick patches in the coldest weeks. If you find a hazard, report it to Cornell Customer Service at 607-255-5322.

```reader "If you're coming from a warm climate or another country"
Plan for temperatures well below what you are used to. Cornell International Services says the average high is 31°F and lows can fall below 0°F. Pack your warmest coat and boots, and ask the International Services office where to shop for the rest.
```

```reader "If you're on a tight budget"
Cornell's Access Fund, run by the Dean of Students through First-Generation and Low-Income Student Support, gives up to $100 for boots or $150 for a coat, hat and gloves ([Cornell Graduate School](https://gradschool.cornell.edu/announcements/grad-tips-winter-clothing-resources)). The same list names stores, thrift shops and free clothing closets at Catholic Charities of Tompkins/Tioga and the Women's Opportunity Center. ## How do I spot frostbite?
```

Watch for prickling, numbness, a color change or waxy-looking skin, and do not rub the area or apply direct heat ([Cornell Health](https://health.cornell.edu/about/news/cold-weather-safety)). Cover your hands and ears whenever you go outside. Cornell Health's 24/7 advice line is 607-255-5155.

## What if I ride a bike in winter?

Cornell publishes no winter riding guidance, and its snow removal page does not mention bike lanes. Gear for cyclists is covered in the [winter biking guide](/cornell/getting-around/biking-in-ithaca-winter-cornell/). Most first-years walk and ride TCAT, which is covered in the [first-year getting around guide](/cornell/getting-around/getting-around-cornell-first-year/).

## Where can I buy winter clothes in Ithaca and how much will it cost?

The sources behind this guide name no store, price range or sale dates. Cornell's graduate school lists stores and thrift shops on its winter clothing page, linked above. Compare prices there, then check second-hand shops before you buy new. The Access Fund caps are the only dollar figures Cornell publishes for winter gear.

## What to do next

1. Check the packing list against what you own.
2. Buy boots and a coat first; they cost the most.
3. If money is tight, apply to the Access Fund before the first snowfall.
4. Save 607-255-5155 in your phone.
