```glance
- About 95% of Cornell grad students rent private housing; Cornell's own grad complex, Hasbrouck, has 338 units.
- A Hasbrouck 1BR costs $1,415 a month for 2026-27, with gas, electricity and internet included.
- A typical Collegetown 1BR costs about $4,400 a year more than Cornell's grad housing budget.
- Look for an August lease in March or April, and apply to Hasbrouck the day applications open.
```

On March 24, 2023, Cornell graduate students marched to the College of Veterinary Medicine while the trustees met inside. One of them, Elias Beltran, told the crowd he could not afford to rent a home in Ithaca for his family. He and his wife found a clean, affordable place in Newfield instead. Between them, that meant almost two hours of commuting a day ([Cornell Daily Sun](https://cornellsun.com/2023/03/24/cornell-graduate-students-united-protests-for-competitive-wages-additional-university-support/)).

His choice is the grad housing story in one commute. Cornell owns homes for only a small share of its graduate students. Everyone else rents privately, in a city where undergraduates set the pace in the neighborhoods closest to campus. The trick is to know which parts of that market are built for you, and when they open.

The numbers show the gap. About 95% of Cornell's graduate and professional students live in housing the university does not own ([Cornell Graduate & Professional Housing](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/campus-housing/graduate-professional-student-campus-housing)). About 400 live on campus in a typical year, and just over half of those households are families or couples ([Your Cornell Community](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/campus-housing/graduate-professional-student-campus-housing/your-cornell-community)). Cornell's financial aid office budgets $19,962 for a year of graduate housing in Ithaca for 2026-27 ([Cornell Financial Aid](https://finaid.cornell.edu/graduate-students)). The minimum 12-month stipend for a teaching or research assistant is $48,912 for 2026-27 ([Cornell Graduate School stipend rates](https://gradschool.cornell.edu/financial-support/stipend-rates/)). Cornell's own housing estimate is 40.8% of the minimum stipend. The short version for your situation is on the [grad student start page](/start/grad-student/).

## Every Cornell-run option, side by side

Cornell controls three kinds of grad housing: Hasbrouck Apartments, Maplewood Apartments on Cornell-owned land, and five of its eight student co-ops, which are "also open to graduate and professional students" (Cornell Graduate & Professional Housing). The International Scholar House serves visiting scholars, not degree students. None of it is required: graduate students "can choose on-campus housing, or off-campus options throughout the community," Cornell says.

## How Cornell ended up housing so few grads

Hasbrouck began with a bequest. Charles Hasbrouck died in 1910 without heirs and left his farm at Forest Home to Cornell in his will ([HistoryForge](https://tompkins.historyforge.net/narratives/8978)). Half a century later, Cornell built student apartments on the land, a former poultry farm. The project was meant mainly for married students ([Forest Home Improvement Association timeline](https://www.fhia.org/wp-content/uploads/Highlights/HasbrouckBenchHistoryTimeline.pdf)). In November 1960, The Ithaca Journal reported that the two-story garden apartments, then under construction, would be named for Hasbrouck. The plan called for 246 apartments at a cost of $4,137,000, financed through the State Dormitory Authority ([HistoryForge, quoting The Ithaca Journal](https://tompkins.historyforge.net/narratives/7314)).

The complex has stayed a family place. In 2012, the Cornell Chronicle counted 89 families with about 120 children at Hasbrouck, from 47 countries. Its youth and family coordinator, Candace Mingins, called it "a mini-United Nations" ([Cornell Chronicle](https://news.cornell.edu/stories/2012/03/mingins-brings-international-community-together)). There was one break. While Cornell built its West Campus houses, undergraduates lived at Hasbrouck. By fall 2008 it was back to graduate students, families, visiting scholars and postdocs only ([Cornell Chronicle, 2008](https://news.cornell.edu/stories/2008/09/grad-students-return-hasbrouck)).

The second complex has a shorter, rockier history. Cornell built Maplewood Park on East Hill in 1989. In spring 2015, it announced it would close the complex at the end of July 2016, because repairs and upgrades would cost too much ([Cornell Chronicle, 2016](https://news.cornell.edu/stories/2016/02/board-gives-green-light-new-grad-student-housing)). At the time, Maplewood Park had 394 units housing 480 residents ([Cornell Daily Sun, 2016](https://cornellsun.com/2016/02/03/trustees-approve-plans-expanding-graduate-student-housing/)). Then-President Elizabeth Garrett had promised the graduate student assembly she would make grad housing a priority (Cornell Daily Sun, 2016).

Cornell chose a new model for the rebuild. In January 2016, a trustee committee picked EdR, a national student housing company. Cornell kept the land. EdR would pay for, build and run the new complex (Cornell Chronicle, 2016). That deal is called a public-private partnership: a private company builds and manages housing on a school's land. At the May 2017 groundbreaking, the Graduate and Professional Student Assembly said Cornell had room on campus for only 4 percent of its grad and professional students ([Cornell Daily Sun, 2017](https://cornellsun.com/2017/05/07/renovations-to-grad-only-housing-complex-set-for-august-2018-completion)).

Maplewood opened late. In August 2018, 106 students learned they could not move in on Aug. 20 as planned, because construction ran four to six weeks behind. EdR offered three choices. Students could stay with friends or in an Airbnb, with prorated rent and a $200 gift card each day. They could take a hotel EdR paid for, plus $100 a day, while still paying rent. Or they could cancel their lease without penalty ([Cornell Daily Sun, 2018](https://cornellsun.com/2018/08/12/delay-in-maplewood-housing-opening-affects-over-100-cornell-graduate-students/)). Later that year, EdR merged into Greystar, which runs Maplewood today ([GPSA housing report, 2020](https://assembly.cornell.edu/sites/default/files/gpci_-_housing_dec_2020.pdf)).

Together the two complexes still leave most grads to the private market. In December 2020, the graduate student assembly estimated that Hasbrouck and Maplewood covered the housing needs of 15% to 20% of grad and professional students. Only about 5% lived in housing Cornell owned outright (GPSA housing report, 2020).

## Hasbrouck: the family village on North Campus

Hasbrouck sits on North Campus, a 10-minute walk from central campus and served by frequent buses. It has 338 units, furnished and unfurnished, for 705 residents. Each unit has its own kitchen or kitchenette and one bathroom. The community center, open 24 hours a day, holds a service desk for mail, a TV room, a conference room and laundry. The complex is next to the university golf course and near playing fields, courts and an indoor pool (Hasbrouck Apartments).

![Hasbrouck Apartments](slot:hasbrouck)

Single students can rent a studio, a one-bedroom, or one bedroom in a shared two-bedroom townhouse. Couples and families can rent a one-bedroom, a third-floor two-bedroom apartment or a two-bedroom townhouse. Every contract is a 12-month contract (Hasbrouck Apartments).

Rates include gas, electricity, garbage pickup and internet up to 200 GB a month. Cornell asks for no security deposit. It bills grad residents on 12-month contracts every month through the Bursar, Cornell's student billing office. The first month's rent goes on the August bill (Cornell Housing Rates 2026-27).

That bundle is what makes Hasbrouck cheap. The one-bedroom at $1,415 a month comes to $16,980 a year. The average Ithaca one-bedroom in large buildings rents for $2,063 a month, or $24,756 a year ([RentCafe](https://www.rentcafe.com/average-rent-market-trends/us/ny/ithaca/)). That gap is $7,776 a year. Hasbrouck's rate also covers utilities, while RentCafe does not say whether its rents do. RentCafe's average draws only on buildings with 50 or more units, so it leaves out the small landlords who own much of Ithaca.

Grad students have named the trade-offs. The graduate student assembly's 2020 report asked Cornell to install dishwashers, provide air conditioners with no installation fee and lift the 200 GB data cap for families. It said Hasbrouck had only one faculty fellow and asked for better night and weekend buses. It also asked Cornell to cut the price of the Hasbrouck parking permit, because the buses often could not meet family needs (GPSA housing report, 2020). The resident permit for Hasbrouck costs $752.98 a year ([Cornell Parking for Students](https://fcs.cornell.edu/departments/transportation-delivery-services/parking-campus/parking-students)).

Hasbrouck applications open on a set date, then run on a rolling basis. Current residents could renew from Feb. 3, 2026. New applications for 2026-27 opened at noon on April 1, 2026 ([Apply for Housing](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/housing-and-residential-life-and-faculty-involvement-campus/apply-housing-campus)), and Cornell takes them on a rolling basis after that (Cornell Graduate & Professional Housing). If you have a disability that affects your housing, register with Student Disability Services before you apply.

```reader "If you're coming with a partner or children"
Hasbrouck is built for you. Family units hold two to four people, with no more than two adults (Cornell Graduate & Professional Housing). The unfurnished family townhouse at $1,475 a month comes to $17,700 a year. Children 5 and older who live at Hasbrouck can attend Ithaca City School District schools free, and Cornell's Work/Life office (worklife@cornell.edu) helps with child care (Your Cornell Community). A partner with a Cornell ID can buy the student OmniRide bus pass at 116 Maple Ave. ([Cornell bus passes](https://fcs.cornell.edu/departments/transportation-delivery-services/alternative-transportation-options/bus-services-privileges-omniride-passes)). If you rent privately and only you sign the lease, New York lets a single tenant live with immediate family, plus one more occupant and that person's dependent children. You must tell the landlord the occupant's name within 30 days of move-in ([NY Real Property Law 235-f](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/RPP/235-F)). Our [family housing guide](/cornell/housing/cornell-student-family-housing-ithaca/) covers family units and schools.
```

## Maplewood: the public-private bet on East Hill

Maplewood is the bigger of the two complexes. It sits at 121 Veterans Place, on 17 acres of Cornell land, in five apartment buildings, 21 townhouses and stacked flats (LeChase Construction). Every apartment is furnished, and each resident gets a private bathroom. Units have an in-unit washer and dryer, and the complex is pet-friendly with a free gym (Cornell Graduate & Professional Housing). Greystar says rent includes electricity, trash, internet and cable (Greystar's Maplewood listing).

![Maplewood Apartments](slot:maplewood)

To get a Maplewood rent, call the leasing office at (607) 821-0248; Greystar's page posts none. It does list one fee: a $130 community amenity fee per leaseholder, due at move-in. It also requires renters liability insurance, paid to a third party (Greystar's Maplewood listing). New York law bars landlords from charging most fees before or at the start of a tenancy, apart from a background and credit check capped at $20 ([NY Real Property Law 238-a](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/RPP/238-A)). Ask Maplewood in writing what the $130 covers before you pay it.

The best public price is on Cornell's College of Veterinary Medicine list for prospective students, updated February 2026: $1,075 to $1,330 for studios to four-bedrooms (Cornell vet college housing list). The list does not say whether that is per bed or per unit, so ask the leasing office before you compare it with anything else.

Grad students have mixed views of the place. In its 2020 report, the graduate student assembly said many residents renew after their first year, but resident comments suggested they stay partly because of the location and a lack of better options, not real satisfaction. Residents reported maintenance staff entering apartments without warning. The complex had only six guest parking spots. The report asked for a set share of units held for newly admitted students each year (GPSA housing report, 2020). Those findings date from 2020, so ask current residents whether they still hold.

The same report explains why first-year students land at Maplewood and Hasbrouck. Signing up is easy from far away. You do not have to travel to Ithaca, judge a landlord or decode a lease (GPSA housing report, 2020). That ease is worth something if you are moving from abroad in August.

## The co-ops that take grad students

Five Cornell co-ops take grad students, and they are among its cheapest beds. The students who live in each house run it: they cook, clean, keep up the building and vote on house rules. Houses range from 10 to 35 members, and several mix undergraduates and graduate students ([Cornell Cooperative Housing](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/campus-housing/upper-level-undergraduates/cooperative-housing)).

Redbud Cooperative in Von Cramm Hall is the largest co-op on campus. Its residents mix undergraduates and grad students, and many are international or exchange students. They share nightly dinners (Redbud). Its rates are the lowest in Cornell's co-op table (Cornell Housing Rates 2026-27). A Redbud double costs $3,487 a semester, or $6,974 for the academic year (two semesters). A regular single in a Hasbrouck townhouse costs $620 a month, or $7,440 for a full year.

That comparison hides a catch. Co-op licenses run only for the academic year: from 9 a.m. on Aug. 21, 2026, to 2 p.m. on May 23, 2027. You must also be registered full time, at least 12 credit hours ([Co-op Housing Terms 2026-27](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/housing-and-residential-life-and-faculty-involvement-campus/rates-amenities-licenses/co-op-housing-2026-2027-terms-and-conditions)). A PhD student who works in Ithaca all summer needs somewhere else to live from late May to late August.

Each house runs its own selection. Selection starts in early February and early November, so contact the house you want well before then (Cornell Cooperative Housing).

```reader "If you're a postdoc, visiting scholar or here for one semester"
Cornell offers some visiting faculty and affiliates Hasbrouck apartments as space allows, for a short stay, an academic year or a full year. It does not guarantee a place. Apply through the graduate student process, or email gradhousing@cornell.edu for temporary housing ([Visiting Faculty & Affiliate Housing](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/campus-housing/visiting-faculty-affiliate-campus-housing)). International visiting scholars can also ask about the International Scholar House, which rents furnished rooms month to month. Prof. Jane Marie Law told the Sun in 2024 that some visiting scholars get only $600 to $800 a month in living money from their home governments (Cornell Daily Sun, 2024). If your stay is under a year, avoid a 12-month private lease unless the landlord agrees in writing to a sublet. Our [furnished housing guide for scholars and postdocs](/cornell/housing/visiting-scholar-postdoc-furnished-housing-ithaca/) lists short-stay options.
```

## The private market, neighborhood by neighborhood

Most grad students end up here. Ithaca sits among three hills with the lake to the north, and Cornell is on East Hill ([Reed Group lab manual](https://reedgroup.github.io/GraduateStudentResources/LifeAtCornell.html)). Where you live decides whether your commute is a level walk, a climb or a bus ride. Cornell's own guide to neighborhoods says many graduate students prefer downtown and Fall Creek for lower rents and a city feel ([Cornell Ithaca Neighborhoods](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/campus-housing/housing-search-process/ithaca-neighborhoods)).

**Fall Creek.** This is the classic grad neighborhood. It is mostly subdivided houses, single houses and apartments over shops, at the bottom of East Hill (Cornell Ithaca Neighborhoods). A housing guide written by the Reed Group, a research lab in Cornell's civil and environmental engineering school, calls it a popular place for grad students and puts the bus at about 15 minutes. It notes that big grocery stores need a bus or a car (Reed Group lab manual). On Cornell's listing site, a landlord at 709 N. Tioga St. advertised two one-bedrooms at $1,600 and $1,700 a month. The landlord pays internet and water, and the tenant pays electricity and buys garbage tags (Cornell Off-Campus Housing Marketplace listing).

**Downtown.** Ithaca Renting asks $1,525 to $1,725 a month for its downtown one-bedrooms for 2026-27. Its downtown rents include heat, hot and cold water and trash. Electricity is included only in a few buildings, such as the Colonial Building and Commons Studios (Ithaca Renting downtown). Two people splitting one of its downtown two-bedrooms at the typical $992.50 per bed each pay $11,910 a year (12 times $992.50).

**East Hill and Belle Sherman.** Cornell describes these areas as quieter, with a few apartment complexes and a level walk to campus. East Hill Plaza and Judd Falls Plaza are close by (Cornell Ithaca Neighborhoods). Maplewood is the big complex here.

**Lansing and the Northeast.** These suburbs north of Cayuga Heights have large and small complexes and the malls. Cornell calls them less expensive but recommends a bus or car (Cornell Ithaca Neighborhoods). The Reed Group guide puts Lansing about 20 minutes away by bus (Reed Group lab manual).

**Collegetown.** It is the closest and the most expensive. Cornell says it is popular with mostly undergraduate students, which raises rent, noise and congestion (Cornell Ithaca Neighborhoods). Ithaca Renting's Collegetown one-bedrooms for 2027-28 list at $1,625 to $2,545 a month. Taking the midpoint of each of its nine listings, the middle one is $2,030 (Ithaca Renting Collegetown). Undergraduates also sign Collegetown leases far ahead. By Nov. 10, 2026, Ithaca Renting had already marked 15 of its Collegetown listings sold out.

The neighborhood comparison in more depth is in our guide to [Collegetown, Fall Creek and downtown](/cornell/housing/collegetown-vs-fall-creek-vs-downtown-ithaca-where-to-live/).

### Where the city's rules stop

The neighborhood name matters less than the town line. Ithaca's city rules protect renters inside the City of Ithaca. Since May 31, 2022, a city landlord must give written notice at least 120 days before renewing your lease or advertising your unit to new renters. The rule covers leases of nine months or longer. You can waive it only by initialing a bolded statement on the lease's first page ([WSKG](https://wskg.org/ithaca-landlords-must-now-give-renters-120-days-before-renewing-leases/)). Inside the city, you can also check whether a building has a current Certificate of Compliance, the city's rental safety permit, online or at (607) 274-6508 ([Cornell Rental Listings & Resources](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/campus-housing/housing-search-process/rental-listings-resources)). Lansing, Cayuga Heights and parts of East Hill sit outside the city, so ask your town or village which rules apply there.

```reader "If you're an international grad student"
You don't need a U.S. Social Security number to rent an apartment ([Cornell International Services](https://international.globallearning.cornell.edu/life-cornell/faq-international-students)). New York caps a security deposit plus any advance rent at one month's rent, so refuse a demand for several months up front. A landlord who keeps part of the deposit must send an itemized list within 14 days of move-out ([NY General Obligations Law 7-108](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/GOB/7-108)). For security reasons, only people who can prove a Cornell affiliation can contact landlords on Cornell's listing site (Cornell Rental Listings & Resources). Denise Thompson, who manages Cornell's Off-Campus Living office, said in 2024 that a dispute with a landlord does not affect your visa status or your standing at Cornell ([Cornell Graduate School](https://www.gradschool.cornell.edu/announcements/grad-tips-renting/)). If you cannot visit Ithaca before August, Hasbrouck or Maplewood saves you from signing a private lease sight unseen. Our [international renter's guide](/cornell/housing/off-campus-housing-cornell-international-students/) covers deposits, documents and checking a unit from abroad.
```

## Getting up the hill

Your bus pass depends on your degree and your year. In your first year, every new degree student, grad students included, rides TCAT, the local bus system, free with a Cornell ID. For fall entrants, that ends on Aug. 31 of the next year. After that, PhD students and graduate assistants get free OmniRide passes, which give unlimited rides (Cornell bus passes).

Master's and professional students lose the free daytime rides after year one. They still ride free after 6 p.m. on weekdays and all day on weekends. A daytime ride costs $1.50 in cash, or they can buy the annual OmniRide pass for $200. The pass is prorated by the month and expires on Aug. 31. You can return it for a refund until March 31, minus a $40 fee (Cornell bus passes). The pass pays for itself on the 134th ride ($200 divided by $1.50 is 133.3). Riding up and back each weekday before 6 p.m., that takes just over 13 weeks.

Plan around one gap. No TCAT buses run from 1:30 a.m. to 4 a.m. ([Cornell Sustainability](https://sustainable.cornell.edu/focus-areas/transportation/bus-commuting)). If you work late in a lab and live in Lansing, that matters more than the fare.

```reader "If you're bringing a car"
Your permit depends on where you live and how you are funded. A grad student on a full assistantship who works on the Ithaca campus can buy faculty and staff permits. For commuters who do not live in university housing, that includes the no-fee A permit and the no-fee E permit, which requires you to work in its zone. Hasbrouck residents cannot get either free permit. Other grads can buy the B commuter permit for $359.77 a year, if they qualify as commuters; it is limited and first come, first served (Cornell Parking for Students). Inside the City of Ithaca, the odd-even rule runs from Nov. 1 to April 1 when snow makes it necessary. On odd dates, park overnight on the odd-numbered side of the street, and on even dates on the even side. A car left unmoved for 72 hours can be towed ([City of Ithaca parking rules](https://www.cityofithacany.gov/727/Parking-Regulations-Violations)). Ask any landlord whether a spot comes with the unit and whether it is assigned.
```

## What comes next

Three questions will shape grad housing over the next two years.

The first is pay. The union contract runs until May 1, 2027 ([Cornell Graduate School union page](https://gradschool.cornell.edu/about/union-representation/)). Whatever the next contract sets for stipends will decide whether a private one-bedroom stays inside reach.

The second is Hasbrouck's age. In 2020, the graduate student assembly warned that the complex would eventually need renovation or replacement at significant cost. It asked Cornell to set a timeline and to keep rents close to current levels when the work happens (GPSA housing report, 2020). The buildings date from the early 1960s, and Cornell's Hasbrouck page lists no renovation plan.

The third is supply. Maplewood more than doubled the old Maplewood Park, but Cornell still houses only a small share of its graduate students. Until that changes, most new grad students will do what Elias Beltran did: trade rent against distance, and hope the bus runs when they need it.
