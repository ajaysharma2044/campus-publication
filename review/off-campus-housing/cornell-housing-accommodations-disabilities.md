```glance
- Register with Student Disability Services (SDS) before you apply for accessible campus housing.
- Rising sophomores: SDS approval due April 5, 2027; SDS Room Selection is April 12.
- A disability is not an exemption from Cornell's two-year on-campus rule.
- Accommodations SDS already approved carry over. You do not need re-approval.
```

To get disability housing at Cornell, register with Student Disability Services first, then complete your housing application. SDS is the campus office that reviews disability and chronic health needs. Rising sophomores who are not yet approved must get SDS approval by April 5, 2027, to join the separate SDS Room Selection on April 12 ([Cornell Housing](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/housing-and-residential-life-and-faculty-involvement-campus/apply-housing-campus/current-undergraduate-application-process/spring-housing-selection-rising-sophomores)).

## How do you request a housing accommodation at Cornell?

You start with SDS, not with the housing office. An accommodation is a change SDS approves because of a disability, such as a room you can get into and use. Cornell says students who need accessible housing "for a disability/chronic health-related reason" must register with SDS ([Cornell Housing, new students](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/housing-and-residential-life-and-faculty-involvement-campus/apply-housing-campus/new-undergrad-application-process)).

Registration happens on the [SDS website](https://sds.cornell.edu). Questions go to SDS at 607-254-4545 or sds_cu@cornell.edu. Cornell Housing tells students to start the SDS process as soon as possible, because SDS needs time to review each request.

Approval carries over. Cornell Housing says students who already have approved accommodations do not need SDS to re-approve them for the next selection round.

## What are the deadlines for 2027-28?

The SDS approval deadline in the opening applies to rising sophomores, meaning first-years who will be second-years in fall 2027. Approval by then earns a timeslot in SDS Room Selection, one day before open selection begins ([Cornell Housing](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/housing-and-residential-life-and-faculty-involvement-campus/apply-housing-campus/current-undergraduate-application-process/spring-housing-selection-rising-sophomores)).

## Is a disability an exemption from Cornell's on-campus requirement?

No. Cornell requires full-time, Ithaca-based first- and second-year students to live in university or approved affiliated housing for two years ([Cornell residential policy](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/campus-housing/residential-policy)). The policy's exemptions cover age, marriage, legal dependents, military veterans, some transfers and commuters within 25 miles. None of them is medical.

That means a disability need is met with an accommodation inside campus housing, through SDS. It does not release you to rent off campus. A first- or second-year who signs a lease without an approved exemption "will still be billed for the full year" ([Cornell Housing requirements](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/campus-housing/requirements-qualified-university-recognized-campus-housing)).

The full exemption list, with each condition, is in our guide to [who can live off campus at Cornell](/cornell/housing/cornell-residential-requirement-exemptions-live-off-campus/). The quiz below checks your own case.

## How does it work for incoming first-years and transfers?

New students register with SDS and apply in the same Housing and Dining Portal. Cornell encourages you to apply for housing within 10 days of receiving your NetID, the Cornell login; confirmations go out from mid-June. Unless you apply to a program house, Cornell assigns your residence hall by random auto-allocation, and you do not rank halls ([Cornell Housing, new students](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/housing-and-residential-life-and-faculty-involvement-campus/apply-housing-campus/new-undergrad-application-process)).

Random allocation is the reason early SDS registration matters. Cornell Housing ties registration directly to being "assigned to housing that is accessible to you." A first-year who registers with SDS late in the summer gives SDS less time to review before rooms are set.

New transfer students live on campus for their first year, often in upper-level halls. Their SDS steps are the same as a first-year's. Placement details are in our [Cornell transfer housing guide](/cornell/housing/cornell-transfer-housing/).

```reader "If you're a graduate or professional student"
Graduate housing has no selection round. Cornell accepts graduate housing applications on a rolling basis, and it asks students with a disability-related housing need to register with SDS first ([Cornell graduate housing](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/campus-housing/graduate-professional-student-campus-housing)), so the approval is on file when your application is reviewed.
```

## What if you live off campus?

If you are a junior, senior or exempt student renting in Ithaca, call SDS at the number above and ask what support it offers for an off-campus lease. The SDS housing process covers rooms Cornell assigns, and Cornell's housing pages describe no process for requests to private landlords.

Screen for elevator access first in Ithaca's older rental stock. The buildings below list an elevator or accessible parking. An elevator in a listing does not make a unit accessible, so ask the manager about step-free entry, door widths and the bathroom before you sign.

## What should you ask SDS directly?

Ask SDS what documents it needs, how long its review takes, whether incoming first-years face a fixed SDS deadline, and how it handles assistance animals in campus housing. Cornell's housing pages answer none of these, so get SDS's answers by email to have them in writing.
