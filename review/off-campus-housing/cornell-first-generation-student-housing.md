```glance
- Aid's undergraduate budget covers a room and meals, as if you lived on campus.
- Aid does not cover deposits, first or last month's rent, or rent in months you are not registered.
- The Unlimited meal plan is fully covered by aid for eligible students.
- Cornell's co-ops are the cheapest housing it runs, from $5,530 a year for a triple.
```

Cornell's undergraduate budget includes [$13,744 for housing and $7,604 for food](https://finaid.cornell.edu/cost-to-attend), which together match the on-campus room and meal plan. Aid does not cover security deposits or first month's rent, so an off-campus lease needs cash you must find before you move in. That gap, not the monthly rent, is where first-generation students most often get stuck.

## What does financial aid pay for housing?

Aid works from a budget, not from your lease. The budget also lists books at $1,234 and personal costs at $2,182 for 2025-26, and it excludes premium on-campus or off-campus housing options (Cornell Financial Aid). The housing line equals the 2026-27 double-room rate.

The same page says aid will not cover [security deposits, first or last month's rent, or rent in months you are not registered](https://finaid.cornell.edu/living-campus). Cornell Financial Aid also will not step in if you try to break a lease mid-year.

If you live on campus, the room and meal plan are billed to you, so there is no cash cliff. The Unlimited meal plan is [fully covered by financial aid for eligible students](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/dining/meal-plans-rates/undergraduate-meal-plans).

## What is the cheapest way to live at Cornell?

The cheapest rooms Cornell runs are in its eight co-ops, which are student-run houses where members share chores and cooking. For 2026-27, co-op doubles cost $3,487 to $4,981 per semester, or $6,974 to $9,962 a year, and the cheapest triple costs $5,530 a year ([Cornell housing rates](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/housing-details/rates-amenities-licenses/housing-rates), [Cornell housing cost dataset](/datasets/ithaca-housing-cost-by-living-situation-2026-27/)).

Co-ops count as second-year housing. Each house holds 10 to 35 members and runs its own selection in early November and early February; email co-ops@cornell.edu or call 607-255-3445 ([Cornell Student & Campus Life](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/campus-housing/upper-level-undergraduates/cooperative-housing)). The Cornell Daily Sun reported in 2023 that entry runs through an event and lottery weighted by financial need and diversity factors (The Cornell Daily Sun).

Among residence halls, a single costs $15,566 a year, so the cheapest hall choice is a double or triple ([Cornell housing rates](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/housing-details/rates-amenities-licenses/housing-rates)).

## What does a cheap off-campus bed cost?

Downtown shares and big Collegetown houses are cheapest. Ithaca Renting's downtown three-bedroom shares have a [median of $795 per bed a month](https://ithacarenting.com/downtown/), which is $9,540 over 12 months. Dataset rows put Collegetown 10-bedroom shares at $9,300 a year ([Cornell housing cost dataset](/datasets/ithaca-housing-cost-by-living-situation-2026-27/)).

Against the aid budget's housing line, that bed is $4,204 under ($13,744 minus $9,540). Food, utilities and furniture come out of that gap. The [off-campus versus dorm guide](/cornell/housing/is-off-campus-cheaper-than-cornell-dorm/) runs the full comparison, and the [cheapest off-campus housing guide](/cornell/housing/cheapest-off-campus-housing-cornell/) lists more options.

## How much cash do I need to sign a lease?

New York caps a security deposit plus any prepaid rent at [one month's rent](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/GOB/7-108). On a $795 bed that means up to $1,590 at signing, which aid will not cover. Test your own numbers here:

Ask Financial Aid about timing before you commit. The [office](https://finaid.cornell.edu/living-campus) is at 203 Day Hall, 607-255-5145, finaid@cornell.edu, open in person Monday to Thursday from 10 a.m. to 1 p.m. and 2 p.m. to 4 p.m.

## Do I have to live on campus?

Most first- and second-years do. The [exemptions guide](/cornell/housing/cornell-residential-requirement-exemptions-live-off-campus/) lists who can request one. Collegetown's popular units lease early, so read the [signing calendar](/cornell/housing/when-to-sign-collegetown-lease-cornell/) before you assume a bed will be left in spring.

## What about breaks, when dorms close?

Cornell opens only a short list of halls over winter break: HILC and Loving House are free, and other open North Campus halls charge $125 per week or $500 for the full break, with applications from Oct. 1 to Dec. 1 ([Cornell winter break housing](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/campus-housing/winter-break-housing)). If home is not an option, apply in October.

The [Access Fund](https://gradschool.cornell.edu/announcements/grad-tips-winter-clothing-resources), run through the Dean of Students' First-Generation and Low-Income Student Support office, gives up to $100 for boots. The [hidden costs guide](/cornell/money/cornell-first-gen-low-income-hidden-costs/) covers the other costs aid leaves out.

## What to do next

1. Call Financial Aid and ask what your offer assumes for housing.
2. Price a co-op and a downtown share side by side with the calculator.
3. Save at least one month's rent plus a one-month deposit before you sign.
