```glance
- First- and second-years live in Cornell halls, co-ops, Greek houses or approved affiliated housing.
- Six exemptions: age 21, married, dependents, veteran, junior or senior transfer, local commuter.
- Rising sophomores must request an exemption by April 4, 2027, at 11:59 p.m.
- Sign a lease without approval and Cornell bills the full year anyway: $13,744 for a 2026-27 double.
```

Cornell requires full-time first- and second-year students on the Ithaca campus to live in university housing, with a meal plan, for two years ([Cornell Residential Policy](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/campus-housing/residential-policy)). Year two can be a hall, a co-op, a recognized fraternity or sorority house, or approved affiliated housing. To rent off campus sooner, you need an exemption Housing approves before you sign a lease.

## What is Cornell's two-year housing requirement?

It covers full-time undergraduates on the Ithaca campus in their first two years. Part-time students fall outside it. Anyone living in on-campus housing must also carry a meal plan.

In year one, first-years and new transfers are assigned to university residence halls. In year two, students choose between a residence hall and approved university-affiliated housing. That means a house owned or controlled by a registered or recognized student organization, a recognized fraternity or sorority house, or one of the university-owned co-ops.

The rule ends with the spring semester of your second year. Cornell's application page says the requirement runs "through the spring semester" of year two, and that juniors and seniors are not guaranteed campus housing ([Cornell Housing application process](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/housing-and-residential-life-and-faculty-involvement-campus/apply-housing-campus/current-undergraduate-application-process)). So a junior-year lease needs no exemption, even if you sign it as a sophomore.

Cornell's own lease advice is blunt: first- and second-years "should not sign a lease unless they have an approved exemption" ([Cornell Housing Search Process](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/campus-housing/housing-search-process)).

## Why does Cornell require two years on campus?

Cornell says students who live on campus do better academically, adjust better socially and emotionally, and build stronger communities. It ties the meal plan to nutrition and to guarding against food insecurity, which it says has risen at colleges nationwide ([Cornell Residential Policy](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/campus-housing/residential-policy)).

The sophomore year is the newer half. The Cornell Daily Sun reported that the rule took effect in fall 2022 for all first-years and sophomores ([Cornell Daily Sun](https://cornellsun.com/2021/11/09/as-cornell-requires-university-housing-for-sophomores-students-weigh-options/)). Tim Blair, executive director of housing and residential life, told The Sun that the North Campus Residential Expansion made it possible. Its five new halls held about 800 beds for sophomores and 1,200 for first-years.

The building took six and a half years. Toni Morrison and Ganędagǫ: halls opened in 2021 with 800 beds, and Barbara McClintock, Hu Shih and Ruth Bader Ginsburg halls added 1,200 more in fall 2022 ([Cornell Chronicle](https://news.cornell.edu/stories/2022/08/cornell-welcomes-students-marks-milestone-residential-life)).

Not everyone wanted it. Students quoted in the same Sun story said the rule cut into their independence. Some sophomores had lived off campus before 2022 to cook for themselves and skip the West Campus House Meal Plan (Cornell Daily Sun).

## Where can you live as a sophomore?

Most rising sophomores land in residence halls on North and South Campus. West Campus is harder to get: in past years about 20% of rising sophomores could pick a room in the West Campus House System ([Cornell Room Selection FAQs](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/residential-programs/apply-housing/current-undergrad-application-process/room-selection-faqs)). You choose a room in Spring Housing Selection, the April process that hands each rising sophomore a timeslot to reserve a room.

If you want something other than a hall, you have three kinds of affiliated housing.

**Co-ops.** Cornell owns eight cooperative houses, which residents run themselves: Prospect of Whitby, 660 Stewart, 302 Wait Avenue, Triphammer Cooperative, Von Cramm, Wait Terrace, Wari Cooperative and Watermargin ([Cornell Affiliated Housing](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/campus-living/qualified-off-campus-housing)). Each holds 10 to 35 members and picks its own residents. Selection starts in early November and early February, and four of the eight have meal plans ([Cornell Cooperative Housing](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/campus-housing/upper-level-undergraduates/cooperative-housing)). Questions go to co-ops@cornell.edu or 607-255-3445.

**Fraternity and sorority houses.** Every recognized chapter's house counts, as long as the chapter stays recognized. Three rules matter for a sophomore:

- You must be a member of the chapter when you file the Housing Application.
- Chapters are approved automatically only if they meet recognition rules and have a live-in advisor and four upper-level officers by Jan. 1 each year.
- You must affirm in the Housing Application by March 15, 2027, that you will live in the house. One line on Cornell's selection page still prints the 2026 date, a leftover from last year's cycle; go by 2027.

Timing is tight for first-years. Cornell's deferred recruitment policy makes new students wait one semester before joining. Interfraternity Council and Panhellenic formal recruitment runs the week before classes start each January ([Cornell Sorority & Fraternity Life](https://scl.cornell.edu/get-involved/sorority-fraternity-life/how-join-sorority-or-fraternity)). So a first-year who joins in January has until mid-March to commit to the house.

**Other affiliated houses.** Cornell lists three: Alpha Zeta at 214 Thurston Ave., the Center for Jewish Living at 106 West Ave. and Seal and Serpent at 305 Thurston Ave. Each runs its own intake. Cornell reviews these houses every year, and they must meet its insurance and fire-code terms, including sprinklers throughout ([Cornell affiliated housing requirements](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/campus-housing/requirements-qualified-university-recognized-campus-housing)).

Miss the March 15 affirmation and you complete year two in on-campus housing ([Cornell Spring Housing Selection](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/housing-and-residential-life-and-faculty-involvement-campus/apply-housing-campus/current-undergraduate-application-process/spring-housing-selection-rising-sophomores)). Housing checks rosters from the Office of Sorority and Fraternity Life and from each affiliated house, so confirm with your house leaders that your name is on the list before that date. Costs and how to get in are on the [co-ops and Greek houses guide](/cornell/housing/cornell-co-ops-greek-houses-cost-how-to-join/).

## Who is exempt from the requirement?

Six groups can ask to be exempt. Each exemption must be requested and approved; none applies on its own. Cornell can ask for documents to support any of them.

| Exemption | Who qualifies | Watch for |
|---|---|---|
| Age | 21 or older by the start of the academic year you apply for; or 16 or younger at the start of the year and living with a custodial parent or legal guardian all year | Turning 21 in October does not count for that year |
| Married | Married students | Apply anyway, every year |
| Dependents | Students coming to Ithaca with legal dependents | Expect a request for documents |
| Veteran | Served in active military, naval or air service and discharged under conditions other than dishonorable; includes international students who served in their home country's military | ROTC participants do not qualify |
| Transfer | Two years living on campus at your previous school, or entering Cornell as a junior or senior | A sophomore transfer without two years on campus is not exempt |
| Commuter | Lived at least six months at a permanent home with a custodial parent or legal guardian within 25 miles of campus | Housing and Residential Life may call to discuss it |

Source: Cornell Residential Policy, 2026-27.

Everyone else stays in Cornell or affiliated housing through the end of year two. The quiz below checks which group you fit, and the [exempt first- and second-year start page](/start/exempt-underclass/) collects the next steps once you're approved.

## How do you apply for an exemption, and when is it due?

You request it inside the Housing Application in the Housing and Dining Portal while the application is open. For rising sophomores who want to be exempt for 2027-28, the deadline is April 4, 2027, at 11:59 p.m., and Cornell will not consider a request received after that ([Cornell Spring Housing Selection](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/housing-and-residential-life-and-faculty-involvement-campus/apply-housing-campus/current-undergraduate-application-process/spring-housing-selection-rising-sophomores)).

Cornell won't take an exemption request after you have signed a housing license, the contract for a Cornell room, or during the term that license covers. An exemption lasts one academic year, so a first-year exemption does not carry into year two. Transfers and returning students must reapply for every year they want to be exempt.

Cornell's pages set the rising-sophomore deadline but give no separate exemption window for incoming first-years or new transfers. If that's you, ask Housing Operations when the exemption term opens before you commit to anything ([Cornell Housing directory](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/contact-us/housing-and-residential-life-directory)). The office is at 1520 Clara Dickson Hall, open 9 a.m. to 4 p.m. weekdays, at 607-255-5368 or housing@cornell.edu.

## What happens if you sign a lease without an exemption?

You pay twice. Students who neither meet the requirement nor get an exemption "will still be billed for the full year," Cornell says ([Cornell affiliated housing requirements](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/campus-housing/requirements-qualified-university-recognized-campus-housing)). A residence hall double costs $6,872 per semester, or $13,744 for 2026-27, with gas, electricity, garbage pickup and internet up to 200 GB a month included ([Cornell Housing Rates](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/housing-and-residential-life-and-faculty-involvement-campus/rates-amenities-licenses/housing-rates)). Final 2027-28 rates come out in March 2027.

Say a first-year without an exemption joins three friends in a Collegetown 4-bedroom for sophomore year. The middle 4-bedroom rate on Ithaca Renting's Collegetown list for 2027-28 is $1,246.25 per bed a month ([Ithaca Renting](https://ithacarenting.com/collegetown/)). Its leases run a full year, so 12 months comes to $14,955.00. Add a Cornell double at the 2026-27 rate, and one student's year costs $28,699.00 for one bed in use.

## Can you cancel a Cornell room once you've signed?

Not as a first- or second-year, with three exceptions. Under the 2027-28 housing license, new students, new transfers and second-years can cancel only if they leave Cornell, get an exemption approved, or (second-years only) are accepted to a Greek house, an affiliated house or one of the eight co-ops before the Spring Housing Selection deadline ([Cornell Housing License 2027-28](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/housing-and-residential-life-and-faculty-involvement-campus/rates-amenities-licenses/academic-year-housing-license-2027-2028)). The license also bars handing your room to someone else, and leaving mid-term keeps you liable for the whole term.

Returning juniors and seniors have finished the requirement and can cancel before they move in, for a fee that climbs with the calendar.

| Student | Can cancel? | Fee |
|---|---|---|
| First-year or new transfer | Only if leaving Cornell or exempt | No fee schedule set out |
| Second-year | Also if accepted to a co-op, Greek or affiliated house before the Spring Housing Selection deadline | No fee schedule set out |
| Returning junior or senior | Yes, before check-in | $50 through Jan. 31; $500 Feb. 1 to March 31; $1,000 April 1 to June 30; $2,000 July 1 to Aug. 15; then 50% of the fall room rate |

Source: Cornell Academic Year Housing License, 2027-28.

The spring semester is stricter. Cornell releases students from a spring housing contract only to graduate, go abroad or leave the university, and moving off campus or into a fraternity small residence is not an extenuating circumstance ([Cornell Sorority & Fraternity Life financial obligations](https://scl.cornell.edu/get-involved/sorority-fraternity-life/join/financial-obligations-sororities-fraternities)). To price a junior-year cancellation by date, use the [Cornell housing cancellation fee calculator](/tools/cornell-housing-license-cancellation-fee-calculator/).

## Do transfer students have to live on campus?

Yes, for at least one year unless they qualify for the transfer exemption, and Cornell's two pages on this disagree. The transfer page says all new transfers live on campus for one academic year, and spring transfers stay for their first spring plus the following academic year ([Cornell New Transfer Undergraduates](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/campus-housing/new-transfer-undergraduates)). The residential policy says transfers who lived on campus for two years at their old school, or who enter as juniors or seniors, are not subject to the requirement at all.

Go by the residential policy, because it is the rule that names exemptions. But treat yourself as covered until Housing approves the transfer exemption in writing. Placement, timing and transfer halls are on the [Cornell transfer housing guide](/cornell/housing/cornell-transfer-housing/).

## FAQ

**Can I sublet my Cornell room if I move into a chapter house?** No. The housing license lets only you and roommates Cornell assigns live in the room, and you can't transfer it to anyone else.

**Do I need a meal plan in a co-op or Greek house?** The meal-plan rule covers students in on-campus housing. Co-ops set their own food arrangements, and fraternity and sorority houses set their own room and board.
