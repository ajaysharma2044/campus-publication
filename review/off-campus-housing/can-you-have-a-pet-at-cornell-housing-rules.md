Yes, if your pet is a fish or an approved assistance animal. Cornell's undergraduate residence halls allow fish in tanks no larger than 10 gallons, plus service animals and emotional support animals approved by Student Disability Services ([Cornell housing license, 2027-28](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/housing-and-residential-life-and-faculty-involvement-campus/rates-amenities-licenses/academic-year-housing-license-2027-2028)). For a cat or dog, you need an off-campus apartment that takes pets.

```glance
- Cornell dorms: fish in tanks up to 10 gallons, nothing else unless it's an assistance animal.
- Emotional support animals need approval from Student Disability Services first.
- Rising sophomores need that approval by April 5, 2027, to join the accommodation room selection.
- Off campus, New York's one-month cap applies to any deposit or advance, which includes a pet deposit.
```

## What animals can you keep in a Cornell dorm?

Fish are the only pets allowed in Cornell's undergraduate residence halls, and the tank can hold no more than 10 gallons (Cornell housing license). That rule sits in the "Animals in Residence" section of the housing license, the contract every student signs for a campus room. It applies to every undergraduate residence hall.

The penalty for breaking it is real. If you keep any other animal, Cornell can confiscate it and send you through the student conduct process. That is on top of the university's other remedies under the license. The license also points to a university-wide rule, Cornell Policy 2.8, Pets on Campus, which applies to every animal in university housing.

Two kinds of animals are not treated as pets:

- **Service animals.** These are animals trained to do specific tasks for a person with a disability, as the Americans with Disabilities Act defines them. Cornell allows them in university housing.
- **Emotional support animals.** An ESA is an animal that eases the symptoms of a disability through its company rather than trained tasks. It is allowed only if Student Disability Services approves it as a reasonable accommodation, meaning a change to the usual rules that a disability makes necessary.

Owners of both must still follow Policy 2.8.

## How do you get an emotional support animal approved?

Start with [Student Disability Services](https://sds.cornell.edu/), known as SDS, the office that approves housing accommodations at Cornell. Graduate students get the same instruction: register with SDS first for any housing need related to a disability ([Cornell graduate housing](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/campus-housing/graduate-professional-student-campus-housing)).

Give yourself time, because SDS sets approval deadlines ahead of room selection. Our [accessibility guide](/start/accessibility/) covers disability support at Cornell more broadly.

```reader "If you're a first-year heading into sophomore room selection"
If you don't already have an approved accommodation, SDS must approve it by April 5, 2027, for you to get a timeslot in the accommodation room selection on April 12, 2027. Students already approved don't need to reapply ([Spring Housing Selection for Rising Sophomores](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/housing-and-residential-life-and-faculty-involvement-campus/apply-housing-campus/current-undergraduate-application-process/spring-housing-selection-rising-sophomores)).
```

## Can you have a pet in a Cornell co-op?

Yes, if it's small and lives in a cage or tank. Cornell's cooperative houses allow hamsters, guinea pigs, gerbils, birds and fish, as long as the animal stays caged at all times and any aquarium is under 10 gallons ([Co-op Housing Terms and Conditions, 2026-27](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/housing-and-residential-life-and-faculty-involvement-campus/rates-amenities-licenses/co-op-housing-2026-2027-terms-and-conditions)). That is the widest pet rule in undergraduate campus housing. The same confiscation penalty and the same exceptions for service animals and approved ESAs apply.

## Which Ithaca apartments allow pets?

Plenty do, but the rules change from one building to the next. Collegetown Terrace, on South Quarry Street, lists both cats and dogs as allowed on Cornell's housing marketplace ([Collegetown Terrace listing](https://offcampus.housing.cornell.edu/city/ithaca-college-ny/listing/collegetown-terrace-236694)). Lansing West Apartments, at 2250 N. Triphammer Road, is cat-only: cats are welcome with approval, and dogs are not ([Lansing West FAQ](https://www.lansingwestapts.com/faq/)).

Downtown is harder. Ithaca Renting does not allow pets in its downtown buildings except in a limited number of apartments it sets aside. Tenants in those units pay a pet rent and must give the landlord a copy of their renters insurance before moving in ([Ithaca Renting](https://ithacarenting.com/downtown/)). Its Cityview Apartments, at 118 Prospect St., lists no pets ([Cityview listing](https://offcampus.housing.cornell.edu/city/ithaca-college-ny/listing/cityview-apartments-10186)).

Some landlords set limits on size and type. Winston Square Apartments, on Winston Court in northeast Ithaca, allows one cat or dog per household under 25 pounds at full adult weight. It takes no puppies or kittens, and every pet must be spayed or neutered ([Winston Square FAQ](https://winstonsquareapts.com/faq/)). The Student Agencies Building, at 409 College Ave. in Collegetown, needs the landlord's written consent. It bans snakes, dogs over 80 pounds, several breeds including Akitas, Rottweilers and Siberian Huskies, and fish tanks over 5 gallons ([Student Agencies Building FAQ](https://www.thestudentagenciesbuilding.com/faq)).

## How much does a pet add to rent in Ithaca?

Expect about $540 to $850 for a year, based on the buildings that post their prices. Winston Square charges $45 a month in pet rent with no other pet fees, so 12 months comes to $540. A one-bedroom at 115 Stewart Ave. in lower Collegetown, listed by True Renew Homes, takes one pet of a gentle breed at the same monthly rate, which adds up to the same total over a 12-month lease ([115 Stewart Ave. listing](https://www.ithacarents.com/4304-sunny-1br-apartment-close-to-cornell.-furnished.-january-1st-move-in.-flexible-lease.)). At the Student Agencies Building, a $250 initial charge plus $50 a month for 12 months is $850.

Some landlords don't post the number at all. Triphammer Apartments charges a one-time pet fee plus monthly pet rent and asks renters to call for the amounts ([Triphammer FAQ](https://triphammerapts.com/faqs/)). Get the figures in writing before you sign.

## Does a pet deposit count toward New York's deposit limit?

Yes. Under New York law, a security deposit plus any rent paid in advance cannot exceed one month's rent. The law caps any "deposit or advance" and does not name pets, so a pet deposit falls under the same cap ([N.Y. General Obligations Law 7-108](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/GOB/7-108)). A landlord also can't take last month's rent on top of a full deposit. The cap does not cover seasonal rentals or owner-occupied co-op apartments.

Here is how that works on a real listing. The one-bedroom at 115 Stewart Ave. rents for $1,562 a month on a lease starting Jan. 1, 2027. Its security deposit and any pet deposit together can't go past $1,562. Monthly pet rent is a separate line, so read what each pet charge is called and whether any of it comes back. Our guide to [Ithaca security deposit rules](/cornell/housing/ithaca-security-deposit-rules-tenants/) explains how and when a deposit must be returned.

## Is an emotional support animal a pet in an Ithaca apartment?

Not to the landlords who address it. Lansing West says plainly that assistance animals are not pets and points renters to its process for reasonable accommodation requests. Triphammer Apartments also says assistance animals and service animals are not considered pets. Winston Square allows exceptions to its one-pet limit only as New York or federal law requires. If you have an ESA, ask the leasing office for its accommodation process before you sign, and get its answer in writing.

## What should you check in a lease if you have a pet?

Find the pet clause before anything else. Cornell's own lease checklist names pets twice: once under extra costs ("pet-related charges/deposit") and once under restrictions ([Signing the Lease, Cornell](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/campus-housing/housing-search-process/signing-lease)). A lease that allows "pets" may still limit size, breed or number, as Winston Square and the Student Agencies Building do. Our guide on [how to read an Ithaca lease](/cornell/housing/how-to-read-ithaca-lease-before-signing/) walks through the other clauses that cost students money.

## What to do next

If you live on campus, stick to the fish-tank rule above, or contact SDS well before your next room selection about an assistance animal. If you're moving off campus with a cat or dog, ask each landlord three things in writing: which animals they accept, what the pet rent and any pet deposit are, and whether the deposit total stays under the cap.
