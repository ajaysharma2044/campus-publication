On Dryden Road in Collegetown, one landlord asks two very different prices for the 2027-28 school year. At 202 Dryden Road, Ithaca Renting wants $725 to $825 a month for a bed in a 10-bedroom house. On the same street, at Collegetown Center, 151 Dryden Road, the same company asks $2,445 to $2,545 a month for a deluxe one-bedroom ([Ithaca Renting](https://ithacarenting.com/collegetown/)). The two buildings sit about the same distance from campus. The one-bedroom costs up to three and a half times as much, and the difference is nine roommates.

That street holds the whole Ithaca rental market in small form. What sets your rent is less the neighborhood than how many people share your kitchen and bathroom. Distance, heat, electricity and furniture matter, but group size moves the number most. Asking rents run from $500 a month for a room to $3,449 for a bed downtown (EduExplained inventory).

The yardstick is Cornell's own bill. A residence hall double costs $6,872 a semester for 2026-27, or $13,744 for the year, with gas, electricity, garbage pickup and internet included ([Cornell housing rates](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/housing-and-residential-life-and-faculty-involvement-campus/rates-amenities-licenses/housing-rates)). Spread over a 12-month lease, the double equals $1,145.33 a month (EduExplained calculation: the yearly rate divided by 12). For graduate students in Ithaca, Cornell budgets $19,962 a year for housing in 2026-27 ([Cornell Financial Aid](https://finaid.cornell.edu/graduate-students)). That works out to $1,663.50 a month (EduExplained calculation: $19,962 divided by 12). Those two lines, the dorm line and the grad budget line, split the ladder into five steps.

If you only need to know whether leaving the dorm saves money, see [is off-campus cheaper than a Cornell dorm](/cornell/housing/is-off-campus-cheaper-than-cornell-dorm/).

## Where these prices come from

The prices here come from an EduExplained inventory of 413 Ithaca buildings and houses, compiled in November 2026. It draws on 35 landlord and listing sites, plus Tompkins County and New York State parcel records for owners and building years. Of those 413 rows, 244 print at least one dollar figure. Some buildings appear twice under two address forms, so the 244 rows come down to 216 separate buildings. Every one is in the full list at the end of this page, linked to its listing pages (EduExplained inventory).

An asking price is what a landlord posts, not what a tenant ends up paying. Ithaca Renting tags its Collegetown prices 2027-28 and most of its downtown prices 2026-27. Most other listings print no year, so treat those prices as the latest posted asking rent (EduExplained inventory).

Every price below is one person's cost for one month. A per-bed price stays as printed. A whole-apartment price is split evenly among its bedrooms, so a three-bedroom at $2,100 becomes $700 a person (EduExplained calculation: $2,100 divided by 3).

## How to read an Ithaca price

Ithaca rents come three ways. Student buildings quote a price per bed, which is what each roommate pays. Family and graduate buildings quote a price per apartment. Cornell's co-ops and residence halls quote a price per semester. Mixing them up makes a bed look cheaper or pricier than it is. Our guide to [Collegetown rent per bed](/cornell/housing/collegetown-rent-prices-per-bed-one-bedroom/) walks through the difference with Collegetown's own numbers.

Some listings make the mix-up for you. On the Cornell and Ithaca College off-campus housing sites, the price field is labeled "per unit" even for rooms. A 10-bedroom house at 611 E. Seneca St. is listed at $885 "per unit" ([Ithaca College off-campus housing](https://offcampushousing.ithaca.edu/listing)). Whole houses of nine to 14 bedrooms list for $9,855 to $16,730 on the same site, so $885 can only be one person's share. Twenty-five of the 216 priced buildings carry a price like that, or only a price range with no basis. The full list marks them "ask." Get the basis in writing before you sign.

The lease length changes the math too. Most Ithaca leases run a full year, while Cornell bills two semesters. Some new buildings run longer. City Centre, at 301 E. State St., prices its smallest studio on a 15-month term ([City Centre Ithaca](https://citycentreithaca.com/floorplans/)).

## Why a bed costs what it does

Four forces shaped this market, and two of them came from Cornell.

**Cornell moved the first lease to junior year.** Beginning in fall 2022, Cornell required all first-years and sophomores to live in campus or university-affiliated housing ([The Cornell Daily Sun](https://cornellsun.com/2021/11/09/as-cornell-requires-university-housing-for-sophomores-students-weigh-options/)). The North Campus expansion made room for that, with about 800 new beds for sophomores and 1,200 for first-years. The same project let Cornell admit more undergraduates. Off-campus Ithaca became a market of juniors, seniors and graduate students.

**Enrollment kept growing.** The Class of 2029 is the largest in Cornell's history, and demand for student housing stayed high ([The Cornell Daily Sun](https://www.cornellsun.com/article/2025/09/301-college-avenue-available-for-student-housing-in-2026-2027-academic-year)). Cornell bought 301 College Ave. for $15 million in March 2025, after a bank foreclosed on it, and turned it into university housing for 2026-27.

**Developers built up, and built high-end.** Collegetown sits at Cornell's doorstep, and The Ithaca Voice describes its student renters as a "captive market" for developers ([The Ithaca Voice](https://ithacavoice.org/2026/07/cornell-collegetown-construction/)). Three midrise buildings were under construction there in summer 2026. The Ivy, at College Avenue and Dryden Road, has 52 units and 81 beds. The Summit, at 201 Oak Ave., replaced an 11-bedroom house with 46 units and about 88 bedrooms. The Bear, at 215 College Ave., will hold 84 units and 150 beds. Developer Charlie O'Connor of Modern Living Rentals is behind all three, with Nick Robertson as his partner on The Ivy and The Bear.

None of the three is cheap. Amenities at The Summit include LED fireplaces, smart TVs, in-unit washers and dryers, a sauna and a gaming lounge (The Ithaca Voice). At The Ivy, the one-bedrooms were spoken for in May 2026, before renters could tour a finished unit ([Tompkins Weekly](https://www.tompkinsweekly.com/news/three-new-developments-offer-different-answers-to-ithacas-housing-needs-30cfa148)). "Students just wanted something new," said Celine Wang, who works with Modern Living Rentals.

Average rents dipped slightly over the past year. RentCafe puts the average Ithaca apartment at $2,263 a month as of Oct. 30, 2026, down 0.59% from $2,277 a year earlier ([RentCafe](https://www.rentcafe.com/average-rent-market-trends/us/ny/ithaca/)). That average counts only buildings with 50 or more units, so it leaves out the houses and small buildings where many students live. Todd Fox, whose Visum Development Group built The Pinnacle on Gun Hill, told Tompkins Weekly that more supply gives renters more choices and puts downward pressure on rents.

**The city capped some increases, but not at the new towers.** Ithaca's Common Council voted 8-2 in July 2024 to opt into New York's Good Cause Eviction law ([WAMC](https://www.wamc.org/2024-07-12/ithaca-is-the-latest-upstate-city-to-opt-into-good-cause-renter-protections)). Under it, a yearly rent increase above 10%, or above 5% plus inflation if that is lower, is presumed unreasonable ([New York Homes and Community Renewal](https://hcr.ny.gov/good-cause-eviction)). Apartments built after 2009 are exempt for 30 years. Ithaca also exempts a landlord who owns just one rental property. So at renewal, an older Collegetown house faces a limit on rent increases that a 2026 tower does not.

## Under $800 a month: a room in a big house

Fifty-five of the priced buildings offer something for less than $800 a person. Almost all of it is shared. It is a bed in a house of six to 10 bedrooms, a room in someone's home, or a third of a three-bedroom downtown or on South Hill. Downtown holds 15 of these buildings and Collegetown 13 (EduExplained inventory). This is also the farthest-out step. The typical building here sits 0.81 miles from Ho Plaza in a straight line, against about half a mile at every higher step.

In Collegetown, the cheapest 2027-28 beds are in Ithaca Renting's biggest units. A bed in a six-bedroom at Aces Apartments, on Eddy Street, runs $695 to $760. A standard room at 210 Dryden Road starts at the same price and tops out at $810. The 10-bedroom at 202 Dryden Road has two and a half bathrooms, or four people per bathroom (EduExplained calculation: 10 people for 2.5 bathrooms). These Collegetown apartments come furnished, with heat and hot water included, and most tenants pay their own electricity (Ithaca Renting).

Downtown, the same landlord lists beds for 2026-27 that are still open. A share of a three-bedroom at the Old Cigar Factory on East State Street tops out at $795. A five-bedroom at Cityview Apartments, 118 Prospect St., runs $745 to $845 a bed ([Ithaca Renting downtown](https://ithacarenting.com/downtown/)). Downtown rents there include heat, water and trash removal.

Smaller landlords fill the rest of this step, some with fewer extras. PPM Homes lists a six-bedroom house at 119-121 Prospect St. at "$650 a room," fully furnished, with tenants paying utilities ([PPM Homes](https://www.ppmhomes.com/listings/detail/3f1544f9-76c1-4375-91f6-b2400a52dbbf)).

Two Cornell options belong here too. A triple at Red Bud Cooperative in Von Cramm Hall costs $2,765 a semester for 2026-27, or $5,530 for the year, the lowest semester rate on Cornell's housing rate list (Cornell housing rates). Graduate students can rent a regular single in a two-bedroom Hasbrouck townhouse for $620 a month, furnished, on a 12-month contract. That comes to $7,440 a year with utilities included (EduExplained calculation: $620 times 12).

## $800 to $1,145: still under the dorm line

This is the biggest step. A total of 103 buildings offer something in this range, and almost every one of them is shared. Collegetown has 45 of them, downtown 19 and the streets north of campus 19. Only 15 of the offers here are studios or one-bedrooms for one person (EduExplained inventory).

At this price a group of four or five can live in a Collegetown tower. A bed in a premium five-bedroom at Collegetown Court, 208 Dryden Road, runs $925 to $985 for 2027-28. At Collegetown Plaza, 111 Dryden Road, a room listed as available now runs $895 to $995. Trash pickup is included at Collegetown Court, Plaza and Center (Ithaca Renting).

Over a full year, a Collegetown Court bed at the low end of that range costs $11,100. That is $2,644 less than the Cornell double, before electricity (EduExplained calculation: the low end times 12, minus the double's yearly rate).

Downtown, this budget buys a share of a two-bedroom. The Old Cigar Factory lists two-bedroom shares at $850 to $875, and 154 The Commons at $950 to $1,050. A premium room at Whiton Hall, 222 S. Aurora St., runs $965 to $1,065 and includes electricity and Wi-Fi (Ithaca Renting downtown).

Sharing has a catch beyond the bathroom line. Most local leases bind roommates "jointly and severally," which means each one is responsible for the others' rent and damages for the whole lease ([Cornell Housing](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/campus-living/living-campus/study-abroad/housemate-compatibility-questionnaire)). A cheap bed in a big house stays cheap only while every other bed stays filled.

## $1,146 to $1,664: one roommate, or a small place alone

At this step the choice opens up. A total of 87 buildings offer something here. About half the offers are shares of two- and three-bedrooms. Many of the rest are studios and one-bedrooms for one person (EduExplained inventory).

With one or two roommates, this is Collegetown's middle market. Ithaca Renting lists a bed in a standard two-bedroom at Collegetown Plaza for $1,330 to $1,350 for 2027-28. A premium three-bedroom at Collegetown Court runs $1,185 to $1,200 a bed (Ithaca Renting).

Alone, this budget buys a small studio downtown or an older one-bedroom north of campus. The Old Cigar Factory lists budget studios at $1,175 to $1,275, and Commons Studios at $1,275 to $1,375. A furnished one-bedroom at 312 Farm St. in Fall Creek lists at $1,545 a month, plus water at $33 a person a month ([True Renew Homes listing](https://www.ithacarents.com/4385-cute-furnished-1-br-apt-in-fall-creek-that-is-closest-to-cornell)). Electricity, gas and heat are included there.

Farther out, Lansing West, at 700 Warren Road, lists one-bedrooms from $1,302 ([Lansing West on RentCafe](https://www.rentcafe.com/apartments/ny/ithaca/lansing-west-apartments/default.aspx)). It advertises rent-free living until January 2027 for renters who apply by Nov. 14, 2026, and move in by Dec. 1, with offers that vary by floor plan.

Cornell's graduate apartments sit at this step as well. At Hasbrouck, a studio costs $1,355 a month for 2026-27, and an unfurnished one-bedroom $1,415, with utilities included (Cornell housing rates). The Pinnacle, the new building on Gun Hill, lists two-bedrooms at about $3,000 a month, or $1,500 a person when split (The Ithaca Voice). Its tenants are at least 80% graduate students (Tompkins Weekly).

```reader "If you're a graduate student"
Measure against the grad budget line, not the dorm line, and price everything on a 12-month lease. PhD students and graduate assistants get a free TCAT OmniRide pass, so a cheaper place downtown or in Fall Creek costs nothing extra to reach. Hasbrouck's rent includes gas, electricity and internet; check what a private one-bedroom includes before you compare.
```

## $1,665 to $2,200: alone near campus

Above the grad budget line, the market narrows. Only 33 buildings list anything here. About half the offers are studios and one-bedrooms for one person, mostly in Collegetown and downtown (EduExplained inventory).

This is the price of living alone a short walk from class. Collegetown Center lists its studios for 2027-28 from $1,695 to $1,950. A standard one-bedroom at Collegetown Plaza runs $1,970 to $2,070 (Ithaca Renting). At the same landlord, a downtown studio costs less. The [Collegetown vs. downtown dataset](/datasets/collegetown-vs-downtown-rent-per-bed-2026/) puts the median studio gap at $370 a month.

Three or four roommates in a new tower land here too. The Summit's four-bedrooms rent for $7,200 a month (The Ithaca Voice). Split four ways, that is $1,800 a person (EduExplained calculation: the monthly rent divided by 4). The Bear's three-bedrooms are listed at $6,000, or $2,000 a person split three ways.

The Ivy's price depends on whom you ask. The Cornell Daily Sun reported in December 2025 that its three-bedrooms ranged from $5,500 to $5,800 a month ([The Cornell Daily Sun](https://www.cornellsun.com/article/2025/12/the-ivy-to-establish-new-luxury-housing-in-collegetown-opens-fall-2026)). The Ithaca Voice reported in July 2026 that they rent for up to $4,900. Split three ways, that is $1,633.33 to $1,933.33 a person (EduExplained calculation: each figure divided by 3). The two reports disagree, so get the current price from the leasing office in writing.

## Over $2,200: the new towers

Twenty buildings list something above $2,200 a person. Only four of them list nothing below it. This step is almost all one-bedrooms and studios for one person (EduExplained inventory).

Collegetown's top is the deluxe one-bedroom at Collegetown Center, at $2,445 to $2,545. The Ivy's studios start at $2,595, The Summit's studios and one-bedrooms at $2,700 and The Bear's studios at $2,850 (The Ithaca Voice). A year in a $2,595 studio costs $31,140. That is 2.27 times the Cornell double (EduExplained calculation: the studio's rent times 12, divided by the double's yearly rate).

Downtown's newest buildings price the same way. At City Centre, a studio has a base rent of $2,335 to $2,478. Its total monthly leasing price is $2,475.75 to $2,618.75 on a 15-month term (City Centre Ithaca). Theory Ithaca, at 445 E. State St., leases by the bed. Its prices run from $2,015.66 to $3,449.00 a month ([Theory Ithaca listing](https://offcampus.housing.cornell.edu/city/ithaca-college-ny/listing/theory-ithaca-239997)).

What the money buys is newness and amenities, such as in-unit laundry, gyms, rooftop terraces and study rooms (The Ithaca Voice). What it does not buy is distance. The typical building at this step sits 0.56 miles from Ho Plaza, about the same as a $900 share.

## Same money, different neighborhood

Living past a mile saves about $163 a month; adding roommates saves far more. Within half a mile of Ho Plaza, the typical priced building's cheapest spot costs $1,000 a month. Between a half mile and a mile, it is $900. Beyond a mile, it is $837.25. That is $162.75 a month less than the closest ring, or $1,953 a year (EduExplained calculation: the two medians subtracted, times 12).

At Ithaca Renting's Collegetown medians for 2027-28, a bed in a six-bedroom costs $800 and a one-bedroom alone costs $2,025 ([EduExplained housing cost dataset](/datasets/ithaca-housing-cost-by-living-situation-2026-27/)). That gap is $1,225 a month, more than seven times the distance savings (EduExplained calculation: $1,225 divided by $162.75).

## What the rent leaves out

Two places with the same rent can cost very different amounts. Electricity is one big difference. Ithaca Renting's Collegetown tenants pay their own, while its downtown rooms at Whiton Hall, Colonial Studios and Commons Studios include it (Ithaca Renting downtown). Water can be extra, as at 312 Farm St. For a full monthly bill with power, internet, insurance and trash tags, see [what a month off campus costs at Cornell](/cornell/housing/what-a-month-off-campus-costs-cornell/).

The money due at signing has a ceiling. New York law says a deposit, plus any rent paid in advance, cannot exceed one month's rent. A landlord who does not return the deposit with an itemized list within 14 days of move-out loses the right to keep any of it ([New York General Obligations Law 7-108](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/GOB/7-108)).

```reader "If you're a parent comparing this with Cornell's bill"
Compare a full year with a full year: a lease's 12 months of rent, plus electricity and internet, against the double's $13,744 with utilities included. A share at $1,000 a month comes out ahead unless electricity and internet add more than $145.33 a month (EduExplained calculation: the double's monthly equivalent minus that rent). A studio alone near campus does not come out ahead. For a student's full monthly budget beyond rent, see the [Cornell cost of living guide](/cornell/money/cornell-cost-of-living-ithaca-student-monthly-budget/).
```

```reader "If you're a sophomore planning junior year"
Pick your group size before you tour, because it sets your budget step. The big Collegetown shares go first. On Nov. 10, 2026, Ithaca Renting already marked 14 of its 67 Collegetown listings sold out. If your group wants a six- or 10-bedroom for 2028-29, start looking in September 2027.
```

## What comes next

The top of the market keeps growing. The Bear is scheduled to open for the 2027 academic year with 150 beds (Tompkins Weekly). Prestige Ithaca, a 77-unit building at the old Collegetown fire station, 309 College Ave., is planned for completion in summer 2028 (The Ithaca Voice). The Ruby, a 35-unit project planned for 228 Dryden Road, is seeking new approvals.

Cornell's own yardstick will move too. Final 2027-28 housing rates will be posted in March 2027 ([Cornell 2027-28 housing license](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/housing-and-residential-life-and-faculty-involvement-campus/rates-amenities-licenses/academic-year-housing-license-2027-2028)). The prices shown at fall housing selection are projections that still need approval from the president and trustees.

The open question is whether all those new beds pull down the price of an old room. Every student who signs at a new tower is one fewer renter for an older house. If the towers keep coming, the cheapest steps of this ladder could get cheaper, or the oldest houses could sit empty longer. The answer will show up first in the price of a bed in a big Collegetown house each fall.
