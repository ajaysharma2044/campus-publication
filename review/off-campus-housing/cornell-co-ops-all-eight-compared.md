To win a room at Triphammer Cooperative, you first cook dinner for strangers. The big blue house sits at the corner of Triphammer and Jessup roads on North Campus ([Student & Campus Life](https://scl.cornell.edu/news-events/news/triphammer-co-op-fosters-community-and-growth-cornellians)). Each applicant must come to two of the house's open events and help one cook group make the evening meal. Only then does a name go into the house lottery ([Triphammer Cooperative](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/campus-housing/upper-level-undergraduates/cooperative-housing/triphammer-cooperative)).

Across campus, at 103 McGraw Place, the oldest of the co-ops is counting down. In 2024, Watermargin's president, Remy Kageyama, told The Cornell Daily Sun that the house roof would fail within nine years. He said the fix would cost $500,000 and that Cornell would not pay for it ([Cornell Daily Sun](https://cornellsun.com/2024/10/21/co-op-residents-face-deteriorating-houses-increasing-rent-costs/)).

That is the deal at Cornell's eight cooperative houses, or co-ops. Cornell calls them among the least expensive housing on campus ([Cooperative Housing](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/campus-housing/upper-level-undergraduates/cooperative-housing)). They count toward the rule that keeps first- and second-year students in university housing. Cornell owns the buildings, sets the rents and sends the bills (Cornell Daily Sun). The students who live inside run nearly everything else: who gets in, who cooks, who cleans and how the house votes. Each house is a small republic with its own rules, food and history.

The price explains the pull. A double in a residence hall costs $6,872 per semester for 2026-27 ([Cornell Housing rates](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/housing-and-residential-life-and-faculty-involvement-campus/rates-amenities-licenses/housing-rates)). The co-op doubles in the box above cost 51 to 72 percent of that. The catch is space. The eight house pages list 158 residents in total. The Sun counted about 161 co-op residents in eight houses back in 2001, so the system has barely grown in 25 years ([Cornell Daily Sun, 2001](https://cornellsun.com/2001/10/12/housing-alternatives-cornell-student-cooperatives)).

If you are weighing a co-op against a dorm or a fraternity or sorority house, our [co-op, Greek and dorm cost comparison](/cornell/housing/cornell-co-ops-greek-houses-cost-how-to-join/) puts the three side by side.

## How a co-op works

Cornell owns and manages the eight buildings. Inside each one lives an independent student organization that governs itself ([Cornell Student & Campus Life](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/campus-living/qualified-off-campus-housing)). Members write the house rules and do the routine cleaning and upkeep. They make big decisions by vote at house meetings. Most houses are coed, a few house only women, and several mix undergraduates with graduate students (Cooperative Housing).

Co-ops have no live-in staff, and residents elect their own officers, such as a house manager, a treasurer and a food steward. The Sun reported in 2019 that each house also sends one representative to an Inter-Cooperative Union, which speaks for the co-ops to the university ([Cornell Daily Sun, 2019](https://cornellsun.com/2019/02/11/open-house-day-highlights-unique-qualities-of-cornell-co-ops/)). On the university side, the program sits with Julie Paige, Cornell's director of Off-Campus, Cooperative and Graduate Living ([Student & Campus Life, 2023](https://scl.cornell.edu/news-events/news/look-inside-cooperative-living-cornell)).

The paperwork is a Cornell housing license, not a lease. A license is permission to live in a room, and Cornell can revoke it. The co-op license says plainly that it creates no landlord-tenant relationship. It becomes binding the moment you accept your room in StarRez, Cornell's housing system. For 2026-27 it runs from 9 a.m. on Aug. 21, 2026, to 2 p.m. on May 23, 2027 ([Co-op Housing Terms and Conditions](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/housing-and-residential-life-and-faculty-involvement-campus/rates-amenities-licenses/co-op-housing-2026-2027-terms-and-conditions)).

A few license rules matter more than the rest:

- **Full-time only.** You must be registered full time, at least 12 credit hours, to live in a co-op during the academic year.
- **No deposit.** Cornell charges no security or key deposit. Rent and any damage charges go on your bursar bill, Cornell's student account.
- **Leaving early costs money.** If you move out mid-year without an approved cancellation, you still owe the whole term. If you will not be at Cornell for spring 2027, tell Housing by Dec. 1, 2026.
- **Small pets only.** Hamsters, guinea pigs, gerbils, birds and fish are allowed if they stay caged, and fish tanks must hold under 10 gallons.
- **House rules count.** Breaking your co-op's own constitution or house rules is grounds for losing the license.

Cornell bills fall rent in August and spring rent in December ([Cornell Housing rates](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/housing-and-residential-life-and-faculty-involvement-campus/rates-amenities-licenses/housing-rates)).

## How the co-ops began

The co-op story starts with veterans. In 1947, a group of World War II veterans studying at Cornell on the G.I. Bill formed the United World Foundation. They had come home to discrimination and segregation, and they wanted to live another way. The group leased a house from Cornell starting in fall 1948. Its later name, Watermargin, comes from Pearl Buck's 1933 English title for the Chinese novel "Shui Hu Zhuan." Cornell calls it the first racially integrated, interfaith living unit on any U.S. college campus. Eleanor Roosevelt, Langston Hughes, Margaret Mead, Malcolm X and Marian Anderson all visited in its early years ([Cornell Chronicle](https://www.news.cornell.edu/stories/2024/01/more-75-years-students-have-made-watermargin-home)).

Watermargin was not yet a co-op in the modern sense. Women could join by the 1960s, but the house stayed all male until about 1970. It took on its present cooperative form, with shared cooking, cleaning and self-government, in the late 1960s and early 1970s (Cornell Chronicle).

Other houses followed. Von Cramm, now home to Redbud Cooperative, was founded in 1956 ([Redbud Cooperative](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/campus-housing/upper-level-undergraduates/cooperative-housing/redbud-cooperative-von-cramm-hall)). Prospect of Whitby opened in 1965 at 228 Wait Avenue, in the former house of the Alpha Xi Delta sorority ([Student & Campus Life, 2023](https://scl.cornell.edu/news-events/news/unexpected-delights-prospect-whitby)). In 1968, a group of Black women founded Wari to house women of the African diaspora ([Cornell diversity timeline](https://diversity.cornell.edu/about-us/our-historic-commitment)). Cornell's alumni magazine credits Irene Smalls, Class of 1971, as Wari's founder ([Cornellians](https://alumni.cornell.edu/cornellians/cbaa-50th/)). A resident told the Sun in 2001 that Wari dated to 1967, but Cornell's records and the house itself give 1968, so go by 1968.

Cornell's pages give no founding dates for Triphammer, Wait Terrace, Wait Avenue Cooperative or the co-op at 660 Stewart. They do say the 660 Stewart building is a 1902 estate and the Wait Avenue house is more than 100 years old (Wait Avenue Cooperative).

Cornell's own count in 2023 was 156 residents (Student & Campus Life, 2023). In 2024, residents told the Sun that Cornell had neither expanded the program nor promoted it (Cornell Daily Sun).

## The eight, house by house

Five co-ops sit on North Campus, three of them on a single block of Wait Avenue. The other three sit on or just below West Campus.

### Prospect of Whitby: 228 Wait Avenue

Whitby, as residents call it, is a 19-person house with 7 singles and 6 doubles. It has a living room with a fireplace and a piano, a big kitchen, bike storage and laundry in the basement, a porch swing and a little parking. Members prepare dinners on an all-vegetarian house meal plan, and food staples are open to everyone. Elected officers manage the house, and members split weekly house jobs ([Prospect of Whitby](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/campus-housing/upper-level-undergraduates/cooperative-housing/prospect-whitby)).

Whitby is open to transfer and upper-level undergraduates. Its rates sit in the middle of the eight, almost to the dollar the same as Watermargin's. In a 2023 Cornell profile, a transfer from Hunter College who moved in mid-winter said the house became her home at Cornell. Another member, who had moved from Pakistan at 15, described finding the community he had missed during a remote first year (Student & Campus Life, 2023).

### Wait Avenue Cooperative: 302 Wait Avenue

Farther along Wait Avenue, Wait Avenue Cooperative houses 14 women. They are a mix of undergraduates and graduate or professional students. The house is more than 100 years old, one of the oldest buildings on North Campus, with a wide front porch. One or two house managers run daily operations. There is no house meal plan: staple foods are provided, and residents cook for themselves or buy a Cornell Dining plan. Laundry, parking and summer storage are free ([Wait Avenue Cooperative](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/campus-housing/upper-level-undergraduates/cooperative-housing/wait-avenue-cooperative)).

Rooms are singles and doubles. Singles cost $5,340 to $5,693 per semester and a double is $4,715, the same as at Wait Terrace next door (Cornell Housing rates).

### Wait Terrace: 308 Wait Avenue

Wait Terrace holds up to 13 residents in 7 singles and 3 doubles. Its porch has a hammock, a porch swing and a barbecue. Inside are a common room with a TV, a shared kitchen and a washer and dryer. There is no house meal plan, so residents cook in the house kitchen or buy a Cornell Dining plan ([Wait Terrace](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/campus-housing/upper-level-undergraduates/cooperative-housing/wait-terrace)). It takes transfer, upper-level, graduate and professional students. In 2001, a resident told the Sun that 308 Wait was in poor shape (Cornell Daily Sun, 2001).

### Triphammer Cooperative: 150 Triphammer Road

Triphammer is the house from the opening, near RPCC (the Robert Purcell Community Center) and the Africana Library. It holds 20 residents in 11 singles, 3 doubles and 1 triple (Triphammer Cooperative). Its single costs $4,889 per semester, its double $4,317 and its triple $3,972, the only triple besides Redbud's on Cornell's rate table (Cornell Housing rates).

Food is the center of the house. Triphammer's meal plan is required. It covers homemade vegetarian dinners Sunday through Thursday and access to pantry staples such as flour, oil and spices. Each resident joins a cook group that makes dinner for everyone one night a week. The house describes itself as welcoming to students from marginalized backgrounds. It has free parking and free laundry, and it sometimes opens its meal plan to students who live elsewhere.

Triphammer posts its mosey schedule on Instagram at @triphammercoop and takes application questions at moseytriphammer@cornell.edu. In a 2024 Cornell feature, its house manager, a transfer student named Daniel Dixon, said living with 20 housemates taught him how to manage a home that many people share (Student & Campus Life, 2024).

### Wari Cooperative: 208 Dearborn Place

Wari's full name is Wanawake Wa Wari. It sits near the Africana Center, just off Triphammer Road, and is home to 10 undergraduate women of color. The house has 8 singles and 1 double, a TV room, a large kitchen and laundry. There is no house meal plan ([Wari Cooperative](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/campus-housing/upper-level-undergraduates/cooperative-housing/wari-cooperative)). Singles are the priciest of any co-op, at $5,640 to $6,014 per semester. A double is $4,981.

The house was founded to build community and confidence among Black women at Cornell. Residents run an Asante Sana series, named for the Swahili phrase for thanks, with activities such as painting and pumpkin carving. In August 2023, Wari helped host the first Black Women Delegate Meeting for Black women's groups on campus ([Student & Campus Life, 2024](https://scl.cornell.edu/news-events/news/wari-co-op-creates-space-women-color-build-community)). The Sun reported in 2019 that Wari used interviews as part of choosing members (Cornell Daily Sun, 2019).

One detail conflicts. A 2023 Sun article called Wari one of the few co-ops with a meal plan ([Cornell Daily Sun, 2023](https://cornellsun.com/2023/03/06/underserved-and-minority-students-find-belonging-and-support-in-co-op-housing/)). Wari's current Cornell page says it has none. Go by the current page, and ask the house before you budget for food.

### Watermargin: 103 McGraw Place

On West Campus, Watermargin houses 23 residents in singles and doubles, in what Cornell describes as a five-story house (Cornell Chronicle). It has two fireplaces, a ping-pong table, a foosball table, a large kitchen and laundry. Its house meal plan serves vegetarian and non-vegetarian food. Students who do not live there can join as nonresidential members or sign up for the meal plan ([Watermargin](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/campus-housing/upper-level-undergraduates/cooperative-housing/watermargin)).

The founding mission still shapes who gets in. Watermargin admits members by random lottery to keep the house diverse. It hosts talks, films and workshops about living with difference (Watermargin). The house is open to transfer, upper-level, graduate and professional students.

### Redbud Cooperative in Von Cramm Hall: 623 University Avenue

Von Cramm is the largest co-op, with 34 residents. The house added "Redbud" to its name to remember a wooded area that once stood behind the building. Residents are a mix of undergraduates and graduate students, and many are international or exchange students. Everyone shares cooking, cleaning and upkeep, and the house eats dinner together each night. It has a fireplace, a dining room, a piano, a pool table, a projector and a big back yard with a grill (Redbud Cooperative).

Redbud is also the cheapest. A single is $3,949 per semester, a double $3,487 and a triple $2,765. That triple is the lowest-priced undergraduate bed on Cornell's rate table. In 2024, Redbud's president, Allan Chu, told the Sun that his first-year dorm triple had cost close to $5,000 and that the co-op was far cheaper. He added that Cornell's recent rent increases had made things harder for the house (Cornell Daily Sun).

### 660 Stewart Avenue

The last house sits in a 1902 estate just below West Campus, with a broad lawn and views of Cayuga Lake. It is the second-largest co-op, with 25 residents in singles, doubles and triples. Bedrooms come with an extra-long twin or queen bed. The first floor holds a fireplace, a library, a projector room, a big kitchen, two porches and a pool table. Laundry is free and parking is on site ([660 Stewart Avenue](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/campus-housing/upper-level-undergraduates/cooperative-housing/660-stewart-avenue)). Parking needs a permit from Transportation Services ([Student & Campus Life, 2024](https://scl.cornell.edu/news-events/news/inside-creative-collaboration-660-stewart-avenue)).

The house runs an optional meal plan with dinners five nights a week, and joining means cooking one night a week. A food staples program is required of everyone. Residents can also buy a Cornell Dining plan and eat at the West Campus houses next door. Its double ties Wari's for the highest double rate of the eight. The house is open to transfer and upper-level students. In 2023, the Sun reported that 660 Stewart and Watermargin both draw LGBTQ+ students looking for a safe space (Cornell Daily Sun, 2023).

## What a co-op really costs

Every co-op room costs less than a dorm double. The most expensive co-op single, at Wari, tops out $858 a semester below the hall double ($6,872 minus $6,014). Each house's operating costs set its rates, which vary by house and sometimes by room (Cornell Housing rates).

Over a full year, a co-op double saves $3,782 to $6,770 against a hall double. The low end is 660 Stewart or Wari ($13,744 minus $9,962). The high end is Redbud ($13,744 minus $6,974). The cheapest bed of all, Redbud's triple, comes to $5,530 for the year. That is $7,114 less than a hall triple at $12,644 ($6,322 per semester times two), all from the 2026-27 rates (Cornell Housing rates).

Food is the wild card. Four houses run their own meal plans: Prospect of Whitby, Triphammer, Watermargin and 660 Stewart. A 2023 Cornell feature said five, but Cornell's main co-op page says four, so go by four ([Cooperative Housing](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/campus-housing/upper-level-undergraduates/cooperative-housing)). Cornell's rate page does not say whether a house meal plan is billed inside the room rate or on top of it. Ask the house treasurer for the full yearly cost, food included, before you compare it with a dorm.

Sophomores need one more check. Cornell's residential policy says students living in on-campus housing must have a meal plan ([Residential Policy](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/campus-housing/residential-policy)). The co-op license says nothing about one. If you are a second-year heading to a co-op, ask co-ops@cornell.edu whether a Cornell Dining plan will be added to your bursar bill.

The off-campus market is the other yardstick. The cheapest kind of Collegetown bed, a room in a six-bedroom house, had a median asking rent of $800 a month for 2027-28. Over a 12-month lease that is $9,600, and most of those tenants pay their own electricity ([EduExplained Ithaca housing dataset](/datasets/ithaca-housing-cost-by-living-situation-2026-27/)). So the cheapest Collegetown share costs about what the priciest co-op double does. Every other co-op double comes in lower, with utilities included and no summer rent. The two prices cover different years, 2027-28 asking rents against 2026-27 co-op rates, so treat the match as close, not exact. For the full picture of the cheap end of the market, see our guide to the [cheapest off-campus housing near Cornell](/cornell/housing/cheapest-off-campus-housing-cornell/), and for the dorm math, [whether off-campus is cheaper than a Cornell dorm](/cornell/housing/is-off-campus-cheaper-than-cornell-dorm/). Students watching every dollar can start at our [low-budget guide](/start/low-budget/).

## Getting in: the mosey

Co-ops do not take part in Cornell's room selection. Each house runs its own intake, called the mosey. Cornell says the word was suggested as an alternative to "rush," the term fraternities and sororities use ([Student & Campus Life, 2023](https://scl.cornell.edu/news-events/news/co-op-mosey-open-house-spring2024)). Houses start choosing members in early February for the next fall and in early November for spring openings (Cooperative Housing).

Most houses follow the same arc (Cornell Daily Sun, 2023). The mosey opens with an afternoon of open houses at the co-ops. Then comes about two weeks of house events, such as game nights, dinners and cook groups (Student & Campus Life, 2023). In the 2023-24 cycle, the fall open house was Saturday, Nov. 4, 2023, with decisions on Nov. 29. The spring open house was Sunday, Feb. 11, 2024. Decisions went out on Feb. 28, and students had one day to accept ([Student & Campus Life, 2024](https://scl.cornell.edu/sub/news-events/news/co-op-mosey-2024)). Cornell said then that most houses also take applications on a rolling basis.

```figure

rows:
  - label: Go to the open house afternoon and tour several co-ops
  - label: Fill out each house's application form
  - label: Attend the house's required events (two at Triphammer, plus one cook group)

  - label: Accept your room and sign the co-op housing license online
```

Sources: the Triphammer Cooperative page and Cornell's 2023 and 2024 mosey notices.

Each house picks in its own way. Triphammer and Watermargin use lotteries. Final choices elsewhere come by resident vote or lottery, the Sun reported in 2019. The Sun reported in 2023 that 660 Stewart and Watermargin required three events, and that the lottery weighed financial need along with diversity factors such as race and disclosed LGBTQ+ identity (Cornell Daily Sun, 2023). Applicants with financial need who missed their first-choice house had a better chance at another one. Getting in is competitive. In February 2017, the Sun counted more than 100 students at the opening mosey day ([Cornell Daily Sun, 2017](https://cornellsun.com/2017/02/07/co-ops-open-doors-for-mosey)).

Only Cornell's eight count. The Sun described Cayuga Lodge in 2017 as an Ithaca co-op that Cornell does not own (Cornell Daily Sun, 2017). It is not on Cornell's list of eight, which is the list that satisfies the sophomore rule (Affiliated Housing for the Second-Year Housing Requirement).

### The sophomore rule

Cornell requires first- and second-year students to live in university housing. In year two, a co-op counts ([Residential Policy](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/campus-housing/residential-policy)). First-years cannot live in a co-op, because Cornell assigns them to residence halls.

The deadline that matters is March 15, 2027. A rising sophomore who plans to live in a co-op must say so on the 2027-28 housing application by then. Miss it, and you finish the requirement in a residence hall. Cornell also tells students to confirm with house leaders that they are on the house roster before that date ([Spring Housing Selection](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/housing-and-residential-life-and-faculty-involvement-campus/apply-housing-campus/current-undergraduate-application-process/spring-housing-selection-rising-sophomores)). One line on that page still prints the year 2026, a leftover from the last cycle. The 2027-28 application runs in spring 2027, so go by 2027.

The early February round matters most to first-years, because its decisions came out ahead of that deadline in 2024. The 2027-28 license lets a second-year cancel a dorm assignment if accepted to a co-op, chapter house or affiliated house before the Spring Housing Selection deadline ([Academic Year Housing License 2027-2028](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/housing-and-residential-life-and-faculty-involvement-campus/rates-amenities-licenses/academic-year-housing-license-2027-2028)). Our [affiliated housing starting page](/start/affiliated-housing/) collects the other options that count. If you are hoping to leave campus housing entirely, read our guide to [Cornell's residential requirement and exemptions](/cornell/housing/cornell-residential-requirement-exemptions-live-off-campus/) first.

### Juniors and seniors who already hold a dorm room

Upperclassmen chose 2027-28 dorm rooms in the fall. The application ran from Sept. 1 to Sept. 27, and selection followed in early October ([Fall Housing Selection](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/housing-and-residential-life-and-faculty-involvement-campus/apply-housing-campus/current-undergraduate-application-process/fall-housing-selection-rising-juniors-and-seniors)). That timing creates a trap. If you hold a dorm room and then win a co-op spot in the spring mosey, cancelling the dorm costs money. Cancellation is $50 through Jan. 31. It jumps to $500 from Feb. 1 to March 31, then $1,000 from April 1 to June 30 (Academic Year Housing License 2027-2028).

Say a junior wins a co-op spot in late February. Dropping a dorm room then costs $500 (Academic Year Housing License 2027-2028). Moving mid-year works differently: students accepted from a residence hall must request a room change in the Housing Portal and name the co-op (Student & Campus Life, 2023). ## The trade-offs

The co-ops are cheap and close-knit. They charge in other ways: work, aging buildings and little say over price.

The first cost is work. Everyone cleans and goes to house meetings, and in five of the eight houses residents also cook for each other. Not everyone likes it. In 2001, one student told the Sun that cooking duty was a hassle, and a Collegetown renter said the co-ops felt too much like dorms (Cornell Daily Sun, 2001). There is no staff member on site when a housemate stops doing chores, and the house has to settle it.

The second cost is the buildings. They are old, and the Sun's 2024 reporting described caved-in roofs and collapsing floors. Watermargin's president said floors had collapsed in four of the eight houses over a few years. Residents told the Sun that their contracts make them responsible for such damage, and that they resent managing so much of their own upkeep (Cornell Daily Sun). The complaint is old. In 2001, residents said the university made them pay for small repairs, down to a light bulb, while using their unpaid labor for others (Cornell Daily Sun, 2001).

The third issue is who sets the price. A Triphammer resident told the Sun in 2024 that the house once priced rooms by size. Then Cornell required co-ops to standardize rates within each house and across houses. Redbud's president said Cornell sets the prices, though residents can suggest changes (Cornell Daily Sun). The 2026-27 table fits that pattern: Whitby, Triphammer and Watermargin share the same $4,889 single rate and nearly the same double.

Cornell's answer, in a statement to the Sun, was that co-ops get a dedicated staff team for maintenance and building care, plus a financial advisor for budgeting and forecasting. The university also handles licenses, billing, communications and recruitment advertising (Cornell Daily Sun).

## What comes next

Three questions will shape the co-ops over the next few years.

The first is Watermargin's roof. If its president's 2024 warning holds, the house has until about 2033 to raise the money (Cornell Daily Sun). Spread across its 23 residents, $500,000 comes to $21,739.13 each. That is more than two years of a Watermargin double at $8,634 a year. Whether Cornell, alumni or the house pays is the open question.

The second is price. The co-op license for 2026-27 told students that final rates would be posted in March 2026 (Co-op Housing Terms and Conditions). If Cornell keeps that schedule, 2027-28 co-op rates will be posted in March 2027, after the February mosey ends. Students moseying in February would then be choosing a house before they know next year's price.

The third is size. Eight houses and about 160 beds is the same system students had in 2001 (Cornell Daily Sun, 2001). Residents raised repair costs in 2001 and again in 2024. Whether Cornell puts money into the eight houses, or adds a ninth, will decide whether the co-ops stay among the cheapest beds on campus.
