```glance
- Ask the landlord in writing first: the lease and state law decide whether you can take over.
- An assignment moves the whole lease to you; a sublease leaves the original tenant liable.
- Get the deposit handled in writing before you pay anyone.
- Four or more units: the landlord's consent cannot be unreasonably withheld, and silence counts as yes.
```

To take over a Collegetown lease, get the landlord's written consent before you pay or move in. An assignment hands the whole lease to you, and the original tenant is released in the usual case. A sublease leaves the original tenant liable for you, per New York [Real Property Law 226-b](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/RPP/226-B).

```reader "If you're taking a summer sublet"
A summer take-over is a sublease, not an assignment, because the leaver comes back in August. Ithaca Renting's Collegetown page says sublets must be registered and approved, are limited to Cornell students, and run one per term with no stay under one month. The page lists the sublet fee as both $15 and $20, so ask the rental office which applies to your dates. The [summer sublet guide](/cornell/housing/summer-sublet-ithaca-finding-housing-may-august/) covers where to look.
```

```reader "If you're a spring transfer or arriving mid-year"
A take-over fits an arrival after the fall rush, because the best units sign months ahead. You inherit the lease's end date, which for most Collegetown leases falls on or near July 31. Dryden South's listing shows leases that [end May 31](https://offcampus.housing.cornell.edu/city/ithaca-college-ny/listing/dryden-south-50961), so confirm the date before you commit.
```

## Is it an assignment or a sublease?

The two look alike and work differently. Cornell Law's tenant guide says assigning is the better route when the leaving tenant does not plan to move back in, per its [breaking a lease page](https://sites.lawschool.cornell.edu/tenants-advocacy/breaking-a-lease).

| | Assignment | Sublease |
|---|---|---|
| What moves | The whole lease and the rest of its term | Part of the term or part of the unit |
| Who owes the landlord | You, after consent | The original tenant, still |
| Original tenant's risk | Released from your rent and damage in the usual case | Stays liable for you |
| Landlord's right to refuse | Can refuse without cause under the statute | Consent cannot be unreasonably withheld in a building of four or more units |
| Best for | A permanent hand-off | A summer or a semester abroad |

Source: [RPL 226-b](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/RPP/226-B) and Cornell Law's guide.

The [statute](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/RPP/226-B) is stricter on assignment than on subletting. A tenant cannot assign without written consent, and the owner can refuse without a reason. If the refusal is unreasonable, the tenant's only remedy is release from the lease on 30 days' notice. Cornell Law's guide reads this more generously, saying a landlord cannot unreasonably deny either request, so expect the landlord to cite the statute.

## Can the landlord say no?

The answer turns on the building and the form of the take-over. In buildings with four or more residential units, a sublet needs the landlord's advance written consent, which cannot be unreasonably withheld. In buildings of three or fewer units, [the lease controls](https://sites.lawschool.cornell.edu/tenants-advocacy/breaking-a-lease). Ithaca Renting's downtown policy adds house rules: all named tenants must agree, and the rental office must approve, per its [downtown page](https://ithacarenting.com/downtown/).

The request has two clocks, which the [sublet request guide](/cornell/housing/cornell-summer-sublet-how-to-sublet-apartment-ithaca/) explains in full. The landlord can ask for more information within 10 days, and must answer within 30 days of the request or the extra information, whichever is later. Silence counts as consent.

## What are the steps to take over a lease?

The leaving tenant sends the request, but you need to supply the paperwork. Work through it in this order.

```figure
kind: steps
title: Taking over a lease, in order
rows:
  - label: Ask the leaver for a copy of the full lease and every addendum
  - label: Check the building's unit count to learn which consent rule applies
  - label: Leaver sends the request by certified mail with return receipt
  - label: Provide your name, addresses, the reason and the proposed sublease or assignment
  - label: Wait for the 10-day and 30-day windows to run, or for written consent
  - label: Settle the deposit in writing and get a receipt
  - label: Do a move-in inspection and photograph the unit
```

The request lists the term, the subtenant's name and addresses, the reason, the tenant's address during the term, any co-tenant or guarantor consent, and a copy of the proposed sublease. A sublet done without these steps is a substantial breach of the lease.

## What do I inherit?

You inherit the lease as written: its rent, end date, fees and rules. Read the whole document before you agree. The [lease reading guide](/cornell/housing/how-to-read-ithaca-lease-before-signing/) pairs each clause with its rule. Three items matter most for a taker.

First, the rent basis. A per-bed price and a per-unit price differ by the number of bedrooms. Ask which one the leaver pays. Ithaca Renting quotes per bed, with a [2027-28 Collegetown 4-bedroom median of $1,246.25](https://ithacarenting.com/collegetown/).

Second, joint liability. If the lease binds all housemates for the full rent, you take on that exposure with your share. Ask to meet the housemates first.

Third, the term. A take-over covers only the time left, so a lease that ends July 31 sends you back to the market within months. Most Collegetown 12-month leases start in June, July or August on set dates, per [Ithaca Renting](https://ithacarenting.com/collegetown/).

If the leaver stays on the lease, they remain liable if you fall behind, which is why many landlords prefer a sublease to an assignment.

## How does the deposit move?

The deposit is the leaver's money, held for the landlord's lease. New York caps a deposit plus any rent advance at one month's rent, under [General Obligations Law 7-108](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/GOB/7-108), and the landlord must return the balance with an itemized statement within 14 days after the tenant vacates. The same statute says a new owner or assignee is liable for a deposit it knew of. That language is about the landlord's side of a sale.

For a take-over, the safest route is a written note signed by the leaver, you and the landlord saying who holds the deposit, how much it is, and who gets it back at the end. If you pay the leaver the deposit amount directly, get a receipt. Do not pay a second deposit to the landlord on top, because the cap applies to the total.

## What fees can I be charged?

The landlord cannot demand any payment for processing, reviewing or accepting an application, per [RPL 238-a](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/RPP/238-A). A background or credit check costs the lesser of actual cost or $20, and the landlord must first give you the check and invoice. Sublet fees are allowed only if the lease permits them and they are reasonable. Ithaca Renting's $15 or $20 sublet fee is an example of a fee a lease can set.

## What goes wrong?

**A verbal yes.** A friend's promise or a text from a property manager is not consent. Get it in writing.

**Paying the leaver before consent.** If the landlord says no, you have no right to the unit and a leaver you have to chase for the money. Wait for the written yes.

**Unclear damage history.** The leaver's move-in inspection record covers the leaver, not you. Ask for it, and walk the unit yourself before you sign.

**A leaver who stops paying.** An unpaid subtenant can be pursued in court for breach of contract, but the landlord can still turn to the original tenant. If you are the leaver, the [breaking a lease guide](/cornell/housing/breaking-a-lease-early-ithaca-cornell/) covers your options, including the landlord's duty to re-rent under [RPL 227-e](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/RPP/227-E).

## What to do next

Ask the leaver for the lease today. Confirm the unit count and the rental office's sublet rules, then send the request. Cornell Law's tenant hotline, [607-301-1560](https://sites.lawschool.cornell.edu/tenants-advocacy/tenants-rights/), answers questions about a specific lease for free.
