At 6 p.m. on Sept. 11, 2019, three Cornell sophomores got in line at the Ithaca Renting Company. Leasing would open at 9 a.m. the next day, 15 hours later. Staff let the first groups wait inside until about 9 p.m. After that, the line lived on the sidewalk. They wanted a Collegetown three-bedroom at $1,160 a person per month, for a school year that would start 11 months later. One of them, Sophie Matton, compared the wait to Black Friday ([The Cornell Daily Sun](https://cornellsun.com/2019/09/19/cornell-students-sleep-on-sidewalk-for-cheaper-more-desirable-leases/)).

Seven years on, the calendar has not moved. On Nov. 10, 2026, the same company's Collegetown page already marked 14 of its 67 listings sold out for the 2027-28 lease year ([Ithaca Renting](https://ithacarenting.com/collegetown/)). Move-in is not until August 2027.

Collegetown is the dense block of student housing that touches the southwest end of Cornell's campus. Cornell calls it a "miniature city" of shops and restaurants, 0.1 to 0.5 miles from campus, and very popular with undergraduates, which pushes up rent, noise and congestion ([Cornell Ithaca neighborhoods](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/campus-housing/housing-search-process/ithaca-neighborhoods)). It runs on rules that surprise most first-time renters. Apartments lease a year ahead. Prices are quoted per bed, not per apartment. And at the last public count, eight owners held about two-thirds of its bedrooms, while new towers now rise beside the older houses.

At Ithaca Renting, the median 2027-28 bed costs $800 a month in a six-bedroom and $2,025 in a one-bedroom alone ([EduExplained dataset](/datasets/collegetown-rent-per-bed-2027-28/)). A Cornell residence hall double costs $13,744 for 2026-27, with gas, electricity, garbage and internet included ([Cornell housing rates](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/housing-and-residential-life-and-faculty-involvement-campus/rates-amenities-licenses/housing-rates)). Spread over a 12-month lease, that double works out to $1,145.33 a month (EduExplained calculation: that annual rate divided by 12). Most Collegetown beds cost more.

For the rest of Ithaca, from Fall Creek to Lansing, see the [off-campus housing guide](/cornell/housing/cornell-off-campus-housing-guide/).

## Where Collegetown starts and stops

Most of the buildings in this guide stand on College Avenue, Dryden Road and Eddy Street. The walk to campus is up to 15 minutes. From lower Collegetown it is uphill all the way, though downtown's shops are close (Cornell Ithaca neighborhoods). Cornell describes the housing as a mix of "luxury/average" apartment complexes, houses split into several units, and single rooms for rent.

Cornell itself owns part of it. Three university residences sit in Collegetown: Cascadilla Hall, Sheldon Court and 301 College Avenue ([Cornell residence halls](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/campus-housing/upper-level-undergraduates/residence-halls/sheldon-court)). They are campus housing on a Cornell license, open to juniors, seniors and transfers. Collegetown has no Cornell dining hall. A Sheldon Court resident put the nearest one, on West Campus, at about a 12-minute walk ([The Cornell Daily Sun](https://www.cornellsun.com/article/2026/04/the-misunderstood-dorm-sheldon-court)).

A car costs extra. The City's Dryden Road Garage, at 118 Dryden Road, sells a 24-hour monthly permit for $232 and recommends it for Collegetown residents ([City of Ithaca](https://www.cityofithacany.gov/718/Dryden-Road-Garage)). Kept all year, that is $2,784 (EduExplained calculation: $232 times 12). From November through April, its uncovered fourth and fifth floors close to parking from 2 a.m. to 8 a.m.

## How Collegetown got this way

**A market held by a few owners.** In 2013, The Cornell Daily Sun matched city occupancy records with county assessments. It found that eight landlords owned 4,013 of an estimated 6,110 Collegetown bedrooms, or 65.7% ([The Cornell Daily Sun](https://cornellsun.com/2013/05/02/collegetown-housing-market-scrutinized/)). Their property was assessed at about $153 million. John Novarr and Jason Fane each held more than $38 million, and Novarr alone owned 1,559 bedrooms. The Sun credited his Collegetown Terrace project with a big jump in student housing. The other six were the Avramis family, Travis Hyde, the Lambrou family, Pam Johnston, the Lower family and Matoula Halkiopoulus. A Sun columnist argued in 2025 that if you point to any three decent-looking buildings on College Avenue, the same agency owns them ([The Cornell Daily Sun](https://www.cornellsun.com/article/2025/09/maia-on-the-mic-the-collegetown-housing-hustle)).

**A rush that kept moving earlier.** Julie Paige, then an assistant dean of students, told the Sun in 2013 that in her time at Cornell, which began in 1987, the main signing period had shifted from January to late October or November. The August and September push, she said, was only four to six years old ([The Cornell Daily Sun](https://cornellsun.com/2013/09/12/despite-new-law-housing-rush-persists-in-collegetown/)). That year the City passed a notice rule. Landlords had to give current tenants 60 days' written notice before renewing, showing the unit or signing new tenants. The clock could start on the first day of the lease, so showings began anyway. Ithaca Renting emailed tenants on Sept. 6, 2013, that its 2014-15 season had started, with more than 300 inquiries in hand.

**Zoning built for growth.** In March 2014, Ithaca's Common Council unanimously adopted the Collegetown Area Form Districts, new zoning meant to bring more building to Collegetown ([The Cornell Daily Sun](https://cornellsun.com/2014/09/07/multiple-developments-proposed-for-collegetown)). Proposals followed within months. They included Pat Kraft's Dryden South, a six-story, 40-bedroom tower at the Kraftees building on Dryden Road, a 28-apartment tower at 327 Eddy St. and Josh Lower's Collegetown Crossing, with 43 apartments over a GreenStar grocery. Jason Fane asked for a 12-story tower at 330 College Ave., where the zone allowed six, and needed a variance.

**A wave of new buildings.** Three buildings opened in August 2016: 307 College Ave., 327 Eddy St. and 205 Dryden Road. Together they added nearly 200 bedrooms ([The Cornell Daily Sun](https://cornellsun.com/2016/09/26/landlords-say-collegetown-housing-hassle-unlikely-to-change/)). The big group houses grew scarcer and more contested as the new towers went up, the Sun reported in 2018. Catherine Commons came next, on both sides of Catherine Street at College Avenue. It replaced eleven two-story houses, won city approval on March 23, 2022, and was scheduled to finish in August 2024 ([The Cornell Review](https://www.thecornellreview.org/cornells-construction-crunch-ithacas-housing-crisis-and-the-catherine-commons/)). Its builder lists 356 apartments and 483 beds, 206 of the apartments studios ([Welliver](https://buildwelliver.com/projects/integrated-acquisition-development-corp-iad-catherine-commons-210-college-avenue)).

**Cornell's own decisions.** From fall 2022, Cornell required first-years and sophomores to live in campus or affiliated housing ([The Cornell Daily Sun](https://cornellsun.com/2021/11/09/as-cornell-requires-university-housing-for-sophomores-students-weigh-options/)). The North Campus expansion made that possible with about 2,000 new beds, about 800 of them for sophomores. That moved the first Collegetown lease to junior year, and the search for it to the fall of sophomore year. The same expansion let Cornell admit more students. A 2022 Cornell Review column estimated a net gain of 1,000 to 1,100 undergraduates, who would land in Collegetown as juniors and seniors. Cornell then bought into Collegetown directly. It paid $15 million in March 2025 for 301 College Ave., which Tompkins Community Bank had foreclosed on in February 2024 ([The Cornell Daily Sun](https://www.cornellsun.com/article/2025/09/301-college-avenue-available-for-student-housing-in-2026-2027-academic-year)). The Sun tied the purchase to the Class of 2029, the largest in Cornell's history.

**The newest tower.** The Ivy, an eight-story building with 52 units at College Avenue and Dryden Road, leased nearly every unit before it was built ([The Cornell Daily Sun](https://www.cornellsun.com/article/2025/12/the-ivy-to-establish-new-luxury-housing-in-collegetown-opens-fall-2026)). Its leases were set to begin Aug. 17, 2026.

## What a bed costs for 2027-28

Collegetown rent is quoted per bed. A "four-bedroom at $1,246.25" means each of four tenants pays that much a month. The bigger the group, the cheaper each bed. Ithaca Renting posts its full Collegetown price list, which most landlords do not. Its prices are ranges, so the medians below use the middle of each range (Ithaca Renting).

Group size is the biggest lever you control. Moving a group from a six-bedroom to a four-bedroom costs each person $446.25 a month, or $5,355 over a 12-month lease (EduExplained calculation: $1,246.25 minus $800.00, times 12). Only the six-bedroom, the ten-bedroom, rooms in shared suites and, on a two-listing average, the five-bedroom come in under the Cornell double.

The listed price is not the whole bill. Ithaca Renting includes heat and hot water at every property and furnishes every Collegetown apartment. Tenants pay their own electricity, except in a few places such as the shared suites on the second and third floors of 210 Dryden Road ([Ithaca Renting](https://ithacarenting.com/collegetown/)). Trash pickup is included only at Collegetown Center, Plaza and Court. Prices can change without notice until you sign. Once you sign, the price is fixed for the lease.

**Other landlords price differently.** At The Ivy, three-bedrooms rented for $5,500 to $5,800 a month for leases starting in 2026. The cheapest unit was a basement two-bedroom at $1,595 a person after a 20% early discount, against a full price of $4,000 a month for the unit (The Cornell Daily Sun, 2025). Split three ways, the three-bedrooms come to $1,833.33 to $1,933.33 a person, or $22,000 to $23,200 a year (EduExplained calculation: $5,500 and $5,800 divided by 3, times 12). That is 1.6 to 1.7 times the Cornell double. Dryden South, at 205 Dryden Road, listed suites at $1,395 to $1,990 a person for 2026-27, with heat included and all leases ending May 31, 2027 ([Dryden South listing](https://offcampus.housing.cornell.edu/city/ithaca-college-ny/listing/dryden-south-50961)). At the cheap end, a room in a five-bedroom at 209 Stewart Ave. listed for $850 a month "all in," with electricity and Wi-Fi included, for 2026-27 ([209 Stewart Ave. listing](https://offcampushousing.ithaca.edu/city/ithaca-college-ny/listing/209-stewart-avenue-235095)). The Sun columnist's 2025 survey put Collegetown houses at about $800 to $1,300 a person, many "not in the greatest condition."

**What the averages say.** RentCafe's Collegetown average was $2,815 a month per apartment on Oct. 30, 2026, down 2.59% from $2,890 a year earlier ([RentCafe](https://www.rentcafe.com/average-rent-market-trends/us/ny/ithaca/collegetown/)). Its two-bedroom average was $3,414. That is $659 more than a whole two-bedroom at Ithaca Renting's median (EduExplained calculation: $1,377.50 times 2 is $2,755). RentCafe counts only buildings with 50 or more units, so it leaves out the houses and small buildings where many students live.

**How much prices have moved.** The 2019 three-bedroom those sophomores camped for cost $1,160 a person. Ithaca Renting's Collegetown Court premium three-bedroom lists at $1,185 to $1,200 for 2027-28, which is $25 to $40 more, or 2.2% to 3.4% (EduExplained calculation from the 2019 Sun story and the Ithaca Renting page). The top of the market moved much more. In 2019, a three-bedroom at 312 College Ave. cost up to $4,150, about $1,383.33 a person (The Cornell Daily Sun, 2019). The Ivy's cheapest three-bedroom costs $450 a person more, 32.5% higher. These are asking prices for different buildings, not a measured rent index.

**Downtown is the trade.** At the same landlord, a bed downtown costs 20% to 28% less than the same unit size in Collegetown; the downtown list is mostly 2026-27 units ([Ithaca Renting downtown](https://ithacarenting.com/downtown/)). A two-bedroom share downtown has a median of $992.50 a bed, against $1,377.50 in Collegetown: $385 a month, or $4,620 a year. The price of that saving is a 20- to 30-minute uphill walk (Cornell Ithaca neighborhoods). The [neighborhood comparison](/cornell/housing/collegetown-vs-fall-creek-vs-downtown-ithaca-where-to-live/) sets Collegetown against Fall Creek and downtown in full, and the calculator below weighs rent against your commute.

## Who owns Collegetown

**Ithaca Renting Company** has the most Collegetown listings in this guide. It runs Collegetown Court, Plaza and Center, Aces Apartments on Eddy Street, 705 E. Buffalo St., several Dryden Road houses and a downtown portfolio ([Ithaca Renting property list](https://ithacarenting.com/all-properties/)). It leased to more than 600 tenants near campus in 2019 (The Cornell Daily Sun, 2019). It is also the reason for the famous lines. Its rental manager, Lisa Everts, told the Sun in 2012 that the company signs apartments on the spot, first come, first served, which is why students camped on Dryden Road ([The Cornell Daily Sun](https://cornellsun.com/2012/10/01/as-collegetown-housing-rush-intensifies-students-sleep-outside-rental-agency/)).

**John Novarr** built Collegetown Terrace and, with Phil Proujansky, bought 301 College Ave. in June 2017 for the same price Cornell paid after the bank foreclosed (The Cornell Daily Sun, 2025). **Jason Fane**, the other owner above $38 million in 2013, sought the 12-story tower at 330 College Ave. in 2014.

**Modern Living Rentals**, run from 200 E. Buffalo St., lists 309 College Ave., The Bear, The Ivy, The Summit and The Outlook ([Modern Living Rentals](https://www.modernlivingrentals.com/)). Its home page ran a "Lock-In November Rates" countdown in November 2026, with no rates shown.

**Student Agencies** runs the building at 409 College Ave. Its site says every unit there has been leased for 2027-28 and offers a waitlist (Student Agencies Building).

**The Lambrou family** rents houses on Elmwood Avenue and Eddy Street. Nick Lambrou told the Sun in 2018 that the best houses go to rising juniors who have seen them through older friends, already have a solid group and want housing settled before prelims ([The Cornell Daily Sun](https://cornellsun.com/2018/11/07/desirable-collegetown-leases-leave-market-up-to-16-months-before-residents-move-in/)). His son Costa Lambrou said the maintenance crew works 10 hours a day, seven days a week in the first weeks of each term.

**Josh Lower of Urban Ithaca** built 307 College Ave. and Collegetown Crossing. In 2016 he said he starts renting in early fall and usually has most units rented by the end of November, with no single date when everything clears. George Avramis of Student Rentals Ithaca said the same fall that he was 70% to 75% leased by late September (The Cornell Daily Sun, 2016).

To check a landlord before you sign, use the steps in [how to check a Collegetown landlord](/cornell/housing/ithaca-landlords-to-avoid-collegetown-reviews/); no public list rates them. For where listings are posted, from Cornell's portal to landlord sites, see [where to find Cornell off-campus listings](/cornell/housing/cornell-off-campus-housing-listings-where-to-find/).

## When to sign for 2027-28

The Sun's record of dated Collegetown signings, nine cases from 2017 to 2025, puts them 8 to 16 months before an August move-in, mostly 10 or 11 ([EduExplained dataset](/datasets/collegetown-lease-signing-lead-times-2017-2025/)). That is September or October of the year before. For a junior-year lease, it is the fall of sophomore year.

Big houses go earliest. The residents of the 12-bedroom house at 410 Elmwood Ave. signed in April 2017 for an August 2018 move-in, 16 months ahead (The Cornell Daily Sun, 2018). One of them, Jonny Levenfeld, said another group was circling and the house would have been gone within a week or two. The cost of signing that early showed up later. Another student, Ian Chu, signed for a ten-bedroom on North Quarry Street in May 2017. His group found out after signing that the walk to class was much longer than they had thought.

The 2027-28 season is following the pattern. As of Nov. 10, 2026, Ithaca Renting had sold out every unit of eight or more bedrooms except the ten-bedroom at 202 Dryden Road, including the houses at 214 Dryden Road (14 bedrooms) and 105 Dryden Court (8 bedrooms), plus every whole apartment at 210 Dryden Road. The Student Agencies Building's site said all units had been leased and offered a waitlist for 2027-28 early access ([Student Agencies Building](https://www.thestudentagenciesbuilding.com/)). Collegetown Terrace was offering to lock in rates for applicants by Nov. 15, 2026 (Collegetown Terrace). Studios, one-bedrooms and rooms in shared suites were still on the market at Ithaca Renting.

The City has tried to slow the rush. Since May 31, 2022, a City landlord must give current tenants at least 120 days' written notice before renewing their lease or advertising the unit to new renters ([WSKG](https://wskg.org/ithaca-landlords-must-now-give-renters-120-days-before-renewing-leases/)). The rule covers leases of nine months or more. A tenant can waive it only through a statement that is written, bolded and initialed on the lease's first page. The 2022 rule's author, then-Alderperson Patrick Mehler, was a Cornell undergraduate. The signing record shows no shift after either the 2013 or the 2022 rule.

If you hold a Cornell room as a backup, watch the cancellation fees. A returning junior or senior pays $50 to cancel a 2027-28 Cornell license through Jan. 31, 2027, then $500 from Feb. 1, $1,000 from April 1 and $2,000 from July 1 ([Cornell 2027-28 housing license](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/housing-and-residential-life-and-faculty-involvement-campus/rates-amenities-licenses/academic-year-housing-license-2027-2028)). Cornell first let rising juniors and seniors cancel this way in its October 2025 selection, which ran Oct. 2 to Oct. 8 ([The Cornell Daily Sun](https://cornellsun.com/article/2025/11/new-changes-to-fall-housing-selection-leave-some-rising-juniors-seniors-disappointed)). Housing and Residential Life told the Sun that housing is not guaranteed beyond the first two years.

## What a landlord can and cannot charge

New York's rules apply to every Collegetown lease. A security deposit plus any rent paid in advance cannot exceed one month's rent ([N.Y. General Obligations Law 7-108](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/GOB/7-108)). After you move out, the landlord has 14 days to return the deposit with an itemized list of anything kept. A landlord who misses that deadline loses the right to keep any of it. Before you move in, the landlord must offer you an inspection, and if you accept, you both sign a written record of the unit's condition.

A landlord cannot charge you a fee to apply ([N.Y. Real Property Law 238-a](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/RPP/238-A)). A background and credit check costs at most $20, and is free if you bring one done in the last 30 days. A late fee is allowed only after rent is five days late, and is capped at $50 or 5% of the monthly rent, whichever is less.

Ithaca's rent rules depend on the building's age. Ithaca's Common Council voted 8-2 in July 2024 to opt into New York's Good Cause Eviction law ([WAMC](https://www.wamc.org/2024-07-12/ithaca-is-the-latest-upstate-city-to-opt-into-good-cause-renter-protections)). In covered units, a rent increase above the lower of 10% or 5% plus inflation is presumed unreasonable. If a tenant challenges it, the landlord must justify it in court ([Cornell Law Tenants Advocacy](https://sites.lawschool.cornell.edu/tenants-advocacy/rent-increases)). The law exempts buildings that got their certificate of occupancy on or after Jan. 1, 2009, for 30 years. The Ivy, Dryden South, Catherine Commons and the other buildings finished since 2016 fall under that exemption. Older buildings such as Collegetown Court, Plaza and Center are covered unless another exemption applies.

Every City building with three or more units needs a Certificate of Compliance, the City's safety permit, renewed after an inspection every three years ([City of Ithaca](https://www.cityofithacany.gov/146/Certificate-of-Compliance)). So does a single unit with six or more unrelated tenants. The Building Division answers questions at 607-274-6508 from 8 a.m. to 4 p.m. on weekdays.

One rule is not a law but a lease clause. Cornell warns that most local leases bind housemates "jointly and severally," so each tenant is responsible for the others' rent and damage for the whole lease ([Cornell Housing](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/campus-living/living-campus/study-abroad/housemate-compatibility-questionnaire)). In a six-bedroom, that means five other people's rent can land on you.

## Full-year leases, summers and parking

Ithaca Renting's leases run a full year, as does the one at 209 Stewart Ave. Ithaca Renting's start in June, July or August, on fixed dates set for each apartment ([Ithaca Renting](https://ithacarenting.com/collegetown/)). A Cornell double covers only the academic year. That gap changes the comparison. If you sublet for the summer and pay rent for only 10 months yourself, the break-even with the double rises to $1,374.40 a month (EduExplained calculation: $13,744 divided by 10). Without a subtenant, you pay for the summer too.

In a bigger building, subletting is your right. New York lets a tenant in a building with four or more units sublet with the landlord's written consent, and the landlord cannot refuse without a good reason ([N.Y. Real Property Law 226-b](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/RPP/226-B)). You ask by certified mail. The landlord has 30 days to answer, and silence counts as a yes. You stay responsible for the lease even after a sublet is approved. The law does not reach buildings with three units or fewer.

Landlords add their own rules on top. Ithaca Renting requires every Collegetown sublet to be registered and approved. It allows Cornell students only, one subtenant per term, and nothing shorter than a month, so no Airbnb. Its page lists the registration fee as $15 in one place and $20 in another, so ask which applies before you post an ad (Ithaca Renting). Tenants who stay or sublet over the summer must keep the electricity on.

Parking is its own contract. Ithaca Renting sells only full-year parking licenses, with no monthly or one-semester option (Ithaca Renting). Its lots sit behind Collegetown Plaza and Collegetown Center on Dryden Road, on the 300 block of College Avenue and on the 700 block of East Buffalo Street, with room for more than 100 cars. Every space is assigned. Cars longer than 15.5 feet are not allowed, which rules out most pickup trucks and full-size SUVs. Tenants get first pick of spaces.

## The trade-offs

**New buildings can open late.** In 2016, Fontana Apartments at 327 Eddy St. delayed its opening five times. More than 50 students with leases starting Aug. 1 began the semester without a home ([The Cornell Daily Sun](https://www.cornellsun.com/article/2016/09/delayed-collegetown-apartment-opening-leaves-e2-80-88students-homeless)). The city had not issued a certificate of occupancy, and a water main broke. Landlord Steve Fontana offered $30 for each day or a room at a hotel by the Ithaca Mall, but students said the payments started only on Aug. 21. Cornell's off-campus living manager, Denise Thompson, held a meeting with city officials for the students.

**Even Cornell opened late.** 301 College Ave. opened in August 2026 with 43 students while work went on ([The Cornell Daily Sun](https://www.cornellsun.com/article/2026/09/301-college-ave-opens-for-student-housing-resident-describes-it-as-incomplete-rushed)). Resident Eleanor McNamara described a refrigerator running at about 55 degrees, a bathroom not cleaned at move-in, kitchens with no stoves and construction outside six days a week from 7:30 a.m. She also said she liked the apartment layout and her room. Cornell said it was handling concerns through its service request system.

**Older houses are cheaper for a reason.** The 2018 Sun story found a 14-person house that a tenant called "falling apart at the seams," with squirrels in the roof. Ian Chu's room had no electricity or light when he moved in. The 2025 Sun columnist wrote that many Collegetown houses sit down hills steep enough to make every trip to class a climb.

**Luxury does not always win.** Ivy Ng, a member of the Class of 2027, was offered The Ivy's 20% early discount in 2025. The price was close to what she already paid. She stayed put because the unit looked smaller than her current apartment and she could not see the inside (The Cornell Daily Sun, 2025).

**Closeness has a cost of its own.** Cornell names rent, noise and congestion as the price of Collegetown's location. A Sheldon Court resident said lower rooms facing College Avenue can be loud day and night.

```reader "If you're a sophomore planning junior year"
Settle your group size before you tour; it moves your rent more than any building choice. Big houses for 2027-28 were sold out by early November 2026, so a group that wants one for 2028-29 should start looking in September 2027. Sign only with people you would trust with your rent.
```

```reader "If you're a parent"
Expect to pay first rent and a deposit at signing, in the fall, 10 or more months before move-in for a popular unit. Compare any lease with the Cornell double on a full-year basis, using the summer math above. Read the joint and several clause with your student before anyone signs.
```

```reader "If you're on a tight budget"
The cheapest Collegetown beds for 2027-28 are in big shares: Aces six-bedrooms on Eddy Street from $695, the ten-bedroom at 202 Dryden Road and rooms in shared suites at 210 Dryden Road, where some floors include electricity. A room "all in," with utilities included, removes the surprise of winter bills. If those are gone, look downtown at the same landlord.
```

```reader "If you're a grad student"
You can skip the fall rush: the overnight lines and early sold-out lists are an undergraduate story. Start with Collegetown Center and Collegetown Court, the two towers that court graduate tenants.
```

## What comes next

Three things will shape the next two seasons. Cornell expects 301 College Ave. to reach its full 105 residents by fall 2027, which adds Cornell-run apartments in the middle of Collegetown. In March 2025 the City proposed raising height limits in Collegetown's mixed-use districts, from six stories to eight in the MU-2 district, with a council vote set for April 2, 2025 ([FingerLakes1](https://www.fingerlakes1.com/2025/03/12/ithaca-proposes-zoning-changes-to-collegetown-area-allowing-taller-buildings)). Check the City's code on eCode360 for the height limits now in force before counting on taller buildings. And the Class of 2029, the largest in Cornell's history, is the class shopping for 2027-28 leases in fall 2026.

New towers have added beds since 2016, but the newest carry the highest prices. The groups that win the best houses still do what Nick Lambrou described in 2018: they have seen the house, know their housemates and sign before prelims (The Cornell Daily Sun, 2018). Settle those three things before the fall rush, and the rest is a matter of price.
