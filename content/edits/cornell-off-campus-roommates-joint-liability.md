- The joint-liability point repeats across the glance, intro and six reader blocks. Each reader block has to stand alone, so I cut only the repeats that added nothing for that reader (transfer, international).
- The two group-size examples look like a second example proving the first. They show different failures (the group shrinks to a smaller unit; a leaver is never replaced), so both stay.
- The "Not confirmed:" line under the occupancy table covers a different gap from the parent-block line. That is one line per gap, not a repeat.
- Common mistakes and FAQ restate body facts by design (lint-required sections).
- "Three details catch groups." is a list lead-in, not throat-clearing. The three details follow at once.

## For the style guide (at most 3 patterns worth a rule or an exemplar note, with the evidence)
1. Reader blocks end on a slogan that restates the glance ("Strangers on a joint lease each owe the whole rent."; "Your housemates are not your exit."). Rule: end a reader block on that reader's action or fact, never on the page's general thesis.
2. Method notes keep slipping into first person ("our hand-read sample"). Suggest a house form for data provenance: "<n> rows read on <date>", with "our" kept only in "(our arithmetic: ...)".
3. Gaps written as sentences mid-paragraph ("Whether X is not confirmed.") instead of a "Not confirmed:" line. Exemplar note: put the gap last in the block, in the house form.
