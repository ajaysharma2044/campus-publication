This quiz tells you whether you apply to Cornell as a transfer, as a first-year, or not at all, and then checks the one college and major you pick. You enter where you stand: high school or not, college credits, semesters finished by August 2027, your school, the Cornell college and major, your GPA, whether you have the required courses, years lived on campus elsewhere and citizenship. It answers with your applicant type, your likely standing (sophomore or junior), whether that college and major are open to you, your GPA set against the college's own figure, how many of your credits fit under the cap, whether the one-year housing rule applies, and the deadlines. The rules are Cornell's pages for the fall 2027 cycle, read on 2026-11-11.

## Who it is for

- **Sophomore transfers** at a four-year school checking a college and major before spring applications.
- **Junior-entry transfers**, for whom standing, the credit cap and closed doors (CALS, Hotel, Engineering, Global Development) decide the answer.
- **Community college students**, including those finishing an associate's degree.
- **Transfer Option holders** checking the Arts and Sciences conditions.
- **Engineering and computer science applicants**, whose required course block decides whether the application is read.
- **International applicants**, who face the March 15 aid deadline.
- **Anyone hoping for spring entry**, which Cornell does not offer to external transfers now.
- **Veterans and students out of school two or more years.**

## The inputs

| Input | What it means |
|---|---|
| When you want to start | Fall 2027 or a spring semester. External transfers start in the fall only. |
| Still in high school | Dual enrollment and early college students who have not graduated apply as first-years, whatever their credits. |
| Bachelor's degree held | Bachelor's holders cannot apply as transfers or for a second bachelor's. |
| College credits after high school | Credits done by August 2027. AP, IB and other exam credit does not count toward the 12. |
| Your school's calendar | Quarter credits are multiplied by 2/3 to get semester credits. |
| Enrolled full-time now | Full-time enrollment meets the definition even before you reach 12 credits. |
| Semesters finished by August 2027 | Count the one you are in. Three quarters equal two semesters. |
| Where you study now | A four-year school or a two-year or community college. |
| Associate's degree by August 2027 | In CALS it means junior standing. |
| College and major | The one college (and usually one major) you apply to. |
| Cumulative college GPA | Set against the college's minimum, typical or competitive figure. |
| Required courses | Done, in progress or planned; missing; or not sure. |
| Years lived on campus elsewhere | Two years exempts you from the housing rule. |
| Citizenship | Domestic, undocumented (treated as domestic) or international. |
| Transfer Option, out of school two or more years, veteran | Each adds the rules that apply to you. |

## The rules and numbers

**Applicant type.** You apply as a transfer with a high school diploma or equivalent plus at least 12 semester hours of college credit after high school, or full-time enrollment at another institution ([admissions](https://admissions.cornell.edu/apply/transfer-applicants)). Cornell accepts transfers who have completed two to four semesters elsewhere; students in their first college semester are not eligible ([FAQ 330](https://faq.enrollment.cornell.edu/kb/article/330-when-should-i-apply-to-cornell-as-a-transfer/)). Students who finished high school with fewer than 12 college credits apply as first-years ([Transfer Option instructions](https://admissions.cornell.edu/resources/transfer-option-instructions)), and so does anyone not yet graduated from high school. Bachelor's holders are not eligible. The quiz checks these in order: bachelor's, high school, credits and enrollment, semesters.

**Standing.** The quiz maps 2 semesters to first-semester sophomore, 3 to second-semester sophomore and 4 or more to junior. CALS states it outright: two semesters means sophomore standing; four semesters or an associate's degree means junior standing, and a junior-eligible student cannot choose sophomore entry; more than four semesters at a four-year school is not eligible ([CALS](https://cals.cornell.edu/education/admissions/undergraduate-admissions/transfer-applicants)). Engineering enrolls sophomores (first or second semester) or first-semester juniors ([Engineering](https://www.duffield.cornell.edu/admissions/transfer-applicants/requirements)). The Hotel School takes first-years, sophomores and rising juniors at their current school, not current juniors or seniors ([Hotel](https://business.cornell.edu/admissions/undergraduate/bs-hotel-administration/application-requirements/)).

**Closed doors.** Global and Public Health Sciences takes no transfers; Global Development takes sophomores only; Applied Economics and Management applies through Dyson, and CALS transfers cannot add it later (CALS page). AAP is not accepting external transfers for the fall 2027 B.Arch. ([AAP](https://aap.cornell.edu/undergraduate-admissions/transfer-applicants/)). Engineering admits you into one major, with no undecided or Independent Major option, and you stay in it at least one academic year ([FAQ 150](https://faq.enrollment.cornell.edu/kb/article/150-as-a-transfer-student-does-it-matter-which-engineering-major-i-apply-to/)). Missing any of Engineering's math, physics and chemistry courses means the application is not reviewed.

**Credits.** Credits accepted = the smaller of your credits and the cap. Most colleges cap transfer credit at 60, including exam credit; Architecture at 64 ([registrar](https://registrar.cornell.edu/students/credit-evaluation-services)); Engineering at 33 per year of full-time study and 66 in all, so its cap is the smaller of 66 and 33 x (semesters / 2). Example: 75 credits into Arts and Sciences: 75 - 60 = 15 credits would not count. Example: 3 semesters into Engineering: 33 x 1.5 = 49.5.

**Housing.** New transfers live on campus for one academic year ([transfer housing page](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/campus-housing/new-transfer-undergraduates)). You are exempt with two years of on-campus residence at a previous school or by entering as a junior or senior ([residential policy](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/campus-housing/residential-policy)). First-year applicants fall under the first-year rule: on campus through the spring of the second academic year.

## Assumptions and what it does not cover

- Standing from semesters is an estimate. Each college sets standing after the offer.
- The credit line assumes every course earns credit up to the cap. In practice each course needs a C or better and about an 80% match to a Cornell course, decided after admission ([registrar](https://registrar.cornell.edu/students/credit-evaluation-services)).
- Engineering's per-year cap is applied as 33 x semesters / 2, assuming full-time semesters.
- The Biological Sciences course list comes from a search summary of the CALS major page.
- "Students with fewer than two years elsewhere are not admitted directly to a major" in Arts and Sciences comes from a search summary of its transfer page.
- A&S is the only college whose Transfer Option conditions the quiz states; each other college sets its own.
- The 2025-26 Common Data Set marks "No" for a minimum number of credits; the quiz follows the admissions page's 12-hour rule, which is the instruction Cornell gives applicants.
- Not in the brief: Dyson's own GPA figure and course list for external transfers (the quiz uses the Cornell-wide 3.3); transfer admit rates by college or major; the fall 2027 Transfer Option deadline (fall 2026's was March 15, 2026); English test minimums for international applicants; how many transfers enter as sophomores versus juniors.

## How to read the result

- **"Yes. You can apply"** means you meet Cornell's transfer definition. Read the college line next: "Open to you" or "Closed to you as things stand," with the reason.
- **"You apply as a first-year"** means you are still in high school, or you have fewer than 12 college credits and are not enrolled full-time. Use the first-year application.
- **"Not yet"** means you would be applying in your first college semester. Apply in your second semester for the following fall.
- **"Not eligible"** means you hold a bachelor's degree.
- **The GPA line** names what kind of figure the college gives. Below a minimum (CALS, AAP) closes the door; below a typical or competitive figure (ILR, Human Ecology, Brooks, Hotel, Cornell-wide) does not, but the rest of the file has to carry more.
- **The housing line** says whether the one-year on-campus rule reaches you. A junior exemption depends on Cornell admitting you as a junior.
