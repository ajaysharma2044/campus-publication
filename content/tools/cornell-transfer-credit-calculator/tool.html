How many of your credits count, and how long you will be at Cornell

Cornell's caps: most colleges accept up to 60 credits, AP and IB included; Engineering up to 66 (no more than 33 per full-time year); Architecture up to 64. You must earn at least 60 credits at Cornell (Architecture 90). Rules checked Nov 11, 2026.

Cornell converts quarter credits to semester credits at two-thirds.
Count only letter-graded courses with a C or better; those are the ones that can transfer.
Semesters, or quarters if your school uses quarters. Two semesters is sophomore entry; four is junior entry.
Cornell applies its own score rules, not your current school's. If an exam and a college course cover the same material, only the course counts, so leave that exam out.
Changes tuition only at the contract colleges: CALS, Human Ecology, ILR, Brooks and Dyson.

How it works: Cornell accepts at most 60 transfer credits in most colleges, so credits past 60 are lost. You still need at least 60 credits and, in Arts and Sciences, ILR and Brooks, four semesters at Cornell. A 90-credit junior keeps 60, loses 30 and spends ten semesters in college.
