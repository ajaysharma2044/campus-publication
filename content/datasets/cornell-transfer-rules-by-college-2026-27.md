Each Cornell college sets its own transfer rules, and they differ more than any one-line summary suggests. **Only CALS (3.0, or 3.5 for Biological Sciences), Dyson (3.5) and AAP (2.7) print a minimum GPA.** Hotel (3.3) and ILR (3.4) give what admitted transfers usually have, Human Ecology and Brooks call 3.5 competitive, and Arts and Sciences and Engineering publish no GPA figure for external transfers. Cornell's own FAQ says admitted transfers are "generally" 3.3 or better ([FAQ 325](https://faq.enrollment.cornell.edu/kb/article/325-does-cornell-prefer-transfer-applicants-from-certain-colleges-or-types-of-colleges/)).

The table puts nine colleges side by side on eight rules: the GPA figure and what kind of number it is, the credit cap and the exam-credit sub-cap, the minimum time at Cornell, standing limits, prerequisite courses, closed majors, the deadline, and whom to contact. Every row links the college page it came from.

Use it if you are:

- **A sophomore applicant from a four-year college.** Read the prerequisites column first and take those courses this year, for letter grades. CALS and Human Ecology refuse pass/fail. Global Development (CALS) takes sophomores only.
- **Thinking about entering as a junior.** Look at standing limits. CALS bars anyone with more than four semesters at a four-year school, Hotel bars current juniors, and Engineering takes juniors only in their first semester of junior standing. Juniors also face longer course lists in ILR and Human Ecology.
- **At a community college.** In CALS, an associate's degree means junior standing, with no option to enter as a sophomore. Cornell cannot deny credit because a course was taken at a community college or online, but it must be about 80% similar to the Cornell course and graded C or better ([Registrar](https://registrar.cornell.edu/students/credit-evaluation-services)).
- **Aiming at Engineering or computer science.** Engineering has the only fixed course block: missing MATH 1910, MATH 1920, PHYS 1112 with 1110, or CHEM 2090 with 2091 means the file is not reviewed. Its cap is 66 credits, the highest, but no more than 33 per full-time year.
- **A New York resident.** Five of the nine colleges (CALS, Human Ecology, ILR, Brooks, Dyson) charge NY residents $49,816 in 2026-27 tuition, against $73,946 at the four endowed colleges, a gap of $24,130 a year ([Cornell Chronicle](https://news.cornell.edu/stories/2026/03/board-trustees-approves-2026-27-budget-parameters)).

## What the table shows

- **GPA figures come in four kinds.** Minimum: CALS 3.0 (Biological Sciences 3.5), Dyson 3.5, AAP 2.7. Typical of admits: Hotel 3.3, ILR 3.4. Competitive: Human Ecology 3.5, Brooks 3.5. None published: Arts and Sciences, Engineering. A 3.0 clears CALS's floor but sits below every typical or competitive figure.
- **The cap is 60 credits almost everywhere.** Arts and Sciences, CALS, Hotel, ILR and Brooks print 60. Engineering allows 66, Architecture 64 ([Registrar](https://registrar.cornell.edu/students/credit-evaluation-services)). Human Ecology and Dyson follow the registrar's 60.
- **Five colleges limit exam credit inside the cap.** CALS 15 (AP), Hotel 15 (AP, IB, GCE), Human Ecology 15 (pre-college credit), Dyson 15 (test credit), ILR 12 (AP). Dyson gives no test credit for calculus, statistics, microeconomics or macroeconomics, so transfers take those four in college ([Dyson](https://business.cornell.edu/admissions/undergraduate/bs-aem/application-requirements/)).
- **Residence floors differ.** Arts and Sciences: 60 credits and four semesters. ILR: 60 credits and four full-time semesters. Brooks: four semesters on the Ithaca campus. Engineering: 60 credits. Architecture needs 90 credits at Cornell under the registrar's rule. Everywhere else the registrar's 60 credits at Cornell apply.
- **March 15 is the deadline for every college.** Engineering, Hotel, ILR, AAP and Dyson repeat it on their own pages; Human Ecology and Brooks review applications on a rolling basis. The central dates: March 15, 2027 for the application and fee, April 15, 2027 for everything else ([Undergraduate Admissions](https://admissions.cornell.edu/apply/transfer-applicants)).
- **Three colleges close or limit majors.** AAP takes no external B.Arch. transfers for fall 2027. CALS closes Global and Public Health Sciences to transfers and sends AEM applicants to Dyson. Engineering admits you to one major, with no undecided option, and you stay in it at least a year.

## Method

1. **List the colleges.** Take the nine undergraduate colleges and schools with external transfer rules: Arts and Sciences, CALS, Engineering, Hotel, Human Ecology, ILR, AAP, Brooks and Dyson. That gives 9 rows.
2. **Open each college's own transfer page** (the source_url column) and record its rules in the words the page uses.
3. **Classify the GPA figure.** `minimum` is a floor the college says applicants must meet. `typical` is what admitted transfers usually have. `competitive` is a figure the college says helps. `no published figure` means the college publishes no GPA figure for external transfers.
4. **Record the credit cap and any exam-credit sub-cap** from the college page. Where the college sets no cap of its own, the registrar's university rule applies: up to 60 credits including exam credit, Engineering 66, Architecture 64 ([Registrar](https://registrar.cornell.edu/students/credit-evaluation-services)).
5. **Record the minimum time at Cornell** the same way. The registrar's university floor is 60 credits at Cornell (Architecture 90).
6. **Record standing limits, prerequisite courses, closed or limited majors, and the deadline exactly as the college page prints it.** Arts and Sciences and CALS follow the central March 15 deadline.
7. **Record the admissions contact** from the college page. Where the office serves admitted students, the cell says so.
8. **Add tuition.** `tuition_basis` marks the five contract colleges (CALS, Human Ecology, ILR, Brooks, Dyson) and the four endowed ones. `ny_resident_tuition` is the 2026-27 figure a New York resident pays: $49,816 at contract colleges, $73,946 at endowed. Non-residents pay $73,946 everywhere ([Cornell Chronicle](https://news.cornell.edu/stories/2026/03/board-trustees-approves-2026-27-budget-parameters)).
9. **Count across rows** for the summary above: GPA kinds (3 minimum, 2 typical, 2 competitive, 2 with no published figure), college-set credit caps (6 of 9), exam sub-caps (5 of 9), college-set residence floors (4 of 9), March 15 on the college's own page (5 of 9).

**Credit loss under the cap.** With a 60-credit cap, 120 credits to graduate and a four-semester residence floor, at 15 credits a semester: 60 credits earned elsewhere lose nothing and finish in 8 semesters in all. 75 lose 15 (9 semesters), 90 lose 30 (10 semesters), 105 lose 45 (11 semesters). In Engineering, two full years elsewhere at 33 credits a year reach the 66 cap exactly. This model assumes every credit is accepted up to the cap; real evaluations are case by case.

Sample size: 9 colleges, each from its own admissions page.

## Caveats

- **Nine rows, one date.** Each row is one college's rules as of 2026-11-11. Colleges revise these rules between cycles; the ILR guidelines are the fall 2026 edition, updated 2026-02-10.
- **A GPA figure is not an admit line.** Only CALS, Dyson and AAP print a floor. The typical and competitive figures describe admitted students or what helps.
- **Some contacts serve admitted students.** Engineering Advising and the Dyson Office of Student Services work with transfers after admission; before you apply, write to the admissions contact on the college page ([Tatkon Center](https://scl.cornell.edu/get-involved/tatkon-center-new-students/new-students-cornell/transfer/course-enrollment/college-school-links)).
- **Two easy-to-miss items.** Engineering's core is the four math, physics and chemistry courses; CS 1110 or CS 1112 appears on some majors' own lists, such as Computer Science. Hotel applicants upload a resume or CV through the Common App ([Undergraduate Admissions](https://admissions.cornell.edu/apply/transfer-applicants)).
- **Credits are evaluated after admission.** Every cap is a ceiling. The college decides course by course, needs a C or better and about 80% similarity, and most colleges will not pre-approve courses.
