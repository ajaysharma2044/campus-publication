In fall 2025, **7,381 students applied to transfer to Cornell, 864 were admitted (11.7%) and 625 enrolled**, so 72.3% of admitted transfers came. Those are census counts from Cornell's own [Common Data Set 2025-26](https://irp.cornell.edu/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/Cornell-Common-Data-Set-2025-26.pdf), the only year with all three numbers from Cornell. Transfers made up **14.0% of new undergraduates in fall 2025 and 14.1% in fall 2026**, in line with Cornell Housing's "almost 15%" ([Cornell Housing](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/housing-details/apply-housing/new-undergrad-application-process)).

The table puts 12 entering terms side by side, fall 2017 to fall 2026, including two spring terms from before Cornell went fall-only. Every row says where its numbers came from, graded from Cornell's census data down to third-party sites, so you can see which figures to lean on.

Use it if you are:

- **A sophomore applicant from a four-year college.** The fall 2025 row is the one to quote: about 1 in 8.5 applicants was admitted. Rates for older years come from third-party sites and range from 9.3% to 18%.
- **Thinking about entering as a junior.** Cornell publishes no split by entering class standing, so these counts mix sophomores and juniors.
- **At a community college.** In fall 2021, 96 of 674 incoming transfers (14.2%) came from two-year or community colleges. That is the only official count. Cornell's FAQ says "about a quarter" ([FAQ 325](https://faq.enrollment.cornell.edu/kb/article/325-does-cornell-prefer-transfer-applicants-from-certain-colleges-or-types-of-colleges/)).
- **A parent sizing up the odds.** Transfer admission was about 1.4 times as likely as first-year admission in fall 2025 (11.7% against 8.4%). It is still selective: 625 transfers enrolled in fall 2025 and 603 in fall 2026.
- **An international applicant.** In fall 2021, 86 of the incoming transfers (12.8%) were international ([IRP 2021](https://irp.cornell.edu/wp-content/uploads/2021/08/Profile2021-Fall-Transfers.pdf)). No newer count by citizenship has been published.

## What the table shows

- **Fall enrollment stays between 542 and 674.** The counts are 613 (2017), 645 (2018), 674 (2021, expected), 542 (2022), 556 (2024, third-party), 625 (2025, census) and 603 (2026, announced).
- **Applications rose and admits swung.** On third-party counts, applicants grew from 6,581 in fall 2023 to 7,218 in fall 2024. The CDS gives 7,381 for fall 2025, 800 more than 2023 (+12.2%). Admits fell 164 (-19.7%) from 2023 to 2024, then reached 864 in 2025.
- **No steady admit rate can be quoted across years.** The third-party rates run 13% (2019), 18% (2020), 15.7% (2021), 13.8% (2022), 12.7% (2023) and 9.3% (2024). The rate moved 3.4 points in one year, and only the 2025 figure is Cornell's.
- **Most admitted transfers enroll.** In fall 2025, 625 of 864 did, a 72.3% yield. Of all applicants, 8.5% ended up enrolled.
- **Spring entry was small.** 88 transfers were expected in spring 2021 and 72 enrolled in spring 2023, out of 584 applications. Cornell's current admissions pages take external transfers for fall only.

## Method

Every step follows the topic brief, sections 3.1, 3.2, 3.5, 10.5 and 10.6.
1. **Collect the terms.** Take every entering term with a sourced applicant, admit or enrollee count, or a stated admit rate: fall 2017 to fall 2026 and spring 2021 and 2023. That gives 12 rows. Fall 2019 and fall 2020 have a stated rate only.
2. **Grade each source.** `cds_primary` is the Cornell Common Data Set 2025-26, sections C1 (first-years) and D2 (transfers), census date 2025-10-15. `irp_official` is Cornell Institutional Research and Planning's 2021 Fall Transfers profile, July 2021 deposit data. `chronicle` is the Cornell Chronicle. `sun` is the Cornell Daily Sun quoting a Cornell official or spokesperson. `third_party` is a consultant or aggregator site with no Cornell source named. Admissions counts and enrollment counts are graded separately because they sometimes come from different places.
3. **Record what kind of enrollment count it is.** Census (fall 2025), expected from deposits (2021), a class story or announcement written before the census (fall 2022, fall 2026), a spokesperson's figure (2017, 2018) or a sum of a blog's by-college table (fall 2024).
4. **Compute the admit rate** as admitted divided by applicants, only where both counts come from the same source: 834 / 6,581 = 12.67% (fall 2023); 670 / 7,218 = 9.28% (fall 2024); 864 / 7,381 = 11.71% (fall 2025). Where a site gives only a rate (2019 to 2022), record the rate as stated and mark it "third-party stated rate."
5. **Compute applicants per admit:** 6,581 / 834 = 7.89; 7,218 / 670 = 10.77; 7,381 / 864 = 8.54.
6. **Compute yield** as enrolled divided by admitted, only from one source: 625 / 864 = 72.3% (fall 2025). Fall 2024 gets no yield because its 556 enrollees and 670 admits come from different sites. **Enrolled per applicant:** 625 / 7,381 = 8.5% (fall 2025); 72 / 584 = 12.3% (spring 2023, enrolled over applications, not an admit rate).
7. **Compute transfers' share of new students** as transfers / (first-years + transfers): 625 / (3,827 + 625) = 625 / 4,452 = 14.0% (fall 2025, CDS); 603 / (3,681 + 603) = 603 / 4,284 = 14.1% (fall 2026, Chronicle).
8. **Compare with first-year odds:** 6,077 / 72,523 = 8.4% first-year admit rate in fall 2025 (CDS C1), and 11.7 / 8.4 = 1.4.

Sample size: 12 entering terms; 9 with an enrollment count, 4 with applicant counts, 3 with applicant and admit counts, 1 (fall 2025) with all three from Cornell.

## Caveats

- **One year is Cornell's own census.** Only fall 2025 has applicants, admits and enrollees from the Common Data Set. Every rate before 2025 is a third-party figure that cannot be traced to a Cornell source, and two sites disagree on 2019 (13% against 17.09%).
- **Enrollment counts are not all the same kind.** Fall 2021 and spring 2021 are expected numbers from July deposits. Fall 2022 and fall 2026 come from Chronicle stories written before the census. Fall 2024 is a blog's sum. Compare them as rough sizes, not as a precise trend. Fall 2025 (census) and fall 2026 (announcement) are not directly comparable.
- **Two-term spring series.** Spring 2021 and spring 2023 are the only spring terms with counts. Cornell now takes external transfers for fall only, and when spring entry ended is not dated.
- **Excluded figures.** An undated 535 from collegetransfer.org is left out as unreliable. A 2008 Sun figure (about 2,900 applied, 616 enrolled) has no recorded URL. Press claims of "500 to 600 each semester" (Sun, 2020) and "about 700 a year" (a 2017 Sun column) are reporters' estimates, not counts.
- **Rows with blank cells.** Fall 2017 to fall 2022 have no applicant or admit counts, fall 2019, fall 2020 and fall 2023 have no enrollment count, and fall 2026 has no applicant or admit counts yet; they are due in the 2026-27 Common Data Set. Cornell publishes no admit rate by college, major or entering class standing.
