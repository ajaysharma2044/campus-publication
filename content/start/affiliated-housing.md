You live in one of Cornell's eight co-ops or a recognized fraternity or sorority house, or you want to, and either one counts as your second-year housing. The decisions in front of you are how to get a room, what it costs against a dorm, how to keep Cornell's housing application and license in order so you are not billed twice, and where you go for junior year. Read these in order; each guide carries the numbers and their sources.

1. [Off-campus housing at Cornell: the 2026-27 guide](/housing/cornell-off-campus-housing-guide/). The pillar: how the whole Ithaca market works and where affiliated housing sits in it. Read it first for the map.
2. [Cornell co-ops and Greek houses: cost and how to get in](/housing/cornell-co-ops-greek-houses-cost-how-to-join/). Your main guide: what each co-op charges, how selection works, what Greek rent covers. Read it before the next co-op round or chapter housing decision.

## Dates to watch

- Early November and early February: co-op selection rounds; each house runs its own process.
- January 1: chapters need a live-in advisor and four upper-level officers for auto-approval.
- January 31 and February 1: the Cornell license cancellation fee for returning juniors and seniors is $50 through Jan 31 and $500 from Feb 1.
- March 15 (the 2026 date): Greek in-house affirmation in the Housing Application; confirmed members skip the April Spring Housing Selection for Rising Sophomores.
- April 6 (the 2026 date): housing application due for every first- and second-year, including those expecting a chapter house.
