On Sunday evenings at 8:30, students gather in the main lounge of Low Rise 10 on North Campus for Unity Hour, the weekly talk series of Ujamaa Residential College ([Cornell Housing](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/campus-housing/upper-level-undergraduates/program-houses/ujamaa-residential-college)). Ujamaa was founded in 1972 as a home for Black students. In May 2026, a resident advisor there told [The Cornell Daily Sun](https://www.cornellsun.com/article/2026/05/oq5rriymdyfz) that about 30% of its residents were Black.

That gap between what a house was built for and who ends up living in it is the open question hanging over Cornell's program houses. A program house is a themed residence hall: a dorm, or part of one, organized around a shared interest, identity or way of living. Cornell lists 13 of them for 2027-28, from an arts community in a 1913 hall to a substance-free floor that opened in fall 2026 ([Cornell Housing](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/campus-housing/upperlevel-undergraduate-campus-housing)). They cost exactly what any other hall costs. What you are choosing is the community, and whether that community shows up depends on who else chooses it.

The 13 houses hold at least 1,078 students: 1,040 in the other 12 houses, plus 38 in Sober Living in fall 2026. More than 500 of them are first-year and transfer students ([Cornell Housing](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/housing-details/apply-housing-campus/new-undergrad-application-process)). For a new student, a program house is the only part of campus where you choose your building.

## What a program house is, and what it is not

Cornell describes program houses as close-knit communities with regular events, often run with related academic departments ([Cornell Housing](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/campus-housing/new-transfer-undergraduates/program-houses)). Most have Faculty and Community Fellows who come to events, eat with residents and lead trips. Some go further. Loving House has a faculty member living in Mews Hall with her family.

Two things set a program house apart from an ordinary hall. First, you opt in. When you list a house on the housing application, you check a box saying you have read and support its mission ([Cornell Housing](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/campus-housing/first-year-undergraduates/program-houses/program-house-mission-and-expectations)). Second, the house expects you to take part. Loving House spells it out: a retreat each fall and spring, at least two Fellow events per semester, and a Learning Where You Live course for new first-year and transfer residents.

You can also join a house without living in it. Cornell calls this out-of-house membership. You request it through a form, and the house's residence hall director sends the details. Out-of-house members get many of the same benefits, which makes it a way to try a community before you apply to live there.

Not all 13 are program houses in the strict sense. Cornell says the Sober Living Community is not a true program house, and the Language House page says it is not a traditional one either. Both still sit on Cornell's program-house list and go through the same selection step ([Cornell Housing](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/campus-housing/new-transfer-undergraduates/program-houses)).

As in any residence hall, every undergraduate living in a program house must buy a meal plan ([Cornell Dining](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/dining/meal-plans-rates)).

## How Cornell got 13 of them

Program houses began with art. Prudence Risley Hall opened in 1913 as Cornell's first step north of Fall Creek Gorge. In 1970 it became home to Cornell's first program house ([Cornell Housing](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/campus-housing/upper-level-undergraduates/program-houses/risley-residential-college)). The same year, Cornell opened the North Campus Low Rises. Low Rise 8 has housed the International Living Center since its first year.

The next two arrived fast, and for different reasons. Ecology House was established in 1971. Ujamaa followed in 1972. The Cornell Daily Sun traces Ujamaa to the 1969 Willard Straight Hall takeover, a 36-hour occupation by the Afro-American Society to protest racial injustice on campus. The takeover also produced the Africana Studies and Research Center ([Cornell Daily Sun](https://www.cornellsun.com/article/2026/05/oq5rriymdyfz)).

A second wave came in the 1980s and 1990s. The Language House opened in 1984 with German, French and Spanish ([Cornell Chronicle](https://news.cornell.edu/stories/2025/04/language-house-opens-students-learning-asl)). Akwe:kon and the Multicultural Living Learning Unit both date to 1991. The Latino Living Center followed in 1994.

The newest houses answer newer needs. Loving House, for LGBTQ+ students and allies, opened in 2019 on the first floor of Mews Hall ([Cornellians](https://alumni.cornell.edu/cornellians/program-houses/)). Veterans House opened in fall 2021 after years of lobbying by student veterans. Its first 26 residents included 20 undergraduate veterans ([Cornell Daily Sun](https://www.cornellsun.com/article/2021/09/new-program-house-opens-creating-community-among-student-veterans)). The Sober Living Community opened in fall 2026 on the fourth floor of Bauer in Court-Kay-Bauer Hall ([Cornell Daily Sun](https://www.cornellsun.com/article/2026/11/oasis-on-north-campus-cornell-opens-sober-living-community-in-court-kay-bauer-hall)).

## The houses, one by one

Eleven of the 13 houses are on North Campus, where all first-years live. Two sit near West Campus and Collegetown ([Cornell Housing facilities](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/facilities)).

### The Low Rises: HILC, JAM and Ujamaa

Three houses fill Low Rises 8, 9 and 10, buildings from 1970 near the Robert Purcell Community Center and Appel Commons. All three use the same layout. Each cluster has one double, one triple, two singles and a shared bathroom. That is seven people to a bathroom. Clusters are single gender; floors are mixed. None of the three has air conditioning (Cornell Housing).

The Jerome H. Holland International Living Center, called HILC, has more than 160 residents in Low Rise 8 ([Cornell Housing](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/campus-housing/upperlevel-undergraduate-campus-housing/program-houses/holland-international-living-center-hilc)). International and American students live together, and a student-run council helps shape house life. In 1985 the house was renamed for Jerome H. Holland, a 1939 graduate who became a university president and diplomat. HILC stays open over winter break, which matters to students who cannot fly home for it.

Just About Music, or JAM, holds 166 residents in Low Rise 9 ([Cornell Housing](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/campus-housing/upper-level-undergraduates/program-houses/just-about-music-jam)). It has a concert stage with sound and lighting, a recording studio, a 24-hour piano and practice room, and shared instruments. It hosts an open mic every month. You do not need to be a music major. Cornellians reported that only a handful of residents are.

Ujamaa Residential College has 144 residents in Low Rise 10 ([Cornell Housing](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/campus-housing/upper-level-undergraduates/program-houses/ujamaa-residential-college)). It celebrates the cultures of the African diaspora and is open to students of every background. Its calendar includes faculty-hosted dinners, Black History Month events, the State of Black America Conference and the Festival of Black Gospel. Each unit of clusters shares a study room, lounge and kitchen.

### Risley and the Latino Living Center

Risley Residential College is the largest house, with 194 residents. Its building looks like a castle and has its own dining room, modeled on the hall at Christ Church, Oxford. Risley Dining is Cornell's dining room certified free of gluten, tree nuts and peanuts. Upstairs, rooms are singles and doubles on corridors, with all-gender bathrooms above the first floor and a kitchen on every floor. Residents run three hall councils and seven major programs a year ([Cornell Housing mission page](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/campus-housing/first-year-undergraduates/program-houses/program-house-mission-and-expectations)). Risley also has creative shops, a theatre and a dance studio.

Across from Risley, Anna Comstock Hall holds the Latino Living Center ([Cornell Housing](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/campus-housing/upperlevel-undergraduate-campus-housing/program-houses/latino-living-center-llc)). Its 55 residents live in singles and doubles arranged in suites, with a kitchen, library, computer room and study lounge. Its building is named for Anna Botsford Comstock, the first woman at Cornell to hold the rank of professor. Residents are greeted with a Spanish welcome, Bienvenidos a Su Casa, or Welcome to Your Home. Cornellians reported a weekly Café con Leche discussion series in 2023.

### Akwe:kon and Ecology House

Akwe:kon (ah-GWAY-gohn) has 35 residents ([Cornell Housing](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/campus-housing/upper-level-undergraduates/program-houses/akwekon)). It was one of the first university residences in the country built to celebrate American Indian culture, and it is the only Cornell building designed and built as a program house. Its name is the Mohawk word for all of us. Cornellians reported that Haudenosaunee people shaped its design, from the longhouse-style ceilings to a row of boulders for the six nations. Cornell's mission page says about half of residents are Indigenous. Sun reporting from 2025, described below, found students placed there from outside that community. Rooms are singles and doubles, with a kitchen, library, computer room and two study lounges.

Ecology House, called Eco, fills Hurlburt House off Triphammer Road ([Cornell Housing](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/campus-housing/upper-level-undergraduates/program-houses/ecology-house)). The building opened in 1953 as the Cornell Heights Residential Club and became student housing in 1966. Its 96 residents all live in doubles, each with its own private bathroom. Each room has its own heating and air conditioning unit. The house has a greenhouse, a young apple orchard, a woodshop, a swap shop and a backyard for campfires. Upper-level residents get a free bus pass, a perk first-years already have. Eco also runs an annual retreat in the Adirondacks.

### Inside bigger halls: McLLU, Loving House, the Language House and Sober Living

Four communities fill part of a larger building.

The Multicultural Living Learning Unit, McLLU (pronounced "McClue"), has 47 residents on two floors of Clara Dickson Hall, at 21 Northcross Road ([Cornell Housing](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/campus-housing/upper-level-undergraduates/program-houses/multicultural-living-learning-unit-mcllu)). Students founded it to build a community across race, religion, gender, sexuality and ability. Rooms are singles and triples. Some rooms have no overhead light, so Cornell tells residents to bring a floor lamp. Its "McLLUnity Nights" bring in faculty and staff, and the house has taken trips to New York City, Philadelphia and Washington, D.C.

Loving House holds 30 residents in Mews Hall, at 172 Cradit Farm Drive ([Cornell Housing](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/campus-housing/upper-level-undergraduates/program-houses/loving-house)). It centers LGBTQ+ students and welcomes all identities. Rooms are singles and doubles in pods of three or four rooms sharing one bathroom. Every pod is mixed gender, and all bathrooms are all-gender. Claire Menard, a senior lecturer in Romance Studies, is the faculty member in residence.

The Language House is an immersion residence in Toni Morrison Hall for upper-level and transfer students ([Cornell Housing](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/campus-housing/upper-level-undergraduates/program-houses/language-house)). For 2026-27 its languages are Spanish, French, Mandarin, Korean and American Sign Language. It has more than 50 residents in suites with shared bathrooms, and its rooms are temperature-controlled. Each language group has a residential language fellow, a fluent or native speaker. Cornell surveys students each year to decide which languages to add or drop. Arabic, Farsi, Japanese, Italian and Russian have all been offered in past years.

The Sober Living Community is for students who choose not to use alcohol or drugs, for any reason ([Cornell Housing](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/campus-housing/upper-level-undergraduates/program-houses/sober-living-community)). It is open to undergraduates in every class year. Residents agree to keep their home substance-free. It took 38 residents in its first semester, with room to grow, the Sun reported. Jacob Carver, who coordinates alcohol and drug services at Cornell Health, called it an "oasis on North Campus." Before it, a privately run Cornell Sober House served students from 2016 to 2022. Its nonprofit blamed the pandemic for the closing. Ask housing@cornell.edu about room types and air conditioning on the floor before you apply.

### Off North Campus: EEC and Veterans House

The Equity & Engagement Community, or EEC, sits at 2 Forest Park Lane, at the base of Libe Slope near West Campus ([Cornell Housing](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/campus-housing/upper-level-undergraduates/program-houses/equity-engagement-community-eec)). It is part of South Campus and a walk from Collegetown. Its 37 residents, including two resident advisors, live in singles, super singles and doubles. A super single is the highest-priced single on Cornell's rate table. The house runs programs on identity, social justice and talking across differences. It is open to transfer and upper-level students only.

Veterans House has 26 residents, including one resident advisor ([Cornell Housing](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/campus-housing/upper-level-undergraduates/program-houses/veterans-house)). It supports student veterans, but you do not need to have served. ROTC students, students with military families, and students in government or policy fields all qualify, as do graduate students. Every room is a super single. The house runs year-round, so window air conditioners are available on request at no charge.

Veterans House is in the former Alpha Tau Omega house at 625 University Ave., the Sun reported when it opened. Cornell's housing directory gives the same address ([Cornell Housing facilities](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/facilities)). Ask veterancommunity@cornell.edu which address applies to your year.

## What a year costs

A 2026-27 program house double costs $13,744 for the academic year, or $6,872 per semester. A single is $15,566, a triple $12,644 and a super single $16,595. Program houses share this rate table with every residence hall and West Campus house ([Cornell rates](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/housing-details/rates-amenities-licenses/housing-rates)). The rate covers gas, electricity, garbage pickup and internet up to 200 GB a month. There is no security deposit.

Room type, not the house, sets your price. In the Low Rises and McLLU, a triple saves $1,100 a year against a double. At Veterans House, every room is a super single, so every undergraduate there pays the top rate (Cornell rates).

The meal plan is the other half of the bill. First-years must buy the Unlimited plan, at $7,604 a year for 2026-27 ([Cornell Dining](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/dining/meal-plans-rates/undergraduate-meal-plans)). After the first year, program house residents can choose the cheaper Bear Traditional plan, at $7,056 a year with up to 14 meals a week. Most plans also carry a $50 administrative fee each year.

A co-op is the cheaper way to stay in Cornell-approved housing. The Red Bud double in Von Cramm costs $6,974 for two semesters, $6,770 less than a program house double before meals. Our guide to [co-ops and Greek houses](/cornell/housing/cornell-co-ops-greek-houses-cost-how-to-join/) covers how those work. Our [cost table of 28 ways to live at Cornell](/datasets/ithaca-housing-cost-by-living-situation-2026-27/) sets the hall double against Collegetown shares, and our analysis of [whether off-campus is cheaper than a dorm](/cornell/housing/is-off-campus-cheaper-than-cornell-dorm/) runs the full comparison for juniors and seniors.

A first-year pays the same bill in any building, so listing a house costs nothing extra.

## How to get in, year by year

You apply to a program house the same way in every year: you list it as a preference on your Cornell housing application, in the Housing and Dining Portal. You can list as many communities as you like as a new student, and each runs its own selection, so you can be accepted to more than one ([Cornell Housing](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/campus-housing/new-undergraduates/program-houses)). Three houses ask for more. The Language House, Veterans House and the Sober Living Community each have an extra application inside the housing application, and you must be approved before you can pick a room.

At a Black Students United town hall on March 10, 2026, administrators said most program houses have no formal application process beyond that preference ([Cornell Daily Sun](https://www.cornellsun.com/article/2026/03/bsu-town-hall-addresses-housing-financial-aid-and-federal-policy-concerns)). They said placement depends on student preferences, open rooms, disability accommodations and roommate groups, and that ranking a house does not guarantee a bed.

### As a new student

New first-years and transfers apply within 10 days of getting a NetID, Cornell's student login ([Cornell Housing](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/housing-details/apply-housing-campus/new-undergrad-application-process)). There is no fee. The first question is whether you want a program house or a residence hall. Unless you list a program house, your hall is chosen by a random auto-allocation from your room-type preferences. You do not rank halls. Housing confirmations start arriving in mid-June. Our [first-year start page](/start/first-year/) collects the rest of the first-year housing steps.

```reader "If you're an incoming first-year"
Ten of the 13 houses take first-years: Akwe:kon, Ecology House, HILC, JAM, the Latino Living Center, Loving House, McLLU, Risley, Sober Living and Ujamaa. List only houses whose mission you would support.
```

```reader "If you're a transfer student"
Transfers can live in every program house, including the three closed to first-years: EEC, the Language House and Veterans House. Placement is not certain. At the March 2026 town hall, the Sun reported, one speaker said they applied for Ujamaa as a transfer and were placed in a double on West Campus. List two or three houses, and treat any one of them as a hope, not a plan.
```

### As a rising sophomore

Every second-year must live in Cornell housing or approved affiliated housing, and a program house counts ([Cornell Housing](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/housing-details/apply-housing-campus/current-undergraduate-application-process)). You can return to your first-year house, move to another, or leave program houses for a regular hall. Cornell's sophomore selection for 2027-28 runs on fixed dates ([Cornell Housing](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/housing-details/apply-housing-campus/current-undergraduate-application-process/spring-housing-selection-rising-sophomores)). The application opens Feb. 1, 2027, and closes April 4 at 11:59 p.m. On it, you can name up to three program houses. Timeslots go out April 7.

Priority Program House Selection is April 12, 2027, a day before Rising Sophomore Open Room Selection begins, so program house students pick rooms first (Cornell Housing). You can block with up to four friends, and your group gets one random timeslot. Students needing disability accommodations must have them approved by April 5, 2027.

If you are weighing a program house against a co-op or chapter house for sophomore year, the deadline that matters comes earlier. You must state your plan for affiliated housing in the application by March 15. Students seeking a full exemption from the second-year requirement apply by the same deadline as the application; our guide to [exemptions from the residential requirement](/cornell/housing/cornell-residential-requirement-exemptions-live-off-campus/) explains who qualifies. Our [sophomore start page](/start/sophomore/) lays out the whole second-year path.

### As a rising junior or senior

Cornell does not guarantee housing after the second year. The fall round for 2027-28 has already run ([Cornell Housing](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/housing-details/apply-housing-campus/current-undergraduate-application-process/fall-housing-selection-rising-juniors-and-seniors)). Applications ran from Sept. 1 to Sept. 27, 2026, and Priority Program House Selection was Oct. 1. Open Room Selection came a week later. Cornell warns that program house beds are not promised in the later stages.

That makes a program house the earliest door back to campus for upper-level students. It matters more now. In fall 2025, the first year of a new selection system, rising juniors and seniors told the Sun that rooms went fast and late timeslots left few choices ([Cornell Daily Sun](https://www.cornellsun.com/article/2025/11/new-changes-to-fall-housing-selection-leave-some-rising-juniors-seniors-disappointed)). Housing told the paper it must balance upper-level demand with its duty to house first-years, transfers and sophomores. If you want to stay on campus for 2028-29, list a program house you would enjoy in the fall 2027 round.

Juniors and seniors who picked a program house room in the October round can still cancel, at a price that rises through the spring and summer ([Cornell housing license](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/housing-details/rates-amenities-licenses/academic-year-housing-license-2027-2028)). Canceling costs $50 through Jan. 31, 2027, then $500 through March 31, $1,000 through June 30, and $2,000 through Aug. 15. After that, the charge is half the fall room rate. Study away, a leave or graduation lets you cancel the fall term free by June 15, 2027. Check your own fee in our [license cancellation fee calculator](/tools/cornell-housing-license-cancellation-fee-calculator/).

## Who actually lives there

Not everyone in a program house chose it. Cornell fills empty beds through general housing assignment, and two Sun reports, from December 2025 and May 2026, describe what that has meant at Akwe:kon and Ujamaa (Cornell Daily Sun).

In December 2025, Indigenous student leaders told the Sun that Akwe:kon was taking in overflow housing as Indigenous enrollment fell ([Cornell Daily Sun](https://www.cornellsun.com/article/2025/12/even-if-our-numbers-are-lower-were-still-here-indigenous-student-engagement-persists-despite-enrollment-drops)). Cornell enrolled two American Indian first-year students in 2024 and four in 2025, down from 10 in 2023. One student leader said the house was becoming general housing. Another said its two guest rooms for visiting scholars and its TV room had become student rooms. Housing and Residential Life said vacancies sometimes must be filled to meet student needs, and that living in Akwe:kon is not limited to Indigenous students.

In May 2026, the Sun reported a sharper version of the same story at Ujamaa ([Cornell Daily Sun](https://www.cornellsun.com/article/2026/05/oq5rriymdyfz)). Besides the RA's estimate, a former resident said about two-thirds of residents were Black in 2023. A first-year told the paper she was placed there without asking. Residents also described heating and water problems, black mold, and dirty bathrooms. A university spokesperson said students are placed in Ujamaa either by expressing interest or through general housing assignment.

These reports land during federal pressure on race-conscious programs. In February 2025, a Department of Education letter singled out the use of race in dormitories and gave schools two weeks to comply ([Cornell Daily Sun](https://www.cornellsun.com/article/2025/02/bsu-holds-emergency-meeting-as-affinity-groups-program-houses-are-threatened-by-department-of-education-guidelines)). Cornell later reached a settlement with the Trump administration. At the March 2026 town hall, President Michael I. Kotlikoff said the agreement changes none of Cornell's policies ([Cornell Daily Sun](https://www.cornellsun.com/article/2026/03/bsu-town-hall-addresses-housing-financial-aid-and-federal-policy-concerns)). He and other administrators said student organizations must already be open to everyone under federal civil rights law.

Every program house is open to any student who supports its mission. Ujamaa residents told the Sun that events and turnout fell as fewer residents had chosen the house. If you want the community, list the house and go to its events before you move in.

## The trade-offs

Air conditioning is the clearest physical trade-off. Of the 13 houses, eight say their rooms have none (Cornell Housing house pages): Akwe:kon, EEC, HILC, JAM, the Latino Living Center, McLLU, Risley and Ujamaa. Ecology House has in-room units, the Language House rooms are temperature-controlled, Veterans House offers window units, and Loving House has an air circulation system. For the Sober Living floor, ask housing@cornell.edu.

Bathrooms differ just as much. Ecology House gives each double its own bathroom. The Low Rise houses put seven people on one bathroom. Loving House, and Risley above its first floor, use all-gender bathrooms. Veterans House uses single-sex corridor bathrooms.

Location cuts both ways. EEC and Veterans House are a walk from Collegetown but apart from the 11 houses on North Campus (Cornell Housing).

Then there is the expectation to take part. Loving House asks the most. The Language House encourages residents to speak their target language at home (Cornell Chronicle). Most houses ask for less, but all of them ask you to support a mission. A student who wants a quiet room and no obligations is better served by a regular hall.

## What comes next

Three open questions will shape program houses over the next two years.

The first is placement. Students asked Cornell in March 2026 to explain how it fills Ujamaa and other identity houses, and administrators pointed to existing rules (Cornell Daily Sun). Whether Cornell changes how it fills empty program-house beds, or keeps using them for overflow, will decide what these houses feel like for students arriving in fall 2027.

The second is the Language House. Cornell's page says its future location is still being decided, so check where it will be before you list it.

The third is the newest house. Cornell told the Sun it will review the Sober Living Community in its regular residential assessment, with the first review in mid-fall 2026. Feedback from fall 2026 and spring 2027 will shape the house for 2027-28, and Housing said it can add beds if interest grows (Cornell Daily Sun).

## Before you list a program house

- Read the house's mission statement, because you will check a box saying you support it.
- Visit an event first, or sign up as an out-of-house member.
- Check the room types the house offers, since room type sets your price.
- Check for air conditioning and the bathroom setup if either matters to you.
- If you want the Language House, Veterans House or Sober Living, finish the extra application inside the housing application.
- As a rising sophomore, name up to three houses before the April application deadline.
- If you might study away, note the June 15, 2027, penalty-free cancellation deadline.
