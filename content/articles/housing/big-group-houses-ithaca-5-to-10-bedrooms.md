A 5-to-10-bedroom house is the cheapest way to live within walking distance of campus, from $695 a bed in [Ithaca Renting's 2027-28 listings](https://ithacarenting.com/collegetown/). The catch is timing and size: the biggest houses sell out first, and a lease makes every housemate responsible for every bed.

```reader "If you're organizing the group"
Fill the house before you sign, because the group's size sets the price for everyone. Cornell says most local leases bind housemates "jointly and severally", so one person who backs out leaves the rest paying. Settle who covers a vacancy in writing before you sign, and check that each person can afford a full share. The [roommates and joint liability guide](/cornell/housing/cornell-off-campus-roommates-joint-liability/) covers how to set that up.
```

```reader "If you're a parent paying part of the rent"
Check what the house costs per bed per year, not per month. A bed at the 6-bedroom median is $9,600 for the year, below Cornell's [$13,744 for a residence hall double](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/housing-details/rates-amenities-licenses/housing-rates). The savings disappear if the group shrinks, so ask your child how many names are on the lease.
```

Early. ## What happens if someone drops out?

Everyone pays more. Cornell says most local leases bind housemates "jointly and severally", so each is responsible for the others' rent and damages for the whole term, per its [housemate guidance](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/living-off-campus/tenant-best-practices/study-abroad/housemate-compatibility-questionnaire). A tenant who overpays can [sue a co-tenant](https://sites.lawschool.cornell.edu/tenants-advocacy/understanding-your-lease) for the difference, but the landlord can collect from anyone.

In a 10-bedroom house at [Ithaca Renting's median bed price](https://ithacarenting.com/collegetown/), one empty bed means nine people split its rent: $86.11 more each a month, or $1,033.33 over the year ($9,300 divided by 9). A group that shrinks from a 6-bedroom to a 4-bedroom pays $446.25 more per person a month ($1,246.25 minus the 6-bedroom median), or $5,355 a year.

## What rules apply to a house this size?

The City of Ithaca treats dwellings with three or more units, or individual units with six or more unrelated persons, as multiple dwellings that must hold a Certificate of Compliance, its rental safety permit, valid for three years, per the [Building Division page](https://www.cityofithacany.gov/146/Certificate-of-Compliance). A large house with many unrelated tenants is the case that rule targets.

So check each house before you sign. Cornell's [safety tool](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/living-off-campus/housing-search-process/safe-living-environments) shows sprinklers, smoke and carbon monoxide alarms, construction type and property use for City addresses. Call the Building Division at 607-274-6508 with the address and ask whether the certificate is current and what occupancy it allows.

## What should I check before I sign?

Walk the whole house and photograph every room. After signing, New York requires the landlord to offer a move-in inspection, and defects you both list cannot come out of the deposit, per [GOL 7-108](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/GOB/7-108). That law also caps the deposit plus any rent advance at one month's rent. For a 10-person house, that is a single month of the whole house, not a month per person.

Put every adult living there on the lease. New York's [roommate law](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/RPP/235-F) requires a tenant to give an occupant's name to the landlord, and occupants have no right to stay if a tenant leaves. In a big house, that gap leaves someone with no standing when the lease changes.

## What to do next

Decide group size first, then pick a house that fits it. Ask the landlord in writing for the price per bed, the lease start and end dates, what utilities the price includes and the certificate's occupancy figure. Use the calculator to compare the house's rent against a smaller unit with a shorter commute.
