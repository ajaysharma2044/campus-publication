```glance
- Collegetown is the walk-to-class choice, and a bed there costs up to $2,025 a month for 2027-28.
- At the same landlord, a downtown bed costs about 20% to 28% less than one in Collegetown.
- Only City of Ithaca addresses get the 120-day renewal rule and Cornell's safety tool.
- Lansing and Northeast need a bus or car; West Hill riders change buses downtown.
```

On the night of Sept. 11, 2019, three Cornell sophomores spread blankets on the sidewalk outside the Ithaca Renting office. They wanted the only three-bedroom in their favorite Collegetown building, at $1,160 a person each month. They settled in 15 hours before the office opened, with an extension cord run through a hole in the door. Move-in was nearly 11 months away ([Cornell Daily Sun](https://cornellsun.com/2019/09/19/cornell-students-sleep-on-sidewalk-for-cheaper-more-desirable-leases/)).

Down the hill, the same company's downtown apartments draw no such line. On Nov. 10, 2026, with leasing for 2027-28 open, Ithaca Renting marked 14 of the 67 Collegetown unit types it lists as sold out ([Ithaca Renting, Collegetown](https://ithacarenting.com/collegetown/)). Its downtown list was still mostly units for 2026-27 or open to rent now, and none were marked sold out ([Ithaca Renting, downtown](https://ithacarenting.com/downtown/)).

That gap is the story of where Cornell students live. Ithaca is a hill, a flat valley and a city line. The hill sets how long your walk to class is. The valley sets how much cheaper your bed is. The city line sets which rules protect you. Ten areas sit on that map, from Collegetown out to Lansing, West Hill and Varna. Cornell's neighborhoods page covers all but Northside and Varna.

Cornell's residence hall double costs $13,744 for the 2026-27 academic year, with gas, electricity, garbage and internet included ([Cornell Housing rates](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/housing-and-residential-life-and-faculty-involvement-campus/rates-amenities-licenses/housing-rates)). Spread over a 12-month lease, that is $1,145.33 a month. Juniors and seniors are not guaranteed a campus room ([Cornell Housing](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/housing-and-residential-life-and-faculty-involvement-campus/apply-housing-campus/current-undergraduate-application-process)). About 95% of graduate and professional students live in housing Cornell does not own ([Cornell graduate housing](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/campus-housing/graduate-professional-student-campus-housing)).

## Why the map looks like this

Collegetown costs the most and signs the earliest because one rule, a few owners and a decade of zoning fights shaped it. Since fall 2022, Cornell has required first-year and sophomore students to live in university or affiliated housing ([Cornell Daily Sun, 2021](https://cornellsun.com/2021/11/09/as-cornell-requires-university-housing-for-sophomores-students-weigh-options/)). The North Campus expansion made that possible. It added about 2,000 beds, about 800 of them for sophomores. So the off-campus market is a junior, senior and graduate market. Its most sought-after leases go to sophomores shopping a year ahead ([Cornell Daily Sun, 2018](https://cornellsun.com/2018/11/07/desirable-collegetown-leases-leave-market-up-to-16-months-before-residents-move-in/)).

Collegetown grew up to serve that market, and a few owners came to hold much of it. In 2013, The Sun traced about $153 million in Collegetown property to eight landlords. Together they held 4,013 of an estimated 6,110 bedrooms, almost two-thirds of the neighborhood ([Cornell Daily Sun, 2013](https://cornellsun.com/2013/05/02/collegetown-housing-market-scrutinized/)). John Novarr, whose Collegetown Terrace project had greatly expanded student housing, alone held 1,559.

Then the city changed the rules for building there. In August 2009, the Common Council endorsed the Collegetown Urban Plan after students pushed for taller buildings and neighbors pushed back ([Cornell Daily Sun, 2009](https://www.cornellsun.com/article/2009/08/common-council-endorses-c-town-plan)). A 2010 zoning code based on the plan was repealed after Collegetown property owners challenged it ([Cornell Daily Sun, 2013](https://cornellsun.com/2013/03/13/city-of-ithaca-considers-changes-to-zoning-that-could-transform-collegetown/)). In March 2014, the council unanimously adopted the Collegetown Area Form Districts, a zoning code that steers new height to central Collegetown and protects the streets around it. Developers had held their designs until it passed ([Cornell Daily Sun, 2014](https://cornellsun.com/2014/09/07/multiple-developments-proposed-for-collegetown)). In 2025, the city proposed raising the limit in its MU-2 mixed-use district from six stories to eight stories and 85 feet ([FingerLakes1](https://www.fingerlakes1.com/2025/03/12/ithaca-proposes-zoning-changes-to-collegetown-area-allowing-taller-buildings)). By 2026, an eight-story building in that district could go ahead without a variance, a special exception from the zoning rules ([Ithaca Times](https://www.ithaca.com/news/ithaca/former-collegetown-fire-station-site-slated-for-16-5m-apartment-development/article_daeb3b07-ae00-497b-9e13-ac15184dc19e.html)).

Charlie O'Connor, who runs Modern Living Rentals, put the result plainly to The Sun: "the only [thing] Collegetown can do is go up" ([Cornell Daily Sun, 2025](https://www.cornellsun.com/article/2025/12/the-ivy-to-establish-new-luxury-housing-in-collegetown-opens-fall-2026)). Everywhere else, the older pattern holds. Big houses on the hillsides were split into apartments, and the valley kept its mix of families, workers and graduate students.

## Ring one: Collegetown

Collegetown is the closest area and the priciest. It sits against the southwest edge of campus, 0.1 to 0.5 miles away. Cornell calls the area a miniature city of shops and restaurants. It also warns that the crowd of mostly undergraduate renters pushes up rent, noise and traffic. From lower Collegetown, the walk to class is uphill, and Cornell puts the longest walk at 15 minutes ([Cornell neighborhoods page](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/campus-housing/housing-search-process/ithaca-neighborhoods)).

The cheapest beds are in the biggest units. Ithaca Renting posts per-bed prices for the 2027-28 lease year. The median bed costs $800 a month in a six-bedroom, $1,246.25 in a four-bedroom and $2,025 for a one-bedroom alone ([EduExplained dataset](/datasets/collegetown-vs-downtown-rent-per-bed-2026/)). A median is the middle price when all the listed prices are lined up. For the full side-by-side with Fall Creek and downtown, see [Collegetown vs Fall Creek vs downtown](/cornell/housing/collegetown-vs-fall-creek-vs-downtown-ithaca-where-to-live/).

Whole apartments follow the same pattern. RentCafe, which tracks buildings with 50 or more units, put the average Collegetown rent at $2,815 a month in October 2026 ([RentCafe, Collegetown](https://www.rentcafe.com/average-rent-market-trends/us/ny/ithaca/collegetown/)). The Ithaca-wide average was $2,263 ([RentCafe, Ithaca](https://www.rentcafe.com/average-rent-market-trends/us/ny/ithaca/)). That puts Collegetown 24.4% above the city ($552 divided by $2,263).

### The towers going up

Four new buildings add about 431 beds to central Collegetown between 2026 and 2028. The Ivy, at College Avenue and Dryden Road, opened in August 2026 with 52 apartments and 81 beds ([Tompkins Weekly](https://www.tompkinsweekly.com/news/three-new-developments-offer-different-answers-to-ithacas-housing-needs-30cfa148)). The Summit, at 201 Oak Ave., replaced an 11-bedroom house with 46 units and about 88 bedrooms. The Bear, at 215 College Ave., is due to open for the 2027-28 year with 84 units and 150 beds (Tompkins Weekly; [Ithaca Voice](https://ithacavoice.org/2026/07/cornell-collegetown-construction/)). Prestige Ithaca, on the old fire station site at 309 College Ave., plans eight stories with 77 units and 112 bedrooms, with building set for October 2026 to July 2028 (Ithaca Times).

That is 81 + 88 + 150 + 112 beds. Against the 6,110 bedrooms The Sun counted in 2013, the last full count, it is about a 7% addition (Ithaca Voice; Ithaca Times; Cornell Daily Sun, 2013).

These buildings are priced for the top of the market. Studios at The Ivy start at $2,595 and three-bedrooms reach $4,900. The Summit's studios start at $2,700 and its four-bedrooms cost $7,200. The Bear runs from $2,850 for a studio to $6,000 for a three-bedroom (Ithaca Voice). Split evenly, a Summit four-bedroom is $1,800 a person and a Bear three-bedroom is $2,000. In December 2025, The Sun quoted Ivy three-bedrooms at $5,500 to $5,800 ([Cornell Daily Sun, 2025](https://www.cornellsun.com/article/2025/12/the-ivy-to-establish-new-luxury-housing-in-collegetown-opens-fall-2026)). Go by the newer July 2026 figure, and ask the building for its 2027-28 rate before you plan around it.

The Ivy's one-bedrooms were spoken for in May 2026, before anyone could tour a finished unit. Shortly after opening, about 25% of its apartments were already leased for the next year (Tompkins Weekly).

Cornell is also a Collegetown landlord now. It bought 301 College Ave. out of foreclosure for $15 million in March 2025 and opened it as a dorm in August 2026. Forty-three students moved in, and Cornell expects 105 by fall 2027. One resident told The Sun the building felt incomplete and rushed. She described a refrigerator running at 55 degrees, kitchens with no stoves and construction outside her window from 7:30 a.m. ([Cornell Daily Sun, 2026](https://www.cornellsun.com/article/2026/09/301-college-ave-opens-for-student-housing-resident-describes-it-as-incomplete-rushed)).

### The older houses

Most Collegetown renters still live in older houses. A Sun columnist wrote in 2025 that houses in poor condition rent for about $800 to $1,300 per person a month. Many sit down hills so steep they are hard to reach without a car ([Cornell Daily Sun, 2025](https://www.cornellsun.com/article/2025/09/maia-on-the-mic-the-collegetown-housing-hustle)).

The biggest houses go first. The 12 residents of 410 Elmwood Ave. signed in April 2017 for a move-in 16 months later. Landlord Nick Lambrou said rising juniors with a set group of friends win these houses. The Sun also reported that the number of houses for eight, 10 or 14 people had shrunk as new buildings went up ([Cornell Daily Sun, 2018](https://cornellsun.com/2018/11/07/desirable-collegetown-leases-leave-market-up-to-16-months-before-residents-move-in/)).

### Parking

Central Collegetown is built for walking. The City's Dryden Road Garage sells a monthly permit for $232, which the City recommends for Collegetown residents. The permit does not guarantee a space ([City of Ithaca](https://www.cityofithacany.gov/718/Dryden-Road-Garage)). On Oct. 28, 2026, the City announced a plan to replace the aging garage with a hotel, restaurant and new garage, keeping the same number of public spaces. The plan is preliminary, and the old garage would likely close during construction ([Tompkins Weekly](https://www.tompkinsweekly.com/news/ithaca-proposes-collegetown-garage-project-while-facing-parking-losses-f7b7425d)).

### The rent rule the new towers skip

New Collegetown towers fall outside Good Cause Eviction's rent limits until about 30 years after they open. Ithaca's Common Council opted into the New York law in July 2024 ([WAMC](https://www.wamc.org/2024-07-12/ithaca-is-the-latest-upstate-city-to-opt-into-good-cause-renter-protections)). The law limits rent hikes and lets tenants who pay and follow the lease renew in most cases. Under state rules, buildings built after 2009 are exempt for 30 years ([Cornell Daily Sun, 2024](https://cornellsun.com/2024/07/10/landlords-and-advocates-grapple-with-good-cause-eviction-law-at-public-hearing)). The Ivy, opened in 2026, stays outside the law until about 2056. If your renewal rent matters, ask the year the building opened; only one from 2009 or earlier can fall under the law.

```reader "If you're a sophomore planning junior year"
Your group's size matters more than the building. Dropping from a six-bedroom to a four-bedroom costs $446.25 a person a month at the Ithaca Renting medians above (EduExplained dataset). If you want a big Collegetown house, settle the group and the neighborhood early in fall of sophomore year.
```

## Ring two: the walkable hillsides

### Belle Sherman and East Hill

East of Collegetown, the hillside turns quiet. Cornell lists Belle Sherman at 1 to 2 miles and East Hill at 0.75 to 2. It gives both the same "15-minute level walk," with East Hill Plaza and Judd Falls Plaza nearby (Cornell neighborhoods page). That time only fits the near edge. At 3 mph, the far edge is a 40-minute walk.

The name East Hill means two places. Cornell's East Hill lies east of Collegetown. The Ithaca Times uses the name for the steep blocks between downtown and Collegetown, from Fall Creek Gorge to Six Mile Creek. It says that, along with Collegetown, it is where most Cornell students move when they leave campus. Many big houses on Stewart Avenue and Quarry Street were split into apartments. Belle Sherman, around its elementary school, is mostly single-family homes built after World War II, with newer complexes off Mitchell Street, Veteran's Place and Dryden Road ([Ithaca Times](https://www.ithaca.com/special_sections/newcomers_guide/ithaca-s-neighborhoods-have-subtly-distinctive-personalities/article_7186dd20-23c0-11ed-a9b6-1798d19eb7a1.html)).

East Hill crosses the city line. The Town of Ithaca's plan says its own East Hill holds a large part of Cornell, small commercial centers and most of the Town's people ([Town of Ithaca](https://townithacany.gov/wp-content/uploads/2022/01/town_of_ithaca_comp_plan_09_2014.pdf)). Cornell's graduate housing at Maplewood Apartments sits on East Hill ([Reed Group guide](https://reedgroup.github.io/GraduateStudentResources/LifeAtCornell.html)). Greystar manages it, with furnished studios to four-bedrooms and all utilities included (Cornell graduate housing). East Hill and Belle Sherman have no published typical rent, so compare listings one by one against the Cornell double.

### Cayuga Heights and Cornell Heights

Cornell lists these two together, but they differ on noise and on the city line. Both sit north of campus, across Fall Creek Gorge. Cornell Heights has apartment buildings, apartments in houses and many fraternities and sororities, so residents hear music and parties at times. Cayuga Heights is a quiet area where many faculty live, with few shops in walking distance. Cornell gives a distance of 1 to 4 miles and a walk of up to 30 minutes (Cornell neighborhoods page). At 3 mph, that half hour covers only 1.5 miles.

Cornell Heights was built as a planned suburb between 1898 and 1937, tied to Cornell's growth. Many early residents were Cornell professors and students. The City named it a local historic district in 1989 ([City of Ithaca](https://www.cityofithacany.gov/DocumentCenter/View/1589/Cornell-Heights-Historic-District-Significance-Statement)). That puts it inside the City, with City renter rules.

Cayuga Heights is a separate village with its own code office. Its posted rental rules cover short stays of 28 days or less, which need a yearly permit ([Village of Cayuga Heights](https://cayugaheights.gov/departments/code-enforcement/)). None of the City's renter rules apply there.

The party side of Cornell Heights changed in fall 2026. On Oct. 13, 2026, President Michael I. Kotlikoff canceled all parties and social events in Interfraternity Council houses for the rest of the 2026-27 year ([Cornell Daily Sun](https://www.cornellsun.com/article/2026/10/breaking-cornell-suspends-all-fraternity-parties-social-events-for-2026-27-school-year)). A week earlier, the Common Council had paused new event permits for Cornell student groups, set to expire Nov. 5 ([Ithaca Times](https://www.ithaca.com/news/ithaca/common-council-sets-moratorium-on-cornell-event-permits-following-gang-rape-allegations/article_6c84a345-d112-483a-98bc-316f3b64f0a1.html)). Cornell says a review will decide whether and how fraternity life continues. If you are weighing a lease near Thurston Avenue for 2027-28, watch what that review decides.

### Fall Creek

Fall Creek runs from the north side of downtown up to Ithaca High School and Stewart Park. Its tree-lined streets are known for families, Porchfest and Halloween crowds (Ithaca Times). Cornell puts it 0.5 to 1 mile from campus, with a 20- to 30-minute uphill walk and frequent buses. It says many graduate students like its lower rents (Cornell neighborhoods page). A 2023 guide by a Cornell graduate research group calls it about a quarter-hour by bus (Reed Group guide).

Fall Creek rents by the apartment, not the bed. Two one-bedrooms at 709 N. Tioga St. list at $1,600 and $1,700 a month. The landlord pays internet and water; the tenant pays electricity and trash tags ([Cornell off-campus listings](https://offcampus.housing.cornell.edu/city/ithaca-college-ny/listing/cosy-quiet-comfortable-fall-creek-gem.-238258)).

The newest building here is The Pinnacle, on the old Ithaca Gun factory site at 121-125 Lake St. It opened Aug. 1, 2026, with 77 apartments, and its manager says at least 80% of tenants are graduate students. Developer Todd Fox says rents run 15% to 20% below the newer Collegetown buildings (Tompkins Weekly). Two-bedrooms list at about $3,000 a month (Ithaca Voice).

## Ring three: the flats

### Downtown and the Commons

Downtown is where the bed price drops. It sits at the base of East Hill, 0.5 to 1.5 miles from campus, with the Commons at 1 mile and a 20- to 30-minute uphill walk. Most units are split houses, single homes and apartments over shops (Cornell neighborhoods page). Downtown has no big grocery store (Ithaca Times).

Ithaca Renting's downtown medians are $1,395 for a studio, $1,625 for a one-bedroom, $992.50 a bed in a two-bedroom and $795 a bed in a three-bedroom. Every downtown rent there includes heat, water and trash, and Whiton Hall, Colonial Studios and Commons Studios add electricity (Ithaca Renting, downtown). Against Collegetown, that is about 20% to 28% less for the same size unit (EduExplained dataset).

The math favors the bus rider. A two-bedroom bed costs $385 a month less downtown than in Collegetown ($1,377.50 minus $992.50). Over 12 months that is $4,620. Take out a $200 OmniRide pass and you keep $4,420 (EduExplained dataset).

Two cautions from 2026. The Seneca Street Garage closed for good on April 10, 2026, taking 430 spaces out of downtown (Tompkins Weekly). And in March 2026 the City ordered about 200 residents out of Asteri, a 181-unit affordable building on the Commons that opened in 2024, over fire and safety concerns ([Cornell Daily Sun](https://www.cornellsun.com/article/2026/03/asteri-s-aftermath-displacement-dangerous-conditions-and-a-predatory-cash-for-keys-scheme)). Asteri is not student housing, but it shows that a new building is not proof of a safe one.

### Northside

Northside is the City neighborhood south and west of Cascadilla Creek, down to Washington Park and Route 13 (Ithaca Times). Its residents formed Northside United in 2014. They told the Ithaca Voice in 2015 that it is one of the city's least affluent neighborhoods, with public housing and a large refugee population. One organizer said landlords on Lake Avenue tell new renters their address is in Fall Creek ([Ithaca Voice](https://ithacavoice.org/2015/07/often-overlooked-ithacas-northside-residents-try-building-clout/)).

No rent survey covers Northside. If a listing says "Fall Creek" and the address is near Lake Avenue or Third Street, check a map. The renter rules do not change, since both are inside the City.

### South Hill

South Hill belongs to Ithaca College the way Collegetown belongs to Cornell. Cornell puts it 1.5 to 3 miles from campus. Some graduate students live there, walk 15 minutes downhill to downtown, then take a bus up (Cornell neighborhoods page). The streets around the college, such as Hudson, Prospect and Pleasant, are mostly student rentals, and the Ithaca Times says noisy house parties are common there.

South Hill also crosses the city line. The Town of Ithaca's plan names South Hill as home to Ithaca College (Town of Ithaca). An address near the college can sit outside the City's rules, so check it before you sign.

## Ring four: bus and car country

### Northeast and Lansing

North of Cayuga Heights, the map turns suburban. Cornell lists Northeast at 4 miles and Lansing at 5 to 10 miles. It says both have big and small apartment complexes, malls, regular buses and lower rents, and it recommends a bus or a car (Cornell neighborhoods page). The Reed Group guide puts Lansing about 20 minutes from campus by bus.

Prices here are by the apartment. Lansing West, at 700 Warren Road, lists base rents from $1,302 for a one-bedroom, $1,625 for a two-bedroom and $2,072 for a three-bedroom. Its November 2026 offer gave free rent until January 2027 to renters who applied by Nov. 14 and moved in by Dec. 1 ([RentCafe](https://www.rentcafe.com/apartments/ny/ithaca/lansing-west-apartments/default.aspx)). Base rent leaves out fees, so ask for the full monthly cost.

TCAT Route 30 runs between the Commons and the Ithaca Mall, straight through campus ([Cornell Daily Sun, 2024](https://cornellsun.com/2024/11/03/non-first-year-cornell-students-no-longer-able-to-access-tcat-for-free/)). Northeast Ithaca is a Town of Ithaca neighborhood (Town of Ithaca), and the Town of Lansing has its own code office at (607) 533-7054 ([Cornell Law Tenants Advocacy Program](https://sites.lawschool.cornell.edu/tenants-advocacy/building-issues-maintenance)).

### West Hill

West Hill is across the inlet, four miles out along the lake. Cornell calls it rural and cheaper, but inconvenient for Cornell students. It warns that units sold as cottages are probably not insulated and that winter heating bills can be huge. It suggests calling NYSEG at (800) 572-1111 for a unit's average yearly heating bill (Cornell neighborhoods page). The Ithaca Times describes older homes near the city, newer suburban streets farther up, and garden apartments near Cayuga Medical Center. The Town of Ithaca counts West Hill's EcoVillage and medical center as Town land.

The bus is the weak point. TCAT suspended Route 14S, which linked West Hill to the Wegmans area, in October 2023. Its board voted on April 25, 2024, to keep it off indefinitely. TCAT told riders to take Route 14 to downtown instead ([Cornell Daily Sun, 2024](https://cornellsun.com/2024/04/25/facing-operator-and-bus-shortages-tcat-board-prolongs-suspension-of-routes-14s-and-83/)). From downtown, Route 10 climbs to campus, so a West Hill commute means a transfer.

### Varna

Varna is a small community east of campus along Dryden Road, in the Town of Dryden. Its student building is Ivy Ridge, at 802 Dryden Road, a 108-bedroom townhouse complex finished in 2019 next to the east side of Cornell and aimed mostly at graduate and professional students ([Ithacating](https://ithacating.com/2019/09/08/802-dryden-road-construction-update-9-2019/)). The Town of Dryden's code office is at (607) 844-8888 (Cornell Law Tenants Advocacy Program).

```reader "If you're a graduate or professional student"
Cornell budgets $19,962 for a year of housing for Ithaca graduate students in 2026-27 ([Cornell Financial Aid](https://finaid.cornell.edu/graduate-students)). That is $1,663.50 a month, which covers a Fall Creek or downtown one-bedroom but not a Collegetown one. The Reed Group guide calls March and April the best time to find an August lease, with a few places left in June and July. PhD students and graduate assistants ride TCAT free, which makes Lansing, Northeast and Varna cheaper than they look.
```

## The city line test

The single most useful thing to know about an address is which government it sits in. The Town of Ithaca calls itself a donut, with the City of Ithaca as the hole ([Town of Ithaca](https://townithacany.gov/welcome-new-resident-guide/)). An "Ithaca, NY 14850" mailing address covers many places. The Town's own pages put the count at nine on one page and 12 or more on another. Its [address checker](https://townithacany.gov/address-verification/) tells you which one you are in.

Inside the City, three protections follow you. A landlord must give current tenants 120 days' written notice before renewing a lease or showing the unit to others. The rule covers leases of nine months or longer. A tenant can waive it only with bold, initialed language on the lease's first page ([WSKG](https://wskg.org/ithaca-landlords-must-now-give-renters-120-days-before-renewing-leases/)). Cornell and the City's Building Division run an online safety tool that shows sprinklers, alarms and construction type for City rentals. It leaves out properties beyond the city limits ([Cornell safety page](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/campus-living/housing-search-process/safe-living-environments)). And Good Cause Eviction applies, with the new-building exemption above.

Outside the City, other offices take over. The Town of Ithaca issues its own operating permits for buildings with three or more units, and keeps a registry for rented single-family homes ([Town of Ithaca](https://townithacany.gov/operating-permits/)). The Town does not pick up trash, so tenants or landlords hire a hauler (Town of Ithaca). Cayuga Heights, Lansing and Dryden each run their own code offices. Our guide to [City of Ithaca vs Town, Lansing and Cayuga Heights](/cornell/housing/city-of-ithaca-vs-town-lansing-cayuga-heights-renters/) goes rule by rule.

```figure

  - label: If it is in the City, look the building up in Cornell's safety tool
  - label: If it is in the City, read page one of the lease for a 120-day waiver
```

## Getting to campus

After your first year, a daytime bus ride costs money, so the commute from each area depends on the pass. New degree students ride TCAT free for their first year. After that, registered students ride free after 6 p.m. on weekdays and all day on weekends. A daytime weekday ride costs $1.50, and an OmniRide pass costs $200 a year. PhD students and graduate assistants get the pass free ([Cornell Transportation](https://fcs.cornell.edu/departments/transportation-delivery-services/sustainable-transportation-options/bus-services-privileges-passes)). No buses run from 1:30 to 4:00 a.m. ([Cornell Sustainability](https://sustainable.cornell.edu/focus-areas/transportation/bus-commuting)).

Upper-year students rode free at all hours for part of 2024 because of an ID card glitch. That ended on Oct. 16, 2024, when Cornell updated its list of pass holders (Cornell Daily Sun, Nov. 3, 2024). Older advice that "students ride free" is out of date for daytime rides.

Two daytime rides a weekday cost $15 a week. The pass pays for itself after 134 rides (the pass price divided by $1.50), just over 13 weeks of weekday round trips. If you live in Fall Creek, downtown, Northside, Lansing or West Hill and ride to daytime classes most weekdays, the pass is worth buying. A Collegetown renter rarely needs it. For more, see [getting around Ithaca as an off-campus renter](/cornell/getting-around/getting-around-ithaca-off-campus-renters/) and [when TCAT is free for Cornell students](/cornell/getting-around/tcat-free-for-cornell-students-hours-routes/).

## The trade-offs

Collegetown buys time. You walk to class, you skip the bus, and you are near friends. You also sign early and lock in before you know your plans. One 2018 renter said his group found out months before moving in that the walk to class was longer than they had thought, and by then they could not change course (Cornell Daily Sun, 2018). The Sun's 2025 columnist found Collegetown rentals overpriced for what they are, and wrote that one agency owns many of the nicer buildings on College Avenue (Cornell Daily Sun, 2025).

The rush itself is partly a choice. Denise Thompson, Cornell's off-campus living manager, said in 2019 that students can find housing later in the year. She called the rush a myth that students and landlords both keep alive. She also said many students live in places they cannot afford because friends talked them into it ([Cornell Daily Sun, 2019](https://cornellsun.com/2019/02/06/students-and-landlords-both-perpetuate-myths-about-housing-university-panelist-says/)). Both are true: the biggest Collegetown houses go a year ahead, while the graduate market in Fall Creek, downtown and beyond still has places in spring (Reed Group guide).

The new towers buy finishes and amenities: in-unit laundry, gyms, saunas, rooftop decks. One student offered a 20% discount at The Ivy turned it down. She worried the unit was smaller than her current one and that she could not see inside before signing (Cornell Daily Sun, 2025).

The valley buys a lower rent. It costs you the climb, a bus pass and, downtown, a trip out for big groceries. If you head home only once a day, the climb matters less. If you like to stop home between classes, it matters more.

The far areas buy lower rents per apartment and cost you a bus or a car.

## What comes next

More Collegetown beds arrive by 2028: The Bear for 2027-28, 301 College Ave. at full capacity in fall 2027 and Prestige Ithaca by July 2028. Developer Todd Fox told Tompkins Weekly that more supply is giving renters more choices and pushing rents down. Whether that narrows the gap between Collegetown and downtown is the open question for 2027-28 (Tompkins Weekly).

Three other decisions will shape 2027-28. Cornell's review of fraternity life will decide how loud Cornell Heights gets. The City's garage plan will decide where Collegetown drivers park during construction. And the Collegetown height rules will decide how many more towers can rise. Watch the Common Council's agendas for all of them (Tompkins Weekly; FingerLakes1).
