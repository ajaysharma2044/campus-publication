Downtown is the cheaper side of town at the bottom of the hill, 0.5 to 1.5 miles from campus, with the Ithaca Commons about a mile away. Cornell calls the walk 20 to 30 minutes uphill ([Cornell Student & Campus Life](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/living-off-campus/housing-search-process/ithaca-neighborhoods)). A bed there costs about 20 to 28 percent less than the same size in Collegetown.

## How much cheaper is downtown than Collegetown?

At Ithaca Renting, a studio costs a median $1,395 a month downtown against $1,765 in Collegetown, a one-bedroom $1,625 against $2,025, and a bed in a two-bedroom $992.50 against $1,377.50 ([Collegetown vs downtown dataset](/datasets/collegetown-vs-downtown-rent-per-bed-2026/)). The gaps are $370, $400 and $385 a month. The downtown prices are mostly for 2026-27 and the Collegetown prices for 2027-28, so the lease years do not match.

Larger shares cost the least. Three-bedroom per-bed rows run $695 to $795 at the Old Cigar Factory, $745 to $845 at 156 The Commons and $815 to $895 at Cityview for 2026-27 or available now ([Ithaca Renting](https://ithacarenting.com/downtown/)). RentCafe, which counts only buildings of 50 or more units, puts the downtown average at $2,747 a month per unit ([RentCafe](https://www.rentcafe.com/average-rent-market-trends/us/ny/ithaca/downtown-ithaca/)).

## What buildings are downtown?

Ithaca Renting's downtown buildings include Cityview at 118 Prospect St., Commons West at 107 E. State St., the Old Cigar Factory at 108-114 E. State St., Whiton Hall at 222 S. Aurora St., the Colonial Building, Commons Studios, 154 and 156 The Commons, and the Ivy Building at 111 S. Cayuga St. Leases run 12 months. Heat, water and trash are included at all of them, and electricity only at Whiton Hall, the Colonial Building and Commons Studios. Whiton Hall includes Wi-Fi as well, at $965 to $1,065 per bed for a premium room in 2026-27 (Ithaca Renting).

Commons West allows subletting, with a two-month minimum and rental office approval (Ithaca Renting). Cityview is furnished, bans pets, and wants the first month and deposit before move-in. Its electricity terms conflict between pages, so ask in writing.

Other landlords have downtown buildings too, among them [Cayuga Place](https://livehhcayuga.com/) at 131 E. Green St., [Gateway Commons](https://www.travishyde.com/downtown-ithacaf4ba8d70/gateway%20commons) and [Theory Ithaca](https://offcampus.housing.cornell.edu/city/ithaca-college-ny/listing/theory-ithaca-239997) at 445 E. State St. Ask each landlord what its rent covers.

## How do you get from downtown to campus?

Walk uphill, or ride. TCAT Route 10 links downtown with campus ([Cornell Sustainable Campus](https://sustainable.cornell.edu/focus-areas/transportation/bus-commuting)), and Route 30 runs between the Commons and the Ithaca Mall through campus ([The Cornell Daily Sun](https://cornellsun.com/2024/11/03/non-first-year-cornell-students-no-longer-able-to-access-tcat-for-free/)). Commons West says buses from its Green Street stop run every 15 to 20 minutes to Cornell and Ithaca College ([Ithaca Renting](https://ithacarenting.com/property-details/?pid=7)).

## Where do intercity buses and cars go?

OurBus's Ithaca stop moved from East Seneca Street to 235 S. Cayuga St. beside the South Cayuga garage in November 2026, as TCAT shifted downtown stops (City of Ithaca release, relayed by [Finger Lakes 1](https://www.fingerlakes1.com/2026/11/06/ourbus-moves-ithaca-stop-to-south-cayuga-street-monday-as-tcat-shifts-stops/)). Greyhound stops at 131 E. Green St. ([Greyhound](https://www.greyhound.com/bus-routes/ithaca-ny-new-york-ny)). Our [intercity bus stops guide](/cornell/getting-around/ithaca-intercity-bus-stops-cornell-downtown/) has the rest.

Downtown garages on Green, Dryden and Cayuga streets charge $2.25 an hour, and each has its own free hours ([City of Ithaca](https://www.cityofithacany.gov/705/Parking-Information)). Our [parking guide](/cornell/getting-around/parking-collegetown-downtown-ithaca-garages-permits/) covers monthly permits.

## What happened at Asteri?

Asteri, a downtown building of 181 apartments owned by Vecino Group, opened in August 2024. The City ordered about 200 residents out on March 4, 2026, over a blocked stairwell among more than 40 violations, and lifted the order March 20. Residents were offered $2,000 to leave within five days, an offer a city official and the Ithaca Tenants Union called predatory, and the county set aside $50,000 for legal aid ([The Cornell Daily Sun](https://www.cornellsun.com/article/2026/03/asteri-s-aftermath-displacement-dangerous-conditions-and-a-predatory-cash-for-keys-scheme)). Our [vacate order guide](/cornell/housing/ithaca-eviction-vacate-order-what-to-do/) explains your rights if a building is ordered vacated.
