A House Professor-Dean is a Cornell professor who leads one of the five West Campus Houses and lives in the House with their family. Cornell calls the Houses a community with informal interaction with faculty members ([Cornell Student & Campus Life](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/campus-housing/upper-level-undergraduates/west-campus-house-system)).

## What does a House Professor-Dean do?

Cornell describes the role this way: each House is led by a distinguished Cornell professor, who serves as House Professor-Dean and lives in the House with their family. An Assistant Dean and fellows support the House ([Cornell Student & Campus Life](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/campus-housing/upper-level-undergraduates/west-campus-house-system)).

The team around the Professor-Dean has four parts:

- An Assistant Dean.
- Graduate Resident Fellows, who live in the House and offer guidance and mentorship.
- Undergraduate Resident Fellows, who do the same for their peers.
- House Fellows, primarily faculty members, who meet residents during meals and events.

Students help govern the House too. Active Citizens, House Councils and other student leaders help shape programs and activities (Cornell Student & Campus Life).

## Why does Cornell have Professor-Deans?

The Kramnick Report of 1984 came first. In 1997, President Hunter Rawlings put housing reform in a seven-point plan, and point five became the West Campus project. It ran from 1998 to 2009, and Cornell reported that the $225 million building phase finished two years early and within budget. The Houses hold 1,800 upperclassmen ([Cornell Chronicle](https://news.cornell.edu/stories/2009/08/west-campus-house-system-officially-complete)).

## What does a Professor-Dean mean for your day-to-day life?

Mostly, a House feels smaller than a dorm. Each has its own House Office for mail and keys, a common room, lounges, a library, study rooms and laundry. Residents get priority for one-credit "Learning Where You Live" courses held in the Houses (Cornell Student & Campus Life). On Wednesdays from 6 to 7 p.m., each House's dining room serves only its residents, fellows and guests ([Cornell Dining](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/dining/eateries-menus/dining-rooms/west-campus-house-dinners)).

Dining is built into the deal. The House Meal Plan is $7,604 a year, covers meals in ten residential dining rooms and includes $400 in Big Red Bucks per semester and eight guest meals per semester. It is required and cannot be changed or cancelled while you live on West ([Cornell Dining](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/dining/meal-plans-rates/undergraduate-meal-plans)). The [meal plan guide](/cornell/money/cornell-meal-plan-worth-it/) covers whether it pays off.

## Who lives in a House, and how do you get in?

Cornell says Houses have historically been about 65% sophomores, 25% juniors and 10% seniors, with about 15% transfer students. A sophomore can stay on West as a junior and senior through continued occupancy (Cornell Student & Campus Life). In past years about 20% of rising sophomores were able to select a West Campus space, and most live on North or South Campus. A student who does not select is assigned what is left.
