At least 26 Ithaca buildings list a bed at that price or less. The cheapest is a room in a downtown 6-bedroom house ([PPM Homes](https://www.ppmhomes.com/listings/detail/3f1544f9-76c1-4375-91f6-b2400a52dbbf)). Most are 5- to 10-bedroom shares in Collegetown, Fall Creek, Cornell Heights and downtown, so the more housemates you sign with, the less each bed costs.

## Which Collegetown buildings rent beds under $800?

Collegetown, the student neighborhood just south of campus, has the cheapest beds closest to class, but only in big units. Ithaca Renting, the one Collegetown landlord that prints a per-bed price for every unit, lists a bed in a standard 6-bedroom at Aces Apartments, 418 to 428 Eddy St., at $695 to $760 a month for 2027-28 ([Ithaca Renting](https://ithacarenting.com/collegetown/)). A bed in the 10-bedroom at 202 Dryden Rd. lists at $725 to $825. The ranges are wide because price varies within a building, and Ithaca Renting says rents can change until a lease is signed.

Smaller landlords on the Ithaca College marketplace list cheaper big-house beds a few blocks away. 404-406 Oak Ave. lists a 6-bedroom, 4-bath home at $714 a bed with electricity included ([Ithaca College marketplace](https://offcampushousing.ithaca.edu/listing)). PJ Apartments rents beds in 6- to 8-bedroom units at 109 and 111 DeWitt Pl., priced in the table below.

## Which Fall Creek and Cornell Heights houses come in under $800?

Fall Creek, the older house neighborhood below North Campus, and Cornell Heights, the hillside north of Fall Creek gorge, have the widest spread of cheap rooms, many in 3- to 5-bedroom apartments priced as a whole unit.

The best value on the list is a room at 130.5 University Ave. Goldenrod Real Estate lists three rooms there at $750 a person, with all utilities and in-unit laundry included. Kelvin Place Apartments, 219 Kelvin Pl., rents furnished single rooms at $795 to $825 with electricity, heat, internet and laundry included ([Cornell marketplace listing](https://offcampus.housing.cornell.edu/city/ithaca-college-ny/listing/kelvin-place-apartments-237474)).

Travis Hyde's Ravenwood, at 516 University Ave., lists a private bedroom in a 5-bedroom, 2-bath apartment at $700 a month, with water and sewer included on that room-by-room listing ([Travis Hyde](https://www.travishyde.com/west-campus/ravenwood)). The same building's whole 3-bedrooms run $2,225 a month, which is $741.67 a bed.

Auden Ithaca, 210 Lake St., advertises individual leasing, with a listed price range of $689 to $1,389 ([Cornell marketplace listing](https://offcampus.housing.cornell.edu/city/ithaca-college-ny/listing/auden-ithaca-7919)). The listing shows "Call For Price" for most floor plans and does not say which unit or basis the $689 applies to, so confirm it before you count on it. Its rent includes water, trash, cable and internet, and the building runs a private shuttle to campus.

## What does an $800 bed cost over a year?

A bed at this page's top price, on a 12-month lease, costs $9,600 a year, $4,144 less than a 2026-27 Cornell residence hall double at $13,744 ([Cornell Housing rates](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/housing-details/rates-amenities-licenses/housing-rates)). Spread over 12 months, the double is $1,145.33 a month, so every bed on this page undercuts it on rent alone. The double covers gas, electricity, garbage and internet, so add your share of the bills a cheap house leaves to you.

Electricity is the bill that most often sits outside the rent. Ithaca households that report to EnergySage pay an average of about $282 a month ([EnergySage](https://www.energysage.com/local-data/electricity-cost/ny/tompkins-county/ithaca/)). That is a household average from self-reported users, not a student-house bill. Split six ways, $282 is $47.00 a person, which lifts a $750 bed to $797.00. Heat is a bigger risk in an old house in January, so ask the landlord for last winter's bills before you sign at 174 Kendall Ave., 119-121 Prospect St. or 310-312 S. Aurora St.

Ithaca Renting includes heat and hot water at every property, but tenants pay electricity except in the shared suites on floors 2 and 3 of 210 Dryden Rd. and in units rented by the room ([Ithaca Renting](https://ithacarenting.com/collegetown/)). Its leases run one year, starting in June, July or August on a preset date per apartment.

## Why are the cheap beds all in big shares?

The rent per bed falls as the unit gets bigger, because a landlord spreads one kitchen, one living room and one lot over more tenants. At Ithaca Renting, the 2027-28 median bed costs $1,246.25 in a 4-bedroom, $800 in a 6-bedroom and $775 in a 10-bedroom, so a 6-bedroom bed costs $446.25 a month less than a 4-bedroom one ([EduExplained Collegetown dataset](/datasets/collegetown-rent-per-bed-2027-28/)). Cornell's own advice says rooms in a student or private house tend to be the cheapest option ([Cornell checklists](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/living-off-campus/housing-search-process/checklists-and-considerations)).

The catch is the lease. Cornell says most local leases bind housemates "jointly and severally," which means each tenant owes the whole house's rent and damages if someone else stops paying ([Cornell Off-Campus Living](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/living-off-campus/tenant-best-practices/study-abroad/housemate-compatibility-questionnaire)). In PPM's 7-bedroom on S. Aurora St., one housemate who leaves in March leaves that room's rent for the other six to cover, or $116.67 each. The rules on who pays, and how to protect yourself, are in [our guide to roommates and joint liability](/cornell/housing/cornell-off-campus-roommates-joint-liability/).

Check the building before you check the price. The Cornell and City of Ithaca [Safety Program for Off-Campus Living](https://safety.offcampusliving.cornell.edu/) lets you look up whether a rental holds a current certificate, the city's safety inspection permit. Several of the cheapest houses on this page name no owner or manager on their listings, so read [our guide to Ithaca landlords and tenant reviews](/housing/how-to-check-an-ithaca-landlords-record/) before you send a deposit.

## FAQ

**What does Collegetown Terrace's starting rate cover?**
Collegetown Terrace, 115 S. Quarry St., advertises "rates starting at $605" without saying for which unit or period ([Collegetown Terrace](https://www.collegetownterrace.com/)). Its marketplace listing prints a range from that figure to $2,265 with no per-bed basis. Ask which floor plan carries that price and whether it is per person before you count it.

**Is a listed price per bed or per apartment?**
Ithaca Renting and most room listings print a price per bed. Many house and apartment listings print the rent for the whole unit, such as $2,300 for a 3-bedroom. Divide by the number of bedrooms, which assumes one person per room and an even split, and confirm the basis with the landlord before you tell your housemates what each person owes.

**Can a Cornell co-op beat these beds?**
Yes. A double at Redbud, one of Cornell's eight university-owned co-ops, costs $6,974 for the 2026-27 academic year, which is $2,626 less than the $9,600 year above. Co-ops pick members house by house, starting in early November.

## What to do next

Pick your group size first. Then shortlist three buildings from the tables above, look up each one's certificate, and ask each landlord two questions in writing: is the price per bed, and which utilities are included.
