Before you sign, check an Ithaca landlord's record three ways: confirm the building's Certificate of Compliance with the City Building Division, look the address up on Cornell's safety tool, and call two past tenants with Cornell's reference questions. The certificate is what the City issues after an inspection finds no code violations ([City of Ithaca Code](https://ecode360.com/8390192)).

## How do you check a Certificate of Compliance?

Every rental in the City of Ithaca needs a valid certificate, from a one-family house to a fraternity. A certificate lasts up to five years for a one- or two-family house. It lasts up to three years for a building with three or more units or a unit with five or more unrelated residents, and one year for a dormitory, fraternity or sorority ([City of Ithaca Code](https://ecode360.com/8390192)).

The lease is the first place to look. The same code section requires every lease to state whether the rental has a valid certificate, when it expires and a link to the City's certificate database, where the certificate can be verified. Ask for the lease before you sign and check all three.

Then call the Building Division at 607-274-6508 ([City of Ithaca](https://www.cityofithacany.gov/146/Certificate-of-Compliance)) or email ibd@cityofithaca.org. Ask three questions:

1. Does this address have a valid certificate, and when does it expire?
2. Are there open complaints or violations?
3. Is the occupancy on the lease legal for this unit?

The office is at 108 E. Green St., City Hall, fourth floor, open 8:30 a.m. to 4 p.m. on weekdays ([Building Division](https://www.cityofithacany.gov/130/Building-Division)). For copies of a building's inspection and complaint records, file a Freedom of Information Law request with the City, online or on paper ([City of Ithaca](https://www.cityofithacany.gov/182/Freedom-of-Information-Law-Requests)). If the certificate has lapsed, ask the landlord in writing when the inspection is booked and wait for the answer before you sign.

The stakes are real. A rental without a valid certificate violates the City housing code and can be ordered vacated until it complies, and verified over-occupancy revokes the certificate at once ([City of Ithaca Code](https://ecode360.com/8390192)). In 2018, tenants at 117 Thurston Ave. learned after signing that the certificate had expired in November 2015. It took 53 days from the first complaint to a reported fix of the heater ([The Cornell Daily Sun](https://cornellsun.com/2018/11/25/cornell-student-abandons-house-sleeps-in-library-after-months-long-dispute-with-landlord/)). If an order to vacate reaches your building, see [what to do after an eviction or vacate order](/cornell/housing/ithaca-eviction-vacate-order-what-to-do/).

## What does Cornell's safety tool show?

Cornell Off-Campus Living and the Building Division run an online tool for City rentals. It shows sprinklers, extinguishers, smoke and carbon monoxide alarms, construction type, the applicable code and property use, and it updates daily ([Cornell Student and Campus Life](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/campus-living/housing-search-process/safe-living-environments)). It launched in 2017 with one-to-five-star fire and building safety ratings ([Cornell Chronicle](https://news.cornell.edu/node/289146)). The current page shows no scoring scale. It does not measure landlord quality, looks or neighborhood safety, and it covers City rentals only.

| Check | What it tells you | What it misses |
|---|---|---|
| Certificate call | Valid certificate, expiry, open complaints | Repair speed, deposit habits |
| Safety tool | Sprinklers, alarms, construction type | Landlord behavior |
| Past tenants | Repairs, deposit return, notice, entry | Whether it was one bad year |

Source: City of Ithaca, Cornell Student and Campus Life.

## What should you ask the landlord and past tenants?

Cornell's lease page gives the questions, and landlords expect them. Ask how long the landlord has been in business, how many properties they own, how long the last tenants stayed and where they went, who does repairs, and how to reach them in an emergency. Ask references whether the landlord answered repair requests and handled late rent fairly, whether they got their deposit back, whether notice came before entry, and whether they would rent again ([Cornell Student and Campus Life](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/campus-housing/housing-search-process/signing-lease)).

These questions target the complaints that recur. The Ithaca Tenants Union formed in 2020 citing poor maintenance, one-sided leases, deposits above the legal cap and entry without 24 hours' notice ([The Cornell Daily Sun](https://cornellsun.com/2020/03/06/as-students-struggle-to-find-housing-three-ilr-juniors-form-new-ithaca-tenants-union/)). The deposit plus any advance rent cannot exceed one month's rent ([GOL 7-108](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/GOB/7-108)), and a landlord cannot charge an application fee ([RPL 238-a](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/RPP/238-A)). Test any lease against both caps with the checker.

## What should you look for on a visit?

Look past fresh paint. Check for two exits and working smoke detectors, ask what heats the unit, and knock on a current tenant's door. Cornell also suggests checking public records for code compliance, the Better Business Bureau and Chamber complaints. If the problem is already underway, see [what to do when a landlord will not fix heat, mold or bed bugs](/cornell/housing/ithaca-landlord-wont-fix-heat-mold-bed-bugs/).

## Do any buildings have reviews?

Two buildings near Cornell have a run of graduate reviews on Veryapt. Cliffside Terrace at 405 Lake St. scores 7.4 out of 10 on five reviews, four of them shown, all tagged Cornell Law students from January 2017 to October 2022. They praise fast repairs, space and quiet, and complain about distance, limited bus service, assigned spots taken, thin floors and minor pests ([Veryapt](https://www.veryapt.com/ApartmentReview-a16145-cliffside-terrace-apartments-ithaca)). Ivy Ridge in Varna scores 6.4 on seven reviews from Johnson MBA students ([Veryapt](https://www.veryapt.com/ApartmentReview-a23680-ivy-ridge-ithaca)). The samples are small, so read them as questions to ask.

```reader "If you're a sophomore signing for junior year"
The Collegetown rush runs in your sophomore fall, and speed is no reason to skip the certificate call. Both calls fit in an afternoon. Skipping them costs more than a missed unit.
```

```reader "If you're a parent paying or co-signing"
You cannot see Cornell's listing marketplace without a NetID, but you can make the two City checks yourself. Then check the money against the caps above. Cornell aid does not cover deposits or first and last month's rent.
```

```reader "If you're a graduate or professional student"
You shop later, so you have time to vet. Ask what share of tenants are undergraduates. Ivy Ridge sits in Varna, outside the City, so it has no certificate or safety-tool entry. Cornell's Off-Campus Living does not represent students in landlord disputes ([Cornell Student and Campus Life](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/campus-housing/housing-search-process/signing-lease)). Rules outside the City are in the [City versus town guide](/cornell/housing/city-of-ithaca-vs-town-lansing-cayuga-heights-renters/).
```

```reader "If you're an international student signing from abroad"
You probably sign sight unseen, so the public checks replace the visit. You do not need a Social Security number to rent an apartment ([Cornell Global Learning](https://international.globallearning.cornell.edu/life-cornell/faq-international-students)). A landlord dispute does not affect your visa or standing, according to Graduate School staff ([Cornell Graduate School](https://www.gradschool.cornell.edu/announcements/grad-tips-renting/)).
```

## Who do the big Collegetown landlords turn out to be?

The names students ask about, such as Ithaca Renting, Modern Living Rentals, Lambrou Real Estate and Urban Ithaca, publish their own sites and no review scores. Ask each for its record in writing. A landlord who is new to owning rentals is a reason to ask more, not to walk away. To become a landlord yourself, read the [City rental rules](/cornell/housing/becoming-ithaca-landlord-student-rental-rules/). If a lease ends in a vacate order, see [what to do after an eviction or vacate order](/cornell/housing/ithaca-eviction-vacate-order-what-to-do/).

## What to do next

1. Call 607-274-6508 and ask the three certificate questions.
2. Run the address through the safety tool.
3. Read the lease with the [lease reading guide](/cornell/housing/how-to-read-ithaca-lease-before-signing/) before you sign.
