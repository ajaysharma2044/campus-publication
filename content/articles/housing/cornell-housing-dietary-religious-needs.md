If you keep kosher or halal, living on campus with a meal plan works, because Cornell Dining runs both. 104West! is certified kosher, and Morrison Dining has a dedicated halal and kosher station for lunch and dinner every day. If your diet is stricter, an apartment with your own kitchen gives you full control.

## Which dining options cover kosher and halal needs?

Cornell Dining lists these official options for 2026-27 ([Cornell Dining](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/dining/eateries-menus/nutrition-special-diets/food-allergies-special-diets)):

| Need | Where | Details |
|---|---|---|
| Kosher | 104West! | Star K certified for meat and pareve; STAR-D for dairy |
| Kosher and halal | Morrison Dining | Dedicated station, lunch and dinner daily |
| Halal on request | Keeton House Dining Room | Dinner seven days; lunch and brunch Saturday and Sunday |
| Vegan | Okenshields and Morrison Dining | Dedicated vegan stations |
| Allergy-friendly | Risley Dining | Sources food without gluten, peanuts and tree nuts; coconut allowed |

Source: Cornell Dining's [special diets page](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/dining/eateries-menus/nutrition-special-diets/food-allergies-special-diets) and [vegan and vegetarian page](https://scl.cornell.edu/sub/sub/residential-life/dining/eateries-menus/nutrition-special-diets/vegan-and-vegetarian-resources-campus).

Pareve means food with no meat or dairy. Shabbat, holiday and special meals at 104West! can carry extra charges, with reservations at kosher.scl.cornell.edu. The halal page adds that Willard Straight's Take Us Home items are halal certified and can be pre-ordered, dining rooms label alcohol and pork, and Ramadan brings extended hours, take-out containers and pre-fast boxes ([Cornell Dining halal page](https://scl.cornell.edu/sub/sub/residential-life/dining/eateries-menus/nutrition-special-diets/halal-resources)).

For allergies, [residential dining rooms](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/dining/eateries-menus/nutrition-special-diets/food-allergies-special-diets) label milk, egg, fish, tree nuts, peanuts, shellfish, soy, sesame, gluten and coconut. Cornell's culinary team and managers are trained yearly with AllerTrain, and the dining program has FARECheck Silver status, which means at least 90% of the workforce has food allergy training. Plant Forward options appear at every location, and cafe menu boards mark vegetarian items "VG" and vegan items "VN".

## How do meal plans fit in?

Residence hall residents must have a meal plan. First-years must take Unlimited, which costs $3,802 per semester or $7,604 a year ([Cornell Dining](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/dining/meal-plans-rates/undergraduate-meal-plans)). West Campus residents must take the House Meal Plan at the same price. The six Collegetown-area buildings can take the Collegetown Meal Plan at $7,056 a year.

One building matters here. 104 Mary Ann Wood Drive, at the base of Libe Slope, lists dining at Keeton, 104West! and Okenshields ([Cornell Student & Campus Life](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/campus-housing/upper-level-undergraduates/residence-halls/104-mary-ann-wood-drive)). That puts the kosher hall and a halal-on-request dining room in your dining options.

The [meal plan guide](/cornell/money/cornell-meal-plan-worth-it/) weighs plans against cost. For off-campus students, the [meal plan versus cooking guide](/cornell/money/cornell-off-campus-meal-plan-or-cook-yourself-cost/) compares the totals.

## When should I live off campus and cook?

Cook when no campus option fits your diet. First- and second-year students need an exemption to leave the residential requirement, so check the [exemptions guide](/cornell/housing/cornell-residential-requirement-exemptions-live-off-campus/) first. A diet by itself is not on Cornell's exemption list ([Cornell residential policy](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/campus-housing/residential-policy)). Ask Housing Operations at 607-255-5368 how a documented religious or medical need is handled.

## What about co-ops and Greek houses?

Cornell's eight co-ops are student-run houses that count as second-year housing. Four have meal plans ([Cornell co-op page](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/campus-housing/upper-level-undergraduates/cooperative-housing)). Co-ops cook for themselves, so a house can shape its menu around its members. Greek houses are self-governed, and room and board vary by house ([Cornell Student & Campus Life](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/campus-living/qualified-off-campus-housing)). Ask a house before you join what its kitchen can do. The [co-ops and Greek houses guide](/cornell/housing/cornell-co-ops-greek-houses-cost-how-to-join/) covers cost and how to join.

## Which option should I choose?

- **Kosher:** on campus, near 104West!, with a plan.
- **Halal:** on campus near Morrison Dining or Keeton, or off campus with your own kitchen.
- **Vegan or allergy needs:** compare Okenshields, Morrison Dining and Risley, then cook if none fits.
- **Stricter rules than the campus kitchens follow:** off campus, once exempt.

## What to do next

1. Visit the dining room you would use and ask what is certified.
2. Ask Housing Operations how a diet or religious need affects assignment.
3. Price your plan against groceries with the meal plan guide.
