Mold and pests are the landlord's to fix. Every New York lease carries a [warranty of habitability](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/RPP/235-B), a promise that the home is fit to live in. Put the problem in writing, file with the code office for your address, and call the free Cornell Law tenant hotline at 607-301-1560 before you hold back rent.

## What does the landlord have to fix?

The warranty covers the home and shared areas. They must be fit to live in and not dangerous to life, health or safety, and a lease clause waiving the warranty is void. Conditions you caused do not count.

The [Cornell Law guide](https://sites.lawschool.cornell.edu/tenants-advocacy/building-issues-maintenance) lists what it covers: no heat, hot or cold water, electricity or plumbing; pests; mold; structural defects; lead paint; unsanitary conditions; and broken appliances the landlord supplied. A lack of air conditioning does not breach the warranty, though it may breach your lease. A code violation alone is not automatically a breach. A code violation is a failure to meet the city or town building rules, and the warranty asks whether your health or safety is affected.

| Problem | The rule | What you do |
|---|---|---|
| Mold | No standalone rule; treated as a habitability problem | Keep photos, any tests and medical records |
| Bed bugs | Landlord exterminates even if you are suspected of bringing them, unless it proves misconduct | Report in writing; do not treat the unit yourself |
| No heat | In Ithaca, 68F by day and 62F at night, Sept. 15 to May 31 | Log the indoor temperature and tell the landlord in writing |
| Utility cut-off (landlord pays) | At least 18 days' posted notice in apartment buildings, 15 days mailed in two-family houses | Keep the notice and call the hotline |

Source: Cornell Law Tenants Advocacy, [building issues and maintenance](https://sites.lawschool.cornell.edu/tenants-advocacy/building-issues-maintenance).

## What should I do about mold?

Document it first. Take dated photos, keep any test results, and save medical records if the mold affects anyone's health. Those records are your evidence if a code officer or court looks at the unit.

The guide treats moving out over mold as justified only when the landlord ignores a serious health risk, a situation the law calls constructive eviction, meaning conditions so bad you are forced to leave. If you are thinking about leaving, call the hotline first, because the rent stays owed unless the move is justified.

## What should I do about bed bugs and other pests?

Report bed bugs in writing and let the landlord handle the treatment. The landlord must exterminate even when it suspects the tenant brought the bugs in, unless the landlord proves misconduct. It must also give written notice within 72 hours to the units above, below and beside yours, without naming your unit, and post notice in common areas within 72 hours. Self-treating can weaken a damages claim ([Cornell Law guide](https://sites.lawschool.cornell.edu/tenants-advocacy/building-issues-maintenance)).

Tompkins County's guidance, revised May 2021, asks for integrated pest management, licensed pest companies and no pesticides on the body ([Tompkins County Whole Health](https://www.tompkinscountyny.gov/All-Departments/Whole-Health/Community/HNP/Bed-Bugs)). Integrated pest management means fixing the conditions that let pests live there, not only spraying.

## How do I get a repair made, step by step?

Start with a paper trail, then escalate in order.

```figure

title: Getting a repair made in Ithaca
rows:
  - label: Write to the landlord with your name, address, the date and the problem
  - label: Photograph it, log every contact and ask for a timeframe
  - label: Follow up in writing; send certified mail if you need proof
  - label: File with the code office for your address
  - label: Call the free tenant hotline at 607-301-1560
  - label: After a month in the unit and written notice, petition the court for a repair order
```

A letter, text or email works. Follow every phone call with writing. The state sets [no strict repair deadline](https://sites.lawschool.cornell.edu/tenants-advocacy/building-issues-maintenance), so ask the landlord for a date. Cornell Cooperative Extension's version of the steps is to record dates, times and photos, log each attempt, tell the owner, ask for a timeframe, follow up and then [file a formal complaint](https://ccetompkins.org/your-home-money/tenant-landlord-issues).

Let the landlord in. A landlord needs reasonable notice to enter, such as 24 hours, at reasonable hours. It has no right to enter for repairs unless the lease reserves one, but it can enter without notice in an emergency ([Cornell Law landlord access guide](https://sites.lawschool.cornell.edu/tenants-advocacy/landlord-access)).

## Who takes a housing code complaint?

The office depends on your address, not on your landlord. A code complaint is a report to the local government that a rental breaks its building or safety rules, and the office can send an inspector.

| Your address | Code office | Phone |
|---|---|---|
| City of Ithaca (Collegetown, Fall Creek, downtown) | Building Division, 108 E. Green St., City Hall, 4th floor; 8:30 a.m. to 4 p.m. weekdays | 607-274-6508 |
| Town of Ithaca | Town code office | 607-273-1783 |
| Town of Lansing | Town code office | 607-533-7054 |
| Town of Dryden | Town code office | 607-844-8888 |

Source: [City of Ithaca Building Division](https://www.cityofithacany.gov/130/Building-Division); [Cornell Law guide](https://sites.lawschool.cornell.edu/tenants-advocacy/building-issues-maintenance), which also lists Caroline, Danby, Enfield, Groton, Newfield and Ulysses.

City inspectors handle housing inspections, exterior maintenance and complaints, and the Building Division takes them through an online "Report a Concern" form. Ask for any inspection report in writing. Our guide to [city versus town renting](/cornell/housing/city-of-ithaca-vs-town-lansing-cayuga-heights-renters/) helps you tell which side of the line you live on.

## What can a court do?

A court can order repairs and cut your rent. Since January 2024, under the Tenant Dignity and Safe Housing Act, a tenant who has lived in the unit at least 30 days and sent written notice can file a Petition for Judgment Directing Repairs ([Cornell Law guide](https://sites.lawschool.cornell.edu/tenants-advocacy/building-issues-maintenance)).

Courts can also reduce, or abate, the rent. From its own court experience, the Cornell Law guide puts abatements at about 10% to 20% for minor problems, about 30% for moderate ones and 50% to 60% for the most serious ([Cornell Law guide](https://sites.lawschool.cornell.edu/tenants-advocacy/building-issues-maintenance)).

Small claims at Ithaca City Court, 118 East Clinton Street, takes money claims up to $5,000 ([Cornell Law guide](https://sites.lawschool.cornell.edu/tenants-advocacy/building-issues-maintenance)).

## Why is withholding rent the last resort?

Withholding rent invites a nonpayment case. Repair-and-deduct and withholding are last resorts, and the guide says to get free legal advice first. A nonpayment case starts with a 14-day written rent demand, which cannot be served until at least 6 days after rent is due. You then have 14 days to pay or leave before the landlord can file ([Cornell Law eviction guide](https://sites.lawschool.cornell.edu/tenants-advocacy/eviction-proceedings)).

## Can my landlord punish me for complaining?

New York law says no, with one exception. A landlord cannot refuse to renew or raise rent unreasonably because of a good-faith complaint made in the past year, and an eviction within a year of a complaint can be defended as retaliation. The exception is an owner-occupied building with fewer than four units ([Cornell Law lease renewal guide](https://sites.lawschool.cornell.edu/tenants-advocacy/lease-renewal)).

## What does a slow response look like?

In 2018, tenants at 117 Thurston Ave. went about three days without hot water and lived with heat below the legal minimum. They learned after signing that the building's certificate of compliance, the city's inspection permit, had expired in November 2015 ([Cornell Daily Sun](https://cornellsun.com/2018/11/25/cornell-student-abandons-house-sleeps-in-library-after-months-long-dispute-with-landlord/)).

## What mistakes cost the most?

- **Only calling.** A phone call leaves no record, and a rent-cut claim rests on dated written requests.
- **Spraying for bed bugs yourself.** It can weaken a damages claim, and the landlord has to pay for extermination anyway.
- **Withholding rent first.** It hands the landlord a 14-day rent demand and a nonpayment case.
- **Calling the wrong office.** A Varna or Lansing address goes to a town code office, not the City Building Division.
- **Leaving over mold without advice.** The rent stays owed if the move was not justified.

## FAQ

**Does my landlord have to provide air conditioning?** Not under the warranty of habitability, but it may breach your lease if the lease promises it ([Cornell Law guide](https://sites.lawschool.cornell.edu/tenants-advocacy/building-issues-maintenance)).

**Can I change the locks after a break-in if the landlord won't fix the door?** Changing the locks needs the landlord's consent. Ask in writing and call the hotline.

**Does the Sept. 15 heat date apply in Lansing?** The guide gives Ithaca's season as Sept. 15 to May 31 and the statewide season as Oct. 1 to May 31. Ask your town code office which one it enforces.

## What to do next

Write to your landlord today, with photos and the date, and keep a copy. If nothing changes by the date you asked for, file with the code office for your address and call the hotline.
