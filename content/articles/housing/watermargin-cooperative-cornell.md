Watermargin is a Cornell-owned co-op at 103 McGraw Pl. with 23 residents in singles and doubles. It has a house meal plan with vegetarian and non-vegetarian dishes, and it fills rooms by lottery. It takes transfer, upper-level and graduate or professional students (Cornell Housing).

## What is Watermargin?

A co-op is a university-owned house run by its residents. Cornell lists Watermargin at 103 McGraw Pl. with 23 residents ([Cornell Housing](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/campus-housing/upper-level-undergraduates/cooperative-housing/watermargin)). The Cornell Chronicle describes a five-story house with a long history ([Cornell Chronicle](https://www.news.cornell.edu/stories/2024/01/more-75-years-students-have-made-watermargin-home)).

Watermargin began in 1947 as the United World Foundation, formed by World War II veterans on the G.I. Bill. The group leased its house from Cornell from fall 1948. The name comes from Pearl Buck's 1933 translation. Cornell calls it the first racially integrated interfaith living unit on a U.S. campus. The house was all male until about 1970, and it took its co-op structure in the late 1960s and early 1970s. The Chronicle lists Eleanor Roosevelt, Langston Hughes, Margaret Mead, Malcolm X and Marian Anderson among its visitors (Cornell Chronicle).

## What does Watermargin cost?

For 2026-27 a double is $4,317 per semester, or $8,634 for two semesters. A single runs from $4,889 to $5,213 per semester, which is $9,778 to $10,426 for the year ([Cornell Housing](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/housing-details/rates-amenities-licenses/housing-rates)).

A hall double costs $13,744 for the year, so the Watermargin double is $5,110 less. Watermargin is one of four houses that Cornell lists with a house meal plan ([Cornell Housing](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/campus-housing/upper-level-undergraduates/cooperative-housing)). Ask the house what the plan costs before you compare totals. For leases, read [Is off campus cheaper than a Cornell dorm?](/cornell/housing/is-off-campus-cheaper-than-cornell-dorm/) and [cheapest off-campus housing near Cornell](/cornell/housing/cheapest-off-campus-housing-cornell/).

## How do you get into Watermargin?

The house picks residents by random lottery, aimed at a diverse mix, and it allows nonresidential membership. Each co-op runs its own selection, which starts in early November and early February (Cornell Housing). Write to co-ops@cornell.edu or call 607-255-3445 for current steps. Nonresidential membership lets you join house life without a room.

Rising sophomores must state their co-op plan on the 2027-28 application by March 15, 2027 ([Cornell Housing](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/housing-details/apply-housing-campus/current-undergraduate-application-process/spring-housing-selection-rising-sophomores)). A co-op counts as affiliated housing, explained on our [affiliated housing page](/start/affiliated-housing/). To live off campus instead, see the [exemption rules](/cornell/housing/cornell-residential-requirement-exemptions-live-off-campus/).

## What should you watch?

The house needs a new roof. The Sun reported in October 2024 that Watermargin needs about $500,000 for one ([Cornell Daily Sun](https://cornellsun.com/2024/10/21/co-op-residents-face-deteriorating-houses-increasing-rent-costs/)). Spread over 23 residents, that is $21,739.13 each. The same story reported floor collapses in four of the eight co-ops. Ask the house what has been repaired.

All eight co-ops share one license, with no security deposit and a full-time student rule. See [Cornell co-ops compared](/cornell/housing/cornell-co-ops-all-eight-compared/) and our guide to [co-ops and Greek houses](/cornell/housing/cornell-co-ops-greek-houses-cost-how-to-join/).
