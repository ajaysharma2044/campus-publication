```glance
- City of Ithaca rentals: the landlord must give current tenants at least 120 days' written notice before renewing the lease, showing the unit or signing new tenants.
- The rule is City Code section 258-10, in force since May 31, 2022. It replaced a 60-day rule from 2013.
- The only way out is a bolded waiver on page one of the lease that you initial. Ask why before you do.
- It covers leases of nine months or longer, as reported, and stops at the city line (not the Town of Ithaca, Lansing or Cayuga Heights).
- No "earliest date" to show or re-sign a unit can be given yet: when the 120 days start is unresolved.
- State law adds its own 30, 60 or 90 days' notice for non-renewal or a rent rise of 5% or more.
```

Yes, if the apartment is in the City of Ithaca: under [City Code section 258-10](https://ecode360.com/27390826), your landlord must give you at least 120 days' written notice before renewing your lease, showing the unit to prospective tenants or signing with new ones. The one exception is a bolded statement on the lease's first page that you initial, and the rule's sponsor wrote that [why you are waiving the days should be your first question](https://cornellsun.com/2022/04/18/mehler-student-tenants-rights-shifting-in-your-favor/). Our advice: do not initial it by default; ask what the landlord wants it for, and get any trade in writing.

```reader "If you're already renting in the City"
Turn to page one of your current lease. If there is no initialed, bolded statement beginning "As per Chapter 258-10(A)", your landlord owes you 120 days' written notice before pressing a renewal, showing your unit or signing next year's group. Students have reported being pushed to renew within weeks of moving in (The Cornell Daily Sun, 2012). If you did initial, expect that early pitch. Separately, state law requires 30, 60 or 90 days' notice before a non-renewal or an increase of 5% or more. The costly mistake is renewing under pressure before your housemates commit: every co-tenant is usually liable for the whole rent. Free advice: Cornell Law's tenant hotline, 607-301-1560.
```

```reader "If you're a sophomore signing for junior year"
The waiver sits in the lease you are about to sign, so it shapes your junior fall. Initial it, and the landlord may show your new apartment and ask about a senior-year renewal soon after you move in. Popular Collegetown units are signed far ahead (up to 16 months, The Cornell Daily Sun reported in 2018), so you will feel that pressure anyway. Before signing, ask why the waiver is there, ask to strike it, and keep the answer in writing. Off-Campus Living offers one-on-one lease consultations at 607-255-2310. The mistake that costs most: initialing page one in a stack of signatures without reading it.
```

```reader "If you're a parent reviewing or co-signing"
Read the lease's first page before your student signs. The waiver is a bolded line with a space for initials, and The Cornell Daily Sun's 2022 column by the rule's sponsor says both parties must choose to waive. That makes it a term to discuss before signing, not a formality. Ask the landlord what the waiver is for and what happens if your student declines. Get the answer by email. The 120 days apply only inside the City of Ithaca; a lease in Lansing or Cayuga Heights has no such window. For a free read of the lease, the Cornell Law tenant hotline is 607-301-1560.
```

```reader "If you're an Ithaca landlord or thinking of becoming one"
For a City rental, give current tenants written notice at least 120 days before you renew, show or re-let the unit. A waiver counts only if it is explicitly written, bolded and initialed on the lease's first page, as WSKG reported the 2022 amendment. The City Attorney said in 2022 that compliance disputes can go to the City's housing court. Property in the Town of Ithaca, Lansing or Cayuga Heights is outside this section. The state notices still apply on top: 30 to 90 days under Real Property Law 226-c, and Good Cause renewal rules for covered City units. The mistake that costs most is relying on a waiver buried in the lease body.
```

## What Chapter 258-10 requires

The rule sits in Chapter 258, "Rental Housing", Article II, section 258-10, "Renewal of rental agreements; notification to tenants", [amended April 6, 2022 by Ordinance 2022-02](https://ecode360.com/IT1348). The code index was current through May 6, 2026 and still listed the section.

It protects current tenants. The landlord must give at least 120 days' written notice before any of three acts:

1. Renewing your current rental agreement.
2. Showing the unit to prospective tenants.
3. Signing a lease with new tenants.

[WSKG reported](https://wskg.org/ithaca-landlords-must-now-give-renters-120-days-before-renewing-leases/) that Common Council passed it unanimously on April 6, 2022, that it took effect May 31, 2022, and that it raised the old notice from 60 days. The sponsor was Alderperson Patrick Mehler. He [first proposed 180 days, then 120 the next week](https://wskg.org/ithaca-council-members-are-considering-how-renewal-restrictions-hurt-renters/), and the Planning and Economic Development Committee heard it on January 19, 2022. A [December 2021 City draft](https://www.cityofithacany.gov/DocumentCenter/View/13566/Proposed-Amendment-to-258-10-Rental-Housing-Renewal-of-Rental-Agreements-Notification-to-Tenants-of-the-City-Code-) with 180 days and a written waiver never became law.

Scope: WSKG and a [landlord-side legal blog](https://www.leaselenses.com/blog/new-york-ithaca-security-deposit-ordinance/) both say it covers leases of nine months or longer. A 10-month or 12-month Collegetown lease is inside that, as reported.

Not confirmed: the nine-month line in the code's own text, and what penalty applies today ([section 258-11](https://ecode360.com/IT1348) is headed "Penalties for offenses").

## The waiver on page one

A landlord can skip the 120 days only if the waiver is [explicitly written, bolded and initialed](https://wskg.org/ithaca-landlords-must-now-give-renters-120-days-before-renewing-leases/) on the lease's first page. The code's statement [begins "As per Chapter 258-10(A)"](https://ecode360.com/27390826). The Sun printed a sample line in which the tenant initials to say they "willingly waive" the right to 120 days' written notice.

Three things about it matter at signing:

- **Both sides choose it.** Mehler's [Sun column](https://cornellsun.com/2022/04/18/mehler-student-tenants-rights-shifting-in-your-favor/) says both parties must choose to waive.
- **It nearly did not exist.** An early draft removed the waiver from city code; Mehler told WSKG it was added back so the measure would not violate constitutional law.
- **Students flag it.** Reading the lease for a notice waiver is a tip in three Sun pieces, from [2013](https://cornellsun.com/2013/09/12/despite-new-law-housing-rush-persists-in-collegetown/), [2014](https://cornellsun.com/2014/09/08/fischer-jimmys-tale-of-leasing-in-collegetown/) and 2022.

Not confirmed: how the waiver squares with section 258-9, which the [code index](https://ecode360.com/IT1348) heads "Waiver of provisions void".

## Should you initial it?

Initialing does not change your rent or lease end. It changes when the landlord may start the next lease cycle on your unit while you live there. Our advice by situation:

| Your situation | What initialing changes | Our advice |
|---|---|---|
| Undecided about staying, or a group still forming | Renewal pressure and showings can start soon after you move in | Do not initial; you want the full 120 days to decide |
| Planning to stay a second year | The landlord can re-let your unit early if you do not commit early | Do not initial unless the landlord offers something you want, in writing |
| Leaving after this lease (a graduating senior, say) | Showings in your unit can begin sooner | Lower stakes; the lease still governs entry, with notice often 24 hours |
| Landlord says the waiver is required | Not settled: the Sun column says both parties must choose | Ask why by email; call 607-301-1560 before signing |

On entry: [Cornell Law's guide](https://sites.lawschool.cornell.edu/tenants-advocacy/landlord-access) says the lease usually sets the rules, with reasonable notice, often 24 hours, at reasonable hours. A landlord has no right to enter if the lease does not reserve one, except in an emergency.

On joint liability: most local leases make each housemate [jointly and severally liable](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/campus-living/living-campus/study-abroad/housemate-compatibility-questionnaire) for the whole rent. An early renewal signed before the group is sure leaves the people who stay owing for those who leave.

Already initialed? The waiver covers only the 120 days. The state notices in the table below still apply to you.

## When the 120 days start: unresolved

Nobody can yet say the earliest date a landlord may show or re-sign a Collegetown unit. Two readings conflict:

- **Back from the lease end.** Most Ithaca leases [expire July 31](https://cornellsun.com/2024/07/10/landlords-and-advocates-grapple-with-good-cause-eviction-law-at-public-hearing), so the window would open in spring.
- **Forward from the lease start.** [Mehler's own examples](https://cornellsun.com/2022/04/18/mehler-student-tenants-rights-shifting-in-your-favor/) count this way: for a June 1 lease, a renewal decision about October 1; for August leases, about December. The [2013 rule](https://cornellsun.com/2013/09/12/despite-new-law-housing-rush-persists-in-collegetown/) also ran from the lease start, so a June lease let showings begin in late August.

The market does not settle it. On November 8, 2026, [Ithaca Renting](https://ithacarenting.com/collegetown/) was already marketing 2027-28 Collegetown units, with 14 of 67 rows sold out, nine months before an August 2027 start. That could reflect vacant or new units with no current tenant, initialed waivers, buildings outside the City, or a different count. None of those is confirmed.

Not confirmed: when the count starts. Treat Mehler's dates as his examples, not deadlines.

## How the City rule fits with state law

The City rule stacks on top of New York's. [Cornell Law's renewal page](https://sites.lawschool.cornell.edu/tenants-advocacy/lease-renewal), updated February 2026, gives only the state periods and does not mention 258-10, so a tenant reading it alone misses the City window.

| Rule | Who it covers | Notice | Triggered by |
|---|---|---|---|
| City Code 258-10 | City of Ithaca rentals, leases of nine months or longer as reported | 120 days, written | Renewing, showing, or signing new tenants |
| [Real Property Law 226-c](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/RPP/226-C) | Unregulated New York tenancies | 30 days (under a year), 60 (one to two years), 90 (over two) | Non-renewal, or an increase of 5% or more |
| [Good Cause, RPL 216](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/RPP/216) | Covered City units since July 2024 | 30 to 90 days before the lease ends | Removing a tenant who refuses reasonable renewal terms |
| Auto-renew clause (GOL 5-903) | Leases that renew themselves | Reminder 15 to 30 days before your opt-out date | Any automatic renewal |

Two state rules change the math at renewal. Under 226-c, late notice keeps you on your existing terms until the notice period runs. Under Good Cause, an increase above the lower of 10% or 5% plus inflation is [presumed unreasonable](https://hcr.ny.gov/good-cause-eviction). Buildings with a certificate of occupancy from January 1, 2009 on are [exempt for 30 years](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/RPP/214).

A good-faith complaint is protected too. Refusing to renew, or an unreasonable increase, within a year of one can be defended as retaliation, [per Cornell Law](https://sites.lawschool.cornell.edu/tenants-advocacy/lease-renewal), except in owner-occupied buildings with fewer than four units.

## Inside the city line only

Section 258-10 is City code. It does not reach the Town of Ithaca, the Town of Lansing or the Village of Cayuga Heights. There, only the state notices in the table above apply. Our guide to the City and the towns around it sorts out which rules follow your address.

## If your landlord skips the notice

City Attorney Ari Levine told [WSKG](https://wskg.org/ithaca-landlords-must-now-give-renters-120-days-before-renewing-leases/) that compliance disputes can go to the City's housing court.

1. Save every renewal email, text and showing request, with dates.
2. Check page one of your lease for an initialed waiver.
3. Reply in writing that you have not waived the 120-day notice, if that is true.
4. Call the Cornell Law tenant hotline, 607-301-1560, listed on its [lease-ending page](https://sites.lawschool.cornell.edu/tenants-advocacy/breaking-a-lease). Off-Campus Living, 607-255-2310, offers [lease consultations](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/campus-housing/housing-search-process/signing-lease).

Example: a junior moves into a 12-month Collegetown lease on August 1 and did not initial the waiver. In September the landlord emails that the group must renew within a week or the unit will be shown. Under 258-10 the landlord owes 120 days' written notice first. The junior replies in writing, keeps the email, and calls the hotline before anyone signs.

## Common mistakes

- **Initialing page one without reading it.** You give up the 120-day window, and the renewal pitch can arrive within weeks of moving in, as [students reported in 2012](https://cornellsun.com/2012/09/11/new-york-state-official%e2%80%88landlords-students-must-be-reasonable-people/).
- **Trusting a source that gives only the state rule.** You assume a landlord may show your unit any time; in the City, the 120 days come first.
- **Renewing before the group is sure.** Every co-tenant is usually liable for the whole rent, so one dropout's share lands on the rest for 12 months.
- **Applying the rule outside the City.** A Lansing or Cayuga Heights tenant has no 120-day window.
- **Treating a sponsor's example as a deadline.** "About December" for August leases is one reading, not a legal date.
- **Believing the rule ended the rush.** Sun reporting shows leases still leaving the market 16 months ahead in 2018.

## FAQ

**Does the rule cover a 10-month lease?** As reported, it covers leases of nine months or longer, so a 10-month lease is inside it ([WSKG](https://wskg.org/ithaca-landlords-must-now-give-renters-120-days-before-renewing-leases/)). The code's own wording on length is not confirmed.

**Once notice is given, can my landlord walk in with prospective tenants?** Only on the terms your lease sets, with reasonable notice (often 24 hours) at reasonable hours ([Cornell Law](https://sites.lawschool.cornell.edu/tenants-advocacy/landlord-access)).

**Can my landlord raise the rent when I renew?** Yes, within limits. An increase of 5% or more needs 30 to 90 days' notice ([RPL 226-c](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/RPP/226-C)), and in a covered unit a rise above the Good Cause standard is presumed unreasonable ([HCR](https://hcr.ny.gov/good-cause-eviction)).

**Where can I get a lease reviewed for free?** The Cornell Law tenant hotline, 607-301-1560, and Off-Campus Living consultations at 607-255-2310 ([Cornell](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/campus-housing/housing-search-process/signing-lease)).

**Was it always 120 days?** No. The 2013 ordinance set 60 days; the April 6, 2022 amendment raised it to 120 ([WSKG](https://wskg.org/ithaca-landlords-must-now-give-renters-120-days-before-renewing-leases/)).
