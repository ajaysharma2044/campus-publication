```glance
- Deposit cap: one month's rent, and that includes any "last month's rent," rent advance or pet deposit (New York GOL 7-108).
- Banned outright: application fees, cleaning fees and non-refundable deposits. A credit or background check costs at most $20.
- Late fees: only once rent is more than 5 days late, and at most $50 or 5% of the rent, whichever is less.
- Return: the deposit and an itemized statement within 14 days of move-out (by August 14 for a July 31 exit), or the landlord loses the right to keep any of it.
- Willful violations: punitive damages up to twice the deposit; claim in small claims court, up to $5,000 at Ithaca City Court.
- Take the move-in inspection when it is offered, and ask for the move-out inspection; both protect the deposit.
```

In Ithaca, as everywhere in New York, a landlord can take a security deposit of [no more than one month's rent](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/GOB/7-108), counting any rent paid in advance. The landlord must return it with an itemized statement of deductions within 14 days after you move out, or forfeit the right to keep any part of it. Application fees and cleaning fees are [banned](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/RPP/238-A), and a background check is capped at $20.

```reader "If you're a junior or senior already renting"
Your deposit stays capped at one month's rent, but at renewal the landlord [may collect more](https://ag.ny.gov/resources/individuals/tenants-homeowners/tenants) to bring it up to the new monthly rent. If your lease ends July 31, the deposit or itemized statement is due by August 14. Ask in writing for the move-out inspection; it falls one to two weeks before the end, with 48 hours' notice, and the landlord lists intended charges so you can fix them. The costly mistake is skipping it and the photos: then the landlord's list of charges is the only record. If money is withheld unfairly, send a demand letter, then file in Ithaca City Court, which hears claims [up to $5,000](https://sites.lawschool.cornell.edu/tenants-advocacy/building-issues-maintenance).
```

```reader "If you're a sophomore signing for junior year"
Expect to pay first month's rent plus a deposit at signing; [209 Stewart Ave](https://offcampushousing.ithaca.edu/city/ithaca-college-ny/listing/209-stewart-avenue-235095), at $850 a room, asks a one-month deposit with first month due at signing. Refuse any application fee, cleaning fee or non-refundable deposit, and pay no more than $20 for a background check (nothing if you bring one from the past 30 days). A deposit plus a "last month" cannot add up to more than one month's rent. Your Cornell room this year takes [no security deposit](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/housing-details/rates-amenities-licenses/academic-year-housing-license-2026-2027), so this is the first one you will pay. Get a [written receipt](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/living-off-campus/housing-search-process/checklists-and-considerations) for every payment.
```

```reader "If you're a parent paying the deposit"
Cornell aid [does not cover](https://finaid.cornell.edu/living-campus) security deposits, first or last month's rent, or rent in unregistered months, so the signing payment is usually yours. Together, deposit and any rent paid in advance cannot exceed one month's rent; a request for a deposit plus "last month" breaks that cap. The deposit is a trust fund the landlord may not mix with its own money. In a building of six or more units it must sit in a New York interest-bearing account, and you are told the bank in writing. The mistake that costs most is paying a banned fee because the lease asks for it: application, cleaning and non-refundable charges are void, and a waiver in the lease does not change that.
```

```reader "If you're an international student"
You [do not need an SSN](https://international.globallearning.cornell.edu/life-cornell/faq-international-students) to rent. If a landlord asks for several months up front, know that New York counts any rent advance toward the one-month deposit cap; ask the free tenant hotline, 607-301-1560, before you wire money. Pay no application fee; that is banned. A dispute with a landlord [does not affect your visa status](https://www.gradschool.cornell.edu/announcements/grad-tips-renting/), per Cornell Graduate School staff. Leaving the US after your lease? Give the landlord a forwarding address in writing so the 14-day refund or itemized statement can reach you.
```

```reader "If you're on a tight budget"
The signing payment is the hard part: first month plus a deposit, neither paid by Cornell aid. At the 6BR median of $800 a bed at [Ithaca Renting](https://ithacarenting.com/collegetown/) for 2027-28, that is $1,600 per person at signing (our arithmetic). Save on what you should not pay at all: no application fee, no cleaning fee, a $20 ceiling on a background check and a free one if yours is under 30 days old. A late fee cannot run past $50 or 5% of the rent, and only after 5 days. On $800 rent that ceiling is $40. Deductions for routine cleaning are not allowed either.
```

```reader "If you're an alum thinking of becoming a landlord"
Your duties: take at most one month's rent as deposit plus advance, hold it as a trust fund separate from your money, and in a building of six or more units put it in a New York interest-bearing account (you may keep a 1% annual administrative fee). Offer a move-in inspection before the tenant moves in, and tell tenants in writing of their right to a move-out inspection. Return the deposit with an itemized statement within 14 days or lose the right to keep any of it. In court you must prove withheld amounts reasonable, and a willful violation can cost up to twice the deposit. Charge no application or cleaning fee.
```

## How much a landlord can take, and what is banned

The core rule is [General Obligations Law 7-108](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/GOB/7-108). The security deposit plus any rent advance cannot exceed one month's rent. That means no "last month's rent" on top of a deposit, and a pet deposit counts toward the same cap, per the [Cornell Law School Tenants' Rights Guide](https://sites.lawschool.cornell.edu/tenants-advocacy/security-deposits) (updated February 2026). The rule does not cover seasonal-use units or owner-occupied co-op units.

Fees are governed by [Real Property Law 238-a](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/RPP/238-A). A landlord may not charge for processing, reviewing or accepting an application. A background or credit check costs the lesser of the actual cost or $20. The landlord must first give you a copy of the check and the invoice, and must waive the fee if you provide a check from the past 30 days. A non-refundable deposit is unlawful, and a separate cleaning fee or deposit is prohibited, per the Cornell Law guide.

| Charge | What the law allows | Example on $850 a month | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Security deposit plus rent advance | One month's rent in total, pet deposit included | $850 at most, all together | [GOL 7-108](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/GOB/7-108) |
| "Last month's rent" on top of a deposit | Not allowed beyond the cap | Counts inside the $850 | [Cornell Law guide](https://sites.lawschool.cornell.edu/tenants-advocacy/security-deposits) |
| Application or processing fee | None | $0 | [RPL 238-a](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/RPP/238-A) |
| Background or credit check | Lesser of cost or $20; free with a check from the past 30 days | $20 at most | [RPL 238-a](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/RPP/238-A) |
| Cleaning fee or non-refundable deposit | Banned | $0 | [Cornell Law guide](https://sites.lawschool.cornell.edu/tenants-advocacy/security-deposits) |
| Late fee | Only after rent is more than 5 days late; lesser of $50 or 5% | $42.50 at most | [RPL 238-a](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/RPP/238-A) |
| Replacement key | No more than 110% of actual cost (cap lifts after three copies of one key in a year) | $11 for a $10 key | [RPL 235-i](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/RPP/235-I) |

The $850 column uses the per-room rent at 209 Stewart Ave for 2026-27; the dollar figures in it are our arithmetic. The 5% late-fee cap is the lower one for any rent under $1,000 a month. Above $1,000, the $50 cap applies.

## While you live there: trust, interest and top-ups

A deposit is not the landlord's money. The [Attorney General](https://ag.ny.gov/resources/individuals/tenants-homeowners/tenants) treats it as a trust fund that may not be mixed with the landlord's own funds.

In a building with six or more units, the deposit must go in a New York interest-bearing account at the prevailing rate. The landlord tells you the bank's name and address and the amount in writing. The landlord may keep a 1% annual administrative fee. You choose to get the interest each year, as a credit against rent, or at the end of the lease (GOL 7-103).

When the rent rises at renewal, the landlord may collect more to bring the deposit up to the new monthly rent. If the building is sold, a new owner who knew of the deposit becomes [liable for it](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/GOB/7-108).

## The two inspections

**Move-in.** After you sign but before you move in, the landlord must offer an inspection. If you accept, you both sign a written record of the unit's condition. The landlord then cannot keep any deposit money for defects listed on it. Take dated photos of every room as well; Cornell's [move-out page](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/living-off-campus/housing-search-process/moving-and-out) advises photos at move-in and move-out.

**Move-out.** When either side gives notice to end the tenancy, the landlord must tell you in writing that you can request an inspection. If you ask, it happens between two weeks and one week before the lease ends, with at least 48 hours' written notice. The landlord then tells you what it plans to charge, so you can fix it first. The inspection is not required if you give less than two weeks' notice.

## What can come out of the deposit

The landlord carries the burden of proving that any amount withheld is reasonable. Any lease clause waiving these rules is [void](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/GOB/7-108).

| Can be deducted | Cannot be deducted |
|---|---|
| Unpaid rent | Normal wear and tear |
| Damage you caused beyond normal wear | Routine cleaning to prepare the unit for the next tenant |
| Unpaid utilities payable to the landlord | Attorney's fees (since June 14, 2019) |
| Moving and storing belongings you left after the lease | Late fees (since June 14, 2019) |
| Excessive cleaning and trash removal | Defects listed on a signed move-in condition record |

Source: the [Cornell Law guide](https://sites.lawschool.cornell.edu/tenants-advocacy/security-deposits) and GOL 7-108.

## The 14-day deadline

Under [GOL 7-108](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/GOB/7-108), the landlord must return the rest of the deposit with an itemized statement within 14 days after you vacate, so the clock starts the day you leave. Miss that, and the landlord forfeits the right to keep any of it.

## Cornell's cleaning-charge line

Cornell's move-out page says the full deposit should come back if the apartment is clean and in satisfactory condition, but also that "charges for cleaning services will be deducted." Read that with the law beside it. A flat cleaning fee is banned. Cleaning a unit left dirty beyond normal wear may be deducted, but routine turnover cleaning may not. Trust the statute and the Cornell Law guide over the housing page.

## Getting it back when the landlord keeps it

1. **Write first.** Send a demand letter citing GOL 7-108, the 14-day date and the amount, and keep a copy.
2. **Mediate.** The Community Dispute Resolution Center handles tenant cases at [607-273-9347](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/living-off-campus/community-resources).
3. **Complain to the state.** The Attorney General takes deposit complaints after you have tried the landlord: 1-800-771-7755.
4. **Sue in small claims.** Ithaca City Court, 118 East Clinton Street, hears claims up to $5,000; town and village courts cap at $3,000. Ask for actual damages, plus punitive damages up to twice the deposit if the violation was willful.

**Example:** a senior moves out July 31 from a 4BR bed with a $1,246.25 deposit. By August 20 nothing has arrived. The 14 days ran out August 14, so the landlord has lost the right to keep any of it. The senior sends a demand letter, then files in Ithaca City Court for $1,246.25. If the court finds the withholding willful, it may add punitive damages of up to $2,492.50. The total stays under the $5,000 limit.

Deposit fights are an old Ithaca complaint. The Sun reported in [2004](https://cornellsun.com/2004/12/02/good-bad-and-slummy-collegetown-landlords/) that one large Collegetown company had a reputation for fighting over deposits. The founders of the Ithaca Tenants Union cited deposits above the one-month cap among their reasons in [2020](https://cornellsun.com/2020/03/06/as-students-struggle-to-find-housing-three-ilr-juniors-form-new-ithaca-tenants-union/).

## Common mistakes

- **Paying a deposit plus "last month."** Together they cannot pass one month's rent. On a $1,246.25 bed, an extra month is $1,246.25 you should never have paid.
- **Paying an application or cleaning fee.** Both are banned; a $20 background check is the only screening charge allowed.
- **Skipping the move-in inspection.** Without a signed condition record, an old stain or crack becomes your word against the landlord's.
- **Not asking for the move-out inspection.** It is your one chance to see the charges while you can still fix them.
- **Leaving no forwarding address in writing.** The 14-day clock runs, but the refund or statement has nowhere to go.
- **Counting on aid for the signing payment.** Cornell aid covers neither the deposit nor first month, so a $2,492.50 signing bill (first month plus deposit on a $1,246.25 bed) comes from savings.
- **Giving up after 14 days.** A landlord who misses the deadline forfeits the deposit; a demand letter and a $5,000 small-claims court are the next steps.

## FAQ

**Does a landlord have to pay interest on my deposit?** Only in a building with six or more units, where the deposit must earn interest in a New York account, less a 1% annual fee the landlord may keep ([Attorney General](https://ag.ny.gov/resources/individuals/tenants-homeowners/tenants)).

**Can my landlord raise the deposit when I renew?** Yes, if the rent goes up: the landlord may collect enough to bring the deposit up to the new monthly rent.

**Can someone check the deposit clause before I sign?** Yes, for free: the Cornell Law Tenants Advocacy hotline ([607-301-1560](https://sites.lawschool.cornell.edu/tenants-advocacy/tenants-rights/)) takes lease questions.

**Does a Cornell dorm room take a deposit?** No. A Cornell housing license is not a lease and carries [no security or key deposits](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/housing-details/rates-amenities-licenses/academic-year-housing-license-2026-2027).

**What if my landlord sells the building?** A new owner who knew of your deposit is liable for returning it ([GOL 7-108](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/GOB/7-108)).

**Do these rules apply in Lansing or Cayuga Heights?** Yes. The deposit and fee rules are state law, so they apply across New York, inside the City of Ithaca and outside it. Small claims outside the City go to town or village courts, capped at $3,000.

**Can my landlord charge for lost keys?** Up to 110% of the actual cost of a copy, until more than three copies of the same key in a calendar year ([RPL 235-i](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/RPP/235-I)).
