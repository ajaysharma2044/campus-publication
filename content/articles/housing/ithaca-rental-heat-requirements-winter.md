```glance
- Ithaca's heat season starts two weeks before the statewide Oct. 1 start.
- From 6 a.m. to 10 p.m., rooms must hit 68F whenever it is under 55F outside.
- From 10 p.m. to 6 a.m., the minimum is 62F no matter the outdoor temperature.
- Heat shutoffs for a landlord's unpaid bill need a month's notice from Nov. 1 to Apr. 15.
```

If you rent in Ithaca, your landlord must heat your home from Sept. 15 to May 31. The indoor minimum is 68F from 6 a.m. to 10 p.m. when it is below 55F outside, and 62F from 10 p.m. to 6 a.m. ([Cornell Law Tenants Advocacy](https://sites.lawschool.cornell.edu/tenants-advocacy/building-issues-maintenance)).

## When does the heat season start in Ithaca?

Ithaca's season starts two weeks before the statewide one, which runs Oct. 1 to May 31, so an Ithaca rental gets about two more weeks of required heat in the fall ([Cornell Law Tenants Advocacy](https://sites.lawschool.cornell.edu/tenants-advocacy/building-issues-maintenance)).

That matters for a student who moves in at the end of August. A cold stretch in late September is already covered, and you do not have to wait for October to ask for heat.

The guide gives the Ithaca date but does not say which towns outside the city follow it. If you live in the Town of Ithaca, Lansing or Cayuga Heights, ask your town code office which season it enforces. Our guide to [city versus town renting](/cornell/housing/city-of-ithaca-vs-town-lansing-cayuga-heights-renters/) explains how to tell which side of the line your address is on.

## How warm does my apartment have to be?

There are two minimums, one for the day and one for the night.


Source: Cornell Law Tenants Advocacy, [building issues and maintenance](https://sites.lawschool.cornell.edu/tenants-advocacy/building-issues-maintenance).

The day rule has a condition. On a mild afternoon of 58F outside, your landlord does not owe you 68F inside. The night rule has no such condition, so a 62F floor applies even on a cool but not cold night.

A tenant who wants proof should write down the indoor temperature, the time and the outdoor temperature. A cheap thermometer on an interior wall works. Photograph it with a date visible.

## Is no heat a repair problem or a legal problem?

It is a legal problem. New York builds a promise into every residential lease, called the warranty of habitability. The home and shared areas must be fit to live in and not dangerous to life, health or safety, and a lease clause that waives this promise is void ([Real Property Law 235-b](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/RPP/235-B)). The Cornell Law guide lists no heat first among the failures it covers, next to no hot water, no electricity, pests and mold.

Two limits apply. A condition you caused does not count, and a code violation alone is not automatically a breach. The same guide says missing air conditioning generally does not qualify, so summer heat is not covered the way winter cold is.

## What if the landlord does not fix the heat?

Put the problem in writing the same day. A letter, text or email with your name, address and the date works, and certified mail gives proof of delivery. Follow any phone call with a written note ([Cornell Law Tenants Advocacy](https://sites.lawschool.cornell.edu/tenants-advocacy/building-issues-maintenance)).

The state sets no strict repair deadline, so ask for a date in your message. Then use this order:

In the City of Ithaca, the Building Division at 108 E. Green St. takes complaints at 607-274-6508 from 8:30 a.m. to 4 p.m. on weekdays, and it has an online "Report a Concern" form ([City of Ithaca](https://www.cityofithacany.gov/130/Building-Division)). The full order of steps, plus mold and bed bugs, is in the [repairs guide](/cornell/housing/ithaca-landlord-wont-fix-heat-mold-bed-bugs/).

Two legal tools exist, but both carry risk. Repair-and-deduct and rent withholding are last resorts, and withholding invites a nonpayment case that starts with a 14-day written notice. Get free advice first. Since January 2024, a tenant who has lived in the unit for a month and sent written notice can also file a Petition for Judgment Directing Repairs in court (Cornell Law Tenants Advocacy).

## Can a landlord shut off my heat for unpaid bills?

Only after a long warning. If the landlord pays the utilities and falls behind, the utility must give tenants notice before cutting service. The notice is at least 18 days, posted, for apartment buildings and at least 15 days, mailed, for two-family houses. For heat shutoffs from Nov. 1 to Apr. 15, the notice is at least 30 days (Cornell Law Tenants Advocacy).

If you find a shutoff notice on your building, keep it and call the hotline right away. A notice is a warning, not a cutoff, and you have time to act.

## Who pays for heat, and how do I find out before signing?

The lease decides. In some buildings heat is part of the rent, and in others you open your own account with the utility. Before you sign, ask the landlord whether heat is included, and ask for the average annual heating bill. Cornell suggests calling NYSEG at 800-572-1111 for that history, especially for older homes such as the West Hill cottages ([Cornell Student and Campus Life](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/campus-housing/housing-search-process/ithaca-neighborhoods)). Our [winter utility bill guide](/cornell/money/ithaca-utilities-cost-winter-heating-bill-apartment-students/) shows what students pay, and the [lease reading guide](/cornell/housing/how-to-read-ithaca-lease-before-signing/) covers where utility terms hide.

## Do these myths hold up?

**"Heat has to be on all year."** No. The rule covers the heat season only, not summer.

**"My landlord must keep it at 68F around the clock."** No. The 68F minimum applies from 6 a.m. to 10 p.m., and only when it is under 55F outside. At night the floor is 62F.

**"The state gives landlords a set number of days to fix it."** It does not. The guide says there is no strict statutory deadline, which is why a written request with a date matters.

## What to do next

Before the first cold week of the heat season, save the Building Division number and the hotline number in your phone. If you are still choosing a place, ask whether heat is included and request the heating bill history.
