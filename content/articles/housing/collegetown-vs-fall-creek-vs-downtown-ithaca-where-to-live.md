```glance
- Collegetown: 0.1 to 0.5 miles from campus, up to a 15-minute walk; one landlord's 2027-28 beds run $800 (6BR) to $2,025 (1BR alone) a month.
- Downtown: the same landlord's beds cost about 20% to 28% less; the price is a 20 to 30 minute uphill walk or a bus.
- Fall Creek: 0.5 to 1 mile, quiet and grad-heavy; a 1BR on N Tioga St asks $1,600 to $1,700 a month.
- Timing: popular Collegetown units sign 8 to 16 months ahead; downtown and Fall Creek move months later.
- The number to beat: Cornell's 2026-27 double is $13,744 a year, or $1,145 a month over a 12-month lease.
- All three sit inside the City of Ithaca, so the 120-day renewal rule and the Cornell safety tool apply; Lansing and Cayuga Heights do not.
```

Pick Collegetown if walking to class matters most and you can sign 8 to 16 months ahead; pick downtown or Fall Creek if price or quiet matters more and a 20 to 30 minute uphill walk or a bus ride is fine, per [Cornell's neighborhood guide](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/campus-housing/housing-search-process/ithaca-neighborhoods). At Ithaca Renting, the one landlord that posts both price lists, a [Collegetown bed for 2027-28](https://ithacarenting.com/collegetown/) costs 25% to 39% more than a [downtown bed](https://ithacarenting.com/downtown/) of the same unit size. Fall Creek posts the fewest prices, but its 1BR listings ask about $1,600 a month and Cornell calls it a lower-rent area that many grad students prefer.

```reader "If you're a sophomore planning junior year"
Decide group size and neighborhood before October. Ithaca Renting's 2027-28 Collegetown medians run $800 a bed in a 6BR, $1,246.25 in a 4BR and $2,025 for a 1BR alone, and on 2026-11-08 14 of its 67 Collegetown rows were already sold out (https://ithacarenting.com/collegetown/). If your group wants Collegetown, sign in September to November of sophomore year. If downtown or Fall Creek is fine, you can wait into spring and pay about a fifth less per bed. The costly mistake is a group formed in the first weeks of fall that shrinks: everyone left is liable for the whole rent, and dropping from a 6BR to a 4BR median costs $446.25 a person a month.
```

```reader "If you're a graduate or professional student"
Skip the undergrad rush. A grad guide puts the search in March and April for an August start, with a few places left in June and July (https://reedgroup.github.io/GraduateStudentResources/LifeAtCornell.html). Fall Creek is about 15 minutes by bus from Hollister Hall; groceries need a bus or car. Cornell's 2026-27 Ithaca budget allows $19,962 a year for housing, about $1,663.50 a month by our division, which covers a $1,600 Fall Creek 1BR but not the $2,025 Collegetown 1BR median. Ask each landlord what share of tenants are undergrads. Master's students pay $1.50 a daytime ride or $200 a year after year one; PhD students and assistants ride free.
```

```reader "If you're on a tight budget"
Cheap beds exist in all three areas, but only in big units. Ithaca Renting lists a downtown 3BR bed at a $795 median, a downtown 2BR at $992.50, a Collegetown 6BR at $800 and a Collegetown room in a shared suite at $895. A downtown 3BR share comes to $9,540 a year, $4,204 under Cornell's $13,744 double (https://ithacarenting.com/downtown/). Fall Creek mostly lists whole 1BR units at about $1,600, which is more than the double. The mistake that costs most: a Collegetown 1BR alone, $24,300 a year at the median, $10,556 above the double. Downtown adds $200 a year for an OmniRide pass if you ride daytime weekdays.
```

```reader "If you're bringing a car"
The car changes the answer more than rent does. An older Cornell item says central Collegetown has no residential street permits (https://news.cornell.edu/node/278473), so the fallback is the Dryden Road Garage at $232 a month, $2,784 over 12 months (https://www.cityofithacany.gov/718/Dryden-Road-Garage). Outside central Collegetown, a City residential permit is $45 a year in a permit zone, with a lease as proof (https://www.cityofithacany.gov/731/Permit-Issuance). Daytime campus parking is a B commuter permit at $359.77 a year, sold first come from early August (https://fcs.cornell.edu/departments/transportation-delivery-services/parking/parking-students). Not confirmed: which Fall Creek and downtown blocks are permit zones, so ask the landlord whether a spot comes with the unit.
```

## The three neighborhoods side by side

| | Collegetown | Fall Creek | Downtown and the Commons |
|---|---|---|---|
| Where | Directly south of central campus: College Ave, Dryden Rd, Eddy St | Below campus toward the lake: Cascadilla Ave, Lake St, N Tioga St | West of campus at the bottom of the hill |
| Distance (Cornell) | 0.1 to 0.5 miles | 0.5 to 1 mile | 0.5 to 1.5 miles; Commons 1 mile |
| Walk (Cornell) | Up to 15 minutes; lower Collegetown is uphill | 20 to 30 minutes uphill | 20 to 30 minutes uphill |
| Bus | Rarely needed | Frequent service; about 15 minutes from Hollister Hall | Route 10 runs downtown and campus |
| Typical bed or unit | $800 to $2,025 per bed (Ithaca Renting, 2027-28) | 1BR units about $1,600 to $1,700 | $795 to $1,625 per bed (Ithaca Renting, mostly 2026-27) |
| Who lives there | Mostly undergrads | Many grad students | Many grad students; mixed |
| When it leases | 8 to 16 months ahead | Spring for August | Spring for August |

Distances and walk times come from [Cornell's neighborhood guide](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/campus-housing/housing-search-process/ithaca-neighborhoods). That page uses one paragraph for Fall Creek, downtown and the Commons, so their shared "20 to 30 minute uphill walk" is a single estimate, not a per-street measurement. Route 10 comes from [Cornell's bus commuting page](https://sustainable.cornell.edu/focus-areas/transportation/bus-commuting). The Fall Creek bus time comes from a [Cornell grad research group's guide](https://reedgroup.github.io/GraduateStudentResources/LifeAtCornell.html) written in 2023.

**Collegetown.** Cornell says the dense undergrad population raises rent, noise and congestion. Housing mixes luxury complexes, average buildings, multi-unit houses and room rentals. A Cornell Daily Sun columnist wrote in [September 2025](https://www.cornellsun.com/article/2025/09/maia-on-the-mic-the-collegetown-housing-hustle) that many houses sit on steep hills that are hard to reach without a car, and put house rents at about $800 to $1,300 per person, many in poor shape.

**Fall Creek.** Mostly subdivided houses, single-unit houses and apartments over shops, within walking distance of shopping and parks, per Cornell. Reviewers of [Cliffside Terrace at 405 Lake St](https://www.veryapt.com/ApartmentReview-a16145-cliffside-terrace-apartments-ithaca) (five reviews, 7.4 out of 10; the four shown are all tagged Cornell Law, 2017 to 2022) praise a quiet building and repairs often done within a day. They complain of limited bus service and assigned parking spots taken by others.

**Downtown.** Flat, close to the Commons, and the end of Route 10. One caution from 2026: the City ordered the 181-unit Asteri building on the Commons vacated from March 4 to 20, 2026 over fire and safety concerns, the [Sun reported](https://www.cornellsun.com/article/2026/03/asteri-s-aftermath-displacement-dangerous-conditions-and-a-predatory-cash-for-keys-scheme). A new building is not proof of a safe one.

By our arithmetic, a Collegetown studio at $1,765 costs $370 more than downtown's $1,395 (26.5% above). A 1BR at $2,025 costs $400 more than $1,625 (24.6%), and a 2BR bed at $1,377.50 costs $385 more than $992.50 (38.8%). Seen from downtown, that is about 20% to 28% less. Only at 6BR does downtown cost more: $870 a bed against $800. Two caveats: the downtown rows are mostly 2026-27 leftovers that may be discounted, and the 1BR and 3BR samples are small. Heat, water and trash are included downtown; electricity is included only at the Colonial Building, Commons Studios and Whiton Hall.

Fall Creek has no per-bed list. A [1BR listing at 709 N Tioga St](https://offcampus.housing.cornell.edu/city/ithaca-college-ny/listing/cosy-quiet-comfortable-fall-creek-gem.-238258) asks $1,600 and $1,700 for two units, with internet and water paid by the landlord and electricity and garbage tags paid by the tenant. [Rentable](https://www.rentable.co/ithaca-ny/neighborhoods/fall-creek) lists a Fall Creek 1BR at $1,600, undated.

Example: three friends compare a Collegetown 3BR at Ithaca Renting's $1,243.75 mean per bed with a downtown 3BR at the $795 median. The gap is $448.75 a month each, $5,385 over 12 months. Downtown adds an OmniRide pass at $200 a year for daytime weekday rides. Each friend still keeps about $5,185, and the downtown share ($9,540 a year) sits $4,204 under the double.

## Getting to campus from each one

Collegetown is the only one of the three where walking always wins. From Fall Creek and downtown the walk is 20 to 30 minutes and uphill on the way in, so most residents mix walking with the bus.

Bus rules after your first year, per [Cornell Transportation](https://fcs.cornell.edu/departments/transportation-delivery-services/sustainable-transportation-options/bus-services-privileges-passes):

- First-year degree students, including grads and transfers, ride TCAT free at all hours until August 31 for fall entrants.
- After that, registered students ride free after 6 p.m. on weekdays and all day on weekends.
- A daytime weekday ride costs $1.50; the OmniRide pass costs $200 a year, prorated by month.
- PhD students and graduate assistants get the OmniRide pass free.

By our arithmetic, $200 buys 134 rides at $1.50, so a round trip every weekday pays off the pass in about 13 to 14 weeks. That is under one semester. Free all-day rides for upper-years ended in October 2024, when an ID-card glitch was fixed, the [Sun reported](https://cornellsun.com/2024/11/03/non-first-year-cornell-students-no-longer-able-to-access-tcat-for-free/). And no TCAT buses run from 1:30 to 4:00 a.m., per [Cornell's bus page](https://sustainable.cornell.edu/focus-areas/transportation/bus-commuting), so a late night downtown or in Fall Creek ends with a walk up or down the hill.

Not confirmed: which TCAT routes serve Fall Creek, and their times. Cornell's own list names only Route 10 for downtown.

## When each market signs

Collegetown's popular units go first. Leasing for the next year opens as students arrive: one student signed [before Labor Day weekend in 2022](https://cornellsun.com/2022/09/12/hurt-for-housing-begins-early-causing-stress-for-students/), two weeks after arriving. In every dated signing the Sun reported between 2017 and 2025, bar one fallback, students signed 8 to 16 months before move-in. One 12-bedroom house on Elmwood Ave was [signed in April 2017 for August 2018](https://cornellsun.com/2018/11/07/desirable-collegetown-leases-leave-market-up-to-16-months-before-residents-move-in/). The Ivy at 325 College Ave leased nearly all 52 units [before construction finished](https://www.cornellsun.com/article/2025/12/the-ivy-to-establish-new-luxury-housing-in-collegetown-opens-fall-2026). A developer said in 2016 that he [usually rents most units by the end of November](https://cornellsun.com/2016/09/26/landlords-say-collegetown-housing-hassle-unlikely-to-change/).

The rest of the market moves later. Cornell's Off-Campus Living manager called it a [myth that you must lock in housing the day you return](https://cornellsun.com/2019/02/06/students-and-landlords-both-perpetuate-myths-about-housing-university-panelist-says/) in fall. Both are true: Collegetown's best units go early, while downtown, Fall Creek and Lansing still have supply in spring.

## The other neighborhoods, and which side of the city line

Most searches widen past the big three. Cornell's walk times for some areas do not fit its own distances. At a 3 mph walking pace, which is our assumption, Belle Sherman's 1 to 2 miles take 20 to 40 minutes, not the "15-minute level walk" Cornell gives. Cayuga Heights' "up to 30 minutes" covers only about 1.5 of its 1 to 4 miles. Plan from the distance.

| Area | Distance (Cornell) | Walk, corrected where needed | City of Ithaca? | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Belle Sherman | 1 to 2 miles | 20 to 40 minutes at 3 mph | Not stated | Quieter, near East Hill Plaza |
| East Hill | 0.75 to 2 miles | 15 to 40 minutes at 3 mph | Partly; some in the Town | Newer complexes have in-unit laundry |
| Cayuga Heights, Cornell Heights | 1 to 4 miles | 20 to 80 minutes at 3 mph | Cayuga Heights is a separate village | Cornell Heights has many Greek houses |
| South Hill | 1.5 to 3 miles | 15 minutes downhill to downtown, then transit | Partly; some in the Town | Ithaca College students dominate |
| Northeast and Lansing | 4 miles; 5 to 10 miles | Bus or car | No (Lansing) | Lansing about 20 minutes by bus |
| West Hill | 4 miles | Not practical on foot | Not stated | Rural; cottages may be uninsulated |
| Varna | East on Dryden Rd | Bus or car | No | Ivy Ridge, 802 Dryden Rd |

Sources: [Cornell's neighborhood guide](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/campus-housing/housing-search-process/ithaca-neighborhoods) and the [grad guide](https://reedgroup.github.io/GraduateStudentResources/LifeAtCornell.html). Three warnings follow from them. Cornell says West Hill cottages are likely uninsulated with very high heating bills, and suggests asking NYSEG at (800) 572-1111 for the average annual heating cost. Lansing West at 700 Warren Rd showed 1BR units at [$1,038 and $1,193 in November 2026](https://www.rentcafe.com/apartments/ny/ithaca/lansing-west-apartments/default.aspx), cheap but about 20 minutes by bus. Ivy Ridge in Varna scores [6.4 out of 10 on seven reviews](https://www.veryapt.com/ApartmentReview-a23680-ivy-ridge-ithaca) by Johnson MBA students, who describe dripping mini-splits, leaking pipes and slow management.

The city line matters more than the neighborhood name. Inside the City, a landlord must give current tenants [120 days' written notice](https://wskg.org/ithaca-landlords-must-now-give-renters-120-days-before-renewing-leases/) before renewing, showing or re-letting a unit, unless the tenant initialed a waiver. City rentals also appear in Cornell's [safety tool](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/campus-living/housing-search-process/safe-living-environments), which shows sprinklers, alarms and construction type. Neither covers the Town of Ithaca, Lansing or Cayuga Heights. In the Town, a single-family home is limited to one family plus one boarder, a rule upheld in a [2017 case involving Ithaca College students](https://law.justia.com/cases/new-york/other-courts/2017/2017-ny-slip-op-30471-u.html), which can block a group house.

## Common mistakes

- **Choosing a neighborhood by distance alone.** Fall Creek and downtown are 20 to 30 minutes uphill on the way in. Without a pass, two daytime rides a weekday cost $3.00.
- **Comparing a per-bed price with a per-unit price.** Ithaca Renting's $1,377.50 Collegetown 2BR median is per bed; RentCafe's $3,414 Collegetown 2BR is the whole unit. Read the listing's unit before telling roommates.
- **Waiting until spring for a Collegetown house.** The big houses go first: 105 Dryden Court's 8BR and 214 Dryden Rd's 14BR were sold out for 2027-28 by 2026-11-08.
- **Rushing a downtown or Fall Creek lease in October.** That market has supply in spring, and an early signing ties you to a group that may shrink.
- **Renting in central Collegetown with a car and no spot.** The [Dryden Road Garage](https://www.cityofithacany.gov/718/Dryden-Road-Garage) monthly permit runs $2,784 over 12 months, on top of a [$359.77 B commuter permit](https://fcs.cornell.edu/departments/transportation-delivery-services/parking/parking-students) for campus.
- **Taking a West Hill cottage for the rent.** Cornell warns of very high heating bills; get the NYSEG history first.
- **Assuming a Lansing or Cayuga Heights lease has City protections.** The 120-day rule and the safety tool stop at the city line.

## FAQ

**Is Fall Creek or downtown quieter?** Cornell says neither about noise, but Fall Creek's Cliffside Terrace reviewers call it quiet, and grad forum posters steer people away from Collegetown. A [2018 College Confidential thread](https://talk.collegeconfidential.com/t/off-campus-housing-at-cornell-university/2002895) advised asking landlords what share of tenants are undergrads.

**Is June too late to find a place as a grad student?** Not outside Collegetown. [GradCafe posters in 2014](https://forum.thegradcafe.com/topic/51153-cornell-english-phd-help-with-housing-pretty-please) said June was not too late, and the grad guide says a few places remain in June and July.

**Can I park overnight on a Collegetown street?** Central Collegetown has no residential permits, per an [older Cornell item](https://news.cornell.edu/node/278473). A [City permit](https://www.cityofithacany.gov/731/Permit-Issuance) elsewhere is $45 a year, valid through July 31, and landlords cannot buy one for you.

**Where is the cheapest place to live alone?** Of the three, downtown and Fall Creek tie: Ithaca Renting's downtown 1BR median is $1,625 and the N Tioga St 1BR asks $1,600, against $2,025 in Collegetown. Farther out, Lansing West showed 1BRs at $1,038 and $1,193 in November 2026.

**Is Northside an option?** Not confirmed: no source covers Northside rents, transit or student reports.
