High Rise 5 is a tower for first-years on Cornell's North Campus. It opened in 1971 and houses [200-plus students](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/campus-housing/first-year-undergraduates/residence-halls/high-rise-5) in rooms from singles to quints. Rooms have no air conditioning, and each unit shares a kitchen.

## What sets High Rise 5 apart?

Cornell's configurations page lists High Rise 5 as corridor-style, with rooms along a shared hallway ([Cornell Housing](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/housing-and-residential-life-and-faculty-involvement-campus/residence-hall-configurations)).

Quints, rooms for five, exist here and in Jameson Hall and Clara Dickson Hall. The top floor holds a Skylounge that looks out over the golf course and Fuertes Observatory.

## What is living in High Rise 5 like?

It is a 1971 tower, and cooling is the issue. Cornell's hall page lists no air conditioning in rooms, which puts it with 12 North buildings in that group. In 2021 a High Rise 5 resident told The Cornell Daily Sun his hall should cost less because it lacked air conditioning, while a resident of another older hall said nobody should pay less for an older dorm ([Cornell Daily Sun](https://cornellsun.com/2021/09/10/housing-conditions-across-campus-are-they-fair/)). Cornell charges one rate for both.

Cornell's pages do not carry recent resident reviews of the building. If heat or noise matters, bring a fan and ask Housing for the current floor plan.

The wider rules for North halls sit in [North Campus dorms compared](/cornell/housing/cornell-north-campus-dorms-compared/).

## What does a room in High Rise 5 cost?

Cornell prices by room type, not by building, so High Rise 5 costs what the same room costs in a 2022 hall ([Cornell Housing rates](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/housing-and-residential-life-and-faculty-involvement-campus/rates-amenities-licenses/housing-rates)). Cornell's table posts no rate for quints. The rate covers gas, electricity, trash pickup and internet, and there is no security deposit. First-years also buy the Unlimited meal plan ([Cornell Dining](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/dining/meal-plans-rates/undergraduate-meal-plans)).

## How do you get a room in High Rise 5?

First-years apply in the Housing and Dining Portal within 10 days of getting a NetID. Staff assign halls by random auto-allocation, and you cannot rank them ([Cornell Housing](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/residential-programs/apply-housing/new-undergrad-application-process)). Ranking room types matters more, because a triple costs less than a double.

Signing the license commits you. First-years cannot cancel it unless they leave Cornell or win an exemption, and [this guide](/cornell/housing/cornell-residential-requirement-exemptions-live-off-campus/) explains the exemptions. Returning juniors and seniors can cancel a later license for a fee, as the [2027-28 license](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/housing-and-residential-life-and-faculty-involvement-campus/rates-amenities-licenses/academic-year-housing-license-2027-2028) sets out.

```reader "If you're staying over winter break"
High Rise 5 is open in 2026-27 but drops off Cornell's list for 2027-28, when Jameson Hall takes its place ([Cornell Housing](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/campus-housing/winter-break-housing)). Open-hall residents pay $125 for one week or $500 for the full break, and apply between Oct. 1 and Dec. 1.
```

```reader "If you're a parent reading the bill"
The hall does not change the rate. The one way to cut the housing bill is a triple, the lowest rate in Cornell's table. Ask your student what room type they listed.
```
