```glance
- Cornell's October directive stops fraternity parties and social events; it does not close houses or change housing rules.
- A chapter can house second-years only while Cornell recognizes it, so a loss of recognition ends the housing option.
- Chapters need a live-in advisor and four upper-level officers by Jan. 1 to be approved automatically.
- If a plan falls through, sign a lease early: Collegetown leases go 10 to 11 months before August.
```

If you plan to live in a fraternity house in 2027-28, Cornell's October 2026 suspension stops parties and social events but not housing. The rule that matters is recognition: a chapter can house second-years only while Cornell recognizes it ([Residential Policy](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/campus-housing/residential-policy)).

## What did Cornell suspend, and when?

On Oct. 13, 2026, President Michael I. Kotlikoff canceled all parties and social events in Interfraternity Council houses for the rest of 2026-27. Any fraternity that holds an in-house party or social event gets an immediate emergency suspension. The directive also starts a review to decide whether and how fraternity life continues ([Presidential Actions](https://scl.cornell.edu/get-involved/sorority-fraternity-life/policies-and-procedures/presidential-actions)). The Cornell Daily Sun reported the announcement the same day ([Cornell Daily Sun](https://www.cornellsun.com/article/2026/10/breaking-cornell-suspends-all-fraternity-parties-social-events-for-2026-27-school-year)).

Six days earlier, on Oct. 7, the Ithaca Common Council voted 9-1 for a temporary moratorium on new assembly, special-event, Commons and noise permits for Cornell groups, fraternities and sororities. It was set to expire Nov. 5 ([Ithaca Times](https://www.ithaca.com/news/ithaca/common-council-sets-moratorium-on-cornell-event-permits-following-gang-rape-allegations/article_6c84a345-d112-483a-98bc-316f3b64f0a1.html)). Ask the City of Ithaca whether it was extended. Separately, Cornell said the Chi Phi chapter at 107 Edgemoor Lane remains barred from campus ([Cornell Daily Sun](https://www.cornellsun.com/article/2026/09/university-releases-statement-on-alleged-gang-rape-at-chi-phi)).

## Does the suspension close chapter houses?

As reported, the directive covers parties and social events. It does not mention housing, and Cornell's residential policy still lists recognized fraternity and sorority houses as second-year housing. A chapter on interim suspension, Cornell's term for a pause in operations, cannot run activities other than the operation of its residence ([Cornell statement](https://statements.cornell.edu/2017/20170330-alpha-xi-delta.cfm)). The house stays open under that rule.

Cornell has not said how the review will end. Until it does, treat your chapter's recognition as the open question, not your room.

## What rules decide whether a chapter house still counts for 2027-28?

Four rules from the residential policy decide it:

1. The chapter must stay recognized. Recognized chapters earn automatic approval if they have a live-in advisor and four upper-level officers by Jan. 1 each year.
2. A sophomore must be a member when the housing application is filed.
3. You confirm the house plan on the housing application by March 15. If you miss it, you finish the requirement in a Cornell hall.
4. You still file the application. For 2027-28 it is due April 4 at 11:59 p.m. ([Spring Housing Selection](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/housing-and-residential-life-and-faculty-involvement-campus/apply-housing-campus/current-undergraduate-application-process/spring-housing-selection-rising-sophomores)).

The full path for a sophomore is in the [co-op and Greek house guide](/cornell/housing/cornell-co-ops-greek-houses-cost-how-to-join/).

## Which chapters are suspended, and when can they return?

The Organization Scorecard lists return dates for four IFC chapters: Phi Sigma Kappa and Zeta Psi in fall 2027, Phi Delta Theta in spring 2028 and Delta Tau Delta in spring 2029. It lists Chi Phi on interim suspension ([Organization Scorecard](https://scl.cornell.edu/get-involved/sorority-fraternity-life/organization-scorecard)). If your chapter is on that list, ask it in writing whether it can house members in 2027-28.

## What if your chapter house closes?

Sign a lease early. Collegetown leases in the Sun's record go 10 to 11 months before an August move-in, and Ithaca Renting marked 14 of 67 Collegetown rows for 2027-28 as sold out on Nov. 8, 2026 ([Ithaca Renting](https://ithacarenting.com/collegetown/)). A group whose Greek housing fell through in 2018 started hunting in February and found a house for 14 people ([Cornell Daily Sun, 2018](https://cornellsun.com/2018/11/07/desirable-collegetown-leases-leave-market-up-to-16-months-before-residents-move-in/)). That case shows a late search can work for a large group. It does not show it works for a small one.

If you are an underclass student who must live on campus, a lease does not remove the requirement. Check the [exemptions guide](/cornell/housing/cornell-residential-requirement-exemptions-live-off-campus/) before you sign. For leases in general, the [Collegetown lease timing guide](/cornell/housing/when-to-sign-collegetown-lease-cornell/) shows when to start.

```reader "If you're a first-year thinking about recruitment"
You live in a residence hall this year whatever you decide. Chapter housing matters in year two, and only if you are a member when the housing application is filed. Watch Cornell's review before you count on a house for 2027-28, and keep a hall or co-op as your plan B.
```

```reader "If you're a parent"
Chapter rent is billed mostly through the Bursar, Cornell's student account office, and each chapter sets its own charge ([Affiliated Housing](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/campus-living/qualified-off-campus-housing)). A closed house changes where your student sleeps, and it also changes who bills you, so ask the chapter treasurer how a closure would be handled before the first payment.
```

## Why are chapter houses under this much scrutiny?

Cornell has tied housing to conduct before. On May 4, 2018, President Martha Pollack banned hard alcohol in residential chapter houses and set a minimum three-year suspension for hazing that involves coerced alcohol or drugs, sexual misconduct or violence. The reforms also moved chapter leaders into the houses and required a full-time live-in advisor by fall 2021 ([Cornell Chronicle](https://news.cornell.edu/stories/2018/05/citing-health-and-safety-concerns-pollack-initiates-greek-life-reforms)). The Greek system then had 63 chapters and more than 4,500 students, nearly one-third of undergraduates.

A Dec. 18, 2019 letter added independent event monitors, nightly security spot checks and automatic interim suspension for risky violations ([Presidential Actions](https://scl.cornell.edu/get-involved/sorority-fraternity-life/policies-and-procedures/presidential-actions)). The 2026 directive follows that pattern: conduct rules change first and housing follows only if recognition changes.

## What to do next

1. Ask your chapter whether it expects to hold recognition at the start of the year.
2. Start a backup search only if the answer is no; otherwise keep the March and April dates above.
3. Keep the full [off-campus housing guide](/cornell/housing/cornell-off-campus-housing-guide/) handy for neighborhoods, rent and the lease calendar.
