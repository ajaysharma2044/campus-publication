## What is each building like?

**Cascadilla Hall** is Cornell's first residence hall. Cornell took over the 1864 building in June 1869. It has mixed-gender floors, a kitchen, a piano room and laundry, no room air conditioning, and it houses the service center for the Collegetown-area halls ([Cornell](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/campus-housing/upper-level-undergraduates/residence-halls/cascadilla-hall)).

**Sheldon Court** sits above Collegetown Bagels. Charles L. Sheldon Jr., class of 1901, operated it as "Cornell's First Dormitory," and Cornell bought it in 1955. It has singles and doubles on corridors, a TV lounge with a pool table, kitchens and no room air conditioning ([Cornell](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/campus-housing/upper-level-undergraduates/residence-halls/sheldon-court)). A 2026 Sun profile described five floors, a basement laundry with four washers and four dryers, an elevator that breaks down often, and many residents with a double to themselves. The nearest dining hall is about a 12-minute walk ([Cornell Daily Sun](https://www.cornellsun.com/article/2026/04/the-misunderstood-dorm-sheldon-court)).

**Schuyler House** has been a residence hall since 1981. It has mostly singles and a kitchen on each floor. Residents get a free TCAT bus pass, and Cornell lists a 15- to 20-minute walk to Central Campus ([Cornell](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/campus-housing/upper-level-undergraduates/residence-halls/schuyler-house)).

**301 College Ave.** has four floors of studios to six-bedroom units with kitchenettes holding a fridge and microwave, plus shared laundry ([Cornell](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/campus-housing/upper-level-undergraduates/residence-halls/301-college-avenue)). Cornell bought it for $15 million in March 2025 ([Cornell Daily Sun](https://www.cornellsun.com/article/2025/03/cornell-acquires-301-college-avenue-for-15-million-in-collegetown)). It opened with 43 students in August 2026, aiming for 105 by fall 2027. One resident called it incomplete and rushed, citing a fridge at 55 degrees, an unfinished lobby, no stoves and construction from 7:30 a.m. six days a week. Cornell says work continues through its service-request system, and that new flooring, sprinklers, alarms, card access and locks are done ([Cornell Daily Sun](https://www.cornellsun.com/article/2026/09/301-college-ave-opens-for-student-housing-resident-describes-it-as-incomplete-rushed)).

**112 Edgemoor** houses 20 residents in singles with shared single-sex bathrooms. From 2027 it becomes the home of Veterans House, a program house, though another Cornell page lists Veterans House at 625 University Ave. (Cornell). 109 McGraw Place is closed for 2026-27, and 104 Mary Ann Wood Drive had its floors, bathrooms and common areas renovated in 2026.

## What does a year cost?

All of these buildings use the same room rates as the residence halls. A double is $13,744 a year, and a single is $15,566 ([Cornell housing rates](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/housing-and-residential-life-and-faculty-involvement-campus/rates-amenities-licenses/housing-rates)). Residents of 112 Edgemoor, Cascadilla, Schuyler, Sheldon, 301 College Ave. and 104 Mary Ann Wood Drive can take the Collegetown Meal Plan, which is $7,056 a year with up to 10 meals a week, $500 in Big Red Bucks and $436 in City Bucks per semester, and four guest meals ([Cornell Dining](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/dining/meal-plans-rates/undergraduate-meal-plans)).

A double with that plan and the $50 fee is $20,850 (13,744 + 7,056 + 50). A single is $22,672. With Unlimited at $7,604 instead, the double is $21,398, so the Collegetown plan saves $548 ([Cornell housing rates](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/housing-and-residential-life-and-faculty-involvement-campus/rates-amenities-licenses/housing-rates)).

Set against a lease, a Collegetown 4-bedroom bed runs $14,955 a year in the [2027-28 rent dataset](/datasets/collegetown-rent-per-bed-2027-28/). The gap is $5,895 (20,850 - 14,955), or $491.25 a month, which is what food and electricity would have to cost to break even. For the full comparison, read [is off-campus cheaper than a Cornell dorm](/cornell/housing/is-off-campus-cheaper-than-cornell-dorm/).

## Who can live in them?

Cornell lists these buildings for transfer and upper-level students. 301 College Ave. and the South Campus halls form one area in fall selection for rising juniors and seniors. Application ran Sept. 1 to Sept. 27, 2026, with room selection on Oct. 6 and 7 (Cornell Housing). Students who qualify to live off campus can skip the halls: see the [residential requirement exemptions](/cornell/housing/cornell-residential-requirement-exemptions-live-off-campus/).

## What Cornell does not list

Cornell does not publish a list of which Greek chapter houses it owns. The Student & Campus Life organization scorecard shows an ownership label for each chapter, so check it for a specific house.
