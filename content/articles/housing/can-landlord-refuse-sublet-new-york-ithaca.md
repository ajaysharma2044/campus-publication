```glance
- Buildings with four or more residential units: consent to a sublet cannot be unreasonably withheld.
- Buildings with three or fewer units: your lease decides, and the landlord can write a flat no.
- Silence from the landlord after the statutory deadline counts as consent.
- A sublet done without the request steps is a substantial breach of the lease.
```

In a New York building with four or more residential units, a landlord cannot refuse a sublet without a reasonable cause ([Real Property Law 226-b](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/RPP/226-B)). In a building with three or fewer units, the lease controls and the landlord can say no.

## Who has a legal right to sublet in New York?

The right belongs to tenants in buildings with four or more residential units. The statute excludes public housing, units with statutory admission criteria and co-op proprietary leases. Count every residential unit in the building, not only the ones you can see.

| Situation | Who decides | Can the landlord refuse? |
|---|---|---|
| Four or more units | The statute, section 226-b | Only for a reasonable cause |
| Three or fewer units | [Your lease](https://sites.lawschool.cornell.edu/tenants-advocacy/breaking-a-lease) | Yes, if the lease allows |
| Public housing, statutory admission criteria, co-op proprietary lease | Program rules or the proprietary lease | Under those rules |

Source note: [RPL 226-b](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/RPP/226-B); [Cornell Law tenants guide](https://sites.lawschool.cornell.edu/tenants-advocacy/breaking-a-lease).

The law is state law, so it applies the same way in the City of Ithaca, the Town of Ithaca and Lansing. The [city and town comparison](/cornell/housing/city-of-ithaca-vs-town-lansing-cayuga-heights-renters/) shows which local rules differ. The statute dates to 1975, when the original version said a landlord who unreasonably withheld consent had to release the tenant ([Fordham Urban Law Journal](https://ir.lawnet.fordham.edu/ulj/vol9/iss3/16)). The current text gives the tenant a stronger remedy.

## What can a landlord do after you ask?

You ask by certified mail, with the term, the subtenant's name and addresses, your reason, your address during the sublet, any co-tenant or guarantor consent and a copy of the proposed sublease. The [how-to guide](/cornell/housing/cornell-summer-sublet-how-to-sublet-apartment-ithaca/) walks through each item and the timeline tool.

From there the landlord has two choices, and two clocks.

1. Ask for more information within 10 days of your request.
2. Answer within 30 days of the later of the request or the extra information.

If the landlord does not answer, the statute treats the silence as consent ([RPL 226-b](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/RPP/226-B)). A landlord who wants to refuse has to do it in writing inside that window and with a cause that holds up.

## What counts as an unreasonable refusal?

The statute does not list reasons that count as reasonable, and the Cornell Law guide does not define them either. The tenant's side and the landlord's side each have to argue the facts. If you get a refusal, call the Cornell Law Tenants Advocacy hotline at [607-301-1560](https://sites.lawschool.cornell.edu/tenants-advocacy/tenants-rights/) and read the letter to an adviser before you act.

Landlords sometimes publish policies that go further than the statute. Ithaca Renting's Collegetown page says sublets must be [registered and approved, go to Cornell students only, and run one per term](https://ithacarenting.com/collegetown/), with no Airbnb or stays under a month. Its [downtown policy](https://ithacarenting.com/downtown/) requires a two-month minimum and the agreement of all named tenants. A landlord's published policy is not the same as the statute. If a policy blocks a sublet in a building of four or more units, the question is whether the policy is a reasonable cause. The page does not answer that, and only a lawyer or a court can.

## What can you do if the refusal is unreasonable?

You have a remedy in a building of four or more units. The tenant can sublet as requested, and recover costs and attorney fees if the landlord acted in bad faith. A sublet without the request steps is a substantial breach, and the original tenant stays liable for the subtenant's rent and damage. A lease clause that waives these rights is void.

Act in this order:

1. Keep the certified-mail receipt and the refusal.
2. Call the hotline before you let anyone move in.
3. Ask the landlord in writing for the reason.
4. Sublet over the refusal only after an adviser confirms your case.

## How is an assignment different?

An assignment hands the whole lease to a new tenant. The owner can refuse an assignment without cause. If the refusal is unreasonable, your only remedy is release from the lease on 30 days' notice. After an assignment, the original tenant is not liable for the new tenant's rent or damage, which is why the Cornell Law guide calls assigning the better choice for someone who is not coming back. If you need out of the lease entirely, see [breaking a lease early](/cornell/housing/breaking-a-lease-early-ithaca-cornell/).

## Which beliefs about subletting are wrong?

**"A landlord can always refuse."** Not in a building with four or more units, where consent cannot be unreasonably withheld.

**"Once someone sublets, I am off the lease."** You stay liable. Cornell's tenant page says [original tenants often remain responsible](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/campus-living/living-campus) and tells you to get a written sublease.

**"Silence means no."** In a building covered by the statute, silence after the deadline means yes.

**"The landlord can charge any fee."** A fee is allowed only if the lease permits it and it is reasonable, and a background check is capped at [the lesser of $20 or the actual cost](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/RPP/238-A).

## What to do next

Count the units in your building. If the count is four or more, mail the request and keep the receipt. If it is three or fewer, ask the landlord in writing and read the lease clause. In either case, call the [tenants hotline](https://sites.lawschool.cornell.edu/tenants-advocacy/tenants-rights/) at 607-301-1560 before you act on a refusal.
