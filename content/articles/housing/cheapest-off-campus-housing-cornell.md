```glance
- Cheapest downtown: a 3BR share at a $795 median, $9,540 a year, $4,204 under Cornell's $13,744 double.
- Co-ops: the cheapest triple is $2,765 a semester and doubles run $6,974 to $9,962 a year (2026-27).
- Grad students: a Hasbrouck townhouse single is $620 a month, utilities included (2026-27).

- Never pay an application fee, cleaning fee or non-refundable deposit; a deposit plus prepaid rent caps at one month.
```

The cheapest way to live off campus at Cornell is a bed in a big shared unit: a 6BR or 10BR in Collegetown at $695 to $800 a month for 2027-28, or a downtown 3BR at a $795 median, per [Ithaca Renting's Collegetown](https://ithacarenting.com/collegetown/) and [downtown](https://ithacarenting.com/downtown/) listings read on Nov 8, 2026. A co-op room and, for grad students, a Hasbrouck townhouse single are cheaper still. Cornell aid is [calculated the same off campus and refunds what is left for living costs](https://finaid.cornell.edu/living-campus), but it does not cover the deposit, first and last month's rent, or summer.

```reader "If you're paying your own way or living on aid"
Your number to beat is $1,145 a month: Cornell's $13,744 double spread over 12 months, with utilities included. Only big shares beat it: a 6BR at $800, a 10BR at $775, a downtown 3BR at $795, or a co-op. A 4BR at the $1,246.25 median plus a $70 share of electricity runs $15,795 a year, $2,051 over the double. Plan cash for signing day: first month plus a deposit, neither paid by aid. On a 12-month lease, line up a sublet or savings for two or three summer months. Refuse any application or cleaning fee.
```

```reader "If you're a sophomore planning junior year"
You cannot sign for this year without an approved exemption; a sophomore who does is [still billed for the full year](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/campus-housing/requirements-qualified-university-recognized-campus-housing). For junior year, settle group size first, because it moves the price most: a 6BR at $800 a bed against a 4BR at $1,246.25 is $5,355 a year apart. The big Collegetown houses sell first: 105 Dryden Court (8BR) and 214 Dryden Rd (14BR) were already sold out for 2027-28 on Nov 8, 2026. A downtown 3BR is cheaper and can wait until spring. If you keep a backup Cornell room, [cancel by Jan 31 for $50](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/housing-details/rates-amenities-licenses/academic-year-housing-license-2027-2028); it costs $500 from Feb 1.
```

```reader "If you're a grad or professional student"
Cornell's 2026-27 Ithaca budget gives [$19,962 a year for housing](https://finaid.cornell.edu/graduate-students), about $1,663.50 a month. The cheapest Cornell bed is a regular single in a Hasbrouck 2BR townhouse at $620 a month on a 12-month contract, utilities and internet included. A Hasbrouck unfurnished 1BR is $1,415, which is $7,776 a year under the [RentCafe Ithaca 1BR average](https://www.rentcafe.com/average-rent-market-trends/us/ny/ithaca/) of $2,063. Off Cornell's list, Lansing West showed 1BRs at $1,038 and $1,193 in November 2026. PhD students and graduate assistants ride TCAT free; master's students pay $200 for OmniRide.
```

```reader "If you're in a co-op or Greek house"
You are already in some of the cheapest housing near campus. A co-op triple runs $2,765 to $3,972 a semester and a double $3,487 to $4,981 for 2026-27, against $6,872 for a residence hall double. Co-ops pick members in early November and early February. A second-year accepted to a co-op or Greek house before the Spring Housing Selection deadline can [cancel a Cornell license](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/housing-details/rates-amenities-licenses/academic-year-housing-license-2027-2028). Greek rent covers utilities, internet, repairs and building insurance, but not your belongings. Cornell publishes no Greek dollar figures.
```

```reader "If you're a parent paying the bills"
Compare per person, per year. At Ithaca Renting's 2027-28 Collegetown medians, a 6BR bed is $9,600 a year, a 4BR $14,955 and a 1BR alone $24,300, against $13,744 for a Cornell double with utilities. Off campus, the Bursar stops billing housing and your student pays the landlord; your family contribution does not change. The money at signing is yours to find: the deposit and first month are not covered by aid. A deposit plus any "last month" in advance [cannot exceed one month's rent](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/GOB/7-108).
```

## What makes a bed cheap: group size first

Per-bed rent falls as the unit gets bigger. At Ithaca Renting's 2027-28 Collegetown medians, a studio is $1,765 a bed, a 1BR $2,025, a 2BR $1,377.50, a 4BR $1,246.25 and a 6BR $800. Only 6BRs, 10BRs, rooms in shared suites and (on a two-row mean of $1,102.50) 5BRs come in under the double over 12 months.

The catch is the group. Every housemate on most local leases is ["jointly and severally" liable](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/campus-living/living-campus/study-abroad/housemate-compatibility-questionnaire) for the whole rent, so a 6BR needs six people who stay. A group that shrinks from a 6BR to a 4BR pays $446.25 more each a month.

The second lever is location. The same landlord's downtown beds run about 20% to 28% below its Collegetown prices: a studio is $1,395 against $1,765, a 1BR $1,625 against $2,025, a 2BR $992.50 against $1,377.50. The trade is a 20 to 30 minute uphill walk or a bus, per [Cornell's neighborhoods page](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/campus-housing/housing-search-process/ithaca-neighborhoods). After year one, Cornell students ride TCAT free only after 6 p.m. on weekdays and on weekends. Daytime rides cost $1.50 each, or $200 a year for the [OmniRide pass](https://fcs.cornell.edu/departments/transportation-delivery-services/sustainable-transportation-options/bus-services-privileges-passes).

Example: a junior with a $900 a month ceiling and no car. A downtown 3BR at $795 plus the $200 OmniRide pass comes to $9,740 a year. A Collegetown room in a shared suite at $895 is $10,740 with no bus fare. Downtown saves $1,000 a year if the walk or bus works for the schedule.

## Co-ops: the cheapest rooms near campus

Cornell owns eight co-ops: Prospect of Whitby, 660 Stewart, 302 Wait Avenue, Triphammer, Von Cramm, Wait Terrace, Wari and Watermargin. They [count as second-year housing](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/campus-living/qualified-off-campus-housing), and some take grad students. Each holds 10 to 35 members, and four have meal plans, per the [co-op page](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/campus-housing/upper-level-undergraduates/cooperative-housing).


A co-op double costs $6,974 to $9,962 for the academic year, $3,782 to $6,770 below the residence hall double. Red Bud in Von Cramm is the cheapest house, with a $3,949 single, $3,487 double and $2,765 triple.

Getting in takes timing. Selection runs in early November and early February, and each house runs its own process. The Sun [described in 2023](https://cornellsun.com/2023/03/06/underserved-and-minority-students-find-belonging-and-support-in-co-op-housing/) a "mosey" of events plus a lottery weighted by financial need and diversity factors. Questions go to co-ops@cornell.edu or 607-255-3445.

Know what you are buying. A 2024 Sun report said [floors had collapsed in four of the eight co-ops](https://cornellsun.com/2024/10/21/co-op-residents-face-deteriorating-houses-increasing-rent-costs/) in recent years and that Cornell had raised and standardized rents. Residents in that report still praised the community.

Greek houses are the other affiliated option. Cornell says their costs are [generally below residence-hall or off-campus costs](https://scl.cornell.edu/get-involved/sorority-fraternity-life/join/financial-obligations-sororities-fraternities), but gives no numbers. Rent there is mostly billed through the Bursar and covers housekeeping, utilities, internet, repairs and building insurance. Non-residents may owe "parlor" or living-out fees.

Not confirmed: dollar costs for any Cornell fraternity or sorority house.

## Hasbrouck and the grad market

Hasbrouck is Cornell's grad and professional housing, on 12-month contracts. For 2026-27, per the [rates page](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/housing-details/rates-amenities-licenses/housing-rates):

| Hasbrouck unit | Per month | Per year |
|---|---|---|
| Regular single in a 2BR townhouse | $620 | $7,440 |
| Large single in a 2BR townhouse, furnished | $910 | $10,920 |
| Studio | $1,355 | $16,260 |
| 1BR unfurnished | $1,415 | $16,980 |
| 1BR furnished | $1,490 | $17,880 |

Every rate includes gas, electricity, garbage and internet; most private rents do not. The cheapest private 1BRs in the brief are in Lansing. [Lansing West](https://www.rentcafe.com/apartments/ny/ithaca/lansing-west-apartments/default.aspx), at 700 Warren Rd, showed 1BR units at $1,038 and $1,193 in November 2026, with rent free until January 2027 for applicants by Nov 14 who move in by Dec 1.

## What financial aid covers off campus

Cornell's [Living Off Campus page](https://finaid.cornell.edu/living-campus) sets the rules:

| Aid covers or does | Aid does not cover |
|---|---|
| Need is calculated the same as on campus | Security deposits |
| Family contribution stays the same | First and last month's rent |
| Bursar stops billing housing; you pay the landlord | Rent in months you are not registered (summer) |
| A refund for living costs when aid exceeds Bursar charges | A mid-year lease break (aid will not intervene) |

The page tells students to find housing that costs less over 12 months than Cornell's off-campus housing and dining budget. It also says to count trash fees and utilities, with no figures.

Not confirmed: a dollar figure for that off-campus budget. Cornell's [cost-of-attendance page](https://finaid.cornell.edu/cost-attend) lists $13,744 for housing and $7,604 for food for 2025-26, with no separate off-campus line. Ask Financial Aid at 203 Day Hall, 607-255-5145.

The gap is cash at signing and in summer. Example: a student signs a 12-month downtown 3BR at $795 starting Aug 1. At signing, first month plus a one-month deposit is $1,590, none of it from aid. The refund check arrives once the term's aid posts. June and July rent, $1,590 more, also falls outside aid. Total out of pocket before any refund: $3,180.

The cheapest fix for summer is a sublet. In a building of four or more units, a tenant [may sublet with the landlord's consent](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/RPP/226-B), which cannot be unreasonably withheld; in smaller houses the lease controls. The steps are in the [sublet guide](/housing/cornell-summer-sublet-how-to-sublet-apartment-ithaca/).

A cheap bed is not a cheap year if the extras pile up. These are the ones the brief can price:

Electricity: [EnergySage](https://www.energysage.com/local-data/electricity-cost/ny/tompkins-county/ithaca/) puts the Ithaca household average at about $280 a month, which is $70 each split four ways. It is an upper bound: a self-selected household average, likely including electric heat. Ithaca Renting includes heat.

Trash: City tags cost [$6 each from Jan 1, 2026](https://www.cityofithacany.gov/168/Trash-Yard-Waste-Collection-Tags). Insurance: [SafeButler](https://www.safebutler.com/cheap-best-renters-insurance-ithaca-ny) shows Ithaca quotes from about $5 a month, and [Insure.com](https://www.insure.com/renters-insurance/average-renters-insurance-cost-in-new-york/) puts a New York average at $328 a year, about $27 a month.

Meals change the math the other way. First- and second-years in Cornell housing [must have a meal plan](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/campus-housing/residential-policy); a West Campus unlimited plan was [$6,434 a year in 2021](https://cornellsun.com/2021/11/09/as-cornell-requires-university-housing-for-sophomores-students-weigh-options/). Off campus, you cook. Students in that 2021 Sun report, and parents on [College Confidential in 2016](https://talk.collegeconfidential.com/t/upperclassmen-housing-at-cornell/1862613), described off campus as cheaper and roomier than a dorm with a meal plan; one alumna in that thread still called her houses "dumps".

## Cheap but cold: West Hill and far-out places

West Hill, across the inlet, is about 4 miles out, rural and not practical on foot. Cornell's [neighborhoods page](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/campus-housing/housing-search-process/ithaca-neighborhoods) warns that its "cottages" are likely uninsulated with very high heating bills. Before signing, call NYSEG at (800) 572-1111 for the unit's average annual heating cost.

Lansing is cheaper, with larger complexes, but sits 5 to 10 miles out, and Cornell recommends a bus or car.

## Fees you never pay

New York law takes a lot of money off the table. [RPL 238-a](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/RPP/238-A) bars any charge for processing or accepting an application. A background or credit check costs the lesser of actual cost or $20, and is waived if you hand over a check from the past 30 days. Late fees start only after rent is 5 days late, capped at the lesser of $50 or 5%.

[Non-refundable deposits are unlawful](https://sites.lawschool.cornell.edu/tenants-advocacy/security-deposits), and so is a cleaning fee separate from the deposit, per the Cornell Law guide. The deposit plus any rent paid in advance [cannot exceed one month's rent](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/GOB/7-108). The landlord must return it with an itemized statement within 14 days of move-out, or lose the right to keep any of it.

## Common mistakes

- **Taking a Collegetown 1BR alone.** At the $2,025 median, that is $24,300 a year, $10,556 above the double.
- **Counting on aid for signing day.** The deposit and first month, $1,590 on a $795 bed, come from savings or family.
- **A 12-month lease with no summer plan.** Two or three months of rent fall outside aid: $1,600 to $2,400 on an $800 bed.
- **A group that shrinks.** Dropping from a 6BR to a 4BR adds $446.25 a person a month, $5,355 a year, and everyone stays liable for the full rent.
- **Paying a banned fee.** Application, cleaning and non-refundable fees are illegal; a deposit over one month's rent is too.
- **Renting a cheap West Hill cottage without the heating history.** Cornell warns these are likely uninsulated with very high bills.
- **A sophomore signing early.** Without an approved exemption you are billed $13,744 for the Cornell year on top of the rent.

## FAQ

**Is a co-op cheaper than any apartment?** Usually. The cheapest co-op triple is $5,530 for two semesters, below any private bed above, though only four co-ops include meals ([Cornell rates](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/housing-details/rates-amenities-licenses/housing-rates)).

**Can I get my aid refund before rent is due in August?** The refund is issued when aid exceeds Bursar charges for the term ([Cornell Financial Aid](https://finaid.cornell.edu/living-campus)); a lease starting Aug 1 usually asks for money before that, so plan the first payment from savings.

**Is a 10-month lease better than a 12-month one?** Compare the year, not the month. The double works out to $1,374 a month over 10 months but $1,145 over 12, so a 10-month bed priced up to $1,374 still matches it.

**Will Cornell's rates go up for 2027-28?** The rates shown at fall selection are projected; final 2027-28 rates are [posted in March 2027](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/housing-details/rates-amenities-licenses/academic-year-housing-license-2027-2028) after Board of Trustees approval.

**Can my landlord charge me for a credit check?** Yes, up to the lesser of actual cost or $20, and nothing if you provide a check from the past 30 days ([RPL 238-a](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/RPP/238-A)).
