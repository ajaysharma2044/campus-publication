If you sign as guarantor on an Ithaca lease, you owe whatever the guarantee document says you owe. So get the form first. Then read it beside the lease's [joint liability clause](/cornell/housing/cornell-off-campus-roommates-joint-liability/), which can make one student answerable for the whole unit.

## What is a guarantor on a lease?

A guarantor is a person who agrees in writing to pay if the tenant does not. The tenant lives in the unit. The guarantor does not. The lease and the guarantee are two documents, and each has its own wording.

Some Ithaca landlords make a parent guarantee a condition of renting. Ithaca Renting's [Collegetown rental page](https://ithacarenting.com/collegetown/) says each tenant's parent or legal guardian must complete its Lease Guaranty form, for graduate and undergraduate tenants regardless of age, and that it does not run other reference or credit checks. The same page says no keys are issued until the signed guarantee is in. The scope of the guarantee comes from the landlord's own form.

## What could I be liable for?

Start with what your student owes. Cornell says most local leases bind housemates ["jointly and severally"](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/living-off-campus/tenant-best-practices/study-abroad/housemate-compatibility-questionnaire): each is responsible for the others' rent and damages for the whole term. The Cornell Law guide adds that each tenant can be held for the full rent, and a tenant who overpays can sue a co-tenant for the difference.

A guarantee can cover less than that, the same, or more. Only the document tells you. Ask these questions in writing:

1. Does it cover only your child's share of the rent, or every tenant's?
2. Does it cover damage and unpaid utilities, or rent alone?
3. Does it end on a date, or run through any renewal?
4. What happens to it if the landlord releases your child from the lease?

The [lease reading guide](/cornell/housing/how-to-read-ithaca-lease-before-signing/) explains the clauses these answers should match.

## Does the landlord have to cut my exposure if the tenant leaves?

New York law gives the landlord a duty to limit the loss. Under [RPL 227-e](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/RPP/227-E), a landlord whose tenant leaves early must take reasonable and customary steps in good faith to re-rent, at the lower of fair market value or the lease rate. A new lease ends the old one and offsets damages, and the burden of proof sits with the party seeking damages. A waiver of this duty is void.

That rule governs the tenant's liability. Whether it reduces what a guarantor owes depends on the guarantee's wording, so ask a lawyer or the Cornell Law hotline, below. The [breaking a lease guide](/cornell/housing/breaking-a-lease-early-ithaca-cornell/) covers the tenant's side.

## Does a sublet change my exposure?

It can involve you directly. A sublet request in a building of four or more units must go by certified mail, and it must include the [written consent of any cotenant or guarantor](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/RPP/226-B). The original tenant stays liable after a sublet. If your child goes abroad and sublets, expect to be asked to sign.

An assignment, which hands the whole lease to someone else, works differently. After an assignment the original tenant is released from the new tenant's rent and damage in the usual case, per [Cornell Law's summary of the statute](https://sites.lawschool.cornell.edu/tenants-advocacy/breaking-a-lease). Whether your guarantee follows the lease to the new tenant is a question for the guarantee's text.

## Does the guarantee follow a renewal?

Ask this one by name. Under [Cornell Law's lease renewal guide](https://sites.lawschool.cornell.edu/tenants-advocacy/lease-renewal), an expired lease continues month to month unless the landlord gives proper non-renewal notice. A guarantee that runs for "the tenancy" could run through every month of that holdover. A guarantee that names the lease term and its end date stops when the term does. Choose the second wording and have it written in.

Cornell Law's guide also says written-changes clauses bar oral changes, so a landlord's phone promise to narrow your guarantee does not count. Get any change on paper, signed by both sides.

## What does the money at signing look like?

A deposit plus any rent advance cannot exceed one month's rent, under [General Obligations Law 7-108](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/GOB/7-108). Cornell aid does not cover security deposits, first and last month's rent, or rent in unregistered months, per [Cornell Financial Aid](https://finaid.cornell.edu/living-campus). Many parents front that cash. Use the checker to test a landlord's demand against the cap.

## What should I do before I sign?

1. Ask the landlord for the guarantee form separately from the lease.
2. Compare its wording with the lease's liability clause, term and renewal terms.
3. Keep the signed copy and written receipts, as Cornell's [checklist](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/living-off-campus/housing-search-process/checklists-and-considerations) advises.
4. Call the Cornell Law Tenants Advocacy hotline at [607-301-1560](https://sites.lawschool.cornell.edu/tenants-advocacy/tenants-rights/) for free advice on the lease.

The hotline advises on leases. For a close review of the guarantee's wording, a private lawyer can read it with you.

## What if the landlord says the guarantee is standard?

Ask for it in writing anyway. A landlord who calls a form standard can still show it to you. If the wording is broader than the lease, ask to narrow it to your child's share. If the landlord refuses, that is a fact you can weigh against other units. Cornell's [deposit guide](https://sites.lawschool.cornell.edu/tenants-advocacy/security-deposits) shows how much of the rest of the lease the law already limits, and a guarantee is a place where the law gives you less to lean on.
