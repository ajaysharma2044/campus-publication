```glance
- About 95% of Cornell grad and professional students rent off campus; the market for them opens in March and April for an August start.
- Hasbrouck, Cornell's grad housing: $1,415 a month for an unfurnished 1BR in 2026-27, gas, electricity and internet included, $7,776 a year below the Ithaca 1BR average.
- Best fits: Fall Creek (1BRs at $1,600 and $1,700 on one 2026 listing), downtown ($1,625 median 1BR at one landlord), East Hill, or Lansing by bus.
- Collegetown costs most ($2,025 median 1BR for 2027-28) and is mostly undergrads.
- PhD students and graduate assistants ride TCAT free all year; master's students pay $1.50 a daytime ride or $200 a year after year one.
- Cornell's 2026-27 grad housing budget is $19,962 a year, which is $1,663.50 a month by our division.
```

Start looking in March or April for an August lease, and look in Fall Creek, downtown, East Hill or Cornell's own Hasbrouck Apartments rather than Collegetown. A Hasbrouck 1BR costs [$1,415 a month unfurnished in 2026-27](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/housing-details/rates-amenities-licenses/housing-rates) with utilities and internet included, which beats most private 1BRs. Choose by commute: if you are a PhD student or graduate assistant, the bus is free, so distance costs you time, not money.

```reader "If you're a PhD student or graduate assistant"
Cornell gives you [free TCAT OmniRide passes](https://fcs.cornell.edu/departments/transportation-delivery-services/sustainable-transportation-options/bus-services-privileges-passes), so Fall Creek, downtown and Lansing cost you bus time, not fares. Cornell budgets your housing at $19,962 a year for 2026-27, about $1,663.50 a month. A Hasbrouck unfurnished 1BR at $1,415 or a downtown 1BR near $1,625 fits; a Collegetown 1BR near $2,025 does not. Start in March, apply to Hasbrouck on its rolling list at the same time, and ask every landlord what share of tenants are undergrads. The costly mistake is signing in Collegetown for the short walk and living above parties.
```

```reader "If you're coming with a partner or children"
Hasbrouck family units run [$1,475 to $1,530 a month in 2026-27](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/housing-details/rates-amenities-licenses/housing-rates) for a 2BR townhouse, or $1,500 for a third-floor 2BR apartment, on 12-month contracts. They hold [2 to 4 people, no more than two adults](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/campus-housing/graduate-professional-student-campus-housing). The private Ithaca 2BR average is [$2,459 a month](https://www.rentcafe.com/average-rent-market-trends/us/ny/ithaca/) (RentCafe, October 2026). A spouse or partner with a CU ID can buy the OmniRide pass at 116 Maple Ave. For schools, Cornell points families to the [Ithaca City School District and worklife@cornell.edu](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/campus-housing/graduate-professional-students/your-cornell-community). Not confirmed: which neighborhoods feed which schools. Do not wait for a family unit to turn up in June.
```

```reader "If you're an international grad student"
Cornell International Services says [you don't need a US Social Security number to rent an apartment](https://international.globallearning.cornell.edu/life-cornell/faq-international-students). New York caps a deposit plus any rent paid in advance at [one month's rent](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/GOB/7-108), so question a demand for six months up front. On the [Cornell marketplace](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/campus-housing/housing-search-process/rental-listings-resources), only people who prove Cornell affiliation can view and contact landlords. Hasbrouck is the simplest sight-unseen option: one bill, utilities included. Cornell Graduate School staff say a landlord dispute [does not affect your visa or standing](https://www.gradschool.cornell.edu/announcements/grad-tips-renting/). New degree students ride TCAT free all of year one.
```

```reader "If you're a postdoc, visiting scholar or here for one semester"
Cornell does not guarantee housing for visiting scholars. It points them to the International Scholar House, a [month-to-month furnished 5BR open since September 2023](https://cornellsun.com/2024/11/12/high-costs-and-limited-support-plague-visiting-scholars-and-fellows-at-cornell). Hasbrouck takes rolling applications, but its contracts run 12 months. For a semester, look at Ithaca Renting's "Spring/Fall Semester 2027" rows on its [Collegetown list](https://ithacarenting.com/collegetown/) and its downtown "available now" units. The costly mistake is a 12-month lease for a six-month stay. You owe the remaining months unless the unit is sublet or re-rented. New York's [RPL 227-e](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/RPP/227-E) makes the landlord try to re-rent.
```

```reader "If you're bringing a car"
Graduate assistants on full Ithaca assistantships can buy [faculty and staff parking permits](https://fcs.cornell.edu/departments/transportation-delivery-services/parking/parking-students); students living in university housing can buy only the R or WE permits. Other grads buy the B commuter permit at $359.77 a year if eligible; it is limited and first come, first served. A car pushes you toward Lansing, East Hill or Fall Creek with an off-street spot, not central Collegetown. From November 1 to April 1, City streets switch to [odd/even overnight parking](https://www.cityofithacany.gov/727/Parking-Regulations-Violations) when needed, and a car left unmoved for 72 hours may be towed. Reviewers at Cliffside Terrace and Ivy Ridge both complain about parking, so ask whether your spot is assigned.
```

```reader "If you're living without a car"
Pick by hill and bus, not miles. Cornell puts Fall Creek and downtown [0.5 to 1.5 miles away but a 20 to 30 minute uphill walk](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/campus-housing/housing-search-process/ithaca-neighborhoods); Lansing is 5 to 10 miles and needs the bus. In year one every new degree student rides TCAT free. After that, master's and professional students ride free only after 6 p.m. on weekdays and on weekends; daytime rides cost [$1.50](https://cornellsun.com/2024/11/03/non-first-year-cornell-students-no-longer-able-to-access-tcat-for-free/) or $200 a year for OmniRide. No buses run from [1:30 to 4:00 a.m.](https://sustainable.cornell.edu/focus-areas/transportation/bus-commuting), so a late lab night from Lansing ends on foot or in a car. For groceries, Route 15 runs to Wegmans.
```

## Where grad students live, by neighborhood

[About 95% of graduate and professional students](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/campus-housing/graduate-professional-student-campus-housing) live in housing Cornell does not own; about 400 live on campus. From 2014 to 2023, three sources give grads the same advice: skip Collegetown, look at Fall Creek, downtown and East Hill. [GradCafe posters in 2014](https://forum.thegradcafe.com/topic/51153-cornell-english-phd-help-with-housing-pretty-please) called Collegetown loud and undergraduate. The Reed Group's [grad guide](https://reedgroup.github.io/GraduateStudentResources/LifeAtCornell.html) sends readers to Fall Creek, East Hill and Lansing.

| Neighborhood | Distance and walk (Cornell's estimate) | Asking rent seen | Grad fit |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fall Creek | 0.5 to 1 mile; 20 to 30 min uphill; about 15 min by bus | 1BR $1,600 and $1,700 at 709 N Tioga St (2026) | Quiet, popular with grads; groceries need a bus or car |
| Downtown and the Commons | 0.5 to 1.5 miles; 20 to 30 min uphill | 1BR median $1,625 per month; 2BR $992.50 per bed (Ithaca Renting, Nov 2026) | Restaurants, Route 10 to campus |
| East Hill and Belle Sherman | 0.75 to 2 miles | Not confirmed | Quieter; newer complexes such as Maplewood have in-unit laundry |
| Lansing and Northeast | 4 to 10 miles; bus or car; about 20 min by bus | Lansing West 1BR $1,038 and $1,193 "now" units (Nov 2026) | Cheapest per unit; car helps |
| Collegetown | 0.1 to 0.5 miles; up to 15 min | 1BR median $2,025 per month for 2027-28 (Ithaca Renting) | Closest; mostly undergrads; signs a year ahead |

**Fall Creek.** Cornell calls it [popular with grad students](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/campus-housing/housing-search-process/ithaca-neighborhoods), with lower rents, subdivided houses and a frequent bus. On the Cornell marketplace, 709 N Tioga St listed [two 1BRs at $1,600 and $1,700](https://offcampus.housing.cornell.edu/city/ithaca-college-ny/listing/cosy-quiet-comfortable-fall-creek-gem.-238258) in 2026, with internet and water included and electricity and garbage tags on the tenant. The grad guide puts the bus at about 15 minutes from Hollister Hall.

**Downtown.** Ithaca Renting's downtown per-bed medians are [$1,395 for a studio and $1,625 for a 1BR](https://ithacarenting.com/downtown/), mostly 2026-27 rows read November 8, 2026. Heat, water and trash are included; electricity is included only at the Colonial Building, Commons Studios and Whiton Hall. Sharing a downtown 2BR at the $992.50 median comes to $11,910 a year each.

**Lansing.** Lansing West at 700 Warren Rd showed 1BR "now" units at [$1,038 and $1,193](https://www.rentcafe.com/apartments/ny/ithaca/lansing-west-apartments/default.aspx) in November 2026, other 1BRs from $1,599, and a $20 application fee. New York [bans application fees](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/RPP/238-A) and caps a background check at $20, so ask what that charge covers before paying.

**Collegetown.** Ithaca Renting's Collegetown 1BRs run a [$2,025 median for 2027-28](https://ithacarenting.com/collegetown/), and 14 of 67 rows were already sold out on November 8, 2026. An incoming MEng student weighing Collegetown Center against Collegetown Terrace was told on [College Confidential](https://talk.collegeconfidential.com/t/off-campus-housing-at-cornell-university/2002895) that Center was closer but mostly undergrads, and to ask each landlord for the undergrad share.

Ask Maplewood which before comparing.

## When to start looking

Grad leasing runs months behind Collegetown's undergraduate rush. The grad guide says to [start March to April for an August lease](https://reedgroup.github.io/GraduateStudentResources/LifeAtCornell.html), and a few places remain in June and July. The 2014 GradCafe posters said June was not too late because year-long leases start at the end of July. Collegetown's popular units sign 8 to 16 months ahead.

A Hasbrouck 1BR at the 2026-27 rate of $1,415 is $185 a month cheaper than a $1,600 Fall Creek unit before the power bill, and the bus is free either way.

## Getting to campus: the bus pass and parking

Every new degree student, grad included, rides TCAT free in year one; for fall entrants that [ends August 31 of the following year](https://fcs.cornell.edu/departments/transportation-delivery-services/sustainable-transportation-options/bus-services-privileges-passes), and for spring entrants December 31 of the same year. After that, PhD students and graduate assistants get free OmniRide passes. Everyone else rides free only after 6 p.m. on weekdays and all weekend. An ID-card glitch that gave students all-day free rides was fixed in late 2024, the [Sun reported](https://cornellsun.com/2024/11/03/non-first-year-cornell-students-no-longer-able-to-access-tcat-for-free/).

The OmniRide pass is $200 a year, prorated monthly, sold at myparking.cornell.edu. At $1.50 a ride, it pays off at 134 rides, our division: about 13 to 14 weeks of two daytime rides each weekday, under one semester.

Example: a second-year master's student in Fall Creek rides up and back every weekday before 6 p.m. Over 30 weeks that is 300 rides, or $450. The $200 pass saves $250.

Parking: the B commuter permit is [$359.77 a year](https://fcs.cornell.edu/departments/transportation-delivery-services/parking/parking-students), valid August 16 to August 15, refunded prorated if returned by March 31. Graduate assistants on full assistantships can buy faculty and staff permits instead. Not confirmed: whether a GA who rents off campus gets a faculty and staff A or E permit at no fee; ask Transportation Services at 607-255-4600.

## What grad tenants say about two buildings

Only two grad-heavy buildings have enough reviews to read:

- **Cliffside Terrace, 405 Lake St, Fall Creek.** Five reviews, four displayed, all tagged as Cornell Law students, January 2017 to October 2022, averaging [7.4 out of 10](https://www.veryapt.com/ApartmentReview-a16145-cliffside-terrace-apartments-ithaca). They praise repairs often done within a day, spacious units, quiet and pets. They complain the building is farther out, bus service is limited, assigned spots get taken, floors are thin and minor pests appear.
- **Ivy Ridge, 802 Dryden Rd, Varna.** Seven reviews from Johnson MBA students, November 2019 to October 2025, averaging [6.4 out of 10](https://www.veryapt.com/ApartmentReview-a23680-ivy-ridge-ithaca). They like the Arboretum next door, pets and furnished options. They report dripping mini-split units, leaking pipes, slow management and limited parking.

Varna, Lansing, Cayuga Heights and the Town of Ithaca parts of East Hill sit outside the City of Ithaca. The City's 120-day renewal-notice rule and its Certificate of Compliance inspections stop at the city line.

## Common mistakes

- **Signing in Collegetown for the walk.** A Collegetown 1BR runs about $4,300 a year over Cornell's housing budget, among undergrads.
- **Comparing Hasbrouck with a bare rent.** Hasbrouck's $1,415 includes gas, electricity and internet; a $1,600 Fall Creek 1BR does not include electricity.
- **Comparing a per-bed price with a per-unit price.** Ithaca Renting quotes per bed; RentCafe quotes per unit. A $992.50 downtown 2BR price is each person's share, not the whole unit.
- **Assuming master's students ride free like PhD students.** After year one, daytime rides cost $1.50; a weekday commuter spends about $450 a year without the $200 pass.
- **Waiting until July for a family unit.** The Sun reported in 2024 that family housing is scarce with high upfront costs. At signing, a $2,459 2BR can cost $4,918: first month plus a one-month deposit, our sum.
- **Expecting Cornell to step into a landlord dispute.** Off-Campus Living gives advice and consultations; staff say to [contact it as soon as a problem starts](https://www.gradschool.cornell.edu/announcements/grad-tips-renting/), but it does not represent students.

## FAQ

**Can my partner live with me if only I am on the lease?** Yes. New York lets a single tenant live with immediate family plus one more occupant and that person's dependent children, and you must give the landlord the occupant's name [within 30 days](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/RPP/235-F) of move-in or of being asked.

**How many grad households on campus are families?** Of grad students living on campus, [51% are family or partner households](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/campus-housing/graduate-professional-students/your-cornell-community) and 49% single students.

**Is Lansing too far without a car?** Not with the bus: the grad guide puts it at about 20 minutes by bus, and Cornell names [Route 30 to Ithaca Mall](https://sustainable.cornell.edu/focus-areas/transportation/bus-commuting) in that direction. The risk is the 1:30 to 4:00 a.m. gap in service.

**Do grad students have to live on campus their first year?** No. Cornell's residential requirement covers undergraduates, and about 95% of grad and professional students live off campus.

**Can I find something in June?** Usually, outside Collegetown. The grad guide says a few places remain in June and July; March and April offer more.
