```glance
- Unpaid rent: a certified notice, then a 14-day written demand, then a court petition served 10 to 17 days before the hearing.
- At the hearing you can usually ask for one 14-day adjournment; a judge can delay an eviction up to a year for hardship.
- After a judgment: 14 days' notice before removal, on business days only, between sunrise and sunset.
- Non-renewal: in covered City buildings, Good Cause (from July 2024) means the landlord needs a legal reason.
- City vacate order: landlords owe displaced tenants double the monthly rent, prorated per day (2019 rule).
- Day one: LawNY (607) 273-3667 or the tenant hotline (607) 301-1560, before you sign or pay anything.
```

An Ithaca eviction is a court case with fixed steps, set out in the [Cornell Law tenants' guide](https://sites.lawschool.cornell.edu/tenants-advocacy/eviction-proceedings). For unpaid rent that means a certified notice, a 14-day written demand, a hearing, and 14 days' notice before removal, and a judge can delay it up to a year for hardship. If the City orders your building vacated, a [2019 City rule](https://www.cornellsun.com/article/2019/03/city-council-votes-to-stiffen-penalties-for-noncompliant-landlords) makes the landlord owe you double the monthly rent, prorated per day, so call for free advice before you sign any cash offer.

```reader "If you're a junior or senior already renting"
Your risk is usually one of two cases. A nonpayment case starts with a 14-day written rent demand, and withholding rent over repairs invites one; the [Cornell Law guide](https://sites.lawschool.cornell.edu/tenants-advocacy/building-issues-maintenance) calls withholding a last resort. A holdover starts when the landlord will not renew. If your building is covered by Good Cause, the landlord needs a legal ground, and a refusal within a year of a good-faith repair complaint can be defended as [retaliation](https://sites.lawschool.cornell.edu/tenants-advocacy/lease-renewal). If the City orders you out, do not sign a release for cash before calling (607) 301-1560.
```

```reader "If you're an international student"
A landlord dispute does not affect your visa or standing, according to [Cornell Graduate School staff](https://www.gradschool.cornell.edu/announcements/grad-tips-renting/). With a lease, you cannot be evicted for your immigration status unless there is a substantial lease or code violation, per the [immigrant tenant rights page](https://sites.lawschool.cornell.edu/tenants-advocacy/immigrant-tenant-rights). A landlord may not harass you by cutting heat or hot water. The tenant hotline at (607) 301-1560 gives advice with no income or geographic limits. Full representation is only for City of Ithaca residents, per [Cornell International Services](https://international.globallearning.cornell.edu/maintaining-your-status/legal-resources). The costly mistake is leaving early out of fear: housemates stay liable, and you can owe rent until the landlord [re-rents the unit](https://sites.lawschool.cornell.edu/tenants-advocacy/breaking-a-lease).
```

```reader "If you're living with a spouse, partner or children"
The hardship stay matters most to you. After a judgment, a judge may delay the eviction for up to one year, and the [Cornell Law guide](https://sites.lawschool.cornell.edu/tenants-advocacy/eviction-proceedings) lists it among the tools a tenant can ask for, so go to the hearing and explain your children and school. A family on one stipend should also ask LawNY about rental assistance. If the City orders your building vacated, the landlord owes double rent prorated per day under a [2019 City rule](https://www.cornellsun.com/article/2019/03/city-council-votes-to-stiffen-penalties-for-noncompliant-landlords). Call LawNY at (607) 273-3667 for free housing help before you move anyone out.
```

```reader "If you're a grad student"
If you plan to renew for several years, Good Cause is your main protection. In a covered building, a landlord who refuses to renew needs a legal ground, and an increase above the lower of 10% or 5% plus CPI is presumed unreasonable, per [NYS Homes and Community Renewal](https://hcr.ny.gov/good-cause-eviction). Buildings with a certificate of occupancy from 2009 or later are exempt for 30 years. Do not expect Cornell to step in: Off-Campus Living [does not represent students](https://cornellsun.com/2019/02/06/students-and-landlords-both-perpetuate-myths-about-housing-university-panelist-says/) in landlord disputes. The Cornell Law hotline at (607) 301-1560 does give advice.
```

```reader "If you're a parent"
If you co-signed or pay the rent, a nonpayment case is the one to stop early. The landlord must send a 14-day written demand before filing, and paying within those 14 days ends it before court, per the [Cornell Law guide](https://sites.lawschool.cornell.edu/tenants-advocacy/eviction-proceedings). Ask your student to forward any certified letter the day it arrives. If the City orders the building vacated, the landlord owes double the monthly rent per day of displacement, and you should not let your student sign a cash-for-keys release. Free help: LawNY (607) 273-3667, the tenant hotline (607) 301-1560, and CDRC mediation (607) 273-9347, all listed by [Cornell](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/living-off-campus/community-resources).
```

## If the eviction is for unpaid rent

This is a nonpayment case. Each step has a minimum gap, set out in the [Cornell Law guide](https://sites.lawschool.cornell.edu/tenants-advocacy/eviction-proceedings).

```figure
kind: steps
title: A nonpayment eviction in New York, step by step
rows:
  - label: "Rent unpaid within 5 days: the landlord sends a certified-mail notice (not before the 7th for rent due the 1st)"
  - label: "At least 6 days after rent is due: a 14-day written rent demand"
  - label: "14 days to pay or leave before the landlord can file"
  - label: "Petition served 10 to 17 days before the hearing"
  - label: "Hearing: you can usually ask for one 14-day adjournment"
  - label: "Judgment: a judge may grant a hardship stay of up to one year"
  - label: "14 days' notice before removal, business days only, sunrise to sunset"
```

1. **The certified notice.** If rent is not paid within 5 days of the due date, the landlord sends a notice by certified mail. For rent due on the 1st, it cannot go out before the 7th.
2. **The 14-day demand.** A written rent demand cannot be served until at least 6 days after rent was due. You then have 14 days to pay or leave before the landlord can file. Paying in full inside this window is the cheapest exit.
3. **The petition.** If the landlord files, the court papers must be served 10 to 17 days before the hearing. Papers posted on your door or slipped under it must also be mailed to you within one day, under [RPAPL 735](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/RPA/735).
4. **The hearing.** You can usually request one 14-day adjournment on the hearing day. Use it to get a lawyer and gather receipts, texts and repair records.
5. **After a judgment.** A judge may delay the eviction up to one year for hardship. You can appeal within 30 days of service.
6. **Removal.** Only by law enforcement after 14 days' notice, on business days, between sunrise and sunset.

Example: rent of $1,100 is due on November 1 and not paid. The certified notice can go out no earlier than November 7. If the 14-day demand is served that day, you have until November 21 to pay. A petition filed after that must be served at least 10 days before the hearing, so the hearing falls in early December at the soonest. One adjournment adds 14 days. After a judgment, 14 days' notice (business days) comes before removal. Our count from those minimums: about seven weeks from the missed payment to the earliest removal, before any adjournment or hardship stay.

If the rent went unpaid because the landlord would not fix the heat or a leak, say so in court with your written repair requests. The [Cornell Law guide](https://sites.lawschool.cornell.edu/tenants-advocacy/building-issues-maintenance) reports abatements in court of about 10% to 20% for minor problems, about 30% for moderate ones, and 50% to 60% for the most serious.

New York's Emergency Rental Assistance Program could cover up to 12 months of back rent and utilities, per the [eviction guide](https://sites.lawschool.cornell.edu/tenants-advocacy/eviction-proceedings). It stopped taking applications after January 20, 2023, per [New York City's Human Resources Administration](https://www.nyc.gov/site/hra/help/new-york-emergency-rental-assistance-program-erap.page). For help with back rent, call 2-1-1 or LawNY.

## If your landlord won't renew: holdovers and Good Cause

A holdover case is about staying after the lease ends, not about money. Under state law, a landlord who will not renew, or wants a raise of 5% or more, owes you notice of [30, 60 or 90 days](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/RPP/226-C) depending on how long you have lived there. Late notice keeps you on the old terms until the period runs. An expired lease continues month to month unless proper notice is given, per the [lease renewal page](https://sites.lawschool.cornell.edu/tenants-advocacy/lease-renewal).

Ithaca opted into Good Cause Eviction in July 2024. In a covered building, you have a right to renew unless the landlord has one of the grounds below.

| Landlord's reason | Can it end your tenancy? | What applies |
|---|---|---|
| Unpaid rent | Yes | Not if the nonpayment is tied to an unreasonable increase |
| Substantial lease violation | Yes, if not cured | 10 days' written notice to cure first |
| You refuse reasonable renewal terms | Yes | Written notice 30 to 90 days before the lease ends |
| Landlord's own residence, demolition, leaving the rental market | Yes | Listed exceptions to the right to renew |
| You refuse a raise above the lower of 10% or 5% plus CPI | Presumed not | The increase is presumed unreasonable |
| Retaliation for a repair complaint in the past year | No | A defense, except in owner-occupied buildings under four units |

The grounds come from the [lease renewal page](https://sites.lawschool.cornell.edu/tenants-advocacy/lease-renewal) and [RPL 216](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/RPP/216). The rent standard is on the [state's Good Cause page](https://hcr.ny.gov/good-cause-eviction). In a lease-breach holdover, the court must grant a 30-day stay of the warrant, per the [eviction guide](https://sites.lawschool.cornell.edu/tenants-advocacy/eviction-proceedings).

Coverage is the catch. Exempt under [RPL 214](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/RPP/214): buildings with a certificate of occupancy from January 1, 2009 on (for 30 years), owner-occupied buildings of 10 or fewer units, small-landlord premises, units above a high-rent threshold, condos and co-ops, and Cornell's own dorms. Private student apartments are not exempt as a category. The landlord's renewal notice must say whether your unit is covered and, if not, why.

## If the City orders your building vacated

A vacate order comes from the City when a building is unsafe to live in. Since a [2019 Common Council vote](https://www.cornellsun.com/article/2019/03/city-council-votes-to-stiffen-penalties-for-noncompliant-landlords), renting an unsafe unit is a misdemeanor, punishable by a fine of up to $500 and up to 30 days. The same rule makes landlords pay displaced tenants double the monthly rent, prorated per day, until the problems are fixed or the lease ends.

The 2026 example is Asteri, a 181-unit building on the Commons owned by Vecino Group that opened in August 2024. The [Cornell Daily Sun reported](https://www.cornellsun.com/article/2026/03/asteri-s-aftermath-displacement-dangerous-conditions-and-a-predatory-cash-for-keys-scheme) that the City ordered about 200 residents out on March 4, 2026, and lifted the order on March 20. Residents were offered $2,000 to leave within five days and release their claims. A city official and the Ithaca Tenants Union called the offer predatory. The county then set aside $50,000 for legal aid.

Example: you pay $1,200 a month and the City orders the building closed for the same 16 days as Asteri. Our arithmetic: $1,200 times two, divided by 31 days, is $77.42 a day, or $1,239 for 16 days. The $2,000 looks bigger. But it required moving out within five days and releasing claims, so you would give up the apartment and any case for damages. Have a lawyer compare the two for your lease before you sign.

What to do the day an order lands:

1. Photograph the posted order and your unit.
2. Call the Building Division at (607) 274-6508 and ask what the order covers.
3. Write to the landlord asking for the double-rent payment, and keep a copy.
4. Sign nothing that releases claims until LawNY or the hotline has read it.
5. Keep receipts for every night you pay for somewhere else.

Delays at new buildings are a related risk. In 2016, Fontana's late opening at 327 Eddy St left over 50 students with August 1 leases without a home, and the landlord offered $30 a day or a hotel room, the [Sun reported](https://www.cornellsun.com/article/2016/09/delayed-collegetown-apartment-opening-leaves-e2-80-88students-homeless).

## Immigration status and eviction

With a lease, a tenant cannot be evicted because of immigration status unless there is a substantial lease or code violation, per the [immigrant tenant rights page](https://sites.lawschool.cornell.edu/tenants-advocacy/immigrant-tenant-rights). A month-to-month tenant of more than 30 days needs formal notice and a court case to be removed. Harassment, such as denying heat or hot water, is barred. The state housing agency, HCR, created a Fair and Equitable Housing Office. Cornell Graduate School staff say a landlord dispute [does not affect visa status](https://www.gradschool.cornell.edu/announcements/grad-tips-renting/).

## Common mistakes

- **Ignoring the certified letter.** The 14 days after a rent demand are your window to pay before the landlord can file. Miss it and the next step is court papers and a hearing date.
- **Withholding rent over repairs without advice.** It invites a nonpayment case that starts with a 14-day notice, per the [Cornell Law guide](https://sites.lawschool.cornell.edu/tenants-advocacy/building-issues-maintenance). Call (607) 301-1560 first.
- **Signing a cash-for-keys release.** Asteri's offer was $2,000 to leave within five days and give up claims. You lose the apartment and any damages case for a sum a lawyer should check first.
- **Skipping the hearing.** The one 14-day adjournment is requested on the hearing day, and the hardship stay of up to a year is the judge's call. Go, with your papers.
- **Staying quiet about repairs out of fear.** A Solidarity Slate candidate told the [Sun in 2021](https://cornellsun.com/2021/07/23/ithaca-tenants-union-and-solidarity-slate-join-regional-efforts-toward-housing-equity/) that college students are often afraid to call their landlord about repairs for fear of eviction. A good-faith complaint is protected for a year.
- **Expecting Cornell to represent you.** Off-Campus Living [does not represent students](https://cornellsun.com/2019/02/06/students-and-landlords-both-perpetuate-myths-about-housing-university-panelist-says/) in disputes; LawNY and the hotline do.

## FAQ

**Can my landlord change the locks or put my things out?** Removal comes only after a judgment and 14 days' notice, on business days between sunrise and sunset, per the [eviction guide](https://sites.lawschool.cornell.edu/tenants-advocacy/eviction-proceedings). If someone locks you out without that, call LawNY at (607) 273-3667 the same day.

**Where is eviction court in Ithaca?** Ithaca City Court, 118 East Clinton Street, hears housing cases and small claims up to $5,000, per the [Cornell Law guide](https://sites.lawschool.cornell.edu/tenants-advocacy/building-issues-maintenance).

**Does Good Cause apply if I live in the Town of Ithaca or Lansing?** No. Outside New York City it applies only where a municipality opts in, per the [lease renewal page](https://sites.lawschool.cornell.edu/tenants-advocacy/lease-renewal). On the state's [2026 opt-in list](https://hcr.ny.gov/good-cause-eviction-law-notice-august-2026), current to May 4, 2026, the City of Ithaca is the only Tompkins County municipality. For code problems, the Town of Ithaca's office is (607) 273-1783 and Lansing's is (607) 533-7054, per the [Cornell Law guide](https://sites.lawschool.cornell.edu/tenants-advocacy/building-issues-maintenance).

**If my housemates stop paying, am I on the hook?** Yes, on most local leases: Cornell says they bind housemates [jointly and severally](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/living-off-campus/tenant-best-practices/study-abroad/housemate-compatibility-questionnaire), so each is responsible for the others' rent. Pay your share in a way you can prove and call the hotline at (607) 301-1560.

**Can I appeal an eviction judgment?** Yes, within 30 days of service, per the [eviction guide](https://sites.lawschool.cornell.edu/tenants-advocacy/eviction-proceedings).
