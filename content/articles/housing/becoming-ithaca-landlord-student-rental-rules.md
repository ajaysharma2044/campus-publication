```glance
- Good Cause applies in Ithaca since July 2024; the small-landlord exemption covers owners of one unit statewide.
- Deposit plus any advance: one month's rent at most, held in trust, returned with an itemized list within 14 days or forfeited.
```

In the City of Ithaca, every rental needs a [Certificate of Compliance](https://ecode360.com/8390192), renewed by inspection every five years for a one- or two-family house and every three for three or more units. Every student lease then runs on two clocks: the City's [120 days' written notice](https://wskg.org/ithaca-landlords-must-now-give-renters-120-days-before-renewing-leases/) before you re-let, and New York's rule that the deposit comes back with an itemized statement [within 14 days](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/GOB/7-108) or is lost. Ithaca also opted into Good Cause Eviction, and its [small-landlord exemption stops at one unit](https://hcr.ny.gov/good-cause-eviction-law-notice-february-2025), so a second unit anywhere in New York puts you under it unless another exemption applies.

```reader "If you're buying a house near campus to rent"
First check the address. The COC system, the 120-day rule and Cornell's safety tool are City of Ithaca rules, not Town, Lansing or Cayuga Heights ones. Budget the COC fee: $100 for a one-family, $175 for a two-family, $300 for 3 to 8 units. Ask the seller for the current certificate and the deposit records. Under state law a new owner is [liable for a deposit it knew of](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/GOB/7-108). Owning a second unit anywhere in New York ends the one-unit Good Cause exemption. The costly mistake: closing before you know whether the certificate has lapsed.
```

```reader "If you already own a Collegetown building"
Your year runs on the student calendar. [Most Ithaca leases end July 31](https://cornellsun.com/2024/07/10/landlords-and-advocates-grapple-with-good-cause-eviction-law-at-public-hearing), and popular Collegetown units lease 8 to 16 months ahead. Before you show a unit or sign next year's tenants, give the current ones 120 days' written notice, or have their initials on the bolded waiver. Non-renewals and increases of 5% or more need a separate 30, 60 or 90 day notice. Increases above the lower of 10% or 5% plus CPI are presumed unreasonable. The costly mistake: missing the 14-day deposit deadline, which forfeits the whole deposit.
```

```reader "If your rental is outside the city line"
Town of Ithaca, Lansing and Cayuga Heights properties fall outside the City's COC system, its 120-day rule and Cornell's safety tool. State law still applies: the one-month deposit cap, the 14-day return and the fee bans. In the Town of Ithaca, more than two unrelated people count as a family only if the [Zoning Board of Appeals finds a functional equivalent](https://law.justia.com/cases/new-york/other-courts/2017/2017-ny-slip-op-30471-u.html), a rule upheld in a 2017 case involving Ithaca College students. Call the code office before you rent to a group: Town of Ithaca (607) 273-1783, Town of Lansing (607) 533-7054.
```

## Step 1: confirm the property is in the City of Ithaca

Most of what follows is City code. The City's [Housing Inspections page](https://www.cityofithacany.gov/145/Housing-Inspections) says inspections are required for all residential rental dwellings in the City; hotels, motels, mobile homes and temporary housing are exempt. Short-term rentals need a separate permit. The Town of Ithaca, the Town of Lansing and the Village of Cayuga Heights run their own code offices, with phone numbers in the [Cornell Law guide's list](https://sites.lawschool.cornell.edu/tenants-advocacy/building-issues-maintenance).

State law follows you everywhere in New York: deposits, fees, habitability, sublets and the duty to re-rent.

## The Certificate of Compliance: who needs one and what it costs

Every rental dwelling in the City needs one, single-family houses included. City Code [section 210-43](https://ecode360.com/8390192) sets how long each certificate lasts, and the owner books the next inspection before it expires:

- one- or two-family rental: up to five years;
- three or more units, or a unit with five or more unrelated occupants: up to three years;
- dormitories, fraternities and sororities: up to one year.

How it works, per the City's [COC page](https://www.cityofithacany.gov/146/Certificate-of-Compliance) and [fee page](https://www.cityofithacany.gov/147/Certificate-of-Compliance-Fees):

1. Apply on OpenGov (ithacacityny.viewpointcloud.com) before the current certificate expires.
2. Pay the initial fee before the inspection is scheduled. Charges billed after the inspection are due within 30 days of receipt, and one 30-day extension to prepare for the inspection or make repairs is allowed.
3. An inspector checks the exterior and interior, structure, fire protection, egress, heating and ventilation, electrical, plumbing and fire separations. Records are checked against the housing, building and zoning codes.
4. Fix what they find. A missed appointment costs $75.
5. Post the certificate in the main entryway, or inside a kitchen cabinet if there is no central entrance. Section 210-43 also requires every lease to state whether the unit has a valid certificate, its expiration date and a link to the City's certificate database.

| Property | COC fee |
|---|---|
| One-family dwelling | $100 |
| Two-family dwelling | $175 |
| 3 to 8 units | $300 |
| 9 to 20 units | $500 |
| 21 to 100 units | $1,000 |
| 101 to 250 units | $1,750 |
| 251 or more units | $2,500 |
| Live/work | $100 per unit |
| Congregate, co-op or boarding house | $350 |
| Fraternity or sorority | $500 |
| Each violation found | $10 |
| Each violation still uncorrected | $50 |
| Re-inspection | $75 an hour ($75 minimum) |

Source: City Code [section 210-43](https://ecode360.com/8390192), amended through March 5, 2025.

The certificate also decides whether students can find you. Cornell's listing site, the [Off-Campus Housing Marketplace](https://offcampus.housing.cornell.edu/), requires a current COC for City properties, and the Graduate School says listings are [checked against the City's COC database](https://www.gradschool.cornell.edu/announcements/grad-tips-renting/). Cornell and the Building Division also run a [safety tool](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/campus-living/housing-search-process/safe-living-environments) that shows every City rental's sprinklers, extinguishers, smoke and carbon monoxide alarms, construction type and code. Students look you up there.

The Building Division is at 108 E. Green St., City Hall, 4th floor, open weekdays 8:30 a.m. to 4:00 p.m.; the [main line](https://www.cityofithacany.gov/130/Building-Division) is 607-274-6510.

## What a lapsed certificate or an unsafe unit costs

Renting without a valid certificate is a Housing Code violation, and the City can order the unit vacated until it complies, under [section 210-43](https://ecode360.com/8390192). The case that shows the stakes is 117 Thurston Ave. In 2018 its student tenants learned after signing that the certificate had [expired in November 2015](https://cornellsun.com/2018/11/25/cornell-student-abandons-house-sleeps-in-library-after-months-long-dispute-with-landlord/). The Sun reported 53 days from the first complaint to a fixed heater, and one tenant sleeping in a campus library.

In 2019 Common Council made renting an unsafe unit a [misdemeanor, with up to a $500 fine and 30 days](https://www.cornellsun.com/article/2019/03/city-council-votes-to-stiffen-penalties-for-noncompliant-landlords). Landlords also owe displaced tenants double the monthly rent, prorated per day.

At Asteri, a 181-unit downtown building, the City ordered about 200 residents out on March 4, 2026, and lifted the order on March 20. Residents were offered [$2,000 to leave within five days](https://www.cornellsun.com/article/2026/03/asteri-s-aftermath-displacement-dangerous-conditions-and-a-predatory-cash-for-keys-scheme) and release their claims, an offer a city official and the Ithaca Tenants Union called predatory.

## The leasing calendar: the 120-day notice and the waiver

City Code [section 258-10](https://ecode360.com/27390826) requires at least 120 days' written notice to current tenants before you renew their agreement, show the unit to prospective tenants or otherwise suggest it is available, or sign with new ones. It took effect May 31, 2022, replacing a 60-day rule. It does not apply when the current agreement runs less than nine months, or once an eviction summons and complaint has been filed and served.

The waiver is narrow. It must be "explicitly written, bolded and initialed" on the [lease's first page](https://wskg.org/ithaca-landlords-must-now-give-renters-120-days-before-renewing-leases/), and the sponsor's [2022 column](https://cornellsun.com/2022/04/18/mehler-student-tenants-rights-shifting-in-your-favor/) says both sides must choose it. City Attorney Ari Levine said disputes can go to the City's housing court, and a violation carries a civil penalty of up to $500 under [section 258-11](https://ecode360.com/27390826).

The 120 days run from the day you give written notice, which [the code](https://ecode360.com/27390826) allows at any time during the current agreement. Notice given on August 1, the day a lease starts, clears you to show, renew or re-let from November 29. Keep a copy of the notice.

## Good Cause Eviction: are you covered?

Common Council voted 8-2 to [opt in](https://www.wamc.org/2024-07-12/ithaca-is-the-latest-upstate-city-to-opt-into-good-cause-renter-protections) in July 2024. Tenants gain a right to renewal. You can still decline to renew for your own residence, demolition, leaving the rental market or a proven legal ground, such as nonpayment or a violation uncured after 10 days' written notice, per the [Cornell Law guide](https://sites.lawschool.cornell.edu/tenants-advocacy/lease-renewal).

The rent standard: an annual increase above the lower of 10% or 5% plus CPI is [presumed unreasonable](https://hcr.ny.gov/good-cause-eviction). It is a rebuttable presumption, not a hard cap. The state housing agency publishes the CPI figure by August 1 each year. Its [2026 notice](https://hcr.ny.gov/good-cause-eviction-law-notice-august-2026) puts the Northeast region's change, which applies in Tompkins County, at 3.15% for 2024 to 2025, so the line is 5% plus 3.15%, or 8.15%.

Exemptions under [Real Property Law 214](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/RPP/214) that matter to an alum:

- **Small landlord.** Ithaca's threshold is one unit anywhere in New York; the state default is 10. To claim it, you must disclose the owners and their unit counts.
- **Newer buildings.** A certificate of occupancy dated on or after January 1, 2009 exempts the building for 30 years.
- **Owner-occupied** buildings of 10 or fewer units.
- **High rents.** Ithaca exempts units above 345% of HUD fair market rent. With the 2023 1BR figure, that was [$4,402 a month](https://cornellsun.com/2024/07/10/landlords-and-advocates-grapple-with-good-cause-eviction-law-at-public-hearing). Student per-bed rents sit far below it.
- **Dormitories** run by colleges. Private student apartments are not dormitories.

There is no exemption for students or fixed-term student leases. At the 2024 hearing, landlords and the Board of Realtors predicted [$2,000 to $5,000 in legal costs](https://www.wamc.org/2024-07-12/ithaca-is-the-latest-upstate-city-to-opt-into-good-cause-renter-protections) per non-renewal; that is their claim, not a measured figure.

Separate from Good Cause, [Real Property Law 226-c](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/RPP/226-C) requires written notice if you will not renew, or will raise rent 5% or more. The notice must say whether the unit is subject to Good Cause, and why if exempt. Late notice keeps the tenancy on existing terms until the period runs out.

| Tenant's time in the unit, or lease length (the longer) | Notice for non-renewal or a 5%+ increase |
|---|---|
| Under 1 year, no 1-year lease | 30 days |
| 1 to 2 years, or a 1 to 2 year lease | 60 days |
| Over 2 years, or a lease of 2+ years | 90 days |

An auto-renew clause needs its own written reminder 15 to 30 days before the tenant's opt-out deadline. Refusing to renew within a year of a good-faith complaint can be defended as retaliation, except in owner-occupied buildings with fewer than four units.

## Deposits and fees: what you may charge

| Charge | The rule in New York |
|---|---|
| Security deposit | Deposit plus any rent advance at most one month's rent; pet deposits count toward it |
| Holding it | Trust funds, [never mixed with your money](https://ag.ny.gov/resources/individuals/tenants-homeowners/tenants) |
| Interest | 6+ unit buildings: New York interest-bearing account, tenant told the bank in writing; you may keep 1% a year |
| Returning it | Balance plus itemized statement within 14 days of move-out, or you keep none of it |
| Willful violation | Punitive damages up to twice the deposit |
| Application fee | [Banned](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/RPP/238-A) |
| Background or credit check | Lesser of actual cost or $20; waived if the applicant brings one from the past 30 days |
| Late fee | Only after rent is more than 5 days late; at most $50 or 5% of monthly rent, whichever is less |
| Replacement key | At most [110% of cost](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/RPP/235-I) |
| Cleaning fee, non-refundable deposit | Prohibited; a non-refundable deposit agreement is void |

The [Cornell Law guide](https://sites.lawschool.cornell.edu/tenants-advocacy/security-deposits) lists what a deposit may cover: unpaid rent, damage beyond normal wear, unpaid utilities owed to you, storing belongings left behind, and excessive cleaning. Routine turnover cleaning is out, as are attorney's fees and late fees.

Two inspections protect you. After signing and before move-in, you must offer a walk-through. If the tenant accepts, you both sign a condition record, and listed defects cannot come out of the deposit. When either side gives notice to end the tenancy, tell the tenant in writing of the right to a move-out inspection. If requested, it happens one to two weeks before the end, with 48 hours' written notice.

Example: on the $5,000-a-month unit above, the deposit cap is $5,000 for the whole unit. The late fee cap is $50, since 5% would be $250. With four units, no interest-bearing account is required; six units would require one.

## Heat, repairs, bed bugs and entry

Every lease carries a [warranty of habitability](https://sites.lawschool.cornell.edu/tenants-advocacy/building-issues-maintenance) that no clause waives. Ithaca's heat season runs September 15 to May 31, two weeks longer than the state's October 1 start:

- 6 a.m. to 10 p.m.: at least 68°F inside when it is below 55°F outside.
- 10 p.m. to 6 a.m.: at least 62°F, whatever the weather.

If you pay the utilities, a shutoff needs at least 18 days' posted notice in an apartment building and 15 days mailed for a two-family house. For heat between November 1 and April 15 the notice is 30 days.

Bed bugs are yours to exterminate, even if you suspect the tenant brought them, unless you prove misconduct. Within 72 hours, notify the units above, below and beside in writing, without naming the unit, and post in common areas.

Tenants can file a petition for court-ordered repairs after 30 days in the unit and written notice, since January 2024. Courts in the guide's experience abate rent about 10% to 20% for minor problems and 50% to 60% for the worst.

Entry depends on the lease. Without a reserved right, you [cannot enter even for repairs](https://sites.lawschool.cornell.edu/tenants-advocacy/landlord-access) except in an emergency. With one, give reasonable notice, often 24 hours, at reasonable hours. Tenants generally must let code inspectors in with reasonable notice.

## Snow, trash and the outside

City code puts the sidewalk on you: clear the full paved width [within 24 hours after a snowfall begins](https://www.cityofithacany.gov/136/Exterior-Property-Maintenance), including corner ramps. If you do not, the City may clear it and bill you [at least $50 a plowing](https://www.cityofithacany.gov/m/faq?cat=46). A lease may pass the job to tenants; if it does, write it in plainly.

The same code keeps grass under 9 inches, bars couches and mattresses stored outdoors, and limits parking to designated spaces. Trash goes out no more than 24 hours before pickup. Owners and tenants are both responsible for litter, so the party-weekend mess is partly your problem.

## Sublets and tenants who leave early

Students study abroad, intern and graduate early. In buildings with [four or more units](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/RPP/226-B), you cannot unreasonably refuse a sublet. You may ask for more information within 10 days and must answer within 30; silence is consent. In three or fewer units, the lease controls.

If a tenant leaves early, you must make good-faith, [customary efforts to re-rent](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/RPP/227-E) at fair market value or the lease rate, whichever is lower. A new lease ends the old one and offsets what the first tenant owes.

## Renting to second-years as affiliated housing

Second-years may live only in Cornell or affiliated housing unless exempt. A private house can qualify, but the bar is high. Cornell's [requirements](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/campus-housing/requirements-qualified-university-recognized-campus-housing) include:

- a Registered Student Organization running the house, with officers living there;
- sprinklers throughout and full code compliance;
- general liability insurance of at least $1,000,000 per occurrence and $2,000,000 aggregate, naming Cornell as additional insured;
- a current City COC, then yearly COC inspections;
- an annual application to the Assistant Director of Off-Campus Living, with a 30-minute presentation, approved at Cornell's sole discretion.

## Common mistakes

- **Letting the certificate lapse.** The 117 Thurston tenants found an expired certificate after signing; the City tightened penalties the following March, with a misdemeanor and double-rent rules.
- **Missing the 14-day deposit deadline.** You forfeit the whole deposit, and a willful violation can cost up to twice it.
- **Charging an application, cleaning or non-refundable fee.** All banned; the non-refundable agreement is void.
- **Showing a unit before the 120-day notice.** Disputes go to the City's housing court; the waiver only works bolded and initialed on page one.
- **Assuming Good Cause skips you.** Owning two units anywhere in New York ends Ithaca's small-landlord exemption.
- **Offering cash for keys after a vacate order.** Asteri's $2,000 offer was called predatory, and the displacement rule owes double rent anyway.

## FAQ

**What happens if my tenants stay past July 31?** An expired lease continues [month-to-month](https://sites.lawschool.cornell.edu/tenants-advocacy/lease-renewal) unless you gave proper non-renewal notice. A month-to-month tenant generally gives one month's notice to leave.

**Can I charge more deposit when I raise the rent?** Yes. On a renewal at a higher rent you may [top the deposit up](https://ag.ny.gov/resources/individuals/tenants-homeowners/tenants) to the new monthly rent.

**Who handles a tenant's complaint about my building?** The City's code inspectors, through the Building Division's "Report a Concern" form or [607-274-6510](https://www.cityofithacany.gov/130/Building-Division).

**How many unrelated students can share a City house?** Two or three per unit in a one- or two-family house, by zone. [City zoning](https://ecode360.com/8393870) limits a one-family house to one person or a family plus one unrelated person, or plus two in the R-2 and R-3 zones or an owner-occupied R-1 house. Each unit of a two-family house in an R-2 or CR-2 zone allows one person or a family plus two unrelated people. Four or more unrelated people make a "cooperative household", which zoning treats as a multiple dwelling, and four or more college students are presumed not to be a functional family unit. Verified over-occupancy revokes the certificate immediately, under [section 210-43](https://ecode360.com/8390192).

**Can my tenant change the locks?** Generally not without your consent, and you cannot demand a duplicate key unless the lease reserves one, per the [Cornell Law guide](https://sites.lawschool.cornell.edu/tenants-advocacy/landlord-access).
