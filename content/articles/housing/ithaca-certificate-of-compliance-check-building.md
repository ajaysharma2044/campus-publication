A Certificate of Compliance is the City of Ithaca's inspection permit for rental housing, and you check it by calling the Building Division at 607-274-6508 or emailing ibd@cityofithaca.org ([City Building Division](https://www.cityofithacany.gov/130/Building-Division)). Ask whether your address has a valid certificate and when it expires.

This page is general information, not legal advice.

## Who needs a Certificate of Compliance?

The City page names multiple dwellings: buildings with three or more units, or individual units housing six or more unrelated people. Dormitories, fraternities and sororities are covered too. If your group is near that size, ask the Building Division whether your house needs a certificate.

The City says inspections are required for all residential rental dwellings inside the City, including one-family and two-family houses. Hotels, motels, mobile homes and temporary housing are exempt, and short-term rentals need a separate permit ([Housing Inspections](https://www.cityofithacany.gov/145/Housing-Inspections)).

## How long does a certificate last?

A multiple dwelling's certificate lasts three years. A dormitory's, fraternity's or sorority's lasts one year ([City of Ithaca](https://www.cityofithacany.gov/146/Certificate-of-Compliance)). Ask the landlord to show you the certificate when you tour.

## What does the inspection cover?

The owner applies through the City's online permit portal before the current certificate expires, and pays the fee before an inspector is scheduled. The inspector examines the exterior and interior, structure, fire protection, exits, heating and cooling, electrical, plumbing and fire separations, and checks records against the housing, building and zoning codes. Missing the appointment costs the owner $75 ([City of Ithaca](https://www.cityofithacany.gov/146/Certificate-of-Compliance)).

The fee is the owner's cost, not yours. It runs from $100 for a one-family home to $300 for three to eight units and $2,500 for 251 or more units ([Fee schedule](https://www.cityofithacany.gov/147/Certificate-of-Compliance-Fees)).

Cornell points tenants to a Building Department property information site and to the phone and email above ([Cornell Student and Campus Life](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/living-off-campus/housing-search-process/rental-listings-resources)). The easiest route for a student is the [safety tool](https://safety.offcampusliving.cornell.edu/), which Cornell Off-Campus Living and the Building Division run. It shows certificate status, sprinklers, extinguishers, smoke and carbon monoxide alarms, construction type and property use, updated daily ([Cornell Student and Campus Life](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/living-off-campus/housing-search-process/safe-living-environments)).

It covers City rentals only, so Town of Ithaca, Lansing and Cayuga Heights buildings do not appear. Of 92 City addresses looked up on Nov. 10, 2026, 8 showed an expired certificate, so the check is worth the five minutes. The [City versus town guide](/cornell/housing/city-of-ithaca-vs-town-lansing-cayuga-heights-renters/) explains which rules follow your address.

The Building Division is at 108 E. Green St., City Hall, fourth floor, open 8:30 a.m. to 4 p.m. on weekdays ([Building Division](https://www.cityofithacany.gov/130/Building-Division)).

## What if the certificate has expired?

Ask the landlord in writing when the inspection is scheduled, and wait for the answer before you sign or pay a deposit. An expired certificate means the building has not been re-inspected on schedule.

In 2019, the Common Council made renting an unsafe unit a misdemeanor, with a fine of up to $500 and up to 30 days in jail. Landlords also owe displaced tenants double the monthly rent, prorated per day ([The Cornell Daily Sun](https://www.cornellsun.com/article/2019/03/city-council-votes-to-stiffen-penalties-for-noncompliant-landlords)). If the City orders a building vacated, read [what to do after a vacate order](/cornell/housing/ithaca-eviction-vacate-order-what-to-do/).

```reader "If you're an international student signing from abroad"
You cannot tour, so the phone call and the safety tool replace the visit. Do both before you wire a deposit, and keep the Building Division's reply.
```

```reader "If you're a parent paying or co-signing"
Make the call yourself. It takes a few minutes, needs no NetID and gives a written answer if you email ibd@cityofithaca.org.
```

## Does a valid certificate mean the landlord is good?

No. The certificate shows the building passed inspection. It says nothing about repair speed, deposit habits or how the landlord treats tenants. The [landlord checking guide](/housing/how-to-check-an-ithaca-landlords-record/) covers the other steps. Owners who want to rent legally can read the [landlord rules](/cornell/housing/becoming-ithaca-landlord-student-rental-rules/). If you already live somewhere with a defect, see [what to do when a landlord will not fix heat, mold or bed bugs](/cornell/housing/ithaca-landlord-wont-fix-heat-mold-bed-bugs/).

## What to do next

1. Get the address and call 607-274-6508 or email ibd@cityofithaca.org.
2. Search the safety tool.
3. Put the inspection date in writing if the certificate has lapsed.
