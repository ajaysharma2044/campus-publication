```glance
- Recognized chapter houses count as sophomore housing; affirm living in on your housing application by March 15, 2027.
- Chapters set their own rent; nonzero spring live-in charges reported to Cornell run from $2,000 to $14,673.
- Members who live out still owe a parlor fee: a median $1,152 in fraternities, $602 in sororities.
- Fraternity parties are banned through 2026-27 while Cornell decides whether fraternity life continues.
```

A recognized chapter house counts as sophomore housing at Cornell if you affirm it on your housing application by March 15, 2027 ([Spring Housing Selection](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/housing-details/apply-housing-campus/current-undergraduate-application-process/spring-housing-selection-rising-sophomores)). Each chapter sets its own rent. Leaving aside one chapter that lists $0, the live-in charge for housing and dining runs from $2,000 to $14,673 for one spring semester on Cornell's [organization scorecard](https://scl.cornell.edu/get-involved/sorority-fraternity-life/organization-scorecard).

A chapter house is three things at once. It is a club's meeting hall. It is a building owned by the university, an alumni group or a national organization, paid for by student rent ([Financial Obligations page](https://scl.cornell.edu/get-involved/sorority-fraternity-life/join/financial-obligations-sororities-fraternities)). And for a sophomore, it stands in for a dorm that Cornell approves but does not run. The university, the city and the chapter each control a different part of the house. Knowing which one sets which rule is most of what a student needs.

The stakes are large. In 2018, more than 4,500 students, nearly a third of undergraduates, belonged to one of 63 chapters ([Cornell Chronicle](https://news.cornell.edu/stories/2018/05/citing-health-and-safety-concerns-pollack-initiates-greek-life-reforms)). Today, 34 chapters list a house rate on the scorecard, the public page where each chapter reports its own fees. Most of those charges come in below a semester in a Cornell dorm double with a meal plan. The path below runs from joining to living in to living out, then a calendar and a checklist.

## How Cornell's chapter houses got here

Fraternities are older at Cornell than most of the campus. Seven existed by the end of the university's first year, historian Morris Bishop wrote in his 1962 history of Cornell ([Cornell Daily Sun, 2019](https://www.cornellsun.com/article/2019/11/solar-flashback-its-all-greek-to-me-a-history-of-greek-life-policies-at-cornell)). Three of those seven, Alpha Delta Phi, Chi Psi and Delta Upsilon, are recognized chapters with houses on today's list. Three others, Chi Phi, Zeta Psi and Phi Kappa Psi, are suspended or unrecognized (Cornell SFL scorecard).

Most early chapters rented rooms downtown. In 1878 Alpha Delta Phi bought land and built Cornell's first chapter house, about halfway up Buffalo Street ([Cornell Daily Sun, 2013](https://www.cornellsun.com/article/2013/09/ezras-oracle-sept-20-2013)). When Jennie McGraw died in 1881, Chi Psi bought her mansion. Her land was split among other fraternities, which pulled houses toward the north end of West Campus. Cornell opened Risley Hall across Fall Creek Gorge in 1913 and began West Campus dorms in 1914, and more chapters built near the students. Sororities moved into their own houses in the late 1910s and early 1920s, mostly in Cornell Heights, the neighborhood just north of campus (Cornell Daily Sun, 2013).

Kappa Alpha Theta, founded in 1881, was Cornell's first sorority. In 1906, seven Cornell students founded Alpha Phi Alpha, the country's first Black intercollegiate fraternity (Cornell Daily Sun, 2019). Both chapters are still on the list.

Money shaped the houses from the start. In 1887, Bishop wrote, living in a fraternity house cost $200 to $500 more than a lodging house, so the option was "restricted to the wealthy" (Cornell Daily Sun, 2019). The gap in who has a house is still visible. Every Panhellenic sorority and all but one fraternity in the Interfraternity Council list a street address. Of the 15 multicultural chapters, only two do (Cornell SFL scorecard).

Ownership varies house by house. The scorecard marks 15 houses "privately owned" and one, Chi Phi's at 107 Edgemoor Lane, "university owned." In 2010, The Cornell Daily Sun reported that Cornell owned 13 fraternity houses, sold to it by alumni groups that wanted the university to manage them ([Cornell Daily Sun, 2010](https://www.cornellsun.com/article/2010/09/university-turns-former-kappa-sigma-house-into-campus-dorm)).

That arrangement decides what happens when a chapter disappears. In 2010, after Kappa Sigma lost its chapter, Cornell renovated the house at 600 University Avenue. It filled the rooms with nine graduate students and 15 undergraduates, partly through a last-minute Craigslist ad. An official estimated that an empty house cost $80,000 to $90,000 a year to maintain. Cornell's contract with Kappa Sigma said the house had to go back to the fraternity if a chapter returned (Cornell Daily Sun, 2010). One did. Kappa Sigma is back at 600 University Avenue, with 22 members in fall 2025 (Cornell SFL scorecard).

## Year one: you can join, but you cannot live in

A chapter house is not an option in year one. Cornell assigns first-year and transfer students to residence halls for the first year of the residential requirement ([Cornell Residential Policy](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/campus-housing/residential-policy)).

Joining comes later too. Under Cornell's deferred recruitment policy, new students wait one semester before they can join a sorority or fraternity. Student and faculty leaders set that rule in the 1950s ([How to Join](https://scl.cornell.edu/get-involved/sorority-fraternity-life/how-join-sorority-or-fraternity)). The Interfraternity Council and Panhellenic Council hold formal recruitment the week before spring classes start each January. Multicultural chapters run their own intake at times each group sets. For 2027, spring classes begin Jan. 25 ([Cornell Registrar](https://registrar.cornell.edu/calendars-exams/academic-calendar)).

A bid changes your sophomore options right away. A student who accepts a bid from a chapter with a qualified house is eligible to live in, and works out with the chapter whether they will (Cornell Residential Policy).

It can change your meal plan too. Some chapters require members who do not live in the house, including first-years, to join the house meal plan. Cornell Dining lets those members cut back their Cornell plan and switch to the SFL Supplemental plan, also called Bear Supplemental. Members living off campus can buy it too. A member living on campus qualifies only if the chapter is recognized and requires members who live out to carry a plan of at least seven meals a week. The 2026-27 switch deadlines are Sept. 30, 2026, for fall and Feb. 10, 2027, for spring ([Cornell Dining](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/dining/meal-plans-rates/bear-supplemental)).

The plan covers 127 meals a semester plus Big Red Bucks, Cornell's dining money. Cornell lists two prices for it: $2,167.51 a semester on the 2026-27 meal plans page ([Cornell Dining meal plans](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/dining/meal-plans-rates/undergraduate-meal-plans)) and $2,088.77 on the SFL Supplemental page. Budget for the higher figure, which sits with the other 2026-27 meal plan prices, until the Bursar bill shows the charge.

## Year two: the chapter house as a dorm

Since fall 2022, Cornell has required sophomores to live in university housing or approved affiliated housing. Recognized chapter houses count. When the rule was announced, the Interfraternity Council's vice president of recruitment told the Sun he expected little change, because sophomores in Greek life already lived in their houses ([Cornell Daily Sun, 2021](https://www.cornellsun.com/article/2021/11/as-cornell-requires-university-housing-for-sophomores-students-weigh-options)).

A sophomore can live in only on Cornell's four conditions and Cornell's calendar, not the chapter's (Cornell Residential Policy).

1. **You must already be a member.** Any sophomore living in a chapter house must belong to the chapter when the housing application is filed.
2. **The chapter must stay recognized.** Cornell lets recognized chapters house sophomores only while the organization remains recognized.
3. **The chapter must meet staffing rules.** A chapter is approved each year if it meets Cornell's recognition rules and has a live-in advisor and four upper-level officers by Jan. 1.
4. **You still file the housing application.** Every first- and second-year applies, even those who expect to live in a chapter house.

For 2027-28 housing, the application opens Feb. 1 and closes April 4. Students who plan to live in a chapter house must say so on the application by the March deadline above (Spring Housing Selection).

Housing and Residential Life checks your plan against rosters from the Office of Sorority and Fraternity Life. A student who misses the March deadline completes the requirement in on-campus housing. Cornell tells students to confirm with house leaders that their names are on the roster before the deadline (Spring Housing Selection). Students the chapter confirms skip April's room selection and hear from the sorority and fraternity office instead (Cornell Residential Policy).

The staffing rule dates to 2018. Each chapter, not Cornell, employs its live-in advisor ([Our Community](https://scl.cornell.edu/get-involved/sorority-fraternity-life/our-community)). Former President Martha E. Pollack required one in every residential chapter by fall 2021. She also required house leaders to be juniors or seniors who live in the house (Cornell Chronicle). The four-officer rule grew from that. Starting in 2021-22, four officers, including the president, had to be juniors or seniors living in the house ([Cornell Student & Campus Life](https://scl.cornell.edu/get-involved/sorority-fraternity-life/policies-and-procedures/presidential-actions)).

Cornell's meal-plan requirement covers its halls, program houses and the other buildings it lists, and chapter houses are not on that list (Cornell Dining meal plans). A chapter's live-in charge covers dining, and a typical house has a kitchen and dining area (Financial Obligations page).

The other routes through sophomore year are a residence hall, one of the eight co-ops, an approved affiliated house or an exemption. Our guide to [a co-op or Greek house instead of a dorm](/cornell/housing/cornell-co-ops-greek-houses-cost-how-to-join/) shows how a sophomore gets into each. The [rules on exemptions](/cornell/housing/cornell-residential-requirement-exemptions-live-off-campus/) cover students who qualify to rent their own place.

A second-year accepted into an approved chapter house before the Spring Housing Selection deadline can cancel a Cornell housing assignment. First-years and new transfers cannot ([Cornell Housing License 2027-28](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/housing-details/rates-amenities-licenses/academic-year-housing-license-2027-2028)). Mid-year moves are harder still. Cornell releases a spring housing contract only for graduating, going abroad or leaving the university. Moving into a "fraternity small residence" does not count as a reason (Financial Obligations page).

In 2018, before the sophomore rule, a group whose Greek housing plans fell apart went house hunting in February. They ended up in a 14-person house that one of them called "this massive disgusting house" ([Cornell Daily Sun, 2018](https://www.cornellsun.com/article/2018/11/desirable-collegetown-leases-leave-market-up-to-16-months-before-residents-move-in)). A sophomore in that spot today goes to April room selection instead, as long as the housing application is in.

```reader "If you're a transfer student"
You can join a chapter in your first semester at Cornell, because the deferred recruitment rule does not apply to transfers. You still cannot live in the house that first year. Every new transfer lives on campus for one academic year. Spring transfers live on campus for their first spring and the full academic year after it ([New Transfer Undergraduates](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/campus-housing/new-transfer-undergraduates)). The chapter house comes in your second year at Cornell at the earliest. Cornell's residential policy exempts transfers who lived on campus for two years elsewhere or who enter as juniors or seniors ([Cornell Residential Policy](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/campus-housing/residential-policy)). If that is you, request the exemption on the housing application and get Housing's answer in writing before you count on a chapter room.
```

## What the rent buys, and why you pay even when you live out

Each chapter sets its own charges. Cornell describes a typical house budget: common-area housekeeping, utilities, internet and phones, repairs, building insurance and grounds care. The insurance covers the building, not your belongings. Every house must have fire alarms and a sprinkler system throughout (Financial Obligations page). For your belongings, our [renters insurance guide](/cornell/housing/cornell-off-campus-renters-insurance-cost/) prices a policy. In most cases, the rent is billed through the Bursar, Cornell's student account office ([Affiliated Housing list](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/living-off-campus/affiliated-housing-second-year-housing-requirement)).

Members who live out pay the parlor fee because a chapter house is the meeting place for every member. Billing members who live out lowers the rent for those who live in. Cornell also says the fee makes living out cost more, which helps keep the house full. Nearly all chapters require members to live in for at least a semester or two (Financial Obligations page).

Cornell says chapter room and board is "in general less" than a residence hall or off-campus housing (Financial Obligations page), and the scorecard bears that out for most chapters. A dorm double costs $6,872 per semester for 2026-27 ([Cornell Housing Rates](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/housing-details/rates-amenities-licenses/housing-rates)). Add the Unlimited meal plan at $3,802 per semester and the total is $10,674. With the Bear Traditional plan at $3,528, it is $10,400 (Cornell Dining meal plans).

Thirteen chapters report a nonzero spring 2026 charge, and their median is $7,400. That is $3,274 less than a hall double with the Unlimited plan ($10,674 minus $7,400). Only two chapters on the list report a charge above the hall total: Alpha Xi Delta and Sigma Pi, whose $11,000 figure dates to spring 2023. The chapter figures run a year or more behind Cornell's 2026-27 rates and are self-reported, so get your chapter's current number in writing (Cornell SFL scorecard). A spring charge does not tell you the fall rate, so ask the chapter for both semesters. If a chapter charged the median in fall and spring, the year would come to $14,800, against $20,800 for a hall double with the Bear Traditional plan ($10,400 a semester).

Rent is not the whole bill. Dues and new member fees sit on top. Alpha Delta Phi lists chapter dues of $4,850, and Sigma Chi and Sigma Delta Tau each list $5,000 in "new member other fees" (Cornell SFL scorecard). Across 23 fraternities, the median live-out charge is $1,152 a semester. Across 11 sororities it is $602. Sigma Chi lists the highest, $4,600 for spring 2025. Delta Upsilon, Phi Gamma Delta and Delta Delta Delta charge $0.

```reader "If you're watching every dollar"
Ask about aid inside the chapter before you ask about rent. Cornell says chapters may offer scholarships, grants and payment plans (Financial Obligations page). Some are named on the scorecard. Sigma Chi lists the Gordon J. Whiting scholarship, a need-based award for members who get grant and work-study aid from Cornell and hold a leadership role in the chapter. Compare one full year, not one semester: two semesters of live-in charges plus dues, against a hall double and a meal plan. Our [dorm versus off-campus comparison](/cornell/housing/is-off-campus-cheaper-than-cornell-dorm/) shows the same math for apartments.
```

A Bursar bill does not mean a Cornell price; the chapter sets it. Cornell asks students to go over Greek costs with their parents during recruitment (Financial Obligations page). The scorecard lists each chapter's full judicial history. Pollack created it in 2018 to be shared with the campus and with parents of all students (Cornell Chronicle). Read the entry for your student's chapter before the housing deadline, not after.

## Who inspects the house

The City of Ithaca inspects every chapter house inside city limits once a year, on top of Cornell's fire alarm and sprinkler rule. The city requires a Certificate of Compliance, its safety inspection permit for rental housing. Apartment buildings hold one for three years. Fraternities, sororities and dormitories must renew every year ([City of Ithaca](https://www.cityofithacany.gov/146/Certificate-of-Compliance)). The owner pays a $500 inspection fee for a fraternity or sorority house ([City of Ithaca fees](https://www.cityofithacany.gov/147/Certificate-of-Compliance-Fees)). Questions go to the Building Division at 607-274-6508. Our [guide to the city, town and village](/cornell/housing/city-of-ithaca-vs-town-lansing-cayuga-heights-renters/) explains which rules follow which address.

The non-Greek affiliated houses go through a separate Cornell review. Alpha Zeta, the Center for Jewish Living and Seal and Serpent must apply every year. They need liability insurance naming Cornell, sprinklers, junior or senior officers living on site, a second-year program and yearly city inspections if they sit in the city. If one falls short, its second-year residents can be sent back to campus housing. Cornell says this process does not apply to recognized sororities and fraternities ([Cornell requirements page](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/campus-housing/requirements-qualified-university-recognized-campus-housing)).

Most houses share their streets with other renters. Cornell's off-campus office tells apartment hunters that Cornell Heights is home to many chapters, so neighbors hear "music and parties" ([Ithaca Neighborhoods](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/living-off-campus/housing-search-process/ithaca-neighborhoods)).

## Why the rules keep changing

Cornell has tightened its Greek rules after deaths and scandals for more than a century. In 1873, a blindfolded Cornell student fell off a cliff during an initiation. It is considered the first recorded fraternity hazing death in the United States ([CNN](https://www.cnn.com/2026/10/17/us/cornell-greek-life-west-virginia-university-crackdown)).

After a student died in an alcohol-related hazing incident in 2011, then-President David Skorton challenged the system to "end pledging as we know it." Pollack's reforms followed on May 4, 2018, after Cornell upheld a three-year suspension of Sigma Nu for hazing. Hard alcohol was banned from chapter houses at any time. Hazing that involved forced drinking, sexual misconduct or violence now brought a suspension of at least three years (Cornell Chronicle).

Then on Oct. 24, 2019, student Antonio Tsialas was last seen at an unregistered party at Phi Kappa Psi. His body was found in Fall Creek Gorge two days later. In December 2019, Pollack wrote that six Greek organizations had their recognition suspended in the 19 months since her first reforms. She added independent event monitors, nightly security spot checks and an automatic interim suspension for any event that put students at risk. Recruitment had to be substance-free and end by 8 p.m. (Cornell Student & Campus Life).

An interim suspension shuts down the chapter but not the house. Cornell has defined it as a period when the chapter "may not engage in any activities other than operation of its residence" ([Cornell statement, 2017](https://statements.cornell.edu/2017/20170330-alpha-xi-delta.cfm)).

## The Chi Phi case and the 2026-27 review

The 2026-27 review traces to a 2024 report. On Nov. 8, 2024, someone reported to Cornell police being sexually assaulted by several men after being coerced into taking ketamine and other drugs at an Oct. 25 event. Police placed it on the 100 block of Edgemoor Lane. Chi Phi's national organization suspended its Cornell chapter, and the Interfraternity Council canceled social events for a weekend ([The Ithaca Voice](https://ithacavoice.org/2024/11/chi-phi-fraternity-suspended-from-cornell-university-campus-after-sexual-assault-and-drugging-reports/)).

On Sept. 14, 2026, the student, identified as Jane Doe, sued seven fraternity members, Cornell and others. Cornell said its investigation had ended and that the chapter "remains barred from our campus" ([Cornell Daily Sun](https://www.cornellsun.com/article/2026/09/university-releases-statement-on-alleged-gang-rape-at-chi-phi)). Thousands attended a rally on Ho Plaza on Oct. 5. On Oct. 7, Ithaca's Common Council approved a temporary moratorium on student event permits, which falls mostly on Greek organizations ([Cornell Daily Sun, October 2026](https://www.cornellsun.com/article/2026/10/breaking-cornell-suspends-all-fraternity-parties-social-events-for-2026-27-school-year)).

Six days later, on Oct. 13, 2026, Cornell President Michael I. Kotlikoff canceled every party and social event in the university's fraternity houses for the rest of the school year. Any chapter that holds one now faces an immediate emergency suspension ([Cornell Student & Campus Life](https://scl.cornell.edu/get-involved/sorority-fraternity-life/policies-and-procedures/presidential-actions)). The directive covers Interfraternity Council houses, not sororities or multicultural chapters. He said the pause would give Cornell time to decide "whether and how" fraternity life can safely continue. Cornell also hired former U.S. Deputy Attorney General Sally Q. Yates to review how it handles sexual misconduct. New York Attorney General Letitia James was appointed to re-examine the case ([CNN, Oct. 13](https://www.cnn.com/2026/10/13/us/cornell-suspends-fraternity-social-events)). Inside Higher Ed read the word "whether" as a sign that ending fraternities is on the table ([Inside Higher Ed](https://www.insidehighered.com/news/quick-takes/2026/10/14/cornell-bans-frat-parties-it-re-evaluates-greek-life)).

Some students and faculty want the system gone. Associate professor Andrew Moisey told a local station that Cornell has been "in an abusive relationship with fraternities" since 1873. Faculty debated a no-confidence vote in Kotlikoff. Kotlikoff told faculty the problem "doesn't only occur in fraternities" and that he worried about pushing it "into the shadows." A University of Connecticut researcher told CNN that when a university stops recognizing chapters, they can go on operating off campus without oversight (CNN, Oct. 17). At a campus town hall, one student organizer stressed the importance of Black fraternities and sororities, since Cornell is home to the first Black intercollegiate fraternity (CNN, Oct. 17).

Kotlikoff's order reached the parlors and the basements. It did not reach the bedrooms upstairs. Those rooms still count as approved housing for the second year of Cornell's two-year residential requirement (Cornell Residential Policy). Our [guide to the suspension and 2027-28 housing](/cornell/housing/cornell-fraternity-suspension-2026-housing-2027-28/) explains what it means for a plan to live in for 2027-28.

The review reaches into housing. Chi Phi's house at 107 Edgemoor Lane is the one the scorecard marks as university owned. Four suspended fraternities are scheduled to become eligible to return over the next three years: Phi Sigma Kappa and Zeta Psi in fall 2027, Phi Delta Theta in spring 2028 and Delta Tau Delta in spring 2029 (Cornell SFL scorecard). Cornell approves a chapter house for sophomores only while the chapter stays recognized ([Cornell Residential Policy](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/campus-housing/residential-policy)), so ask the Office of Sorority and Fraternity Life at greeks@cornell.edu about your chapter's standing before you affirm.

## Junior and senior years: living in, living out

After sophomore year, Cornell's requirement ends and the chapter's rules take over. The chapter still needs juniors and seniors in the house to meet the four-officer rule above. Members who move to Collegetown pay their rent plus the parlor fee. In 2018 a senior told the Sun her group found its Eddy Street apartment through older sorority members, and all they did was email the landlord (Cornell Daily Sun, 2018).

## What comes next

Three questions will shape chapter housing for 2027-28. The first is the review. Cornell has not yet announced a date for its findings, and the March affirmation deadline will come whether or not it has ended (Cornell Student & Campus Life). The second is the Yates review, which Kotlikoff said would look at all related Cornell policies. The third is which suspended chapters come back, and to which houses.

For a student choosing a house for 2027-28, the safe course is to join, affirm and file on Cornell's dates, and keep a fallback you can live with. For a sophomore, the fallback is April room selection. For anyone older, it is a lease or a Cornell room you can still cancel cheaply. Our [sophomore start page](/start/sophomore/) and [affiliated housing start page](/start/affiliated-housing/) lay out the rest of the year.
