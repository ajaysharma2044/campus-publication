```glance
- In the City of Ithaca, your landlord needs 120 days' written notice before renewing, showing or re-letting your unit, unless you initialed a bolded waiver on page one of your lease.
- A rent increase of 5% or more, or a non-renewal, needs 30, 60 or 90 days' written notice; a 12-month lease usually means 60.
- Under Good Cause (City of Ithaca, July 2024), an increase above the lower of 10% or 5% plus CPI is presumed unreasonable; buildings with a 2009-or-later certificate of occupancy are exempt.
- Heat season in Ithaca runs Sept 15 to May 31: 68F by day when it is under 55F outside, 62F at night. Ask for repairs in writing.
- Ask for a move-out inspection one to two weeks before you leave. The deposit or an itemized list is due within 14 days, or the landlord loses the right to keep any of it.
- Free tenant advice: the Cornell Law hotline, 607-301-1560.
```

If you rent in the City of Ithaca, the law is on your side more than most students assume. Your landlord must give [120 days' written notice before renewing or showing your unit](https://wskg.org/ithaca-landlords-must-now-give-renters-120-days-before-renewing-leases/) unless you initialed a waiver, owes heat from Sept 15 to May 31, and must return your deposit or an itemized statement [within 14 days of move-out](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/GOB/7-108). Put every request in writing, never withhold rent before getting advice, and ask for the move-out inspection.

```reader "If you're a senior whose lease runs past graduation"
Most Ithaca leases end July 31, so a May graduate still owes June and July. Three ways out: sublet, ask the landlord for a written early-termination agreement, or pay. In a building with four or more units you have a legal right to sublet; the landlord cannot unreasonably refuse, and silence after 30 days is consent ([RPL 226-b](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/RPP/226-B)). In a house of three or fewer units, your lease decides. Cornell aid does not cover rent in unregistered months ([Financial Aid](https://finaid.cornell.edu/living-campus)). Give your landlord a forwarding address in writing before you leave, because the deposit clock runs 14 days from move-out. The costly mistake: leaving in May without a sublet, so housemates or you carry two months' rent.
```

```reader "If you're in a building finished in 2009 or later"
Good Cause does not cover a building whose certificate of occupancy dates from January 1, 2009 or later; it is exempt for 30 years ([RPL 214](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/RPP/214)). The Ithacan, built 2023, was cited as exempt until 2053 ([Sun, 2024](https://cornellsun.com/2024/07/10/landlords-and-advocates-grapple-with-good-cause-eviction-law-at-public-hearing)). You keep everything else: the City's 120-day notice, the 30, 60 or 90 days' notice for a 5% or larger increase ([RPL 226-c](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/RPP/226-C)), the one-month deposit cap and the 14-day return. Your landlord's notice must say why the unit is exempt. The costly mistake: assuming no rule limits the increase, then missing that a late notice keeps your old rent until the notice period runs.
```

```reader "If you rent outside the City of Ithaca"
The 120-day rule is City of Ithaca code and stops at the city line, so the Town of Ithaca, Lansing and Cayuga Heights are not covered. Ithaca's Good Cause opt-in was a City vote ([WAMC](https://www.wamc.org/2024-07-12/ithaca-is-the-latest-upstate-city-to-opt-into-good-cause-renter-protections)). Not confirmed: whether those towns or villages have opted in. State law still covers you: the deposit cap and 14-day return, notice before a 5% or larger increase, habitability and retaliation protection. Repair complaints go to the Town of Ithaca (607-273-1783) or Town of Lansing (607-533-7054) code office, and small claims top out at $3,000 in town and village courts ([Cornell Law](https://sites.lawschool.cornell.edu/tenants-advocacy/building-issues-maintenance)). The hotline advises anyone; full representation is for City residents only.
```

```reader "If you're a tenant whose landlord won't answer"
Write it down: email or text with your name, address and the date, and send certified mail if you need proof ([Cornell Law](https://sites.lawschool.cornell.edu/tenants-advocacy/building-issues-maintenance)). Log every contact and take photos. If nothing happens, file with the City Building Division at 607-274-6508 or its online "Report a Concern" form ([City of Ithaca](https://www.cityofithacany.gov/130/Building-Division)). After 30 days in the unit and a written notice, you can petition a court to order repairs, a right since January 2024. Call 607-301-1560 before you hold back any rent. The costly mistake: withholding rent first, which invites a nonpayment case that starts with a 14-day demand.
```

```reader "If you're a parent paying the rent or deposit"
New York caps the deposit plus any rent advance at one month's rent ([GOL 7-108](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/GOB/7-108)), but at a renewal with higher rent the landlord may collect more to bring the deposit up to the new rent ([NY Attorney General](https://ag.ny.gov/resources/individuals/tenants-homeowners/tenants)). The deposit or an itemized statement is due 14 days after move-out; for a July 31 exit that is Aug 14, by our count. A willful violation can cost the landlord up to twice the deposit. Housemates on most local leases are jointly liable, so one leaving can shift rent onto your child. Not confirmed: how Ithaca landlords treat guarantors at renewal. The costly mistake: no move-out inspection, so the landlord's list is the only record.
```

```reader "If you're an international student"
A dispute with a landlord does not affect visa status or academic standing, Cornell Graduate School staff say ([Grad Tips](https://www.gradschool.cornell.edu/announcements/grad-tips-renting/)). With a lease, you cannot be evicted for immigration status unless there is a substantial lease or code violation, and denying heat or hot water as harassment is barred ([Cornell Law](https://sites.lawschool.cornell.edu/tenants-advocacy/immigrant-tenant-rights)). The tenant hotline, (607) 301-1560, gives advice with no income or geographic limits ([International Services](https://international.globallearning.cornell.edu/maintaining-your-status/legal-resources)). If you leave the country after the lease, give a forwarding address abroad in writing so the 14-day deposit return has somewhere to go. The costly mistake: flying home before the move-out inspection, with no photos of the unit.
```

## The renewal pitch and the 120-day waiver

Students are often asked to renew within weeks of moving in, a pattern [the Sun reported in 2012](https://cornellsun.com/2012/09/11/new-york-state-official%e2%80%88landlords-students-must-be-reasonable-people/) and again in 2022. In the City of Ithaca, a landlord must give current tenants at least 120 days' written notice before renewing your lease, showing the unit or signing new tenants. The rule took effect May 31, 2022, replacing a 60-day rule, and [covers leases of nine months or longer, as WSKG reported](https://wskg.org/ithaca-landlords-must-now-give-renters-120-days-before-renewing-leases/).

The catch is the waiver. It counts only if it is "explicitly written, bolded and initialed" on the lease's first page. So the first thing to check when a renewal offer lands in September is page one of your current lease. No initials, no waiver.

The rule's sponsor, Alderperson Patrick Mehler, wrote that [a discussion about why you are waiving the days](https://cornellsun.com/2022/04/18/mehler-student-tenants-rights-shifting-in-your-favor/) should be your first question. Compliance disputes can go to the City's housing court, City Attorney Ari Levine told WSKG; the article did not state penalties.

Not confirmed: whether the 120 days count back from your lease end or forward from its start. The code text could not be read, so this page gives no earliest renewal date.

Renewing is also the default. An expired lease continues month-to-month unless proper non-renewal notice is given, and an auto-renew clause needs a separate written reminder 15 to 30 days before your opt-out deadline ([Cornell Law](https://sites.lawschool.cornell.edu/tenants-advocacy/lease-renewal)). You owe no notice to leave at the end of a fixed-term lease.

## Rent increases: the 5% notice rule and Good Cause

Two laws apply at renewal. The first covers every non-regulated tenant in New York. Under [RPL 226-c](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/RPP/226-C), a landlord who will not renew, or who offers renewal at an increase of 5% or more, must give written notice based on the longer of your time in the unit or your lease term:

| Your situation | Notice required |
|---|---|
| Under one year in the unit and no one-year lease | 30 days |
| One to two years in the unit, or a lease of one to two years | 60 days |
| Over two years in the unit, or a lease of two years or more | 90 days |

An increase under 5% does not trigger this notice. If the notice comes late, your tenancy continues on the existing terms until the notice period runs. The notice must also say whether the unit is covered by Good Cause and, if not, why.

The second is Good Cause Eviction. Ithaca's Common Council [voted 8-2 to opt in](https://www.wamc.org/2024-07-12/ithaca-is-the-latest-upstate-city-to-opt-into-good-cause-renter-protections) in July 2024. A covered tenant has a right to renew, with exceptions such as nonpayment or an uncured violation after 10 days' written notice ([Cornell Law](https://sites.lawschool.cornell.edu/tenants-advocacy/lease-renewal)). An annual increase above the lower of 10% or 5% plus CPI is [presumed unreasonable](https://hcr.ny.gov/good-cause-eviction). That is a presumption a landlord can rebut, not a hard cap.

If you refuse reasonable renewal terms, the landlord can still remove you after written notice 30 to 90 days before the lease ends. Nonpayment tied to an unreasonable increase is not good cause ([RPL 216](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/RPP/216)).

Not confirmed: the CPI figure for 2026. DHCR usually publishes it by August 1.

Example: a junior on a 12-month lease pays $1,200 a month. The landlord offers renewal at $1,284, a 7% increase. Because it is 5% or more, 226-c requires 60 days' written notice. In a covered building, 7% is presumed unreasonable only if CPI is below 2%. Any increase above 10% is always over the line.

### Is your building covered?

Student apartments are not exempt as a category. Only dormitories run by colleges, such as Cornell's halls, are ([RPL 214](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/RPP/214)). The exemptions that actually remove student units:

| Exemption | What it means for a student renter |
|---|---|
| Certificate of occupancy on or after Jan 1, 2009 | Exempt for 30 years; most new Collegetown buildings |
| Small landlord | In Ithaca, a landlord with just one rental property ([WAMC](https://www.wamc.org/2024-07-12/ithaca-is-the-latest-upstate-city-to-opt-into-good-cause-renter-protections)) |
| Owner-occupied, 10 or fewer units | Exempt |
| High rent | Above 345% of fair market rent, about $4,402 a month for a 1BR on the 2023 figure ([Sun, 2024](https://cornellsun.com/2024/07/10/landlords-and-advocates-grapple-with-good-cause-eviction-law-at-public-hearing)) |
| Condos, co-ops, rent-regulated or seasonal units | Exempt |

The high-rent line rarely matters for students. The building's age and the one-unit landlord rule are what usually take a student unit out. Your landlord's 226-c notice has to say which one applies.

## Heat season and repairs in writing

Every New York lease carries a [warranty of habitability](https://sites.lawschool.cornell.edu/tenants-advocacy/building-issues-maintenance). It covers no heat, no hot or cold water, electricity, plumbing, pests, mold, structural defects and broken landlord-supplied appliances. Lack of air conditioning generally does not qualify.

| Rule | Ithaca standard |
|---|---|
| Heat season | Sept 15 to May 31 (statewide Oct 1 to May 31) |
| 6 a.m. to 10 p.m. | At least 68F inside when it is below 55F outside |
| 10 p.m. to 6 a.m. | At least 62F, whatever the outside temperature |
| Bed bugs | Landlord must exterminate and notify neighboring units in writing within 72 hours |
| Heat shutoff (landlord pays the utility) | At least 30 days' notice between Nov 1 and Apr 15 |

Ask in writing, by letter, text or email, with your name, address and the date. There is no strict statutory repair deadline, so ask the landlord for a timeframe. For bed bugs, report in writing and do not treat them yourself, which can weaken a damages claim.

If nothing happens, file with code enforcement: the City Building Division at 607-274-6508 or [its online "Report a Concern" form](https://www.cityofithacany.gov/130/Building-Division). After living in the unit at least 30 days and sending written notice, you can petition the court for an order directing repairs, a right since January 2024.

Rent abatement is the money remedy. The Cornell Law guide's court experience: about 10% to 20% off for minor problems, about 30% for moderate ones and 50% to 60% for the most serious. Withholding rent and repair-and-deduct are last resorts. Withholding invites a nonpayment case, which starts with a 14-day written demand ([Cornell Law](https://sites.lawschool.cornell.edu/tenants-advocacy/eviction-proceedings)).

Example: four housemates each pay $1,246.25 a month, the 4BR median on [Ithaca Renting's 2027-28 Collegetown rows](https://ithacarenting.com/collegetown/). If a court treated a weeks-long heat failure as moderate, a 30% abatement would be about $374 a person for the month. That is our arithmetic on the guide's ranges, not a promise of what a judge awards.

How slow it can get: in 2018, tenants at 117 Thurston Ave waited [53 days from their first complaint letter to a fixed heater](https://cornellsun.com/2018/11/25/cornell-student-abandons-house-sleeps-in-library-after-months-long-dispute-with-landlord/), and one slept in a campus library. The Council stiffened penalties in 2019.

## Retaliation: a complaint is protected

A Solidarity Slate candidate told [the Sun in 2021](https://cornellsun.com/2021/07/23/ithaca-tenants-union-and-solidarity-slate-join-regional-efforts-toward-housing-equity/) that college students are often afraid to call their landlord about repairs for fear of getting evicted. The law answers it. A landlord may not refuse to renew or raise rent unreasonably because of a good-faith complaint made within the past year. An eviction within a year of a complaint can be defended as retaliation ([Cornell Law](https://sites.lawschool.cornell.edu/tenants-advocacy/lease-renewal)). The exception: owner-occupied buildings with fewer than four units. Keep the dated copy of your complaint; it is your evidence.

## The senior's June and July

A lease ending July 31 runs two months past May graduation. Your options, in the order that costs least:

1. **Sublet.** In a building with four or more units, send the request by certified mail with the term, the subtenant's name and addresses, the reason, your address during the term, co-tenant and guarantor consent, and a copy of the sublease. The landlord may ask for more within 10 days and must answer within 30; [silence is consent](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/RPP/226-B). You stay liable for the subtenant's rent and damage.
2. **Ask for early termination in writing**, with the move-out date and the amount owed. A termination fee may be treated as liquidated damages and [is not always enforceable](https://sites.lawschool.cornell.edu/tenants-advocacy/breaking-a-lease).
3. **Pay.** Cornell aid does not cover rent in unregistered months ([Financial Aid](https://finaid.cornell.edu/living-campus)).

The full sublet and early-exit routes are in [How to sublet an Ithaca apartment](/housing/cornell-summer-sublet-how-to-sublet-apartment-ithaca/) and [Breaking or leaving an Ithaca lease](/housing/breaking-a-lease-early-ithaca-cornell/).

## Move-out and the 14-day deposit

The deposit rules are New York's [General Obligations Law 7-108](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/GOB/7-108). When either side gives notice to end the tenancy, the landlord must tell you in writing that you can request a move-out inspection. If you ask, it happens between two weeks and one week before the end, with at least 48 hours' written notice, and the landlord lists intended charges so you can fix them.

The two-weeks and one-week checklist is [Cornell Off-Campus Living's](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/campus-housing/housing-search-process/moving-and-out). What the landlord can deduct, per the [Cornell Law guide](https://sites.lawschool.cornell.edu/tenants-advocacy/security-deposits): unpaid rent, damage beyond normal wear, unpaid utilities owed to the landlord, moving and storing belongings you left, and excessive cleaning and trash removal. Routine turnover cleaning cannot be deducted, and a separate cleaning fee is banned. Cornell's own move-out page says cleaning charges will be deducted; trust the law over that wording.

Late fees and attorney's fees cannot come out of the deposit. If the landlord misses the 14 days, it forfeits the right to keep any of it. A willful violation can bring punitive damages up to twice the deposit, and the landlord must prove withheld amounts were reasonable. In buildings with six or more units, the deposit must sit in an interest-bearing account, minus a 1% annual fee the landlord may keep.

Do not leave a couch or mattress at the curb. The City bars [couches, mattresses and appliances stored outdoors](https://www.cityofithacany.gov/136/Exterior-Property-Maintenance), and garbage goes out no more than 24 hours before pickup. Each bag or can needs a tag, [$6.00 each or $36.00 for a sheet of six](https://www.cityofithacany.gov/168/Trash-Yard-Waste-Collection-Tags) since Jan 1, 2026.

Example: your deposit is $1,246.25. You move out July 31 and hear nothing by Aug 14. The landlord has lost the right to keep any of it. If a court finds the withholding willful, it can award up to $2,492.50 in punitive damages on top, within Ithaca City Court's $5,000 small-claims limit.

## When it goes badly wrong

If the City orders your building vacated, landlords owe displaced tenants [double the monthly rent, prorated per day](https://www.cornellsun.com/article/2019/03/city-council-votes-to-stiffen-penalties-for-noncompliant-landlords), under a 2019 rule. In March 2026, [Asteri residents were offered $2,000 to leave within five days](https://www.cornellsun.com/article/2026/03/asteri-s-aftermath-displacement-dangerous-conditions-and-a-predatory-cash-for-keys-scheme) and release their claims, an offer a city official and the Ithaca Tenants Union called predatory. Do not sign a release before advice.

An eviction cannot happen overnight. A 14-day rent demand comes first, and a court petition must be served 10 to 17 days before the hearing. You can usually ask for one 14-day adjournment, and a judge can delay an eviction up to a year for hardship ([Cornell Law](https://sites.lawschool.cornell.edu/tenants-advocacy/eviction-proceedings)).

## Common mistakes

- **Initialing the 120-day waiver without asking why.** You give up the notice window before your unit is shown or re-let.
- **Withholding rent before getting advice.** It invites a 14-day rent demand and a nonpayment case.
- **Asking for repairs only by phone.** Without a written, dated record, an abatement claim is weaker; self-treating bed bugs weakens it further.
- **Skipping the move-out inspection and photos.** The landlord's list of charges becomes the only record of the unit.
- **Leaving furniture at the curb.** It breaks City rules, and removing what you left can come out of the deposit.
- **Taking a cash-for-keys offer in a vacate order.** The City rule owes double rent per day; the Asteri offer was $2,000 for a release.
- **A senior leaving in May with no sublet.** Two months of rent fall on you or your jointly liable housemates.

## FAQ

**Can my landlord raise the deposit when I renew?** Yes. On a renewal at higher rent, the landlord may collect more to bring the deposit up to the new monthly rent ([NY Attorney General](https://ag.ny.gov/resources/individuals/tenants-homeowners/tenants)).

**Can my landlord enter to show the apartment to next year's tenants?** Only if the lease reserves entry, with [reasonable notice, often 24 hours](https://sites.lawschool.cornell.edu/tenants-advocacy/landlord-access), at reasonable hours. In the City, showing the unit also falls under the 120-day rule unless you waived it ([WSKG](https://wskg.org/ithaca-landlords-must-now-give-renters-120-days-before-renewing-leases/)).

**Do I need renters insurance?** Cornell's checklist says to buy it and names GradGuard as its preferred insurer, with no price published ([Cornell](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/campus-living/living-campus)). Quotes run from about [$5 a month](https://www.safebutler.com/cheap-best-renters-insurance-ithaca-ny) for Ithaca to [$27 a month](https://www.insure.com/renters-insurance/average-renters-insurance-cost-in-new-york/) as a New York average, depending on the source and coverage. Not confirmed: whether Collegetown landlords require it.

**What if a new owner buys my building?** A new owner that knew of your deposit is liable for it ([GOL 7-108](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/GOB/7-108)).

**Can I end my lease because of domestic violence?** Yes, on 30 days' written notice with documentation within 25 days; prepaid rent comes back within 10 days. The Advocacy Center of Tompkins County is at (607) 277-5000 ([Cornell Law](https://sites.lawschool.cornell.edu/tenants-advocacy/breaking-a-lease)).
