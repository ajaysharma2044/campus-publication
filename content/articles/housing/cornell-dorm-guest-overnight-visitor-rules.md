```glance
- An overnight guest can stay up to three nights in any seven-day period.
- Get every roommate's permission first, and register the guest on Cornell's online form.
- Guests sleep in your room only: never in a lounge, hallway or suite common room.
- No overnight guests under age 16, and never lend your key or Cornell ID.
```

Cornell lets a resident host an overnight guest for up to three nights in any seven-day period. Your roommates must agree first, you must register the guest, and you must stay with that person whenever they are in the building ([Community Living Standards 2026-27](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/housing-details/community-rules-standards/community-living-standards-undergrads)).

## Who counts as a guest, a visitor and a host?

A **visitor** is a Cornell student who doesn't live in the room or building they're visiting. A **guest** is anyone with no Cornell connection, such as a high school friend, a sibling or a parent. A **host** is the resident assigned to the room being visited.

The same rules cover visitors and guests. A visitor, as a Cornell student, also answers to university conduct rules. A guest answers through you.

## How many nights can a guest stay in a Cornell dorm?

Three nights in any seven-day period. The window rolls, so count back seven days from any night you plan.

Say your partner from home stays Friday, Saturday and Sunday. That uses all three nights. A fourth night before the next Friday breaks the rule. The same guest can come back the next weekend, because no seven-day stretch would then hold more than three nights.

The standards don't say whether the limit counts per guest or per room. If you plan back-to-back visits from different people, ask your Residence Hall Director (RHD), the full-time staff member who runs your building, before the first one. On West Campus, that person is the House Assistant Dean (HAD).

## Do you need your roommate's permission?

Yes, before any overnight stay, and a roommate can withdraw that permission. A visitor or guest can be asked to leave at any time, daytime visits included, if a roommate doesn't want them there. The roommate who says no wins.

The housing license, the contract you sign for your room, backs this up. Only you and the roommates Cornell assigned may live in the room. You cannot let anyone else share any part of it ([Housing License 2026-27](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/housing-details/rates-amenities-licenses/academic-year-housing-license-2026-2027)). A guest who stays week after week starts to look like an unassigned roommate, which the license forbids.

## How do you register an overnight guest?

In an undergraduate hall or West Campus house, register every overnight guest or visitor, Cornell students included, with Housing and Residential Life or the West Campus House Office. You do it on the [Guest Registration Form](https://cornell.guardianconduct.com/student-portal/new-report?incident_type=residence%20hall/co-op%20overnight%20guest%20registration), an online form you open with your NetID.

## Where can a guest sleep, and how many people fit in a room?

In your room only, never in lounges, suite common rooms, hallways or laundry rooms.

Fire-code occupancy limits cap each room, and they count everyone, residents included.

A double holds you, your roommate and two guests at most. If your roommate also has a guest that night, the room is full.

## Can a guest have a key or use your Cornell ID?

No. Cornell won't issue room keys or temporary access cards to visitors or guests, and you can't hand over your own key or ID card.

Exterior doors stay locked, and only a guest's host can let them in. Service Center and residential staff open a room only for its residents, not for parents or friends, except in an emergency judged by a full-time staff member.

## Do guests follow quiet hours and alcohol rules?

Yes, the same ones you do ([Community Living Standards 2026-27](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/housing-details/community-rules-standards/community-living-standards-undergrads)). Quiet hours begin at 11 p.m. Sunday through Thursday and at midnight Friday and Saturday. They end at 7:30 a.m. Monday through Friday and 9 a.m. on weekends. A student 21 or older can drink only in a room where everyone present is also 21 or older.

## What happens if a guest breaks the rules?

You answer for it, including any damage your visitors and guests cause to the building.

A complaint goes to your RHD or HAD, who can call you to a student conduct meeting and decides there whether you broke the Community Living Standards.

Serious or repeated problems reach the housing license. Cornell can revoke it, move you to other housing or take disciplinary action. A revoked license still leaves you owing housing charges for the whole term, plus a $500 revocation fee ([Housing License 2026-27](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/housing-details/rates-amenities-licenses/academic-year-housing-license-2026-2027)).

## Can you feed a guest on your meal plan?

Yes, through guest meals built into most plans. You cannot spend Big Red Bucks, the dining cash on your card, on a guest's meal ([Cornell Dining](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/dining/meal-plans-rates/undergraduate-meal-plans)).

## Do the rules change in co-ops, Hasbrouck or program houses?

Only in the details. The escort, roommate consent, no keys for guests and no sleeping in common areas hold everywhere Cornell runs housing; registration, age limits and night limits differ.

## What if you live off campus?

Your lease sets the guest rules, not Cornell. Cornell's own lease checklist tells renters to look for charges for overnight or weekend guests and for limits on parties and noise before signing ([Signing the Lease](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/living-off-campus/housing-search-process/signing-lease)).

New York law lets a tenant have someone live with them, within a cap. On a lease with several tenants, the total of tenants plus other occupants can't exceed the number of tenants on the lease. You must give the landlord an occupant's name within 30 days of move-in or of the landlord asking ([N.Y. Real Property Law 235-f](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/RPP/235-F)). A short visit doesn't make someone an occupant. A friend who moves in for the semester does.

Housemates share the consequences of a guest who damages the apartment, which is why Cornell's [Housemate Compatibility Questionnaire](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/living-off-campus/tenant-best-practices/study-abroad/housemate-compatibility-questionnaire) asks, "Do you mind if housemates have overnight guests?" Settle that answer before you sign. How a shared lease splits blame and bills is in our guide to [off-campus roommates and joint liability](/cornell/housing/cornell-off-campus-roommates-joint-liability/), and the city's noise rules for a crowded weekend are in our [Ithaca trash, snow and noise rules guide](/cornell/housing/ithaca-trash-tags-snow-noise-rules-renters/).

```reader "If you're a parent"
Parents get no exception: you need every roommate's agreement and count against the occupancy limit. Younger siblings under 16 can't stay overnight at all. Plan a hotel for family weekends. Our [parent guide](/start/parent/) covers the rest of the first year.
```

## Can a guest park on campus or in a city permit area?

If you rent in a City of Ithaca residential permit area, the city sells residents a visitor pass for $10 ([City of Ithaca](https://www.cityofithacany.gov/731/Permit-Issuance)). Campus visitor parking is in our guide to [getting around Cornell for parents and visitors](/cornell/getting-around/getting-around-cornell-parents-visitors/).

## What to do next

Before you invite anyone, talk to your roommates and agree on how often guests can stay. Then register each overnight guest on the online form before the first night, and plan where they will eat.
