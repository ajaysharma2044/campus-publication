
Split the rent in two parts. Shared space, like the kitchen, living room and hallways, is divided evenly. Each person pays for their own bedroom's square feet. Then the group puts a dollar value on perks such as a private bathroom and shifts that money to whoever has one. The total never changes, and the calculator below does the math.

## How do you split rent fairly when bedrooms are different sizes?

You split by floor area, because that is what rent buys. Start with the whole apartment's square footage, from the listing or the lease. Subtract every bedroom. What is left is shared space, and everyone uses it equally, so everyone pays for an equal slice. Each bedroom is used by one person, so that person pays for it alone.

The formula for one room's base share is rent x (bedroom square feet + shared square feet / number of people) / apartment square feet. A bigger bedroom raises your share only by the extra floor it adds. An even split ignores size completely, and a split by bedroom size alone overcharges the big room, because it treats the kitchen as if it belonged to the bedrooms.

A private bathroom, a window or a closet makes a room worth more than its floor, so the group prices those perks next.

## How much is a private bathroom or a better room worth?

A private bathroom is worth about $100 a month per person in Collegetown, based on how one large landlord prices its own units. Ithaca Renting, which posts its full per-bed price list, rents three kinds of four-bedroom apartments at Collegetown Plaza, 111 Dryden Rd, for 2027-28. The Premium 4-bedroom with one bathroom lists at $1,185 to $1,200 per bed a month. The Premium 4-bedroom with 1.5 bathrooms lists at $1,250 to $1,350. The Deluxe 4-bedroom with two bathrooms lists at $1,295 to $1,395 ([Ithaca Renting](https://ithacarenting.com/collegetown/)).

The two Premium layouts carry the same label, so the bathroom is the clearest difference between them. At the midpoints of their ranges, $1,192.50 and $1,300, the extra half bathroom adds $107.50 per bed a month. That is the calculator's default value for a private bathroom. The Deluxe label can also mean better finishes, so its higher price says less about bathrooms alone. ([Ithaca Renting](https://ithacarenting.com/collegetown/))

The same landlord prices rooms, not just apartments. At 210 Dryden Rd, where tenants rent single rooms around shared kitchens and baths, a Standard Room lists at $695 to $810 a month and a Premium Room at $850 to $950. At the midpoints, $752.50 and $900, the better room costs $147.50 more. The listing does not say what makes a room Premium. It does show that one landlord charges more for a better room than for an extra half bath ([Ithaca Renting](https://ithacarenting.com/collegetown/)).

## How do you move money for a perk without changing the total?

The rooms with a perk pay a little more and the rooms without it pay a little less, so the landlord still gets exactly the rent. Say a perk is worth P a month and k of the n rooms have it. Each room with it pays P x (n - k) / n more. Each room without it pays P x k / n less. A room with the perk ends up exactly P above a room without it.

If every room has the perk, it changes nothing. That is why a closet only matters when one room lacks one.

## A worked example: a four-bedroom on Dryden Road

Take the Collegetown Plaza Premium 4-bedroom with 1.5 bathrooms. At the midpoint per bed ([Ithaca Renting](https://ithacarenting.com/collegetown/)), the unit costs 4 x $1,300 = $5,200 a month. Say the apartment is 1,100 square feet, the bedrooms measure 150, 130, 120 and 100 square feet, and Room 1 has the half bath to itself.

The bedrooms add up to 500 square feet, so shared space is 1,100 - 500 = 600, or 150 square feet each, priced off the [Ithaca Renting](https://ithacarenting.com/collegetown/) rent above. Room 1's base share is (150 + 150) / 1,100 x $5,200 = $1,418.18. With the bathroom valued at $107.50, Room 1 pays three-quarters of that ($80.63) more, and the other three each pay one-quarter ($26.88) less.

The biggest room with the bath pays $343.87 a month more than the smallest. Over a 12-month lease, the Room 4 tenant saves $145.06 x 12 = $1,740.72 against an even split of the [Ithaca Renting](https://ithacarenting.com/collegetown/) rent.

```reader "If you're a sophomore signing for junior year"
Settle who gets which room, and what each pays, before anyone signs. Once the lease is signed the group has no lever, and the person who claimed the big room first has no reason to pay more. Measure the rooms on the showing, or ask the landlord for a floor plan. More on the year ahead is on the [sophomore start page](/start/sophomore/).
```

```reader "If you're on a tight budget"
Offer to take the smallest room, or the one without the bathroom, in exchange for a lower share. In the example above that saves $145.06 a month. Check the room in person first: a small room still needs space for a bed, a desk and your things for a full year.
```

```reader "If you're a parent co-signing"
Your student's share in the group's split does not limit what you sign for. Read the guaranty itself to see whether it covers your student's share or the whole unit's rent.
```

## Does a fair split change what you owe the landlord?

No. Your split is a private deal among roommates, and the lease ignores it. Most Ithaca leases bind housemates "jointly and severally," which means each of you is responsible for the others' rent and damages for the whole lease ([Cornell Off-Campus Living](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/campus-living/living-campus/study-abroad/housemate-compatibility-questionnaire)). If the Room 1 tenant stops paying $1,498.81, the landlord can collect it from anyone on the lease.

You still have a way to get the money back. A tenant who pays more than their share can sue the co-tenant who failed to pay, a right the law calls contribution ([Cornell Law School Tenants Advocacy](https://sites.lawschool.cornell.edu/tenants-advocacy/understanding-your-lease)). A signed, written split is your best evidence of what each person owed. Our guide to [roommates and joint liability](/cornell/housing/cornell-off-campus-roommates-joint-liability/) covers what happens when someone leaves.

Before you sign, check whether the lease is joint or by the room. Our guide on [how to read an Ithaca lease](/cornell/housing/how-to-read-ithaca-lease-before-signing/) shows where to look.

## What goes wrong, and how to fix it

**Nobody measured.** A group guesses at sizes, then fights about them in September. Fix: measure every bedroom on the same day with the same tape, and write the numbers into the agreement.

**The perk values come late.** Once rooms are picked, everyone argues for the value that helps them. Fix: agree on the dollar value of each perk before anyone chooses a room. Then choose.

**The deposit is split evenly.** The rent split is uneven but the deposit is not, so the shares don't match. Fix: split the deposit by the same shares. In New York a deposit can't be more than one month's rent, and the landlord must return it with an itemized list within 14 days after you move out ([N.Y. General Obligations Law 7-108](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/GOB/7-108)). Agree in writing how the group will divide any deductions. The rules for getting it back are in our [security deposit guide](/cornell/housing/ithaca-security-deposit-rules-tenants/).

**Someone wants to renegotiate mid-year.** Fix: write into the agreement that the split holds for the full lease unless everyone agrees in writing to change it.

If a dispute stalls, the Community Dispute Resolution Center mediates roommate conflicts at 607-273-9347 ([Cornell Off-Campus Living resources](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/campus-living/campus-living-resources)). For legal questions about a lease, call the Cornell Law Tenants Advocacy hotline at 607-301-1560 ([Tenants Advocacy](https://sites.lawschool.cornell.edu/tenants-advocacy/tenants-rights/)).

## FAQ

**What if two people share one bedroom?**
Enter that room once in the calculator. The two people then split that room's share between them, usually in half.

**What if the landlord already prices each room separately?**
Then the landlord has set the split, and each person pays their own room's price. The calculator is for a group renting a whole unit at one total rent.
