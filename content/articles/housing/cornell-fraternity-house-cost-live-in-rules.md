```glance
- Living out can cost a parlor fee: a median of $1,152 at fraternities and $602 at sororities.
- Nearly all chapters require a semester or two of living in, and four officers must live in.
- Cornell publishes no rent figure of its own, so ask your chapter for its charges in writing.
```

A Cornell chapter house charges what its members vote and its owner sets, and Cornell collects the numbers on a public scorecard. Live-in housing and dining runs from $2,000 to $14,673 for one spring semester, and the median is $7,400 ([Organization Scorecard](https://scl.cornell.edu/get-involved/sorority-fraternity-life/organization-scorecard)).

## How much does a Cornell fraternity or sorority house cost?

Cornell's Sorority and Fraternity Life office posts each chapter's self-reported live-in charge for housing and dining and its live-out charge for one spring semester. The charges are for spring 2023, 2025 or 2026, depending on the chapter. The scorecard lists 34 chapters with rates. The low end belongs to Delta Kappa Epsilon (spring 2026) and the high end to Alpha Xi Delta (spring 2025). Beta Theta Pi lists $0. Among the 13 chapters with a nonzero charge for spring 2026, the median is the figure above. Across all 33 nonzero charges from 2023 to 2026, the median is $7,150 ([Organization Scorecard](https://scl.cornell.edu/get-involved/sorority-fraternity-life/organization-scorecard)).

The last row is arithmetic on Cornell's 2026-27 rates: a residence hall double is $6,872 per semester ([Cornell Housing rates](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/housing-and-residential-life-and-faculty-involvement-campus/rates-amenities-licenses/housing-rates)), and the Bear Traditional meal plan is $3,528 ([Undergraduate Meal Plans](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/dining/meal-plans-rates/undergraduate-meal-plans)). The two add up to $10,400. The chapter median is $3,000 lower. Only Alpha Xi Delta and Sigma Pi charge more than a hall double plus a meal plan.

Two caveats apply to every number above. Chapters report their own charges, so the scorecard is a starting point, not a bill ([Organization Scorecard](https://scl.cornell.edu/get-involved/sorority-fraternity-life/organization-scorecard)). And a charge for spring does not tell you the fall rate, so ask the treasurer for the full year. If the fall charge matched spring, a year at the median would be $14,800 (two semesters at the median), against $20,800 for a hall double and Bear Traditional.

## What does the rent cover?

Rent pays for housekeeping, utilities, internet, repairs and insurance on the building. It does not cover your belongings ([Financial Obligations](https://scl.cornell.edu/get-involved/sorority-fraternity-life/join/financial-obligations-sororities-fraternities)). Cornell's page prints no cost figures. It says only that chapter houses are expensive to maintain and operate, which is why the scorecard figures matter. Rent is mostly billed through the Bursar, Cornell's student account office, and room and board fees vary ([Affiliated Housing](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/campus-living/qualified-off-campus-housing)). For a renters policy on your own things, see the [renters insurance guide](/cornell/housing/cornell-off-campus-renters-insurance-cost/).

Dues and fees come on top of rent, and each chapter sets its own. Ask for them in writing, along with the length of the live-in term.

## What is a parlor fee, and what does a meal plan add?

A parlor fee is what a member pays to belong to a house without living in it. Live-out charges on the scorecard run from $0 to $4,600 at Sigma Chi (spring 2025). The median is $1,152 across 23 fraternities and $602 across 11 sororities ([Organization Scorecard](https://scl.cornell.edu/get-involved/sorority-fraternity-life/organization-scorecard)).

Meals are a separate question. Cornell's SFL meal-plan page defines on-campus housing as the North and South Campus residence halls, the West Campus House System, program houses, McGraw Place Residences and the Townhouse Apartments, and chapter houses are not on that list. Members who live out can buy the SFL Supplemental plan, called Bear Supplemental: $2,088.77 per semester, or $4,177.54 per year, for 127 meals per semester. It is open to recognized members living off campus, or on campus if the chapter requires a plan of at least seven meals a week for members who live out ([SFL Supplemental](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/dining/meal-plans-rates/bear-supplemental)). The switch deadlines are Sept. 30, 2026, and Feb. 10, 2027.

## Who has to live in the house?

The chapter decides, and nearly all of them require living in for a semester or two. Sophomores face an extra rule: to count a chapter house as second-year housing, you must be a member when you file the housing application ([Residential Policy](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/campus-housing/residential-policy)). The sophomore path, including the March 15 confirmation, is in the [co-op and Greek house guide](/cornell/housing/cornell-co-ops-greek-houses-cost-how-to-join/).

Chapters also owe Cornell live-in leadership. Since 2021-22, four officers, including the president, must be juniors or seniors who live in the house. Each chapter hires its own live-in advisor, and Cornell does not ([Presidential Actions](https://scl.cornell.edu/get-involved/sorority-fraternity-life/policies-and-procedures/presidential-actions)). A recognized chapter earns automatic approval to house second-years when it has an advisor and four upper-level officers by Jan. 1 each year.

## Who owns the houses?

The scorecard labels 15 houses as privately owned and one as university owned, Chi Phi at 107 Edgemoor Lane. The rest carry no label. Of the 15 chapters in the Multicultural Greek Council, 12 list the Office of Sorority and Fraternity Life at 136 Ho Plaza as their address, so most of them have no house ([Organization Scorecard](https://scl.cornell.edu/get-involved/sorority-fraternity-life/organization-scorecard)). Depending on the chapter, the building belongs to Cornell, an alumni group or a national organization ([Affiliated Housing](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/campus-living/qualified-off-campus-housing)).

Cornell's affiliated-housing rules on insurance, sprinklers and yearly city inspections apply to non-Greek houses only ([Cornell Housing](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/campus-housing/requirements-qualified-university-recognized-campus-housing)). A fraternity or sorority house needs a City of Ithaca Certificate of Compliance, and the owner pays $500 for it ([City of Ithaca](https://www.cityofithacany.gov/147/Certificate-of-Compliance-Fees)).

## What should you watch before you commit?

On Oct. 13, 2026, President Michael I. Kotlikoff suspended all parties and social events in Interfraternity Council houses for the rest of 2026-27, pending a review of fraternity life ([Cornell Daily Sun](https://www.cornellsun.com/article/2026/10/breaking-cornell-suspends-all-fraternity-parties-social-events-for-2026-27-school-year)). A chapter on interim suspension cannot run any activity except the operation of its residence ([Cornell statement](https://statements.cornell.edu/2017/20170330-alpha-xi-delta.cfm)). A chapter can house second-years only while Cornell recognizes it, so ask the chapter whether it is in good standing before you sign anything.

The scorecard lists return dates for suspended chapters: Phi Sigma Kappa and Zeta Psi in fall 2027, Phi Delta Theta in spring 2028 and Delta Tau Delta in spring 2029. Chi Phi is listed on interim suspension ([Organization Scorecard](https://scl.cornell.edu/get-involved/sorority-fraternity-life/organization-scorecard)).

## What to do next

1. Open the scorecard and find your chapter's live-in and live-out charges.
2. Ask the treasurer for fall and spring charges, dues and the live-in term in writing.
3. Compare the total with the hall double plus a meal plan, or with a co-op, which is cheaper still ([co-op guide](/cornell/housing/cornell-co-ops-greek-houses-cost-how-to-join/)).
4. If you are a sophomore, check the [junior-year housing guide](/cornell/housing/off-campus-housing-cornell-sophomores/) before you sign a lease.
5. If you plan to live out as an underclass student, read the [exemptions guide](/cornell/housing/cornell-residential-requirement-exemptions-live-off-campus/).
