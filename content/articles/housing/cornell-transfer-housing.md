If you transfer to Cornell as a first-year or sophomore, you live on campus for your first academic year, and Cornell assigns you a room rather than guaranteeing a specific one. You apply through the Housing & Dining Portal [within ten days of getting your NetID](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/housing-details/apply-housing/new-undergrad-application-process), rank your preferences, hear back from mid-June, and most likely land on West Campus, South Campus or in a Program House alongside other transfers. A double runs [$6,872 per semester for 2026-27](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/housing-details/rates-amenities-licenses/housing-rates), meal plan not included. Here is the whole process, what you can control, and where transfers have ended up.

## Do you have to live on campus?

Almost always, yes. Cornell has a two-year residential requirement for new undergraduates, and the transfer version is one academic year: [all new transfer students who have not already met the requirement elsewhere must live on campus for one academic year](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/housing-details/apply-housing-campus). If you start in the spring, the rule covers that spring semester plus the following full academic year. Living on campus comes with a required meal plan.

There are two exemptions, both on the [transfer housing page](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/campus-housing/new-transfer-undergraduates): you already lived on campus for two years at your previous school, or you are matriculating as a junior or senior. If either fits, you can skip the dorms and look in Collegetown or downtown instead (see our [housing guides](/housing/)). You request the exemption inside the housing application itself.

1. **April 1.** The 2026-27 new student application [opens at 12:00 noon on April 1](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/housing-details/apply-housing-campus). You cannot start it without an activated Cornell NetID, so set that up the day your admission packet tells you how.
2. **Within ten days of your NetID.** Cornell asks transfers to [apply within ten days of receiving the NetID](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/housing-details/apply-housing/new-undergrad-application-process). The deadline is relative to you, not a calendar date, which is why two transfers admitted a week apart have different cut-offs.
3. **Mid-June onward.** Confirmations go out [beginning in mid-June](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/housing-details/apply-housing/new-undergrad-application-process): either a building and room, or a spot on the guaranteed waitlist with weekly updates while rooms open up.
4. **Summer.** Once you have an assignment and a signed license, you pick a move-in timeslot in the portal. For fall 2025 the timeslot deadline was [August 1, and new students moved in on August 18 and 19](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/housing-details/moving-campus/move-schedule); the 2026 page was not posted when we checked.

## Where transfers actually get placed

Cornell says transfers [may be placed in the West Campus House System, the South Campus residence halls, the North Campus residence halls, and Program Houses](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/campus-housing/new-transfer-undergraduates), and that transfer rooms are grouped together whenever possible so you arrive next to other new students. A small number of transfers who qualify as first-years go into first-year housing.

- **West Campus** is the five-house system (Alice Cook, Carl Becker, Flora Rose, Hans Bethe, William Keeton) plus the Townhouse Apartments. Each house has its own dining room.
- **South Campus** is the cluster near Collegetown: Cascadilla Hall, Schuyler House, Sheldon Court, 112 Edgemoor, 104 Mary Ann Wood Drive and 109 McGraw Place. Closest to Collegetown food and nightlife, farthest from the Ag quad.
- **Program Houses** are themed halls open to first-years and upper-level students, from Risley (arts) to Holland International Living Center to Loving House. You join one simply by [listing it as a preference and acknowledging its mission](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/campus-housing/new-transfer-undergraduates/program-houses). More than [500 first-year and transfer students live in Program Houses](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/housing-details/apply-housing/new-undergrad-application-process) each year.

What that looks like in practice: a [2025 College Confidential thread](https://talk.collegeconfidential.com/t/cornell-transfer-co-2027-28-for-fall-2025/3684687) from the fall 2025 transfer cycle says transfers are placed after current students, that most land in a West Campus house, and that in at least one recent year many went to Cascadilla and Schuyler on South Campus. Posters there also stress that West is not guaranteed. One described being placed in a suite with four other transfers, which matches Cornell's grouping policy.

The history matters too. The Cornell Daily Sun reported in [2016 that some transfers were temporarily housed in North Campus lounges](https://cornellsun.com/2016/10/03/transfer-students-forced-to-live-in-north-campus-lounges/) and in [2018 that transfers did not have a housing guarantee and some learned their assignment in June](https://cornellsun.com/2018/12/04/isolated-vulnerable-and-nervous-transfer-students-express-frustration-over-not-being-able-to-live-on-campus/). Since then the residential requirement has made on-campus housing mandatory for new transfers, which cuts both ways: you are housed, but you do not choose.

## What it costs

Rates are the same in fall and spring and are per person. Every room includes gas, electricity, garbage pickup and internet up to 200 GB a month, per the [2026-27 rate page](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/housing-details/rates-amenities-licenses/housing-rates). The meal plan is separate.

Co-ops are the cheap option and the one most transfers never hear about. Cornell runs eight cooperative houses that it calls [among the least expensive housing options on campus](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/campus-housing/new-transfer-undergraduates/cooperative-housing); most offer singles and doubles, four have meal plans and all have shared kitchens. Rates vary by house and are not on the central rate page, so you ask each house. Membership is handled by the houses themselves, with selection starting in early February and rolling after that. If you are admitted in the spring or know early, a co-op is worth a call to the Housing Office at 607-255-5368.

## Roommates and room type

You can request a single, double, triple or quad, and Cornell [will do its best](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/housing-details/apply-housing/new-undergrad-application-process) but does not promise the type. You either fill out a short lifestyle questionnaire for matching or name a specific roommate; both are requests, not guarantees. Most new students arrive without a pre-arranged roommate, so the questionnaire is the normal path.

If you are hoping for a single, two things help: apply the day your NetID works, and list the room types you would accept rather than only the one you want. A blank second choice is read as flexibility, not insistence.

## Things you cannot undo

New transfers [cannot cancel a housing assignment](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/housing-details/rates-amenities-licenses/housing-license-cancellation-policy) because the residential requirement binds them; the fee schedule you may see ($50 through January 31, rising to $2,000 between July 1 and August 15) applies to returning juniors and seniors who have already met the requirement. The clause that bites transfers is the no-show rule: if you do not check in by the first day of classes and have not arranged a late arrival, your assignment is cancelled and you are billed half of the fall room rate. International transfers whose visa appointments run late should email Housing before move-in, not after.

## If you are...

- **A junior or senior transfer:** you are exempt. You can still apply for on-campus housing, but you are placed after current students, so start an off-campus search in parallel.
- **A spring transfer:** you live on campus for the spring you arrive and the following full year. Ask about mid-year openings in Program Houses, which turn over more than the houses on West.
- **Transferring from a school where you lived on campus two years:** request the exemption in the application and keep proof of your housing history handy.
- **An international transfer:** expect to arrive later than domestic students; confirm your move-in timeslot and tell Housing your flight date in writing.

## Common mistakes

- Waiting for a "housing deadline" email. The ten-day window starts when your NetID arrives, and nobody reminds you.
- Ranking only West Campus. You are not guaranteed West; a blank list hands the choice to the office.
- Assuming you can cancel if you find a cheaper apartment. New transfers cannot.
- Skipping the co-ops because they are not in the main application. They have their own process and their own prices.

## FAQ

**Is housing guaranteed for transfers?** Cornell's pages guarantee housing for first-year students and require new transfers to live on campus, which in practice means you are housed; the transfer page itself does not use the word guarantee. Apply inside the ten-day window to stay out of the waitlist.

**Can I live with a friend who is a current student?** You can name a roommate on the application, but transfers are assigned with other new students where possible, and the request is not guaranteed.

**When do I find out my room?** From mid-June on, by email, rolling through the summer.

**Can I switch rooms after I arrive?** Cornell runs room-change processes during the year; see our [housing guides](/housing/) for the current windows and the [money guides](/money/) for what a room swap does to your bill.
