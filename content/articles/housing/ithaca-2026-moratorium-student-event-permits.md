Ithaca's Common Council voted 9-1 on Oct. 7, 2026, to withhold new permits for events run by Cornell groups, fraternities and sororities. The measure covers assembly, special-event, Commons and noise permits, and it was set to expire Nov. 5 ([Ithaca Times](https://www.ithaca.com/news/ithaca/common-council-sets-moratorium-on-cornell-event-permits-following-gang-rape-allegations/article_6c84a345-d112-483a-98bc-316f3b64f0a1.html)).

## What does the moratorium do?

A moratorium is a temporary pause. Here, the City stops issuing new permits of four kinds to Cornell groups, fraternities and sororities: assembly, special event, Commons and noise. The Ithaca Times headline ties the vote to gang rape allegations. The Cornell Daily Sun described the permit pause as primarily affecting Greek organizations ([The Cornell Daily Sun](https://www.cornellsun.com/article/2026/10/breaking-cornell-suspends-all-fraternity-parties-social-events-for-2026-27-school-year)).

## How does it relate to Cornell's own action?

Six days after the vote, on Oct. 13, 2026, President Michael I. Kotlikoff canceled all parties and social events in Interfraternity Council houses for the rest of 2026-27. Any fraternity that holds an in-house party or social event faces an immediate emergency suspension. The directive also calls for a review of whether and how fraternity life continues, with an independent review led by Yates and investigators from the New York State Police ([Cornell Presidential Actions](https://scl.cornell.edu/get-involved/sorority-fraternity-life/policies-and-procedures/presidential-actions)).

The two actions are separate. The City's measure limits permits the City issues. Cornell's directive limits what chapters can do under the university's rules. Cornell's scorecard shows that some earlier suspensions run on set dates: Phi Sigma Kappa becomes eligible to return in fall 2027, Zeta Psi on the first day of fall 2027, Phi Delta Theta in spring 2028 and Delta Tau Delta in spring 2029. Chi Phi is listed on interim suspension ([Cornell Organization Scorecard](https://scl.cornell.edu/get-involved/sorority-fraternity-life/organization-scorecard)).

## Is the moratorium still in effect?

The sources used for this guide give an end date and none reports an extension or a lapse. To find out, check the Common Council's agenda and minutes, or call the City Clerk's office at City Hall, 108 E. Green St., at 607-274-6580 ([City of Ithaca](https://www.cityofithacany.gov/731/Permit-Issuance)).

## What does it mean for renters near campus?

The permits withheld are for Cornell groups, fraternities and sororities. The sources do not say that the pause applies to an individual tenant's apartment party, and noise permits for individuals are not described. If you rent near a chapter house, expect fewer permitted events on nearby streets and the Commons. For the city's noise, trash and snow rules that apply to every renter, see the [trash tags, snow and noise guide](/cornell/housing/ithaca-trash-tags-snow-noise-rules-renters/).

If you plan to live in a chapter house in 2027-28, the suspensions and the review of fraternity life matter to your housing plan. The [off-campus housing guide](/cornell/housing/cornell-off-campus-housing-guide/) covers how Greek housing fits the residential requirement, and a lease clause on parties and noise is worth a close read, as the [lease-reading guide](/cornell/housing/how-to-read-ithaca-lease-before-signing/) shows.

```reader "If you live in the Town of Ithaca or Cayuga Heights"
The Common Council is the City's body. Rules in the Town of Ithaca and the Village of Cayuga Heights come from their own boards, so the City's moratorium does not govern permits there. The [guide to City, Town, Lansing and Cayuga Heights rules](/cornell/housing/city-of-ithaca-vs-town-lansing-cayuga-heights-renters/) explains who regulates what.
```
