```glance
- Every North Campus hall charges the same 2026-27 rate by room type, whatever the building's age.
- First-years do not rank halls; you choose program house or residence hall, room type and roommate.
- Twelve North Campus buildings, home to at least 2,384 residents, have no air conditioning in rooms.
- Rising sophomores pick rooms April 13 to 16, 2027; about one in five lands on West Campus.
```

On Monday, Aug. 17, 2026, cars carrying Cornell's newest students lined up at A Lot on North Campus, the queue for first-year move-in ([Cornell Housing move-in instructions](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/housing-details/moving-campus/move-instructions/move-instructions-north-campus)). From there, staff sent each car to a different curb. One went to the loading dock at Balch Hall. Another went to the spaces at Robert Purcell Community Center for Mary Donlon Hall. Another went to Program House Drive for Hu Shih Hall.

Almost none of those students picked their building. Cornell tells new students they "do not need to research and rank residence halls," because staff assign halls from the preferences each student gives ([Cornell Housing](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/campus-housing/first-year-undergraduates-campus-housing)). That same page counts more than 3,000 new Cornellians living on North Campus each year.

North Campus is a village built in five waves over 109 years, from a 1913 stone hall to three halls that opened in 2022. Cornell charges for all of it by room type, not by building ([Cornell Housing rates](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/housing-details/rates-amenities-licenses/housing-rates)). A double in a 1969 Low Rise with no air conditioning costs exactly what a double costs in a hall that opened in 2022. So the things that make one year feel different from another (the building's age, its layout, its cooling, its size and its community) are things money cannot buy here. A few choices can shape them: program house or not, room type, and in year two, how fast you move in a selection timeslot.

The stakes are real. Cornell requires first- and second-year students to live in university or affiliated housing, with a meal plan ([Cornell residential policy](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/campus-housing/residential-policy)). A first-year in a double pays $21,398 for room and board in 2026-27. And a first-year cannot cancel the housing license to leave for a cheaper option.

The hill makes most sense in the order it was built, then along a student's path: assignment, layout, heat, program houses, year two, the bill, winter break and move-in. Every hall and house sits side by side in the table at the end.

## Five waves on one hill

North Campus sits on the far side of Fall Creek Gorge from Cornell's original academic core ([Cornell Housing](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/campus-housing/upper-level-undergraduates/program-houses/risley-residential-college)). Each wave of building left its own kind of room behind. Knowing which wave a hall came from tells you more about daily life there than its name does.

### 1913 to 1961: stone, brick and the women's halls

Risley came first. It opened in 1913, named for Prudence Risley, the mother of financier Russell Sage. Cornell calls it the university's first expansion north of Fall Creek Gorge ([Cornell Housing](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/campus-housing/upper-level-undergraduates/program-houses/risley-residential-college)). Its dining room was modeled on the refectory at Christ Church, Oxford. Since 1970 it has housed Cornell's first program house, a community for students in the arts, with 194 residents.

Balch Hall followed in 1929, built for the growing number of women at Cornell. Allan C. Balch and Janet Jacks Balch paid for it ([Cornell Housing](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/campus-housing/first-year-undergraduates/residence-halls/balch-hall)). It is still for women only, with 230-plus residents in singles and doubles.

Balch is also the newest old building on the hill. It closed for more than two years for a full renovation, its first in six decades. North Balch reopened in August 2024, and South Balch was set to follow in 2025 ([Cornell Student and Campus Life](https://scl.cornell.edu/news-events/news/peek-inside-balch-hall-renovations-near-completion)). The work added an elevator in each of the building's four units and new windows. It also gave each room a thermostat for the first time. Wood paneling and doors from the old building went into the lounges, including pieces from the room where Ruth Bader Ginsburg lived as a student. Two first-years in the class of 2028 told The Cornell Daily Sun they felt more connected in an all-women's hall, and one said she planned to stay all four years ([Cornell Daily Sun](https://www.cornellsun.com/article/2024/10/beginning-at-balch-life-at-north-campuss-newest-dorm)).

Anna Comstock Hall took its name in 1940. It honors the first woman at Cornell to hold the rank of professor. Since 1994 it has been home to the Latino Living Center, a program house of 55 residents in suites ([Cornell Housing](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/campus-housing/upperlevel-undergraduate-campus-housing/program-houses/latino-living-center-llc)).

Clara Dickson Hall opened in 1946 as a women's residence. It is named for the mother of Andrew Dickson White, Cornell's co-founder and first president ([Cornell Housing](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/campus-housing/first-year-undergraduates/residence-halls/clara-dickson-hall)). With 460-plus residents, it is one of the largest halls on North. Rooms run from singles to quints, which means five students in one room. Half of Dickson houses first-years and half houses sophomores.

Hurlburt House opened in 1953 as the Cornell Heights Residential Club. It became student housing in 1966 and has held Ecology House since 1971 ([Cornell Housing](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/campus-housing/upper-level-undergraduates/program-houses/ecology-house)). Its 96 residents all live in doubles, and each room has its own bathroom.

Mary Donlon Hall opened in 1961, the last of this era. Its namesake, Mary Donlon Alger, was the first woman in New York to serve as a federal judge ([Cornell Housing](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/campus-housing/first-year-undergraduates/residence-halls/mary-donlon-hall)). It holds 450-plus first-years in singles, doubles and quads, with six lounges and a theater-style room on the first floor.

### 1969 to 1971: the Low Rises and the High Rises

The next wave came fast. Low Rise 7 opened in 1969, during what Cornell calls a period of rapidly growing enrollment ([Cornell Housing](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/campus-housing/first-year-undergraduates/residence-halls/low-rise-7)). Low Rises 6, 8, 9 and 10 followed in 1970. They are brick-and-glass buildings joined by walkways. Ujamaa lists 144 residents, and the others list 160 or more.

These buildings carry a lot of Cornell history. Low Rise 6 briefly housed students in the Committee on Special Educational Projects (COSEP) in its first semester ([Cornell Housing](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/campus-housing/first-year-undergraduates/residence-halls/low-rise-6)). Low Rise 8 has held the International Living Center since its first year. In 1985 the center took the name of Jerome H. Holland, a Cornell graduate, diplomat and university president ([Cornell Housing](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/campus-housing/upperlevel-undergraduate-campus-housing/program-houses/holland-international-living-center-hilc)). Low Rise 9 has been Just About Music since 2000, with a concert stage and a recording studio ([Cornell Housing](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/campus-housing/upper-level-undergraduates/program-houses/just-about-music-jam)). In Low Rise 10, student leaders founded Ujamaa Residential College in 1972, a program house centered on the heritage of Black people around the world ([Cornell Housing](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/campus-housing/upper-level-undergraduates/program-houses/ujamaa-residential-college)).

The Low Rise layout is a cluster. In Low Rise 6, Ujamaa, JAM and HILC, each group of rooms holds one double, one triple, two singles and a shared bathroom. Each cluster is one gender, while floors are mixed.

Two taller buildings went up in 1971. High Rise 1 was renamed Jameson Hall in 1996 for George S. Jameson, a donor from the class of 1926 ([Cornell Housing](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/campus-housing/first-year-undergraduates/residence-halls/jameson-hall)). High Rise 5 kept its number ([Cornell Housing](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/campus-housing/first-year-undergraduates/residence-halls/high-rise-5)). Each holds 200-plus first-years in "units," groups of rooms that share a study room, a lounge and a kitchen. Both have a top-floor Skylounge that looks out over the golf course and Fuertes Observatory.

### 1986 to 2000: the Townhouses and the program houses

The Townhouse Apartments opened in 1986 as eight clusters, lettered A through H ([Cornell Housing](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/campus-housing/upper-level-undergraduates/residence-halls/townhouse-apartments)). They are the only apartment-style housing on North. Each apartment holds four or six students in two or three double bedrooms, with a full kitchen, a living room and a bathroom. The 300-plus residents are upper-level students. In 2024, Cornell began full bathroom and kitchen renewals there, starting with units G and H ([Cornell Student and Campus Life](https://scl.cornell.edu/news-events/news/residential-improvement-project-underway)).

Akwe:kon opened in 1991. Cornell says it was among the first university residence halls in the nation built to celebrate American Indian culture, and it is the only Cornell building designed from the start as a program house ([Cornell Housing](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/campus-housing/upper-level-undergraduates/program-houses/akwekon)). Its name is the Mohawk word for "all of us." With 35 residents, it is the smallest residence building on North.

The same year, students founded the Multicultural Living Learning Unit, called McLLU (said "McClue"), on two floors of Clara Dickson Hall. It has 47 residents ([Cornell Housing](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/campus-housing/upper-level-undergraduates/program-houses/multicultural-living-learning-unit-mcllu)).

### 2001: Court-Kay-Bauer, Mews and an all-first-year North

In 2001, North Campus became the first-year campus. Two new halls, Court Hall and Mews Hall, opened with a new community center. They fulfilled President Hunter R. Rawlings III's vision of an integrated living and learning environment. With the new halls, only upper-level students lived on West Campus ([Cornell Daily Sun](https://cornellsun.com/2001/09/04/north-campus-grand-opening/)). The two halls housed about 600 of that year's 3,029 first-years.

Court Hall is three linked halls. Two of its wings were later named for donors Robert J. and Virginia Bauer and L. William "Bill" Kay II, and together they are now Court-Kay-Bauer, with 275-plus residents ([Cornell Housing](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/campus-housing/first-year-undergraduates/residence-halls/court-kay-bauer-hall)). Bauer Hall now holds the Sober Living Community, for students who choose a substance-free life. Mews Hall has 260-plus residents next to Appel Commons. Its name is a British word for homes set around a small street or courtyard ([Cornell Housing](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/campus-housing/first-year-undergraduates/residence-halls/mews-hall)). Mews also holds Loving House, a program house for LGBTQIA+ students and allies.

### 2021 and 2022: the expansion that made the sophomore rule possible

The biggest wave was the most recent. Cornell's site plan, released by the City of Ithaca in July 2018, called for 1,200 first-year beds and 800 sophomore beds, plus 75 beds for resident advisers, staff and live-in faculty ([Cornell Daily Sun](https://cornellsun.com/2018/08/27/university-releases-full-plan-to-expand-north-campus-to-house-100-percent-of-freshmen-and-sophomores/)). The goal was to house every first-year and every sophomore in campus or affiliated housing. The plan would raise campus beds from 8,400 to 10,400 and let Cornell grow its undergraduate class. It put the new buildings on the fields above Appel Commons and on the C.C. parking lot. It also took down the Sigma Alpha Mu house at 10 Sisson Place. The architects, ikon.5, had also designed Mews and Court-Kay-Bauer.

Toni Morrison Hall and Ganędagǫ: Hall opened in 2021 with 800 beds. Barbara McClintock, Hu Shih and Ruth Bader Ginsburg halls added 1,200 beds in August 2022, after a project that took six and a half years ([Cornell Chronicle](https://news.cornell.edu/stories/2022/08/cornell-welcomes-students-marks-milestone-residential-life)). Ryan Lombardi, vice president for student and campus life, called it "a truly transformative project." The Chronicle reported that the expansion also made room to upgrade older housing, and Balch was closed for remodeling at the time.

The expansion made the two-year rule work. The first class bound by it entered in 2021, so fall 2022 was the first year sophomores had to live on campus or in affiliated housing ([Cornell Daily Sun](https://cornellsun.com/2021/11/09/as-cornell-requires-university-housing-for-sophomores-students-weigh-options/)). Tim Blair, executive director of housing and residential life, called it one phase of building a strong living and learning environment. Not every student agreed. One first-year in the class of 2025 said the rule "limits our independence." Others objected to the required meal plan.

These are Cornell Housing's resident counts. Toni Morrison Hall lists 306 residents ([Cornell Housing](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/campus-housing/upper-level-undergraduates/residence-halls/toni-morrison-hall)) and Ganędagǫ: Hall 564 ([Cornell Housing](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/campus-housing/upperlevel-undergraduates/residence-halls/ganedago-hall)). Barbara McClintock Hall lists 311 ([Cornell Housing](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/campus-housing/first-year-undergraduates/residence-halls/barbara-mcclintock-hall)), Hu Shih Hall 429 ([Cornell Housing](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/campus-housing/first-year-undergraduates/residence-halls/hu-shih-hall)) and Ruth Bader Ginsburg Hall 504 ([Cornell Housing](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/campus-housing/first-year-undergraduates/residence-halls/ruth-bader-ginsburg-hall)). Welliver, the construction manager, counts beds instead and lists 314 for Toni Morrison and 577 for Ganędagǫ: ([Welliver](https://www.buildwelliver.com/projects/integrated-acquisition-and-development-cornell-university-north-campus-residential-expansion)). Those five counts add up to 2,114 people. That is more than the 2,000 student beds the plan promised, and close to the 2,075 it planned once staff and faculty beds are counted.

The names carry meaning. Ganędagǫ: (said "Gah-NEH-dah-go") is a Cayuga word meaning "it's on the hill," the old name for the land where Cornell's main campus sits. Cornell chose it with Cayuga leaders and its American Indian and Indigenous Studies Program ([Cornell Housing](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/campus-housing/upperlevel-undergraduates/residence-halls/ganedago-hall)). The other four honor Cornell alumni: Toni Morrison, the first African American woman to win the Nobel Prize in Literature; Barbara McClintock, a plant geneticist who won a Nobel Prize in 1983; Hu Shih, a Chinese scholar and diplomat; and Supreme Court Justice Ruth Bader Ginsburg.

Early residents had mixed reviews. In October 2021, first-years in Toni Morrison and Ganędagǫ: told the Sun they liked the big rooms, the hardwood floors and the cooling ([Cornell Daily Sun](https://cornellsun.com/2021/10/13/from-leaky-showers-to-lonely-suites-student-residents-voice-concerns-about-new-north-campus-dorms/)). They also reported shower drains that flooded bathroom floors. Several said the heavy suite doors stayed shut, so they knew few people beyond their own suite. One resident called the feel "very office-building-esque." Another said the closed doors gave her privacy and security. All of them said the good outweighed the bad.

A random process places each first-year from a few choices they make. Cornell asks new students to apply in the Housing and Dining Portal within 10 days of getting a NetID ([Cornell Housing](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/residential-programs/apply-housing/new-undergrad-application-process)).

  - {label: "Choose a program house or a residence hall"}

Source: Cornell Housing, New Undergrad Application Process.

Unless you apply to a program house, a random auto-allocation process picks your hall from your preferences. Cornell will try to honor a roommate request but does not promise it. Most students apply without a roommate. Confirmations arrive from mid-June. Some students get a building and room. Others get a spot on the guaranteed waitlist, which means they will get a room but learn which one later in the summer. Cornell said it would be full for 2026-27 and would take no room change requests. Students could still swap rooms directly with each other from mid-June to mid-July ([Cornell Housing](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/residential-programs/apply-housing/new-undergrad-application-process)).

So the program house box is the one real lever over where you live. More than 500 first-year and transfer students live in program houses ([Cornell Housing](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/residential-programs/apply-housing/new-undergrad-application-process)). The other lever is room type, which also sets your price.

```reader "If you're an incoming first-year"
Spend your effort on the program house box and on ranking room types by what you can live with, because a triple costs less than a double. If you need accessible housing for a disability or a chronic health condition, register with Student Disability Services right away; Cornell says that is how it makes sure your room is accessible to you. The [first-year start page](/start/first-year/) lists what else to settle before August.
```

## Corridor, suite or apartment

Cornell sorts its rooms into three layouts ([Cornell Housing](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/housing-details/residence-hall-configurations)). The layout shapes your day more than the decor does.

**Corridor style** means bedrooms open onto a hallway, and residents share bathrooms down the hall. Most of North is corridor style: Akwe:kon, Balch, Barbara McClintock, Clara Dickson, Court-Kay-Bauer, High Rise 5, Hu Shih, Jameson, Loving House, the Low Rises, Mary Donlon, Mews, McLLU, Risley and Ruth Bader Ginsburg. Some corridor halls have "privacy option" bathrooms, where each shower and toilet sits in its own room with a lock. Ecology House is corridor style too, but every room there has a private bathroom.

**Suite style** means a locked door off the hall leads to a few bedrooms, a common space and a bathroom that only the suite uses. On North, that is Ganędagǫ:, Toni Morrison, Language House and the Latino Living Center.

**Apartment style** adds a kitchen. On North, only the Townhouse Apartments qualify.

The trade-off is social. A corridor puts you in the hall every time you brush your teeth. A suite gives you privacy and a bathroom you share with a few people, but closed suite doors make neighbors harder to meet. A Ganędagǫ: resident told the Sun in 2021 that residents kept their doors shut ([Cornell Daily Sun](https://cornellsun.com/2021/09/10/housing-conditions-across-campus-are-they-fair/)).

Room size varies too. Dickson, Jameson and High Rise 5 have quints. Some High Rise units swap their lounge for a one-room quad. Mews has carpet in most rooms but vinyl tile floors on the third floor of its west wing.

Twelve buildings, holding at least 2,384 of the 5,659-plus residents these pages list (counting each building once), have no air conditioning in rooms. That is about 42% (2,384 divided by 5,659). Cooling also feeds the argument over price. In 2021 a High Rise 5 resident told the Sun his hall "should be cheaper" because it had no air conditioning, while a HILC resident said nobody should pay less for being placed in an older dorm ([Cornell Daily Sun](https://cornellsun.com/2021/09/10/housing-conditions-across-campus-are-they-fair/)).

## Year two on North Campus

Sophomores still live on North, but in fewer buildings. Cornell lists Balch Hall, the north wing of Clara Dickson Hall, Ganędagǫ: Hall, Toni Morrison Hall and the Townhouse Apartments as North's upper-level halls, plus the program houses ([Cornell Housing](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/campus-housing/upperlevel-undergraduate-campus-housing)). Low Rise 7 is open to women in all four years.

Rising sophomores do not get assigned. ## What it costs, and why every hall costs the same

Cornell sets one rate table for its residence halls, program houses and West Campus houses ([Cornell Housing rates](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/housing-details/rates-amenities-licenses/housing-rates)). The price follows the room type, not the building. Rates include gas, electricity, trash pickup and internet up to 200 GB a month. There is no security deposit.

A double costs $13,744 for 2026-27. A triple saves $1,100 a year against a double ($13,744 minus $12,644). A quad costs the same as a double, while a one-room quad is billed like a triple. A single, or a double in the Townhouses, costs $1,822 more than a double (Cornell Housing rates).

The meal plan sits on top. First-years must buy the Unlimited plan at $3,802 per semester, or $7,604 a year, and every plan adds a non-refundable $50 administrative fee once a year ([Cornell Dining](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/dining/meal-plans-rates/undergraduate-meal-plans)). Sophomores in North halls can drop to Bear Traditional, up to 14 meals a week, at $7,056 a year.

Why does a 1913 hall cost the same as a 2022 hall? Cornell's housing office gave its reason in 2004. Pam Zinder, then interim manager of the housing and dining office, told the Sun that Cornell once had "numerous rates to address different room sizes and amenities." That did not feel fair to all students, she said, because amenities, room size and building type are "all in the eye of the beholder." After reviewing its peers, Cornell moved to a condensed rate structure based on the number of people in a room. Zinder also tied the rule to budgeting. If students could rank their choice of building, she said, the office may not be able to forecast its budgets accurately ([Cornell Daily Sun](https://cornellsun.com/2004/05/06/dorm-disparities-cause-controversy/)).

Students have argued both sides in the Sun since. In 2021, a Donlon resident warned that pricing by quality would sort richer students into the new halls ([Cornell Daily Sun](https://cornellsun.com/2021/09/10/housing-conditions-across-campus-are-they-fair/)). In April 2026, a Sun writer from the class of 2029 argued that equal prices are the fair system and that Cornell should shrink the gap between room types instead ([Cornell Daily Sun](https://www.cornellsun.com/article/2026/04/unequal-vs-inequitable-housing-why-all-dorms-should-cost-the-same)). She noted that students who need to save often choose triples, so room type, not building, is where money already changes how students live.

```reader "If you're a parent reading the bill"
Cornell bills housing in August for fall and in December for spring; dining is billed in July and January. A triple is the one way to cut the first-year bill. Plan for heat instead of price: the hall is assigned at random, and air conditioning comes with the hall.
```

```reader "If you're on a tight budget"
Ask for a triple or a one-room quad, the cheapest room types on North, in your first-year application. Cornell says the Unlimited plan is fully covered by financial aid for eligible students. For year two, the eight Cornell co-ops cost far less than any North hall: a co-op double runs $3,487 to $4,981 a semester ([Cornell Housing rates](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/housing-details/rates-amenities-licenses/housing-rates)). The [comparison with off-campus rent](/cornell/housing/is-off-campus-cheaper-than-cornell-dorm/) shows what changes in junior year.
```

Cornell's 2027-28 rates are projected until they are approved. Final rates post in March 2027 ([Cornell housing license 2027-28](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/housing-details/rates-amenities-licenses/academic-year-housing-license-2027-2028)). The license is binding once signed. First-years and sophomores cannot cancel it to move off campus. Returning juniors and seniors can, for a fee that climbs from $50 through Jan. 31 to half the fall room rate after Aug. 15. If you hold a North room for junior year, such as a Townhouse, use the [cancellation fee calculator](/tools/cornell-housing-license-cancellation-fee-calculator/) before you sign a lease.

## Winter break on North

Most halls close over winter break. For 2026-27, Cornell keeps Ganędagǫ:, High Rise 5, HILC, Hu Shih, Loving House, Mews, Toni Morrison and the Townhouses open on North ([Cornell Housing](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/campus-housing/winter-break-housing)). For 2027-28, High Rise 5 drops off that list and Jameson Hall joins it.

```reader "If you're staying over winter break"
Your building matters here more than anywhere. HILC and Loving House residents stay in their own rooms free, paid for by gifts to those communities. Residents of the other open North halls pay $125 for one week after fall semester, $125 for the week before spring, or $500 for the whole break. Students from closed buildings can apply for limited "flex" spaces at $500 for the full break. Apply in the Housing and Dining Portal between Oct. 1 and Dec. 1. New students who will need break housing should check the "Winter Break Housing" option on their first housing application, so Cornell can place them in an open building from the start.
```

## Moving in

Move-in on North runs on timeslots and tickets ([Cornell Housing](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/housing-details/moving-campus)). In 2026, first-years moved in on Monday, Aug. 17, transfers on Tuesday, Aug. 18, and upper-level students on Aug. 21 and 22. Your timeslot is for check-in, not unloading. Print your Move-In Ticket from the portal after Aug. 1. It shows where to park for three hours once your car is empty. Each building has its own unloading zone, such as the North Balch loading dock, the Robert Purcell lot for Donlon, Jameson, High Rise 5 and Ganędagǫ:, or the Helen Newman lot for Court-Kay-Bauer. Risley, Balch, Akwe:kon, Ecology House and the Latino Living Center have separate instructions. Rental trucks and trailers are turned away. Our [move-in parking guide](/cornell/getting-around/cornell-move-in-day-parking-unloading/) has the full routine.

## What comes next

North Campus is not finished changing. The Townhouse kitchen and bathroom renewals that began with units G and H in 2024 have more clusters to reach. The 2018 plan also listed overdue maintenance, including accessibility work, at Balch, Clara Dickson and Risley ([Cornell Daily Sun](https://cornellsun.com/2018/08/27/university-releases-full-plan-to-expand-north-campus-to-house-100-percent-of-freshmen-and-sophomores/)). Balch is the one that has been finished.

The bigger question is the one the Sun's writers keep raising: whether one price for every building stays fair as the gap between old and new halls widens. After year two, most students leave North for Collegetown and beyond, and our [off-campus housing guide](/cornell/housing/cornell-off-campus-housing-guide/) picks up the story there.
