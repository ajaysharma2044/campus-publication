```glance
- About 70% of Ithaca housing is rental, so students and residents compete for one pool of units.
- Cornell houses its first- and second-year students, and the rest look off campus.
- New Collegetown buildings add beds but start at higher rents than older units.
- Downtown shares cost less per bed than Collegetown at the same landlord.
```

Ithaca has a rental-heavy housing market where Cornell undergraduates and city residents compete for the same units. Cornell controls the supply on campus, and what it does with its own beds sets how many students enter the private market. Reporting puts renters at about 73% of Ithaca households ([The Cornell Daily Sun](https://cornellsun.com/2019/10/30/fox-the-case-for-an-ithaca-tenants-union/)).

## How big is the squeeze?

The numbers come from news reports, so they vary by year and source.

- **Renters.** About 73% of households rent, from a 2019 column, or about 70% of housing is rental, from a 2021 article. The 2019 columnist, citing the Ithaca Times, said an average 2BR takes about $49,000 a year in income against a median household income of $29,230 ([The Cornell Daily Sun](https://cornellsun.com/2019/10/30/fox-the-case-for-an-ithaca-tenants-union/)). That is a gap of $19,770, or about 1.7 times the median.
- **Undergraduates off campus.** About 43% of undergraduates, roughly 6,189 students, lived off campus in fall 2013. A 2020 article put it at about 48%, and a 2019 alderperson said more than 7,000 undergraduates seek off-campus housing each year ([The Cornell Daily Sun](https://cornellsun.com/2014/09/08/fischer-jimmys-tale-of-leasing-in-collegetown/)).
- **Graduate students.** About 95% of grads live in non-university housing ([Cornell Graduate and Professional Student Housing](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/campus-housing/graduate-professional-student-campus-housing)).
- **Who owns Collegetown.** In 2013, landlords owned 4,013 of an estimated 6,110 Collegetown bedrooms, and eight landlords held about two-thirds of the property ([The Cornell Daily Sun](https://cornellsun.com/2013/05/02/collegetown-housing-market-scrutinized/)).

These are dated. The fact file has no current city-wide vacancy rate. A source labeled 3% for Tompkins County rental vacancy is an undated aggregator figure, so it is not used here. For a current number, check the Tompkins County Planning Department.

## What does Cornell's own housing change?

Cornell is a supplier and a source of demand. In 2021 and 2022 it opened 2,000 beds on North Campus, and since fall 2022 first- and second-year students must live on campus or in affiliated housing ([Cornell Chronicle](https://news.cornell.edu/stories/2022/08/cornell-welcomes-students-marks-milestone-residential-life)). That moved sophomores out of the private market, but it did not shrink the demand from juniors, seniors and graduate students.

Cornell is adding units in Collegetown itself. It bought 301 College Avenue for $15 million in March 2025 ([The Cornell Daily Sun](https://www.cornellsun.com/article/2025/03/cornell-acquires-301-college-avenue-for-15-million-in-collegetown)). Still, housing beyond the first two years is not guaranteed, and Housing and Residential Life says it must balance upper-level interest with its obligation to house first-years, transfers and rising sophomores ([The Cornell Daily Sun](https://www.cornellsun.com/article/2025/11/new-changes-to-fall-housing-selection-leave-some-rising-juniors-seniors-disappointed)).

## What is being built?

New supply is arriving, mostly at the top of the price range ([The Ithaca Voice](https://ithacavoice.org/2026/07/cornell-collegetown-construction/)):

| Building | Units | Beds | Rents |
|---|---|---|---|
| The Ivy, 325 College Ave. | 52 | 81 | Studios from $2,595 a month; 3BR up to $4,900 |
| The Bear, 215 College Ave. | 84 | 150 | Studios from $2,850 a month; 3BR up to $6,000 |
| The Summit | 46 | about 88 | 4BR $7,200; studios and 1BR from $2,700 |
| The Pinnacle, 121-125 Lake St. | 77 | 111 | 2BR about $3,000 |

Source: The Ithaca Voice, July 2026; Tompkins Weekly for The Pinnacle's bed count. Bed counts for The Pinnacle differ by report (111 and 109).

Developers argue that more supply should help. The Pinnacle opened Aug. 1, 2026, with at least 80% graduate students, and its developer, Todd Fox, says its rents run 15% to 20% below newer Collegetown buildings and that more supply puts downward pressure on rents ([Tompkins Weekly](https://www.tompkinsweekly.com/news/three-new-developments-offer-different-answers-to-ithacas-housing-needs-30cfa148)). Earlier waves tell a similar story. In 2016, three new buildings added nearly 200 bedrooms, and Catherine Commons later added a net 339 bedrooms ([The Cornell Daily Sun](https://cornellsun.com/2016/09/26/landlords-say-collegetown-housing-hassle-unlikely-to-change/)).

## Where is rent lower?

Downtown shares cost less than Collegetown beds at the same landlord, Ithaca Renting. The median asking rent per bed is $1,395 downtown against $1,765 in Collegetown for a studio, $1,625 against $2,025 for a 1BR, and $992.50 against $1,377.50 for a 2BR ([Ithaca Renting Collegetown](https://ithacarenting.com/collegetown/) and [downtown](https://ithacarenting.com/downtown/) price pages). Those are asking rents from one company, not a market average. The [Collegetown rent per bed guide](/cornell/housing/collegetown-rent-prices-per-bed-one-bedroom/) and [Collegetown vs Fall Creek vs downtown](/cornell/housing/collegetown-vs-fall-creek-vs-downtown-ithaca-where-to-live/) show where else to look.

## How does the squeeze reach renters?

It shows up in timing. Every Cornell Daily Sun account from 2012 to 2025 shows the best Collegetown units gone within weeks, though the wider market of downtown, Fall Creek and sublets has supply much later ([The Cornell Daily Sun](https://cornellsun.com/2019/02/06/students-and-landlords-both-perpetuate-myths-about-housing-university-panelist-says/)). Ithaca opted into New York's Good Cause eviction law in 2024, which limits some rent increases, and the [Good Cause guide](/cornell/housing/ithaca-good-cause-eviction-rent-increase-students/) explains who is covered.
