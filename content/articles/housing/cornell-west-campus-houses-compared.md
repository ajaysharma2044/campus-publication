```glance
- In past years about 20% of rising sophomores got a West Campus room; most live on North or South.
- A 2026-27 West double plus the required House Meal Plan and fee costs $21,398.
- Only Carl Becker House has air conditioning, and not every West building has an elevator.
- Rising sophomores apply by April 4, 2027; open room selection runs April 13 to 16.
```

Every Wednesday at 6 p.m., the dining rooms on Cornell's West Campus close to outsiders. For one hour, each of the five Houses eats alone: residents, live-in faculty, their families and invited guests. A House Chef cooks the meal. At 7 p.m. the doors open again to everyone else ([Cornell Dining](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/dining/eateries-menus/dining-rooms/west-campus-house-dinners)).

That hour is the promise of West Campus in small form. Cornell built the five Houses to give upper-level students a small community with professors living in the building. Getting a seat at that table is hard. In past years about one rising sophomore in five could pick a room there ([Cornell Housing FAQs](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/housing-details/apply-housing-campus/current-undergraduate-application-process/room-selection-faqs)).

West Campus is really two places stacked on one hillside. One is five modern House buildings opened between 2004 and 2009. The other is a ring of stone Gothic halls built between 1913 and 1931. Both charge the same room rates and require the same meal plan (Cornell Housing rates; Cornell Dining). But the rooms inside them are very different.

The stakes are real money. A double room plus the required House Meal Plan costs more than $21,000 for 2026-27 (Cornell Housing rates; Cornell Dining), and a sophomore who signs for a West room cannot back out except in narrow cases.

## What a West Campus House is

A House is Cornell's version of a residential college: a dorm with its own faculty leaders, dining room and programs. Each House is led by a House Professor-Dean, a tenured professor who lives in the building with their family. A live-in Assistant Dean, a student affairs professional, runs the daily work. Graduate Resident Fellows and Undergraduate Resident Fellows live among the students. About 30 House Fellows, mostly faculty, join residents for meals and events ([Cornell Housing](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/campus-housing/upper-level-undergraduates/west-campus-house-system)).

Each House has a House Office for mail and keys, a large common room, lounges, study rooms, a library, laundry and seminar rooms. Residents get priority for one-credit "Learning Where You Live" courses, which meet in the Houses. Cornell says the Houses have historically been about 65% sophomores, 25% juniors and 10% seniors, and about 15% transfer students (Cornell Housing).

Students can also stay. West Campus offers "continued occupancy," so a sophomore can keep living on West as a junior and senior through a Same Area round in fall room selection.

## From "The Stables" to five Houses

West Campus did not start as a showcase. For half a century, its middle was filled with six cinderblock dorms called the University Halls. They opened for the fall 1954 semester, cost $4,200,000 and held nearly 1,500 beds ([Cornellians](https://alumni.cornell.edu/cornellians/u-hall-memories)). Architecture students had protested the design in 1953, comparing it to brick barracks. Students called the buildings "The Stables." The six halls were first numbered, then named for the classes of 1917, 1918, 1922, 1926 and 1928 and for Elmer Sperry, the inventor (Cornellians).

The idea of replacing them grew out of faculty worry. In the early 1980s, some professors argued that Cornell was losing strong students to schools that offered closer ties between faculty and undergraduates. Government professor Isaac Kramnick's 1984 report called for a house system. In 1997, President Hunter Rawlings unveiled a seven-point plan to remake campus housing, and point five became the West Campus Residential Initiative ([Cornell Chronicle](https://news.cornell.edu/stories/2009/08/west-campus-house-system-officially-complete)).

Construction began in 2003 ([Cornell Daily Sun, 2009](https://cornellsun.com/2009/03/10/students-lament-w-campus-housing-discrepancies/)), and the Houses opened one a year or so as the old halls came down. Hans Bethe House, for example, rose on the site of Sperry Hall. That phase was a $43 million project adding 300 beds, along with a new Noyes Community and Recreation Center ([Cornell Daily Sun, 2005](https://cornellsun.com/2005/01/28/board-approves-wcri-phase-3)). By the summer of 2007, the last of the "Class of" halls had been torn down ([Cornell Daily Sun, 2007](https://cornellsun.com/2007/07/28/west-campus-gets-new-look-after-recent-renovations)).

Alice Cook House opened in August 2004, Carl Becker House in August 2005, Hans Bethe House in 2007, William Keeton House in August 2008 and Flora Rose House in August 2009 ([West Campus House System](https://wchs.undergrad.cornell.edu/history)). Rose's building was finished a year earlier but served as an ordinary dorm until its House program began. The Chronicle put the building project at $225 million, finished two years early and within budget. Kramnick called the initiative the university's "largest single capital expenditure program" (Cornell Chronicle).

Each House is named for a former Cornell professor. Alice Hanson Cook taught in the School of Industrial and Labor Relations and was one of the first scholars to study working women. Carl Becker was a historian who taught at Cornell from 1917 to 1941 (Cornell Daily Sun, 2007). Hans Bethe was a physicist who came to Cornell as a refugee from Germany in 1935 and won the Nobel Prize in 1967 ([Cornell Housing](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/campus-housing/upper-level-undergraduates/west-campus-house-system/hans-bethe-house)). William Keeton was a biologist whose research showed that homing pigeons use many cues, not one, to find their way ([Cornell Housing](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/campus-housing/upper-level-undergraduates/west-campus-house-system/william-keeton-house)). Flora Rose co-directed Cornell's College of Home Economics, now the College of Human Ecology (West Campus House System).

The Gothic halls are much older than any House. Cornell built them as men's housing in the "collegiate Gothic" style, meant to echo Oxford and Cambridge. The firm of Day & Klauder, later Charles Klauder alone, designed all of them between 1913 and 1931, in small units of 16 to 30 students each ([Cornell Facilities](https://findfacilities-prod.fcs.cornell.edu/facilitypage/3004T)). When the house system arrived, each Gothic hall was folded into a nearby House.

Kramnick ended the project with a warning, not a victory lap. Faculty had shaped the vision, he told the Chronicle in 2009, and he hoped faculty would keep managing it (Cornell Chronicle).

## The five Houses, side by side

On paper the Houses are alike. Cornell says all five are faculty-led, each has its own dining room and programs, and each holds about 350 students ([Cornell Housing FAQs](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/housing-details/apply-housing-campus/current-undergraduate-application-process/room-selection-faqs)). The real differences are in the buildings: how many suites, which rooms come in triples and quads, and whether you sleep in a 2000s brick House or a century-old stone hall.

A suite here means a group of bedrooms that shares its own bathroom and sitting room. Most West Campus suites hold five people in three singles and one double. Cornell counts 152 suites across the new buildings, and not all of them are open during selection (Cornell Housing FAQs). Everyone else lives in singles, doubles, triples or quads along a hallway with shared bathrooms.

### Alice Cook House

Cook is the oldest House and the biggest, with nearly 400 residents in five buildings at the north end of West Campus. About 120 live in Cook Main. Over 250 live in four Gothic halls: North Baker, Baker Tower, Boldt Hall and Boldt Tower. Triples and quads exist only in the Gothics. Cook Main has 16 suites of five to seven people. Cook says about 20% of its residents are transfers, higher than the 15% Cornell gives for West Campus overall. Raina Plowright is House Professor-Dean and Dylan Stratton is Assistant Dean. Its signature programs include Make-Something-Mondays, a weekly hands-on project, and Fun Fridays ([Cornell Housing](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/campus-housing/upper-level-undergraduates/west-campus-house-system/alice-cook-house)).

### Carl Becker House

Becker is the only House in a single building, and the only one with air conditioning. It has more than 350 residents in singles and doubles along hallways, plus 45 five-person suites, the most of any House. Lori Leonard, a professor of global development, is House Professor-Dean, and Amanda Carreiro is Assistant Dean. Its resident-run Active Citizens program puts on about 20 events a semester ([Cornell Housing](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/campus-housing/upper-level-undergraduates/west-campus-house-system/carl-becker-house)).

### Hans Bethe House

Bethe has more than 350 residents: about 315 in Bethe Main and about 60 in McFaddin Hall, a Gothic building at the bottom of the Slope. Quads exist only in McFaddin. The main building has suites of five to seven people; Cornell's FAQ counts 38. Christopher Alabi holds the Dale R. Corson House Professor-Dean title, and Perdita Das-Humphrey is Assistant Dean. There is no air conditioning, and McFaddin is closed for 2026-27 (Cornell Housing, Bethe House page).

### William Keeton House

Keeton is the smallest House at more than 300 residents, all in Keeton Main. It has singles and doubles plus 37 suites that hold four to ten people, the widest range of suite sizes on West. Maria Park is House Professor-Dean and Kay Lewis is Assistant Dean. Keeton's programs lean outdoors and outward: camping in the Adirondacks, trips to Niagara Falls and the state fair, and a "Conversations at Keeton" guest series. Rooms have no air conditioning (Cornell Housing, Keeton House page).

### Flora Rose House

Rose has more than 350 residents: about 135 in Rose Main and about 225 in four Gothic halls, Founders, Lyon, Mennen and South Baker. Lyon Hall is for women only. Rooms include singles, doubles, triples and quads, plus 16 suites of five or six people in the main building. Sharon Sassler is House Professor-Dean and Jeff Godowski is Assistant Dean. Rooms have no air conditioning ([Cornell Housing](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/campus-housing/upper-level-undergraduates/west-campus-house-system/flora-rose-house)).

### 1 Forest Park Lane

One more building sits beside the Houses without being one. 1 Forest Park Lane, at the base of Libe Slope, has singles, doubles and triples on two floors, with men on the second floor and women on the third. Its residents use Keeton's House Office, laundry and dining room. It has no air conditioning ([Cornell Housing](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/campus-housing/upper-level-undergraduates/residence-halls/1-forest-park-lane)). Cornell Facilities lists it under the name "Forest Park Lane 1, Sigma Phi," built in 1932. Ask Cornell Housing at 607-255-5368 which meal plan applies before you choose it.

## What a year on West costs

West Campus rooms cost exactly what North and South Campus rooms cost. Cornell uses one rate table for residence halls, program houses and the West Campus Houses. For 2026-27, a double is $6,872 per semester, or $13,744 for the year ([Cornell Housing rates](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/housing-details/rates-amenities-licenses/housing-rates)). The rate covers gas, electricity, garbage pickup and internet up to 200 GB a month. There is no security deposit. Cornell bills fall housing through the Bursar, the university's billing office, in August and spring housing in December.

The meal plan is where West differs. Every resident of the five Houses must take the House Meal Plan, and Cornell enrolls them automatically ([Cornell Dining](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/dining/meal-plans-rates)). For 2026-27 it costs $3,802 per semester, or $7,604 a year, plus a $50 administrative fee charged once a year ([Cornell Dining meal plans](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/dining/meal-plans-rates/undergraduate-meal-plans)). It covers all meals during service hours in the ten residential dining rooms. It adds $400 a semester in Big Red Bucks, Cornell's tax-free dining money for cafes and stores, and eight guest meals a semester. That is the same package and price as the Unlimited plan first-years must take.

Meals and the fee together add $7,654 a year to any West room ($7,604 plus $50). Add that to the room rate and a West double costs $21,398 for 2026-27 (Cornell Housing rates; Cornell Dining). A Gothic triple is the cheapest West bed. A quad costs the same as a double, so the quad's only advantage is the company.

You also cannot trade down. Students living in the West Campus House System may not change or cancel their meal plan ([Cornell Dining FAQs](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/dining/meal-plans-rates/meal-plan-faqs)). Outside West, Cornell's main meal plan page says upper-level students may opt into the Bear Traditional plan, up to 14 meals a week, at $7,056 a year. That is $548 less than the House plan. Cornell Dining's FAQ lets transfers and juniors reduce to it; a sophomore weighing North against West should confirm with Cornell Dining whether that saving applies.

The House plan has grown more expensive. In 2021, The Cornell Daily Sun reported it cost $6,434 a year, and two sophomores said the required plan was a reason they chose to live off campus that year ([Cornell Daily Sun, 2021](https://cornellsun.com/2021/11/09/as-cornell-requires-university-housing-for-sophomores-students-weigh-options/)). The 2026-27 price is $1,170 higher, an 18.2% rise ($1,170 divided by $6,434). That route is closed to most sophomores now, since the two-year requirement began in fall 2022.

How does a West double compare with an apartment? The answer depends on group size and lease length, and our [comparison of off-campus rent and a Cornell dorm](/cornell/housing/is-off-campus-cheaper-than-cornell-dorm/) works through it. Our [dataset of what a year of housing costs, 28 ways](/datasets/ithaca-housing-cost-by-living-situation-2026-27/) puts every option on one scale.

Students who have lived on West for at least a semester and later move off campus can buy the House Affiliate plan: three meals a week at any West dining room plus $100 in Big Red Bucks a semester, for $1,448 a year (Cornell Dining meal plans).

## Getting a room: the odds and the rules

Most rising sophomores will not live on West. Cornell says more students want West Campus than it has spaces, and most rising sophomores live on North and South Campus (Cornell Housing FAQs). Juniors and seniors face their own limits, because Cornell does not guarantee them campus housing at all (Cornell Housing).

The squeeze has a cause. In fall 2022 Cornell opened the last buildings of its North Campus Residential Expansion so that all first- and second-year students could live on campus or in Cornell-affiliated housing ([Cornell Chronicle, 2022](https://news.cornell.edu/stories/2022/08/cornell-welcomes-students-marks-milestone-residential-life)). The expansion added 800 beds in 2021 and 1,200 in 2022.

### If you're a first-year now: Spring Housing Selection

Rising sophomores choose rooms in the spring. For the 2027-28 year, the application opens in the Housing and Dining Portal on Feb. 1, 2027, and closes at 11:59 p.m. on April 4. Timeslots come out April 7. Open room selection runs April 13 to 16 ([Cornell Housing](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/housing-details/apply-housing-campus/current-undergraduate-application-process/spring-housing-selection-rising-sophomores)).

You can apply alone or in a roommate group, which Cornell calls a block. Timeslots are random. Cornell says there is no advantage or disadvantage to blocking, and everyone in a block gets the same timeslot. During your slot, the room you pick is held for four minutes while you assign beds, so have a first, second and third choice ready. Cornell posts bed availability reports and floor plans before selection.

Two other dates matter. If you plan to meet the second-year requirement in a fraternity, sorority, co-op or approved affiliated house instead, you must say so on your application by March 15, 2027. Miss it and you complete the requirement in campus housing. If you think you qualify for an exemption, file it by the same application deadline; Cornell will not consider later requests. Our guide to [who can live off campus at Cornell](/cornell/housing/cornell-residential-requirement-exemptions-live-off-campus/) explains each exemption, and the quiz below checks yours.

```tool can-i-live-off-campus-cornell-quiz```

Once you sign, you are committed. Cornell requires second-year students to live on campus and does not let them cancel a housing license unless they leave the university, win an exemption, or are accepted to a Greek house, affiliated house or co-op before the spring selection deadline ([Cornell Housing cancellation policy](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/housing-details/rates-amenities-licenses/housing-license-cancellation-policy)). A sophomore who skips the requirement without an exemption is still billed for the full year ([Cornell residential policy](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/campus-housing/residential-policy)).

### If you're already on West: Same Area selection

Juniors and seniors choose in the fall. For 2027-28, the application ran Sept. 1 to Sept. 27, 2026. West Campus residents had their own Same Area round, Oct. 5 at 4 p.m. to Oct. 6 at noon, in which each student could pick only within their current House. Open Room Selection followed on Oct. 8, then a waitlist ([Cornell Housing](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/housing-details/apply-housing-campus/current-undergraduate-application-process/fall-housing-selection-rising-juniors-and-seniors)). In the 2026-27 cycle, Cornell stopped letting students keep the exact same room, though they could still use Same Area ([Cornell Daily Sun, 2025](https://www.cornellsun.com/article/2025/11/new-changes-to-fall-housing-selection-leave-some-rising-juniors-seniors-disappointed)).

Juniors and seniors can now back out. Starting with 2026-27 licenses, a returning junior or senior can cancel before moving in, for a fee that rises with the calendar (Cornell Housing cancellation policy). The cheapest window for a 2027-28 room closes Jan. 31, 2027. That matters if you picked a West room in October and are also chasing a Collegetown lease.

```reader "If you're a transfer student"
Cornell places new transfers in upper-level halls, and the West Campus House System is one of the named options; it groups transfer rooms together when it can. ## What living there is like
```

West Campus has had a two-tier feeling from the start. In March 2009, months before the system was complete, students in the Gothics told The Sun they paid the same rates as residents of Becker and Bethe without air conditioning or in-house laundry. Some Gothic residents said they felt on the edge of their House's community (Cornell Daily Sun, 2009).

Ross Brann, then dean of Cook House, defended the old halls as "an architectural treasure, a part of Cornell's history." He noted that many Gothic rooms are larger than rooms in the new buildings. Gothic residents use the same libraries, laundry and programs as everyone else in their House (Cornell Daily Sun, 2009). That is still how Cornell describes it: each Gothic hall belongs to a House and shares its main building's services.

Small complaints about the Gothics have outlasted the debate. In 2020, a North Baker resident told The Sun her building had no water fountains and no laundry room. She and her roommate bought spider repellent while maintenance requests waited for weeks ([Cornell Daily Sun, 2020](https://www.cornellsun.com/article/2020/11/students-recount-social-struggles-limited-food-options-and-spiders-while-living-on-west-campus)). She also said meeting people was harder on West than on North Campus or in Collegetown, though that was fall 2020, during the pandemic.

The social question is the one to weigh most. A 2009 resident of a new House said the community promise was overstated and that you need friends before you arrive (Cornell Daily Sun, 2009). In 2012, a Becker resident said the many clubs and House events made up for small rooms ([Cornell Daily Sun, 2012](https://cornellsun.com/2012/09/27/architectural-tour-of-west-campus-yields-lessons-in-university-history/)). If you want faculty dinners and House events, West is built around them. If you plan to keep to yourself, residents' accounts in 2009 and 2020 suggest a House can feel quiet.

Wednesday House Dinners are back. Cornell paused them during the pandemic, and residents told The Sun in 2022 that they missed the chance to talk with professors over a meal ([Cornell Daily Sun, 2022](https://cornellsun.com/2022/03/10/west-campus-house-dinners-suspended-leaving-residents-hungry-for-more/)). Cornell Dining now lists them weekly, with musical performances and guest speakers (Cornell Dining).

Location is West's quiet advantage. The Houses sit at the foot of Libe Slope, and Cornell describes them as close to both Central Campus and Collegetown (Cornell Housing).

## McFaddin Hall, crowding and what comes next

West Campus is changing again. McFaddin Hall, the Gothic home to about 60 Bethe residents, was left out of selection for 2026-27 for "significant external construction" expected to start in summer 2026 ([Cornell Daily Sun, 2025](https://www.cornellsun.com/article/2025/09/mcfaddin-hall-will-not-house-students-for-fall-2026-due-to-upcoming-construction)). Bethe's assistant dean told residents the work was expected to run well into the fall 2026 semester, and encouraged McFaddin residents to pick rooms in Bethe Main.

The closure came during a crunch. The Class of 2029 was Cornell's largest, with 3,861 students enrolling, and Housing said it had moved students to South and West Campus to make room for first-years on North (Cornell Daily Sun, 2025). Cornell's 2027-28 license still lists McFaddin as part of Bethe House, and Cornell has not announced when the construction ends. Check the bed availability reports in the Housing and Dining Portal before your timeslot, or call Cornell Housing.

Prices for 2027-28 are not final either. The rates shown during fall selection are projected, and Cornell will post final 2027-28 rates in March 2027 after the president and trustees approve them ([Cornell Housing license](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/housing-details/rates-amenities-licenses/academic-year-housing-license-2027-2028)). A student who signs a 2027-28 license before March 2027 signs at a price that could still change.

The larger questions are the ones Kramnick raised in 2009. Can faculty still lead Houses that now serve mostly required sophomores? Will Cornell spend to renovate the Gothics, which Brann said in 2009 was not possible in that economy? And as class sizes grow, will West stay a choice or become overflow? The answers will shape what a West room is worth for the classes of 2030 and beyond (Cornell Chronicle; Cornell Daily Sun, 2009).

## Moving in

West Campus move-in runs through a central lot, not your building's door. In August 2026, students with a timeslot drove to the University Avenue Parking Lot at 627 University Ave., were guided to their building's unloading zone, signed out bins and checked in at their House Office. Cars then moved to three-hour short-term parking. Students arriving after hours called the Graduate Resident Fellow on call, using the number posted on their House's main doors ([Cornell Housing move-in instructions](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/housing-details/moving-campus/move-instructions/move-instructions-west-campus)).

For after-hours unloading on West Avenue, there are loading spaces across from Baker Tower and a pull-off across from Founders and Lyon on the Slope side. Near Bethe and Keeton, use the loading spaces on Mary Ann Wood Drive behind Keeton or on Forest Park Lane behind the Noyes Community Recreation Center. Our guide to [move-in day parking and unloading at Cornell](/cornell/getting-around/cornell-move-in-day-parking-unloading/) covers the whole campus.

## Before you pick a House: a checklist

- Decide whether air conditioning matters to you. If it does, Becker is the only House that has it.
- Decide between a new building and a Gothic hall. Gothic rooms can be larger, and a Gothic triple is the cheapest bed on West, but the halls have fewer in-building services.
- Settle your block before the spring application deadline, and agree on whether you want a suite. Not every suite is offered in selection.
- Rank at least three plans, including North and South Campus halls, since most sophomores land there.
- Check the bed availability report for McFaddin Hall before choosing Bethe.
- Budget for the full House Meal Plan; it cannot be reduced or cancelled while you live on West.
- If you might live off campus as a junior, read our [sophomore guide to a junior-year lease](/cornell/housing/off-campus-housing-cornell-sophomores/) and the [full off-campus housing guide](/cornell/housing/cornell-off-campus-housing-guide/) before October of your sophomore year. The [sophomore start page](/start/sophomore/) lists everything else in order.
