- The first-and-second-year live-on requirement started fall 2022, as the last halls opened.

Cornell's North Campus Residential Expansion built five residence halls with about 2,000 beds, with the remaining halls finished for fall 2022. The new beds are why Cornell could require first-years and sophomores to live in university or affiliated housing starting that fall ([Cornell Daily Sun](https://cornellsun.com/2021/11/09/as-cornell-requires-university-housing-for-sophomores-students-weigh-options/)).

## What did the North Campus Residential Expansion build?

Toni Morrison Hall and Ganędagǫ: Hall opened in 2021 with 800 beds. Three more halls, Barbara McClintock, Hu Shih and Ruth Bader Ginsburg, added 1,200 beds for fall 2022. The project took six and a half years, and Balch Hall closed for remodeling as the new halls opened ([Cornell Chronicle](https://news.cornell.edu/stories/2022/08/cornell-welcomes-students-marks-milestone-residential-life)).

## Why did Cornell build it?

Cornell's August 2018 plan called for 1,200 first-year beds and 800 sophomore beds, plus 75 for resident advisers, staff and live-in faculty, raising campus capacity from 8,400 to 10,400 beds. The site was the fields above Appel Commons and the C.C. lot, and the Sigma Alpha Mu house at 10 Sisson Place was demolished. The Sun's headline described the aim as housing all first-years and sophomores ([Cornell Daily Sun](https://cornellsun.com/2018/08/27/university-releases-full-plan-to-expand-north-campus-to-house-100-percent-of-freshmen-and-sophomores/)).

North Campus had been remade once before. In 2001, Court Hall and Mews Hall opened with a community center and housed about 600 of 3,029 first-years, and West Campus became upperclass-only (Cornell Daily Sun, [2001](https://cornellsun.com/2001/09/04/north-campus-grand-opening/)).

## What changed for students?

The big change was the rule. All first-years and sophomores must live in university or affiliated housing, with a required meal plan ([Cornell Residential Policy](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/campus-housing/residential-policy)). Today more than 3,000 new students live in North Campus halls and program houses, and upper-level students live in Balch, the north wing of Clara Dickson, Morrison, Ganędagǫ: and the Townhouse Apartments (Cornell Housing).

The price of staying is set by Cornell's table. A 2026-27 double costs $13,744 for the academic year, and the Unlimited meal plan is $7,604 a year ([Cornell Housing rates](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/housing-details/rates-amenities-licenses/housing-rates)). Add the $50 administrative fee, and the year is $21,398. For how that compares with a lease, read [is off-campus cheaper than a Cornell dorm](/cornell/housing/is-off-campus-cheaper-than-cornell-dorm/).

Early residents of Morrison and Ganędagǫ: told the Sun in October 2021 that they liked the space, hardwood floors, air conditioning and heating. They also reported flooding shower drains and heavy, always-shut suite doors that made the halls less social. All said the positives outweighed the negatives ([Cornell Daily Sun](https://cornellsun.com/2021/10/13/from-leaky-showers-to-lonely-suites-student-residents-voice-concerns-about-new-north-campus-dorms/)).

## Can you still live off campus?

Yes, in narrow cases. Married students are not subject to the requirement, and others qualify through exemptions or affiliated housing. The [exemptions guide](/cornell/housing/cornell-residential-requirement-exemptions-live-off-campus/) lists each one, and the quiz below checks yours.

## What does the expansion not tell us?

The pages Cornell publishes on the expansion do not state a construction cost. The [Cornell Chronicle's 2022 coverage](https://news.cornell.edu/stories/2022/08/cornell-welcomes-students-marks-milestone-residential-life) is the place to start looking for a total.
