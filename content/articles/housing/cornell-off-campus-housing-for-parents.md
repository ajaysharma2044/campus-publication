```glance
- Off campus starts in junior year for most students: a first- or second-year who signs a lease without an approved exemption is still billed for the full Cornell year ($13,744 for a 2026-27 double).
- The number to beat is $1,145 a month per person over a 12-month lease; only big shared units (6BR and up, shared-suite rooms, downtown 2BR and 3BR shares) come in under it.
- Popular Collegetown units for junior year sign in the fall of sophomore year, 8 to 16 months ahead.
- New York caps the deposit plus any rent advance at one month's rent, bans application and cleaning fees, and requires the deposit back within 14 days.
- Before you pay, call the City Building Division (607-274-6508) about the Certificate of Compliance and look the address up on Cornell's safety tool.
- Cornell aid does not cover the deposit, first and last month's rent or summer rent.
```

Off campus at Cornell usually costs more than the [$13,744 residence hall double for 2026-27](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/housing-details/rates-amenities-licenses/housing-rates), which includes gas, electricity, garbage and internet, unless your child shares a big unit. Most students cannot rent until junior year, and that lease is signed in the fall of sophomore year. Before anyone signs, cap the deposit at one month's rent, check the building's City safety record, and know that every housemate is liable for the whole rent.

## Who can live off campus, and when

Cornell's [residential policy](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/campus-housing/residential-policy) requires full-time, Ithaca-based first- and second-year students to live in university housing for two years. Year two can be a residence hall, one of the eight university-owned co-ops or a recognized fraternity or sorority house.

Cornell's [housing search page](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/campus-housing/housing-search-process) says first- and second-year undergraduates "should not sign a lease unless they have an approved exemption." A signed lease does not release a student. Students who neither meet the requirement nor get an exemption ["will still be billed for the full year"](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/campus-housing/requirements-qualified-university-recognized-campus-housing). For a double, that is $13,744 on top of rent.

The exemptions are narrow: 21 or older by the start of the academic year, married, coming with legal dependents, a veteran (not ROTC), a transfer entering as a junior or senior, or a commuter living with a parent within 25 miles. Students request one in the Housing application, which was due [April 6 in 2026](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/campus-housing/residential-policy), and it lasts one academic year.

So for most families, the first lease covers junior year. The awkward part is timing: that lease is usually signed during sophomore fall, while your child still lives in Cornell housing.

Both hold: Collegetown's popular units go early, and the wider market has supply into spring.

## What aid covers off campus

Cornell's [aid page for students living off campus](https://finaid.cornell.edu/living-campus) says need is calculated the same as on campus and the family contribution does not change. The Bursar stops billing housing, your child pays the landlord, and if aid exceeds Bursar charges a refund is issued for living costs.

Three things are not covered: security deposits, first and last month's rent, and rent in months your child is not registered. On a 12-month lease, that means June and July, and sometimes August, come out of savings or a sublet.

Example: a student on aid takes a downtown 3BR share at the $795 median. The year costs $9,540, well under the double, but two or three summer months ($1,590 to $2,385, our arithmetic) plus the deposit and first month ($1,590 at signing) are cash the family has to find. The same page advises finding housing that costs less over 12 months than the off-campus budget, and says Financial Aid will not intervene in a mid-year attempt to break a lease. Questions go to Financial Aid at 607-255-5145.

## What you are signing

### The deposit and fees

New York caps a security deposit plus any rent advance at [one month's rent](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/GOB/7-108). A landlord cannot ask for a deposit and last month's rent on top. The same law requires the deposit back with an itemized statement within 14 days of move-out, or the landlord forfeits the right to keep any of it. The landlord must offer a move-in inspection before your child moves in; if accepted, both sign a written condition record.

Landlords may not charge [any application fee](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/RPP/238-A). A background or credit check costs the lesser of actual cost or $20, and is waived if the applicant brings one from the past 30 days. Late fees are allowed only after rent is 5 days late, capped at the lesser of $50 or 5%. Non-refundable deposits and separate cleaning fees are [unlawful](https://sites.lawschool.cornell.edu/tenants-advocacy/security-deposits), per the Cornell Law guide.

### The 120-day waiver box

In the City of Ithaca, a landlord must give current tenants [120 days' written notice](https://wskg.org/ithaca-landlords-must-now-give-renters-120-days-before-renewing-leases/) before renewing, showing the unit or signing new tenants. The tenant can waive it only by initialing a bolded statement on the lease's first page. If that box is there, ask why before your child initials it.

### Joint liability

Cornell says most local leases bind housemates ["jointly and severally"](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/campus-living/living-campus/study-abroad/housemate-compatibility-questionnaire): each is responsible for the others' rent and damages for the whole term. The Cornell Law guide adds that a tenant who overpays [can sue a co-tenant for contribution](https://sites.lawschool.cornell.edu/tenants-advocacy/understanding-your-lease). That is a lawsuit among friends, not a refund from the landlord.

Example: your child signs a Collegetown 4BR at the $1,246.25 median with three friends. In January one friend leaves school and stops paying. The landlord can collect that $1,246.25 a month from the three who remain, about $415.42 each on top of their own rent (our arithmetic). From February through July, that is about $2,493 each.

### Co-signing a guarantee

The Cornell Law guide's lease page [does not address guarantors](https://sites.lawschool.cornell.edu/tenants-advocacy/understanding-your-lease). One rule does involve you: a tenant's sublet request in a building of four or more units must include any [guarantor's consent](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/RPP/226-B). So if your child goes abroad and sublets, expect to sign off.

Not confirmed: whether Collegetown landlords usually require a parent guarantor, and whether a typical guarantee covers your child's share or the whole household's rent.

Our advice: ask the landlord in writing what the guarantee covers, and for how long, before you sign it. The Cornell Law tenant hotline gives free advice on leases.

## Is it safe? A check you can do without a NetID

Cornell's [Off-Campus Housing Marketplace](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/campus-housing/housing-search-process/rental-listings-resources) only lets people who prove Cornell affiliation contact landlords. Two checks are open to anyone.

```figure
kind: steps
title: A parent's safety check before paying a deposit
rows:
  - {label: "Call the City Building Division about a current Certificate of Compliance"}
  - {label: "Look the address up on Cornell's safety tool (City of Ithaca rentals only)"}
  - {label: "Ask the landlord about past tenants, repairs and emergency contact"}
  - {label: "Ask past tenants whether their deposits came back"}
```

1. **Call about the Certificate of Compliance.** The City of Ithaca inspects multiple dwellings, including buildings with three or more units, for structure, fire protection, exits, heating, electrical and plumbing. Certificates for those buildings last [three years](https://www.cityofithacany.gov/146/Certificate-of-Compliance). Call the Building Division at 607-274-6508, 8 a.m. to 4 p.m. weekdays, or email ibd@cityofithaca.org, and ask whether the address has a current certificate.
2. **Look the address up on Cornell's safety tool.** The tool at safety.offcampusliving.cornell.edu, run by Off-Campus Living and the City, [shows sprinklers, extinguishers, smoke and carbon monoxide alarms, construction type and property use](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/campus-living/housing-search-process/safe-living-environments). It covers City of Ithaca rentals only, not the Town of Ithaca, Lansing or Cayuga Heights. It does not measure landlord quality or neighborhood safety.
3. **Ask the landlord Cornell's questions.** Cornell's [lease page](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/campus-housing/housing-search-process/signing-lease) suggests asking how long the last tenants stayed, who does repairs, and how to reach the landlord in an emergency, then asking past tenants about deposit returns.

Why the certificate matters: in 2018 the tenants at 117 Thurston Avenue learned after signing that the certificate [had expired in 2015](https://cornellsun.com/2018/11/25/cornell-student-abandons-house-sleeps-in-library-after-months-long-dispute-with-landlord/). It took 53 days from their first complaint for the heater to be reported fixed, and one tenant slept in a campus library.

New buildings carry a different risk. In 2016 the delayed Fontana Apartments left [more than 50 students with August 1 leases and no home](https://www.cornellsun.com/article/2016/09/delayed-collegetown-apartment-opening-leaves-e2-80-88students-homeless); the landlord offered a hotel or $30 a day. If the building is unfinished when you sign, ask what the lease says about a late opening.

Not confirmed: crime figures by Ithaca neighborhood. A parent on College Confidential called Collegetown effectively part of campus; that is one opinion, not data.

## Renters insurance

Cornell names GradGuard as its [preferred renters insurance](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/campus-living/living-campus), covering theft, fire, smoke and vandalism for students on and off campus, with no price published. Cornell recommends insurance but does not state it as a requirement; whether a landlord requires it depends on the lease. Ithaca quotes run from about $5 a month on [SafeButler](https://www.safebutler.com/cheap-best-renters-insurance-ithaca-ny) to about $27 a month on [Insure.com](https://www.insure.com/renters-insurance/average-renters-insurance-cost-in-new-york/) for a New York policy with $40,000 of property cover.

## Move-in day parking

Leases run on the landlord's dates, often August 1, and [most Ithaca leases end July 31](https://cornellsun.com/2024/07/10/landlords-and-advocates-grapple-with-good-cause-eviction-law-at-public-hearing). City meters cost [$2.50 an hour](https://www.cityofithacany.gov/705/Parking-Information), enforced 9 a.m. to 6 p.m. Monday through Saturday and free on Sundays; the city garages charge $2.25 an hour. The [Dryden Road Garage](https://www.cityofithacany.gov/718/Dryden-Road-Garage) at 118 Dryden Road, in Collegetown, is open 24 hours with an $18 daily maximum. Cars there must move every 24 hours. Use the day to walk the unit with your child and photograph every room before boxes come in.

## Common mistakes

- **Signing for sophomore year.** Without an approved exemption, your child pays rent and is billed $13,744 for the Cornell double anyway.
- **Comparing 12 months of rent with Cornell's academic-year rate.** A $1,246.25 bed is $14,955 over 12 months, not the $12,462.50 that 10 months suggests.
- **Paying a deposit plus last month's rent.** Together they cannot exceed one month's rent; on a $1,246.25 bed, a second month up front is $1,246.25 the landlord should not hold.
- **Signing with a group that later shrinks.** Moving from a 6BR median to a 4BR one costs $446.25 a person a month, $5,355 a year (our arithmetic on the Ithaca Renting medians), and remaining housemates carry a leaver's share.
- **Keeping a backup Cornell room too long.** A returning junior's cancellation costs [$50 through January 31](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/housing-details/rates-amenities-licenses/academic-year-housing-license-2027-2028), then $500, $1,000 from April 1 and $2,000 from July 1.
- **Skipping the certificate call.** The 117 Thurston tenants found the lapse after signing and waited 53 days for heat.

## FAQ

**Can my child cancel a Cornell room after finding an apartment?** Returning juniors and seniors can, for $50 through January 31, rising to $2,000 between July 1 and August 15 and 50% of the fall room rate after that. First-years and second-years cannot, unless they stop attending, are exempt or (second-years only) are accepted to a co-op or Greek house in time.

**Does Cornell housing take a security deposit?** No. Cornell housing is a [revocable license, not a lease](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/housing-details/rates-amenities-licenses/academic-year-housing-license-2027-2028), with no security or key deposits.

**How fast should the deposit come back?** Within [14 days](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/GOB/7-108) of move-out, with an itemized statement of anything kept; a willful violation can cost the landlord up to twice the deposit.

**Can I find listings without a NetID?** Cornell [names other channels](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/campus-housing/housing-search-process/rental-listings-resources): Ithacarents.com, the Cornell Daily Sun, the Ithaca Journal and the Ithaca Times. Cornell does not endorse any landlord on them.

**Is the downtown discount worth the walk?** In our comparison of Ithaca Renting's medians, its [downtown per-bed prices](https://ithacarenting.com/downtown/) run about 20% to 28% below Collegetown, at the cost of a [20 to 30 minute uphill walk](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/campus-housing/housing-search-process/ithaca-neighborhoods) or a bus ride.

**What if the landlord won't fix the heat?** Put the request in writing, then call the City Building Division or the tenant hotline; the [repairs guide](/housing/ithaca-landlord-wont-fix-heat-mold-bed-bugs/) has the steps.
