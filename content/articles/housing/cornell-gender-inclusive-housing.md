Any Cornell undergraduate can share a room or suite with students of any gender, regardless of legal sex ([Cornell Housing](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/housing-and-residential-life-and-faculty-involvement-campus/gender-inclusive-housing)). First-years and new transfers opt in on the housing application. Returning students form a block and get a Housing Office override when they pick rooms.

## What is gender-inclusive housing at Cornell?

It is an opt-in that puts you in the same room or suite as students of a different gender identity or legal sex. Legal sex here means the sex marker in Cornell's records. Without the option, Cornell assigns roommates by that marker.

Students who express their gender outside a typical binary, such as nonbinary, genderqueer, transgender or agender students, get priority. But the option is open to anyone, including cisgender men and women who want to live together, and students who want suitemates of the same identity regardless of legal sex. Anyone who opts in should be open to living with a person of any gender (Cornell Housing, linked above).

The Student Assembly passed Resolution #12, "Adoption of a Gender-Inclusive Housing Policy," in fall 2012, and President David Skorton endorsed it that same semester. The first phase opened in the spring 2013 room selection for 2013-14, in program houses, the Language House, the co-ops, the West Campus Houses and the upper-level residence halls. Since 2017-18, the option has been open to all students (Cornell Housing, linked above).

## How is it different from mixed-gender housing?

Mixed-gender housing puts students of different genders in the same building, but each room stays single-gender. Most Cornell halls work this way. In gender-inclusive housing, the room or suite itself is shared.

Two halls are open only to female-identified students: Balch Hall on North Campus and Lyon Hall, part of Flora Rose House on West Campus. Some other buildings keep same-gender floors (Cornell Housing, linked above). Cornell's upper-level housing page also lists Low Rise 7 as a community for female-identified students in all four years ([Cornell upper-level housing](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/campus-housing/upperlevel-undergraduate-campus-housing)).

Floor rules differ by building: Cascadilla Hall in Collegetown, for example, has mixed-gender floors ([Cascadilla Hall](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/campus-housing/upper-level-undergraduates/residence-halls/cascadilla-hall)). If a same-gender or mixed floor matters to you, check the hall's own page before you choose.

## How do first-year students opt in?

You check a box on the housing application in the Housing & Dining Portal. Cornell asks first-years to apply within 10 days of receiving their NetID, the Cornell login you get after you commit. Before the opt-in counts, you watch a short video and agree to the rules ([Cornell first-year application](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/housing-and-residential-life-and-faculty-involvement-campus/apply-housing-campus/new-undergrad-application-process)). The same screen lets you ask for a female-only community instead.

You can also fill out a roommate questionnaire or name a specific student. Cornell tries to honor a named request but does not promise it. Room confirmations start going out in mid-June.

After you opt in, the Housing Office follows up to work out which placement fits best. Ask for any special placement before assignments go out: singles are limited, and early requests are easier to meet ([LGBT Resource Center](https://scl.cornell.edu/belonging-support-services/centers-student-equity-empowerment-and-belonging/lgbt-resource-center/trans-resources-cornell)).

```reader "If you're a transfer student"
New transfers opt in on the same housing application, by its deadline. Transfers are placed across West, South and North Campus and in program houses, not only in first-year halls ([Cornell transfer housing](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/campus-housing/new-transfer-undergraduates)). The [transfer housing guide](/cornell/housing/cornell-transfer-housing/) covers the full process.
```

## How do rising sophomores, juniors and seniors get a gender-inclusive room?

You form a block, the portal's word for a group that picks rooms together, and the Housing Office approves the shared room during your timeslot. Anyone can join a block regardless of gender identity, but the portal won't put different gender markers in one room on its own. At your timeslot, contact the Housing Office for an override, or ask it to select for you as a proxy ([Cornell Room Selection FAQs](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/housing-and-residential-life-and-faculty-involvement-campus/apply-housing-campus/current-undergraduate-application-process/room-selection-faqs)).

A new group of students gets portal access every 20 minutes, so call or write to the Housing Office as soon as your timeslot opens. Its number is (607) 255-5368, its email is housing@cornell.edu, and its office is at 1501 Clara Dickson Hall (LGBT Resource Center, linked above).

**Watch out:** a block that leaves a bed empty does not keep it. Cornell fills the vacant space with a student who matches the gender identity of those already assigned, or opens it to another student. If a mixed-gender block leaves space, the leftover beds are labeled "Co-Ed" (Cornell Room Selection FAQs).

During selection, each room or suite is marked "M" for male, "F" for female or "D" for dynamic. A dynamic room takes the gender of the first person who reserves a bed in it. A "Co-Ed" room already has more than one gender assigned. If you pick a free bed in someone else's room, you can only choose beds open to your gender marker.

## Which Cornell halls and houses are gender-inclusive?

Any hall can hold a gender-inclusive room, because the opt-in and the override work wherever a room is open. Three communities build inclusion into the building itself, and two of them take first-years.

| Community | Where | Who can live there | Gender setup |
|---|---|---|---|
| Loving House | Mews Hall, North Campus | First-years, transfers, upper-level | 30 residents, singles and doubles, all-gender bathrooms |
| Risley Residential College | Prudence Risley Hall, North Campus | First-years, transfers, upper-level | 194 residents, singles and doubles, all-gender bathrooms above the first floor |
| Language House | Toni Morrison Hall, North Campus | Upper-level only | 50+ residents, suites are gender-inclusive |

Source: Cornell Student & Campus Life pages for [Loving House](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/campus-housing/upper-level-undergraduates/program-houses/loving-house), [Risley](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/campus-housing/upper-level-undergraduates/program-houses/risley-residential-college) and [Language House](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/campus-housing/upper-level-undergraduates/program-houses/language-house).

Loving House is Cornell's program house for LGBTQIA+ students and allies. A program house is a themed living-learning community inside a residence hall. Risley is Cornell's first program house, built around the arts. The Language House sits in Toni Morrison Hall, but Cornell says its future location is still being decided.

For first-years who skip the opt-in but want all-gender bathrooms, the LGBT Resource Center points to Mary Donlon Hall. Donlon has gender-neutral bathrooms on every floor and mixed-gender floors and hallways (LGBT Resource Center, linked above).

The cooperative houses have offered gender-inclusive living since the 2013 first phase. Each co-op sets its own placement, so ask the house directly. The guide to [Cornell co-ops and Greek houses](/cornell/housing/cornell-co-ops-greek-houses-cost-how-to-join/) covers how to join.

## What if your gender marker doesn't match your identity?

Ask the Housing Office for special placement. It places students whose marker has not changed in a hall, floor or wing that matches their identity. That includes trans women who want to live in Balch Hall (LGBT Resource Center, linked above).

You can also change the marker itself. Email a written request to the Registrar at univreg@cornell.edu from your Cornell account, with your name, NetID or student ID, and the marker you want (male, female or undisclosed). The change flows to systems fed by Cornell's student records. It does not change your marker in Cornell Health's systems.

The Housing Office also arranges winter and summer break housing for transgender students who are not safe or welcome at home.

```reader "If you're a parent"
Your student makes the choice in their own application, and the Housing Office deals with the student, not with you. If your student wants a specific placement, remind them to contact the Housing Office before assignments go out.
```

## Does gender-inclusive housing apply off campus?

No. The opt-in covers Cornell-run rooms only. In a private apartment, you and your roommates choose each other and sign the lease together. Read the guide to [off-campus roommates and joint liability](/cornell/housing/cornell-off-campus-roommates-joint-liability/) before you sign with a group. First-years, new transfers and sophomores must live on campus unless Cornell grants an exemption, covered in our [residential requirement guide](/cornell/housing/cornell-residential-requirement-exemptions-live-off-campus/).

## FAQ

**Does gender-inclusive housing cost more?**
No separate fee is listed for the opt-in. Cornell prices housing by room type, and the charge appears on the bursar bill.

**Do graduate students have a gender-inclusive option?**
Cornell's gender-inclusive page covers undergraduates. Graduate students should ask the Housing Office at housing@cornell.edu before they apply.

## What to do next

If you're an incoming student, opt in on your housing application and email housing@cornell.edu early if you need a specific placement. If you're a rising sophomore, line up your block before the April 4 deadline and keep the Housing Office's number ready for your timeslot.
