The Wait Avenue Cooperative at 302 Wait Ave. is a Cornell-owned house with 14 residents, all women, in singles and doubles. Laundry, parking and summer storage are free, and there is no house meal plan (Cornell Housing). It takes both undergraduate and graduate or professional students.

## What is 302 Wait Avenue?

A co-op is a university-owned house run by its residents. Cornell's page lists 14 residents, all women, undergraduate and graduate or professional. The house is more than 100 years old ([Cornell Housing](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/campus-housing/upper-level-undergraduates/cooperative-housing/wait-avenue-cooperative)). It sits beside Wait Terrace at 308 Wait Ave., which has the same rates.

## What does 302 Wait Avenue cost?

For 2026-27 a double is $4,715 per semester, or $9,430 for two semesters. A single is $5,340 to $5,693 per semester, which is $10,680 to $11,386 for the year ([Cornell Housing](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/housing-details/rates-amenities-licenses/housing-rates)).

A hall double costs $13,744 for the year, so the 302 Wait double is $4,314 less. With no house meal plan, you cook for yourself from the provided staples. Cornell does not list the house among the four with a meal plan ([Cornell Housing](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/campus-housing/upper-level-undergraduates/cooperative-housing)). For leases, read [Is off campus cheaper than a Cornell dorm?](/cornell/housing/is-off-campus-cheaper-than-cornell-dorm/) and [cheapest off-campus housing near Cornell](/cornell/housing/cheapest-off-campus-housing-cornell/).

## How do you get into 302 Wait Avenue?

Each co-op runs its own selection, and selection starts in early November and early February (Cornell Housing). Write to waitavenue@cornell.edu for the house's steps, or to co-ops@cornell.edu or 607-255-3445. Cornell's page for this house does not describe its selection process.

Rising sophomores must state their co-op plan on the 2027-28 application by March 15, 2027 ([Cornell Housing](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/housing-and-residential-life-and-faculty-involvement-campus/apply-housing-campus/current-undergraduate-application-process/spring-housing-selection-rising-sophomores)). A co-op counts as affiliated housing, explained on our [affiliated housing page](/start/affiliated-housing/). To live off campus instead, check the [exemption rules](/cornell/housing/cornell-residential-requirement-exemptions-live-off-campus/).

## What should you watch?

The house is old. The Sun reported in October 2024 that four of the eight co-ops had floors collapse in recent years([Cornell Daily Sun](https://cornellsun.com/2024/10/21/co-op-residents-face-deteriorating-houses-increasing-rent-costs/)). Ask the house what has been repaired.

All eight co-ops share one license, with no security deposit and a full-time student rule. See [Cornell co-ops compared](/cornell/housing/cornell-co-ops-all-eight-compared/) and our guide to [co-ops and Greek houses](/cornell/housing/cornell-co-ops-greek-houses-cost-how-to-join/).
