```glance
- Cascadilla holds 366 residents; Sheldon Court holds 164.
- Neither hall has room air conditioning.
- Both qualify for the Collegetown Meal Plan, which costs less than Unlimited.
- Sheldon sits above Collegetown Bagels on College Avenue, so street noise comes with it.
```

Pick Sheldon Court if you want a smaller hall in the middle of Collegetown. Pick Cascadilla Hall if you want a bigger community and the South Campus Service Center in your building. Both have singles and doubles on corridors, no room air conditioning, and the same [2026-27 room rates](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/housing-details/rates-amenities-licenses/housing-rates).

## How do Cascadilla and Sheldon Court compare?

Cascadilla is older and larger. Sheldon is newer by 39 years and has fewer than half the residents. Both are on Cornell's South Campus list of upper-level halls (Cornell Student & Campus Life).

A corridor hall means rooms along a hallway with bathrooms shared by the floor.

## What is each hall like to live in?

Cascadilla was a water-cure sanitarium, a spa that treated illness with water. Ezra Cornell invested in it, bought out the other investors and finished it ([The Cornell Daily Sun](https://cornellsun.com/2013/09/19/ezras-oracle-sept-20-2013)). Cornell's housing page says the building dates to 1864 and became Cornell's first residence hall when ownership transferred in June 1869 ([Cornell Housing](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/campus-housing/upper-level-undergraduates/residence-halls/cascadilla-hall)).

Today it houses transfer and upper-level students, and its ground floor holds the service center for all Collegetown-area halls. That means packages and keys for neighbors such as Schuyler House pass through its doors.

Sheldon Court opened in 1903, run by Charles L. Sheldon Jr. as "Cornell's First Dormitory." Cornell bought it in 1955. A resident account in The Cornell Daily Sun in April 2026 describes five floors plus a basement, three showers, sinks and toilets per bathroom, and basement laundry with four washers and four dryers. It also says the elevator breaks down often, that the nearest dining hall is about a 12-minute walk away on West Campus, and that many residents get a double to themselves ([The Cornell Daily Sun](https://www.cornellsun.com/article/2026/04/the-misunderstood-dorm-sheldon-court)).

The same coverage lists College Avenue noise as a trade-off, since the building sits above Collegetown Bagels. The College & Oak bus stop is across the street.

## What does each hall cost?

Room price depends on room type, not on the building. For 2026-27, a double is $13,744 per academic year and a single is $15,566 (Cornell Student & Campus Life).

Meals are the real difference between these halls and a West Campus dorm. Residents of Cascadilla, Sheldon and four other South Campus and Collegetown buildings can take the Collegetown Meal Plan: $3,528 per semester, or $7,056 a year, with up to 10 meals a week, $500 in Big Red Bucks and $436 in City Bucks per semester ([Cornell Dining](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/dining/meal-plans-rates/undergraduate-meal-plans)). City Bucks are taxable spending money for Collegetown businesses.

A double plus the Collegetown plan plus the $50 annual fee is $20,850 for the year. A single is $22,672. The same double with the $7,604 Unlimited plan is $21,398, so the Collegetown plan saves $548 ([Cornell housing rates](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/housing-details/rates-amenities-licenses/housing-rates)).

## How do move-in and move-out differ?

For fall 2026, Cascadilla residents unloaded at the Eddygate entrance, and drivers then moved their cars to the Williams Street Lot at Williams Street and Stewart Avenue. The same page lists limited spots in the Sheldon Court loading dock ([Cornell move instructions](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/housing-details/moving-campus/move-instructions/move-instructions-cascadilla-hall)).

The Cascadilla Service Center was closed on Aug. 17, open 8 a.m. to 7 p.m. on Aug. 18 to 22, and open 10 a.m. to 4 p.m. on Aug. 23. Plan your key pickup around those hours. The full parking plan is in the [move-in day guide](/cornell/getting-around/cornell-move-in-day-parking-unloading/).

At move-out, both halls use the City of Ithaca garage south of Cascadilla and the Williams Street Garage ([Cornell loading information](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/housing-details/moving-out/important-loading-and-parking-information)). Parking there is tight: a 2023 report said the Williams Street lot's permits, about $700 then, sold out within hours ([The Cornell Daily Sun](https://www.cornellsun.com/article/2023/09/williams-street-parking-lot-struggles-to-accommodate-students)). If you cannot take everything home, see the [storage and shipping guide](/cornell/getting-around/cornell-move-out-storage-shipping-options/).

## Which hall stays open over winter break?

Cascadilla is on Cornell's winter break list for 2026-27, alongside Veterans House and several North Campus halls. Sheldon Court is not on that list ([Cornell winter break housing](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/campus-housing/winter-break-housing)). If you stay in Ithaca in December and January, that favors Cascadilla. Applications run Oct. 1 to Dec. 1.

## Which hall should I choose?

Choose Cascadilla if you stay over winter break, want a bigger hall, or want the service center downstairs. Choose Sheldon if you want the smaller hall, a lounge with a pool table and a College Avenue address, and you can live with street noise and an elevator that breaks down. If you run hot, neither helps: both lack room air conditioning.

## What to do next

1. Check your room-selection timeslot and decide which hall you would take if your first choice is gone.
2. Price the Collegetown plan against Unlimited before you sign.
