There is no current, trustworthy list of good and bad Collegetown landlords, and we will not invent one. What exists is a 2004 Cornell Daily Sun feature, a 2013 ownership analysis, a few student disputes and thin review-site samples. What you can do is check the specific building and owner you are about to sign with, using public records and your own rights. Start with the City of Ithaca's certificate of compliance, then ask the owner the questions below, then read the lease with the 14-day deposit rule in mind.

## What the top results get right and miss

The [2004 Sun feature](https://cornellsun.com/2004/12/02/good-bad-and-slummy-collegetown-landlords/) is a qualitative piece built on interviews. It names landlords and managers, and its themes still hold: responsiveness to repairs, how rent checks and paperwork are handled, fairness on security deposits, and how much a landlord intrudes. It says nothing current, and one line about a management company is a single tenant's suspicion about deposits, not a finding. Treat it as a list of things to ask, not a verdict on anyone today.

The [2013 Sun analysis](https://cornellsun.com/2013/05/02/collegetown-housing-market-scrutinized/) is more useful for one reason: it shows who owns the stock. Using city building records, county assessment data and landlords' own sites, it found that eight landlords owned about $153 million of Collegetown property and 4,013 of an estimated 6,110 bedrooms, almost two-thirds. The Sun noted the numbers do not prove the market was consolidating further. Those figures are from 2013, so ownership has certainly shifted since.

## Who owns Collegetown, as of 2013

The 2013 analysis named these owners, and the first two each held more than $38 million of property:

- John Novarr, who held 1,559 of the beds, largely through the Collegetown Terrace project
- Jason Fane
- The Avramis, Lambrou and Lower families
- Travis Hyde, Pam Johnston and Matoula Halkiopoulus

Being large says nothing about quality. A 2022 Sun opinion column by Niko Nguyen argues that [a small group of landlords and developers controls Collegetown](https://cornellsun.com/2022/08/04/nguyen-the-tale-of-cornells-broken-housing-market/), and it describes his own apartment problems and a friend's mold and burst pipe. It is one writer's view, not a survey. It is a reason to check the owner, not a reason to rule one out.

## How to check a landlord before you sign

```figure
kind: steps
title: Vetting a Collegetown landlord
rows:
  - label: Find the owner or manager's name and every building they run
  - label: Ask whether the unit has a current City of Ithaca certificate of compliance
  - label: Call the Building Division about inspections and complaints
  - label: Search for tenant reports and talk to current residents
  - label: Read the lease, then confirm deposit and renewal terms
```

1. **Identify the owner, not just the leasing office.** Leasing agents change; the owner on the lease is who you will deal with. Then look at their other buildings. Our advice: a walk past three of their houses takes ten minutes and shows how they are kept.
2. **Ask for the certificate of compliance.** The City of Ithaca requires rental owners to apply for and schedule a housing inspection [before the current certificate expires](https://www.cityofithacany.gov/146/Certificate-of-Compliance). A multiple dwelling (three or more units, or a unit with six or more unrelated people) can hold a valid certificate for three years. Ask the landlord for the expiration date in writing.
3. **Call the Building Division.** The city page does not describe a public lookup, so verification is by phone: the [Building Division is at 607-274-6508](https://www.cityofithacany.gov/146/Certificate-of-Compliance), 8 AM to 4 PM, Monday through Friday. Ask whether the address has a valid certificate and whether there are open complaints or violations.
4. **Check Cornell's safety tool.** Cornell and the Building Division built an [online tool showing fire safety, alarms, egress and construction type](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/campus-living/housing-search-process/safe-living-environments) for City of Ithaca rentals, updated daily. It covers safety features, not repair history or landlord behavior, and properties outside the city are exempt.
5. **Look for tenant reports.** Search the address and owner name on Google reviews, r/Cornell and r/ithaca, and the [Ithaca Tenants' Union](https://ithacatu.org). We found few Ithaca-specific threads, and review-site samples for Collegetown buildings are small, sometimes only a handful of reviews. Count how many reviews there are before you weigh the score, and note whether the complaints are about repairs, deposits or entry without notice.
6. **Talk to current tenants.** Knock on the door of the building you are considering and ask about heat, hot water, response time and whether they got their deposit back.

## What a bad situation looks like

The best-documented recent case is the [Sun's 2018 report on 117 Thurston Avenue](https://cornellsun.com/2018/11/25/cornell-student-abandons-house-sleeps-in-library-after-months-long-dispute-with-landlord/). Students there said they learned after signing that the house's certificate of compliance had expired in November 2015. They reported repeated toilet overflows and about three days without hot water, and a city inspection later found insufficient heat. The landlord called the certificate "good to have" but not required, denied the hot-water problem and blamed the students for the toilet; the city's code enforcement director said certificates are required for all dwellings. One student ended up sleeping in a library. The article also says it was unclear whether the Building Department could do more than issue warnings, which is why checking before you sign beats complaining after.

## Know your deposit and renewal rights

- **Deposit return.** Under [New York General Obligations Law 7-108](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/GOB/7-108), a landlord must return the deposit and give an itemized statement of any deductions within 14 days after you move out, and forfeits the right to keep any of it if they miss that window. The same section says a deposit may not exceed one month's rent in the settings it covers.
- **Renewal timing.** A city amendment effective May 31, 2022 requires landlords to give current tenants [at least 120 days' written notice](https://cornellsun.com/2022/04/18/mehler-student-tenants-rights-shifting-in-your-favor/) before renewing, showing the unit or signing a new tenant. Both sides can waive it, but the lease must say so on its first page. If a lease asks you to waive it, you are being asked to give up the protection against the early-leasing rush the [Sun has covered for years](https://cornellsun.com/2022/08/04/nguyen-the-tale-of-cornells-broken-housing-market/).

## Where to get help if something goes wrong

Cornell's [off-campus resources page](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/campus-living/campus-living-resources) lists the City of Ithaca Building Division (607-274-6508) for unsafe conditions, the Community Dispute Resolution Center (607-273-9347) for mediation, Legal Assistance of Western New York in Ithaca (607-273-3667) for free legal help, and the Cornell Law School Tenant Advocacy Practicum (607-301-1560). It also lists the Ithaca Tenants' Union.

## Common mistakes

Our advice, drawn from the above:

- Signing on the first day because of the rush, before checking the certificate.
- Judging a landlord by a star rating with a handful of reviews.
- Assuming a big owner is a bad owner, or a small one a good owner.
- Accepting a verbal promise about repairs. Get it in the lease or an email.
- Waiving the 120-day notice period without asking why.

## FAQ

**Is there an official list of bad Collegetown landlords?** Not one we found. Cornell's resources page points to the Building Division and tenant groups rather than ranking landlords.

**Who owns most of Collegetown?** In 2013, eight owners held almost two-thirds of the bedrooms, per the [Sun's analysis](https://cornellsun.com/2013/05/02/collegetown-housing-market-scrutinized/). Check current assessment and building records for today's picture.

**How do I know a unit is legally rentable?** Ask for the certificate of compliance expiration date and confirm it with the Building Division.

**I am a transfer student; do I need this?** Only if you are exempt from the residential requirement. See [Cornell transfer housing](/housing/cornell-transfer-housing/) for who has to live on campus, and browse more in [housing](/housing/).
