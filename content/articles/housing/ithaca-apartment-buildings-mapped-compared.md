In early December 2025, The Ivy was a concrete base and one finished level at the corner of Dryden Road and College Avenue. Its developers had already leased nearly all 52 apartments. A three-bedroom there was priced at $5,500 to $5,800 a month, and the cheapest bed, in a basement two-bedroom, went for $1,595 a month per person ([The Cornell Daily Sun](https://www.cornellsun.com/article/2025/12/the-ivy-to-establish-new-luxury-housing-in-collegetown-opens-fall-2026)).

A block away, the Student Agencies Building at 409 College Ave. calls itself the closest apartment building to campus. Its home page says every unit has been leased and offers a waitlist for 2027-28 ([Student Agencies Building](https://www.thestudentagenciesbuilding.com/)).

That is the Ithaca rental market in small. The closer a building stands to Cornell's Ho Plaza, the earlier it fills and the less its landlord prints before you call. Farther out, age matters more than distance: downtown's new towers charge more for a studio than many Collegetown landlords charge for a bed. The map below holds 373 apartment buildings and rental houses in and around Ithaca, each with its manager, owner, size, printed rent, lease dates, age, safety certificate and distance from campus (EduExplained building map).

The stakes are a year of rent. Cornell's residence hall double costs $13,744 for 2026-27, utilities and internet included ([Cornell Housing rates](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/housing-and-residential-life-and-faculty-involvement-campus/rates-amenities-licenses/housing-rates)). Spread over a 12-month lease, that is $1,145.33 a month ($13,744 divided by 12). Hold every rent on this map against that number.

## How to read the map

Each row in the tables at the bottom of this page is one property: a building, a complex or a rental house. The rows come from 35 landlord and listing sources, each linked in its row. Cornell's off-campus marketplace, the Ithaca College marketplace and [IthacaRents](https://www.ithacarents.com/), the site run by the Landlords Association of Tompkins County, cover most of the small landlords. Zillow and Apartments.com bar automated reading in their terms and are not included, so some buildings are missing.

Owner of record means the name on the county tax roll, which is often a company rather than a person. It comes from the [Tompkins County 2026 final assessment roll](https://www.tompkinscountyny.gov/files/assets/county/v/1/assessment/documents/assessment-rolls/city-final-2026.pdf) and state and county parcel maps, matched by address for 130 properties. The year shown is the year on that record, which can reflect a renovation or an older building on the same lot.

Distance is a straight line from Ho Plaza to the building, measured from coordinates the [Census Geocoder](https://geocoding.geo.census.gov/geocoder/) matched for 361 of the 373 properties. Hills, gorges and bridges make the walk longer than the line.

Prices are asking rents as each page printed them. A per-bed price is what one person pays for one bedroom in a shared unit. A per-unit price is the rent for the whole apartment or house, split however the tenants agree. Most pages print no lease year, and the tables say so row by row.

## Ring one: Collegetown, where the map is densest

Collegetown is the dense grid at the southwest edge of campus, around College Avenue, Dryden Road, Eddy Street and Linden Avenue. Cornell describes it as student housing packed into a residential neighborhood, 0.1 to 0.5 miles from campus and up to a 15-minute walk ([Cornell's neighborhoods page](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/campus-housing/housing-search-process/ithaca-neighborhoods)). The map agrees. The median Collegetown property sits 0.46 straight-line miles from Ho Plaza, and 151 rentals across the whole map sit within half a mile (EduExplained building map).

Collegetown is also where landlords say the least up front. Only 91 of its 205 properties print a price. On Blair Street, Cook Street and Eddy Street, many listings name a house size, such as seven or eight bedrooms, and a price of "Ask" ([Ithaca College marketplace](https://offcampushousing.ithaca.edu/listing)). Downtown, 47 of 63 properties print a price.

The prices that do appear trace a clear rule: the bigger the shared unit, the cheaper the bed. Ithaca Renting's 2027-28 Collegetown medians run from $800 per bed in a six-bedroom to $2,025 for a one-bedroom alone ([EduExplained dataset](/datasets/collegetown-rent-per-bed-2027-28/)). PJ Apartments prints whole-house rents with no lease year, and the per-bed math lands in the same band. Its 14-bedroom house at 125 Highland Place lists $16,730 a month, or $1,195 a bed ($16,730 divided by 14). Its 12-bedroom house at 202 Williams St. lists $14,340, also $1,195 a bed ([Ithaca College marketplace](https://offcampushousing.ithaca.edu/listing)).

Newer buildings sell on location and finish. Dryden South, at 205 Dryden Road, rents its four-bedroom suites for $1,360 to $1,575 a person for 2027-28, and every suite showed as rented. Its two studios, at $1,990 and $2,190, were still open. All its leases start June 1 and run 12 months ([Dryden South](https://drydensouth.com/availability/)).

### How Collegetown got this way

Collegetown has been a landlord's neighborhood for a long time. In 2013, The Cornell Daily Sun matched city occupancy records against county assessments. It found that eight landlords owned about $153 million of Collegetown property and 4,013 of an estimated 6,110 bedrooms, almost two-thirds. John Novarr alone held 1,559 bedrooms. Novarr and Jason Fane each owned more than $38 million in property. The other six were the Avramis family, Travis Hyde, the Lambrou family, Pam Johnston, the Lower family and Matoula Halkiopoulus ([The Cornell Daily Sun](https://cornellsun.com/2013/05/02/collegetown-housing-market-scrutinized/)).

Several of those names still run the map. The county roll lists Jason Fane or Fane Enterprises as owner of record for 10 of Ithaca Renting Company's properties, from 202, 210 and 214 Dryden Road to Whiton Hall downtown (Tompkins County assessment roll). Ithaca Renting lists 18 properties on its own site ([Ithaca Renting](https://ithacarenting.com/all-properties/)). A partnership called ENP Associates is owner of record for 14 of the 37 properties Lambrou Real Estate lists, and members of the Lambrou family hold several more in their own names ([Lambrou Real Estate](https://www.lambrourealestate.com/apartments)). Lambrou's largest house, at 140 College Ave., has 24 bedrooms.

The big buildings came in waves. In August 2016, three new buildings added nearly 200 bedrooms: Collegetown Crossing at 307 College Ave., the Fontana at 327 Eddy St. and Dryden South ([The Cornell Daily Sun](https://cornellsun.com/2016/09/26/landlords-say-collegetown-housing-hassle-unlikely-to-change/)). Josh Lower, who built Collegetown Crossing and heads Urban Ithaca, said then that his firm rents most of its units by the end of November.

Catherine Commons came next. Developer Novarr Mackesey planned about 360 units on both sides of Catherine Street at College Avenue, replacing 11 two-story houses for a net gain of 339 bedrooms, with completion set for August 2024 ([The Cornell Review](https://www.thecornellreview.org/cornells-construction-crunch-ithacas-housing-crisis-and-the-catherine-commons/)). Its marketplace listing prints a "from" range of $1,615 to $2,479 with no lease year ([Cornell marketplace](https://offcampus.housing.cornell.edu/city/ithaca-college-ny/listing/catherine-commons-236692)).

Then The Ivy. Ithaca's Planning Board gave the eight-story, 52-unit building final approval in April 2025 ([Finger Lakes 1](https://www.fingerlakes1.com/2025/04/25/ithaca-greenlights-major-collegetown-project-eyes-more/)). Its leases were set to begin Aug. 17, 2026. Its developers are Nick Robertson, who worked on Collegetown Terrace and Cornell's North Campus expansion, and Charlie O'Connor, who owns Modern Living Rentals. Modern Living manages 44 properties on this map, more than any other company.

The city has pushed the same direction. In March 2025, it proposed raising Collegetown height limits from five stories to seven in the MU-1 district and from six to eight in MU-2 ([Finger Lakes 1](https://www.fingerlakes1.com/2025/03/12/ithaca-proposes-zoning-changes-to-collegetown-area-allowing-taller-buildings)). The stated reason was to make projects financially workable with modern construction.

## Ring two: Cornell Heights, Fall Creek and Belle Sherman

North and west of campus, the map thins out. This ring holds 58 properties, and its median sits 0.6 straight-line miles from Ho Plaza (EduExplained building map). Travis Hyde Properties manages buildings on Lake Street, University Avenue and Stewart Avenue, including Ravenwood at 516 University Ave. and 934 Edgecliff. Downtown and the Commons hold 63 properties, with a median of 0.87 straight-line miles from Ho Plaza (EduExplained building map). Cornell calls it a 20- to 30-minute uphill walk with frequent buses.

## Who owns the map

Seven companies manage 179 of the 373 properties. Modern Living Rentals manages 44, Lambrou Real Estate 35, Urban Ithaca 26, PJ Apartments 24, Ithaca Renting 18, Travis Hyde 17 and PPM Homes 15 (EduExplained building map). Each counts one property per address, so a 50-unit building and a five-bedroom house weigh the same. Another 124 properties name no manager at all, most of them listings on the two college marketplaces.

Ownership is harder to see. Of the 130 properties with an owner of record on the county roll, at least 100 are held by a limited liability company, a partnership, a trust or a corporation (Tompkins County assessment roll). Many LLCs carry the building's own address as a name, such as "222 Dryden Road, LLC." That is legal. It also means a renter checking a landlord's record needs the manager's name and the owner's name, and the tables give both where the records show them.

The 2013 picture and the 2026 one are not directly comparable. The Sun counted bedrooms and dollars, and this map counts addresses. Both point the same way: a handful of firms and families control much of what a Cornell student can rent within walking distance. Reviews of specific landlords live in our [guide to Ithaca landlords](/cornell/housing/ithaca-landlords-to-avoid-collegetown-reviews/).

## Old bones, new law

Ithaca's rental stock is old. Of the 107 properties with a year on the county roll, 69 list a year before 1920. Only 13 list nothing earlier than 2010 (Tompkins County assessment roll). Those 13 include The Ivy, Collegetown Crossing, 238 Dryden Road, the Carey Building downtown and INHS's 210 Hancock.

That split matters under Good Cause Eviction. Ithaca's Common Council opted into the state law 8-2 in July 2024 ([WAMC](https://www.wamc.org/2024-07-12/ithaca-is-the-latest-upstate-city-to-opt-into-good-cause-renter-protections)). Under it, a rent increase above the lower of 10% or 5% plus the local consumer price index is presumed unreasonable. Units in buildings whose certificate of occupancy, the city's permit to live in a new building, was issued on or after Jan. 1, 2009, are exempt for 30 years ([Cornell Law Tenants Advocacy Program](https://sites.lawschool.cornell.edu/tenants-advocacy/rent-increases)).

By the county's build years, the newest and priciest buildings on the map are the ones most likely to sit outside Good Cause rent limits. A build year is only a guide. Ask the landlord for the certificate of occupancy date before you count on the protection.

## The safety record

The City of Ithaca requires a Certificate of Compliance, a safety inspection permit, for buildings with three or more units or units with six or more unrelated people. A multiple dwelling's certificate lasts three years ([City of Ithaca](https://www.cityofithacany.gov/146/Certificate-of-Compliance)). Cornell and the city's Building Division run an online safety tool built from those inspections. It shows sprinklers, alarms, construction type and property use, updates daily and covers only City of Ithaca rentals ([Cornell Off-Campus Living](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/campus-living/housing-search-process/safe-living-environments)).

The tool returned a record for 105 properties on this map. Of those, 61 showed a certificate whose expiration date had passed by Nov. 10, 2026, and 43 showed one still current. In Collegetown, 44 of 73 had lapsed dates (Cornell-City safety tool). The tool shows the record, not the building. A lapsed date means the record does not confirm a current inspection, which is why a call to the Building Division comes before a signature.

The cost of a missing permit is real. In 2016, the Fontana at 327 Eddy St. missed its Aug. 1 opening after five delays. The city had not issued its certificate of occupancy by late August. More than 50 students with leases were offered a hotel room near the Ithaca Mall or $30 a day ([The Cornell Daily Sun](https://www.cornellsun.com/article/2016/09/delayed-collegetown-apartment-opening-leaves-e2-80-88students-homeless)).

## The trade-offs

New buildings sell amenities. The Ivy offers a rooftop terrace, a sauna, study rooms and a 24-hour coffee bar. One junior offered its early discount stayed put instead. She found the unit smaller than her current one and could not yet see the inside of an unfinished building ([The Cornell Daily Sun](https://www.cornellsun.com/article/2025/12/the-ivy-to-establish-new-luxury-housing-in-collegetown-opens-fall-2026)). Charlie O'Connor told The Sun that "the only [thing] Collegetown can do is go up."

Older houses sell space and price. The cheapest beds near campus are still in the biggest shared units, as the Collegetown figure above shows.

Cornell itself is now a Collegetown landlord. It bought 301 College Ave. out of foreclosure for $15 million in March 2025 and opened it to 43 students in August 2026, with a goal of 105 residents by fall 2027. One resident described a dirty building, a missed bathroom cleaning, a refrigerator running at about 55 degrees, an unfinished lobby and construction starting at 7:30 a.m. six days a week ([The Cornell Daily Sun](https://www.cornellsun.com/article/2026/09/301-college-ave-opens-for-student-housing-resident-describes-it-as-incomplete-rushed)).

Distance cuts both ways. Downtown and Lansing buildings print their prices more often, and they cost time on a bus. Where to draw that line is the subject of our [neighborhood comparison](/cornell/housing/collegetown-vs-fall-creek-vs-downtown-ithaca-where-to-live/).

## What comes next

The map keeps growing at its center. On June 23, 2026, Ithaca's Planning and Development Board unanimously gave final approval to an eight-story building at 309 College Ave., the old Collegetown fire station site ([607 News Now](https://607newsnow.com/news/258852-plans-approved-for-8-story-building-in-ithaca/)). It will hold 77 apartments and 112 bedrooms over ground-floor shops, sits in the MU-2 district and needs no zoning variances ([The Ithaca Voice](https://ithacavoice.org/2026/04/city-planning-board-advances-eight-story-collegetown-housing-proposal/)). Robertson and O'Connor are the developers again. Modern Living already markets it as a home for Cornell students starting fall 2028 ([Modern Living Rentals](https://www.modernlivingrentals.com/309-college-ave)).

Three questions will shape the next version of this map. Will Cornell's own Collegetown building fill up and pull students out of private leases? Will taller Collegetown buildings add enough beds to slow the fall rush, or only add more units outside Good Cause limits? And will more landlords print their prices, as the big managed buildings do, or keep answering "Ask"?
