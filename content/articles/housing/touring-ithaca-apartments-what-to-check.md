```glance
- Ask to see the posted Certificate of Compliance; City rentals need one, valid three years for multiple dwellings.
- Heat season runs Sept. 15 to May 31; ask who pays heat and for last winter's bills.
- Check smoke detectors, two exits, hot water, windows, mold and pests while you are in the unit.
- Ask what parking, trash and snow removal cost, and which bus stop is nearest.
```

Before you sign, see the unit in person and check three things: the City's Certificate of Compliance, which proves the building passed inspection; the heat, because Ithaca requires it for much of the year; and the winter bills. Cornell advises students to view the property in person and read the whole lease before they pay anything ([Cornell Off-Campus Living](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/living-off-campus/housing-search-process/rental-listings-resources)).

## What should you do before the tour?

Look up the address first, and decide what you will ask.

- **Check the City record.** Buildings with three or more units need a Certificate of Compliance, valid three years ([City of Ithaca](https://www.cityofithacany.gov/146/Certificate-of-Compliance)). Cornell and the City's Building Division also run a safety tool that shows sprinklers, fire extinguishers, smoke and carbon monoxide alarms and construction type for City rentals. It does not cover Town, Lansing or Cayuga Heights addresses ([Safe Living Environments](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/living-off-campus/housing-search-process/safe-living-environments)). Call the Building Division at 607-274-6508 with questions.
- **Know which municipality you are in.** The City's inspection, renewal-notice and safety-tool rules stop at the city line. The [City vs Town guide](/cornell/housing/city-of-ithaca-vs-town-lansing-cayuga-heights-renters/) shows what changes.
- **Look up the landlord.** The [landlord reviews guide](/housing/how-to-check-an-ithaca-landlords-record/) shows how to check a name.

## What should you check at the door and in the hall?

Look for the posted certificate. The code puts it in the main entryway, or in a kitchen cabinet if the building has no central entrance. If you cannot find it, ask the landlord to show it, then confirm with the Building Division.

Check the safety basics Cornell lists: a smoke detector, as the law requires, and two means of egress ([Checklists and Considerations](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/living-off-campus/housing-search-process/checklists-and-considerations)). Test the detector button and look at where the exits lead.

## What should you check inside the unit?

Run through the unit room by room. New York's warranty of habitability, which is implied in every residential lease, covers heat, hot and cold water, electricity, plumbing, pests, mold and structural defects ([Cornell Law tenants' guide](https://sites.lawschool.cornell.edu/tenants-advocacy/building-issues-maintenance)). Air conditioning does not qualify, so ask whether the unit has any before you sign for a summer.

- **Heat.** Turn it on, even in October. In Ithaca the heat season runs from Sept. 15 to May 31. From 6 a.m. to 10 p.m. a landlord-heated unit must reach 68F when it is below 55F outside, and 62F overnight (Cornell Law tenants' guide). Ask the landlord to show where the thermostat or the controls are.
- **Water.** Run the hot tap and flush the toilet. Look under sinks and at the ceiling below bathrooms for stains.
- **Windows and walls.** Check that windows close and lock, and look for gaps at frames. Look at the corners of ceilings and closets for mold.
- **Pests.** Look along baseboards, under the sink and behind the bed frame. If a landlord has treated bed bugs nearby, the law requires written notice to the units above, below and beside within 72 hours.
- **Appliances.** Test the oven, the refrigerator and any landlord-provided washer and dryer.
- **Outlets and lights.** Plug a phone charger into each room.

Take photos with the date visible. Cornell tells tenants to send the landlord an inventory and photos after move-in, so the record of damage that was already there is on file. The landlord must also offer a move-in inspection before you occupy the unit.

## What should you ask about winter bills?

Ask who pays for heat and what a typical month costs. Ithaca has no posted insulation standard in the sources here, so last winter's bill is the best proxy. Cornell suggests calling NYSEG at (800) 572-1111 to ask for an address's average annual heating bill, especially for West Hill cottages ([Ithaca Neighborhoods](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/living-off-campus/housing-search-process/ithaca-neighborhoods)). If the landlord pays the utility, a shutoff for unpaid bills needs 30 days' notice from Nov. 1 to Apr. 15 ([Cornell Law tenants' guide](https://sites.lawschool.cornell.edu/tenants-advocacy/building-issues-maintenance)). For typical bills, read the [winter heating bill guide](/cornell/money/ithaca-utilities-cost-winter-heating-bill-apartment-students/).

## What should you check about parking, trash and snow?

- **Parking.** A City residential permit costs $45 a year and expires July 31. Landlords cannot get permits for their residents ([City of Ithaca](https://www.cityofithacany.gov/731/Permit-Issuance)). From Nov. 1 to Apr. 1, odd-even street parking applies from 7 p.m. to 6 a.m. when the City enforces it, and a car left 72 hours can be towed ([Parking Regulations](https://www.cityofithacany.gov/727/Parking-Regulations-Violations)). The Dryden Road Garage charges $232 a month for a 24/7 permit ([Dryden Road Garage](https://www.cityofithacany.gov/718/Dryden-Road-Garage)). Ask whether a space comes with the unit and what it costs.
- **Trash.** City trash tags cost $6.00 each, or $36.00 per sheet of 6 ([City of Ithaca](https://www.cityofithacany.gov/168/Trash-Yard-Waste-Collection-Tags)). One 209 Stewart Avenue listing charges $20 for county solid waste plus $80 a year for garbage, split among five tenants ([209 Stewart Ave.](https://offcampushousing.ithaca.edu/city/ithaca-college-ny/listing/209-stewart-avenue-235095)).
- **Snow.** City code puts sidewalk clearing on the owner, within 24 hours after a snowfall begins, but a lease can pass it to tenants ([City of Ithaca](https://www.cityofithacany.gov/136/Exterior-Property-Maintenance)). Ask who does it.

## How far is the bus stop, really?

Walk to the nearest TCAT stop from the unit, then ask which route serves it. Cornell lists Fall Creek at 0.5 to 1 mile with a 20 to 30 minute uphill walk and a frequent bus, and Lansing at 5 to 10 miles by bus or car ([Ithaca Neighborhoods](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/living-off-campus/housing-search-process/ithaca-neighborhoods)). A TCAT ride costs $1.50, and the OmniRide pass is $200 a year ([Cornell Transportation](https://fcs.cornell.edu/departments/transportation-delivery-services/sustainable-transportation-options/bus-services-privileges-passes)). Walk the route at the hour you would commute.

## What should you ask the landlord?

Cornell's lease-review list is a good script ([Signing a Lease](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/living-off-campus/housing-search-process/signing-lease)):

1. How long have you owned or managed this property, and how many properties do you manage?
2. How long did the last tenants stay, and where did they go?
3. Who makes repairs, and how do I reach you for general and emergency issues?
4. Which utilities are included, and what do the others cost?
5. When does the lease start and end, and what is the renewal notice?
6. Does the lease reserve the landlord's right to enter, and with how much notice?

Then ask for references from current tenants, and visit the neighbors. The lease clauses themselves are in the [lease-reading guide](/cornell/housing/how-to-read-ithaca-lease-before-signing/). If the landlord will not fix heat, mold or bed bugs later, see the [repairs guide](/cornell/housing/ithaca-landlord-wont-fix-heat-mold-bed-bugs/).

## What do you pay before you sign?

New York caps a deposit plus any rent advance at one month's rent ([General Obligations Law 7-108](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/GOB/7-108)). A landlord cannot charge a fee to process an application. A background or credit check costs the lesser of its actual cost or $20 ([Real Property Law 238-a](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/RPP/238-A)). ## What to do next

1. Tour at least two units, and use the same list at each.
2. Photograph every problem, and email the list to the landlord before you sign.
3. Confirm the certificate, the heat cost and the lease dates in writing.
