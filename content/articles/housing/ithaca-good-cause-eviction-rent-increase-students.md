```glance
- Ithaca's Common Council voted 8-2 in July 2024 to opt into New York's Good Cause Eviction law: covered tenants have a right to renew.
- A renewal increase above the lower of 10% or 5% plus inflation (CPI) is presumed unreasonable. It is not a hard cap.
- Private student apartments are covered. Only university-run dorms are exempt as a category.
- What does remove a unit: a certificate of occupancy from 2009 or later (exempt 30 years), a landlord who owns one unit, an owner-occupied building of 10 or fewer units.
- A non-renewal, or an increase of 5% or more, needs 30, 60 or 90 days' written notice, and the notice must say whether Good Cause covers the unit.
- City of Ithaca addresses only; Town of Ithaca, Lansing and Cayuga Heights are outside it.
```

If you rent a private apartment in the City of Ithaca, Good Cause covers you unless your building or landlord is exempt, student or not: Ithaca [opted in by an 8-2 vote in July 2024](https://www.wamc.org/2024-07-12/ithaca-is-the-latest-upstate-city-to-opt-into-good-cause-renter-protections). Your landlord can still raise the rent at renewal, but an increase above [the lower of 10% or 5% plus CPI is presumed unreasonable](https://hcr.ny.gov/good-cause-eviction), and refusing to renew needs a legal reason. The big exception for students is newer buildings: a [certificate of occupancy dated January 1, 2009 or later](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/RPP/214) takes a building out for 30 years.

```reader "If you're a junior or senior already renting"
Most Ithaca leases [end July 31](https://cornellsun.com/2024/07/10/landlords-and-advocates-grapple-with-good-cause-eviction-law-at-public-hearing). On a one-year lease ending then, notice of a 5%-plus raise is due by June 1 (60 days). Before you answer a renewal offer, find out whether your building is covered: ask the landlord for the certificate of occupancy year in writing, or run the checker below. If it is covered and the offer is above 10%, the increase is presumed unreasonable and you can push back. If you complained about heat or mold in the past year, a non-renewal or an unreasonable raise can be [defended as retaliation](https://sites.lawschool.cornell.edu/tenants-advocacy/lease-renewal). The costly mistake: signing a raise you could have contested because you assumed student housing is exempt. Call the free tenant hotline at [607-301-1560](https://sites.lawschool.cornell.edu/tenants-advocacy/tenants-rights/) first.
```

```reader "If you're a graduate or professional student"
You are likely to stay several years, which is exactly when Good Cause matters most. Renewals compound: two years of 10% raises on a $1,600 one-bedroom add $336 a month (our arithmetic). An older Fall Creek or downtown building is covered unless its landlord is exempt; a complex with a 2009-or-later certificate of occupancy is not. Ask before you sign the first lease, not at the first renewal. After two years in the unit, the notice owed before a 5%-plus raise or a non-renewal rises to [90 days](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/RPP/226-C). If your apartment is in Lansing or the Town of Ithaca, the city's opt-in does not reach you.
```

```reader "If you're a student with a spouse, partner or children"
A right to renew is worth most to a household that cannot move every August. If Good Cause covers your unit, a landlord can end the tenancy only on a ground the law lists, such as nonpayment, the owner moving in, demolition or [an uncured violation after 10 days' written notice](https://sites.lawschool.cornell.edu/tenants-advocacy/lease-renewal). Check coverage before you sign: a landlord who owns only one unit statewide, or who lives in a building of 10 or fewer units, is exempt. A renewal at a higher rent also lets the landlord [raise the deposit to the new monthly rent](https://ag.ny.gov/resources/individuals/tenants-homeowners/tenants), so budget for the difference.
```

```reader "If you're a parent paying the rent"
The common belief that student rentals are exempt is wrong for most private Collegetown and downtown units; only [university-run dormitories](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/RPP/214) are exempt as a category. That protects your student at renewal, not at the first signing: Good Cause does not limit the starting rent. What it changes is the second year. A covered landlord who wants more than the lower of 10% or 5% plus CPI has to justify it. A notice of non-renewal or a raise of 5% or more must arrive [30 to 90 days ahead](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/RPP/226-C); if it comes late, the old rent continues until the notice period runs.
```

```reader "If you're an alum who owns or may buy an Ithaca rental"
Ithaca's small-landlord exemption is [one unit anywhere in New York State](https://hcr.ny.gov/good-cause-eviction-law-notice-february-2025), against a state default of 10. Own a second unit and you are covered unless another exemption applies: you live in the building and it has 10 or fewer units, or its certificate of occupancy is from 2009 on. To claim the small-landlord exemption you must [disclose the owners and their unit counts](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/RPP/214). Every non-renewal or 5%-plus renewal notice must state whether the unit is covered and, if not, why. At the 2024 hearing, landlords and the Board of Realtors predicted [$2,000 to $5,000 in legal costs](https://www.wamc.org/2024-07-12/ithaca-is-the-latest-upstate-city-to-opt-into-good-cause-renter-protections) per non-renewal; that is their claim.
```

## What Good Cause is, and when Ithaca joined

New York's Good Cause Eviction law took effect on [April 20, 2024](https://sites.lawschool.cornell.edu/tenants-advocacy/lease-renewal). Outside New York City it applies only where a city, town or village opts in. It sits in [Real Property Law Article 6-A](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/RPP/A6-A), sections 210 to 218, and is scheduled to be repealed on June 15, 2034.

Ithaca's Common Council voted 8-2 to opt in, in a WAMC report dated [July 12, 2024](https://www.wamc.org/2024-07-12/ithaca-is-the-latest-upstate-city-to-opt-into-good-cause-renter-protections). Ithaca was the fourth upstate city to do so, after Albany, Kingston and Poughkeepsie. Mayor Cantelmo and Ithaca Tenants Union co-chair Katie Sims backed it.

The opt-in is the City of Ithaca's. It covers Collegetown, Fall Creek, downtown and the rest of the city. An apartment in the Town of Ithaca, Lansing or Cayuga Heights is outside it.

## The two protections

**A right to renew.** A covered tenant is entitled to a renewal unless the landlord has a ground the law allows. The [Cornell Law tenant guide](https://sites.lawschool.cornell.edu/tenants-advocacy/lease-renewal) lists them: the landlord or a family member will live there, the building will be demolished, the unit is leaving the rental market, or a proven legal ground such as nonpayment, or a lease violation not fixed within 10 days of written notice.

**A test for rent increases.** An annual increase above the lower of 10% or 5% plus CPI is [presumed unreasonable](https://hcr.ny.gov/good-cause-eviction). DHCR, the state housing agency, usually publishes the CPI figure [by August 1](https://sites.lawschool.cornell.edu/tenants-advocacy/rent-increases). The landlord can rebut the presumption, for example with higher costs, so this is a test in court, not a ceiling the law enforces on its own.

The two connect in [section 216](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/RPP/216). A tenant who refuses reasonable renewal terms, including an increase that is not unreasonable, can be removed if the landlord gave written notice 30 to 90 days before the lease ends. But failing to pay an unreasonable increase is not good cause for eviction.

Not confirmed: the CPI figure behind Ithaca's 2026 line (DHCR's Good Cause page lists an August 2026 notice). Until you see it, treat 10% as the outer line.

## What the rent test means in dollars

The rent test caps nothing at signing. It applies at each renewal, to the increase over your current rent. Here is the arithmetic on Ithaca Renting's [2027-28 Collegetown per-bed medians](https://ithacarenting.com/collegetown/), $800 in a six-bedroom and $2,025 for a one-bedroom alone.

| Current rent (per bed, 2027-28 median) | 5%: raise that needs 30-90 days' notice | 10%: the outer line | Highest rent before the presumption, at 10% |
|---|---|---|---|
| $800 (6BR) | $40 | $80 | $880 |
| $1,600 (example) | $80 | $160 | $1,760 |
| $2,025 (1BR) | $101.25 | $202.50 | $2,227.50 |

Our arithmetic. The real line for any year is 5% plus that year's CPI, if that comes to less than 10%.

Example: a senior pays $2,025 a month for a Collegetown one-bedroom in an older City of Ithaca building owned by a company with many units. The renewal offer is $2,300. That is a $275 raise, 13.6%, above the 10% line, so it is presumed unreasonable. Because it is also over 5%, the landlord owes written notice 30, 60 or 90 days ahead, depending on tenure. The senior can counter at or below $2,227.50, ask the landlord to justify the rest, and call the tenant hotline before refusing to pay.

## Who is exempt, and who is not

The myth is that student housing is exempt. A June 2024 WSKG summary said [student housing would not be covered](https://www.wskg.org/regional-news/2024-06-25/landlords-tenants-react-to-ithacas-good-cause-eviction-proposal). The statute says otherwise: it exempts [dormitories owned and operated by colleges](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/RPP/214), which WSKG's [July 2024 report](https://www.wskg.org/regional-news/2024-07-08/new-good-cause-eviction-renter-protections-on-the-table-in-ithaca) called dorm rooms. That means Cornell's halls, not a private Collegetown apartment. Section 214 has no exemption for students or fixed-term student leases.

| Exemption (RPL 214 and Ithaca's thresholds) | Does it remove student rentals? |
|---|---|
| Certificate of occupancy on or after Jan 1, 2009 (exempt 30 years) | Yes, often: newer Collegetown buildings. The Ithacan, built 2023, was [cited as exempt until 2053](https://cornellsun.com/2024/07/10/landlords-and-advocates-grapple-with-good-cause-eviction-law-at-public-hearing) |
| Small landlord: owns no more than one unit in New York State (Ithaca's figure) | Yes: a single rented condo or house from a one-property owner |
| Owner-occupied building of 10 or fewer units | Yes: a room or flat in a house where the owner lives |
| Rent above 345% of HUD fair market rent | Almost never: $4,402 a month for a 1BR on the 2023 rate |
| College-run dormitories | Yes, but only Cornell's own housing |
| Condos and co-ops, income-restricted or rent-regulated units, seasonal-use units, hotel rooms | Rarely relevant |
| Being a student, or a fixed-term student lease | No: not an exemption |

Two cautions on Ithaca's thresholds. The 345% and one-unit figures come from [DHCR's February 2025 list](https://hcr.ny.gov/good-cause-eviction-law-notice-february-2025); later lists may differ, so confirm them with the hotline. The $4,402 figure is the Sun's: [345% of the 2023 one-bedroom fair market rent of $1,276](https://cornellsun.com/2024/07/10/landlords-and-advocates-grapple-with-good-cause-eviction-law-at-public-hearing). The Collegetown one-bedroom median of $2,025 is less than half of it.

## How to find out if your unit is covered

```figure

  - label: Is it a Cornell residence hall? If so, no
  - label: Was the certificate of occupancy issued on or after Jan 1, 2009? If so, no until 30 years pass
  - label: Does the landlord own only one unit in New York, or live in a building of 10 or fewer units? If so, no
  - label: Otherwise, assume yes, and check the landlord's 226-c notice
```

1. **Ask in writing.** Ask the landlord or manager for the building's certificate of occupancy year and whether they claim any exemption. A landlord claiming the small-landlord exemption must disclose owners and unit counts.
2. **Read the notice.** Any notice of non-renewal, or of a renewal at 5% or more, must say [whether the unit is subject to Good Cause, and why it is exempt if not](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/RPP/226-C).
3. **Call the city.** The Building Division answers questions about a building's certificates at [607-274-6508](https://www.cityofithacany.gov/146/Certificate-of-Compliance).

Not confirmed: a public online lookup of certificate of occupancy dates for Ithaca buildings.

## The notice your landlord owes

Good Cause sits on top of a state rule that covers every unregulated tenant. Under [RPL 226-c](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/RPP/226-C), a landlord who will not renew, or who offers renewal with a raise of 5% or more, must give written notice. The period depends on the longer of your time in the unit or your lease term.

| Time in the unit, or lease length | Written notice required |
|---|---|
| Under 1 year, and no lease of 1 year | 30 days |
| 1 to 2 years, or a lease of 1 to 2 years | 60 days |
| More than 2 years, or a lease of 2 years or more | 90 days |

Late notice does not cancel the raise. It keeps you on your existing rent and terms until the notice period runs out.

Example: a junior on a 12-month lease ending July 31 gets an email on July 10 offering renewal at 8% more. The lease is one year, so 60 days' notice was owed. The old rent continues until 60 days after the notice, about September 8.

In the City, a second clock runs. Under [Code section 258-10](https://ecode360.com/27390826), the landlord must give current tenants 120 days' written notice before renewing your lease, showing the unit or signing new tenants, unless you initialed a bolded waiver on the lease's first page. The rule's sponsor, Alderperson Patrick Mehler, wrote in a 2022 Sun column that tenants should first ask [why they are being asked to waive the days](https://cornellsun.com/2022/04/18/mehler-student-tenants-rights-shifting-in-your-favor/). The full rule is on the [120-day renewal page](/housing/ithaca-120-day-lease-renewal-notice-rule/).

## Retaliation and repairs

A landlord may not refuse to renew, or raise the rent unreasonably, because you made a good-faith complaint in the past year. An eviction within a year of a complaint can be [defended as retaliation](https://sites.lawschool.cornell.edu/tenants-advocacy/lease-renewal). The exception is an owner-occupied building of fewer than four units, per the [Cornell Law repairs page](https://sites.lawschool.cornell.edu/tenants-advocacy/building-issues-maintenance).

This matters in Ithaca: students have reported avoiding repair requests for fear of not being renewed, per a [2021 Sun report on the Tenants Union](https://cornellsun.com/2021/07/23/ithaca-tenants-union-and-solidarity-slate-join-regional-efforts-toward-housing-equity/). Put every repair request in writing and keep it. The written record is what proves the timing.

## If it goes to court

A landlord who does not renew and wants you out must bring a holdover case. Where Good Cause applies, the landlord must first give [10 days' written notice to cure](https://sites.lawschool.cornell.edu/tenants-advocacy/eviction-proceedings) a substantial violation. The court must grant a 30-day stay of the warrant in a lease-breach holdover. Free help: the Tenants Advocacy hotline, [607-301-1560](https://sites.lawschool.cornell.edu/tenants-advocacy/tenants-rights/), and LawNY at (607) 273-3667. The eviction steps are on the planned eviction page.

A state bill, [S8612A](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/bills/2025/S8612), would have had DHCR publish the inflation figure by June 1. It was sent back to committee and is not law.

## Common mistakes

- **Believing student housing is exempt.** Only college dorms are. A tenant who accepts an unreasonable 15% raise on a $1,600 rent pays $240 a month more, $2,880 over a 12-month lease, that could have been contested (our arithmetic).
- **Reading 10% as a hard cap.** It is a presumption the landlord can rebut, and the line may sit below 10% when CPI is under 5%.
- **Assuming a new building is covered.** A certificate of occupancy from 2009 on exempts the building for 30 years, so a 2023 building is out until 2053.
- **Paying a late-noticed raise from day one.** If a 5%-plus raise came with less than the 30, 60 or 90 days owed, the old rent runs until the period ends.
- **Skipping written repair requests.** Without them, a retaliation defense to a non-renewal is hard to prove.
- **Withholding an increase you think is unreasonable without advice.** Nonpayment of an unreasonable increase is not good cause, but the case still goes to court. Call 607-301-1560 first.

## FAQ

**Does Good Cause limit the rent on a new lease?** No. It tests increases at renewal for a tenant already in the unit. A new tenant's starting rent is set by the market.

**Can my landlord refuse to renew so they can rent to a bigger group?** Not in a covered unit, unless a ground in the law applies, such as nonpayment or the owner moving in, per the [Cornell Law guide](https://sites.lawschool.cornell.edu/tenants-advocacy/lease-renewal).

**Do I have to renew if my landlord offers?** No. You need [no notice to leave at the end of a fixed-term lease](https://sites.lawschool.cornell.edu/tenants-advocacy/lease-renewal); an expired lease you stay on becomes month-to-month.

**Can my landlord raise the deposit when the rent goes up?** Yes. On a renewal at a higher rent, the landlord may collect more to [bring the deposit up to the new monthly rent](https://ag.ny.gov/resources/individuals/tenants-homeowners/tenants), never above one month.

**When does Ithaca's Good Cause law end?** The state law is scheduled for repeal on [June 15, 2034](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/RPP/A6-A) unless the legislature extends it.
