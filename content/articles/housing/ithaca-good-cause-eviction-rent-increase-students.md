```glance
- Ithaca opted into Good Cause in July 2024, and the law covers private student apartments in the City.
- A renewal increase above the lower of 10% or 5% plus CPI is presumed unreasonable.
- Buildings with a certificate of occupancy dated Jan. 1, 2009 or later are exempt for 30 years.
- The law stops at the city line: Lansing, the Town of Ithaca and Cayuga Heights are outside it.
```

If you rent a private apartment in the City of Ithaca, Good Cause Eviction covers you unless your building or landlord is exempt, and being a student is not an exemption. Your landlord can still raise the rent at renewal, but refusing to renew needs a legal reason ([WAMC](https://www.wamc.org/2024-07-12/ithaca-is-the-latest-upstate-city-to-opt-into-good-cause-renter-protections)).

## What is Good Cause Eviction, and when did Ithaca join?

New York's Good Cause law took effect on April 20, 2024. Outside New York City it applies only where a city, town or village opts in, and it is scheduled to expire on June 15, 2034 ([Cornell Law](https://sites.lawschool.cornell.edu/tenants-advocacy/lease-renewal)). Ithaca's Common Council voted 8-2 to opt in, and WAMC reported it on July 12, 2024. Ithaca was the fourth upstate city to do so, after Albany, Kingston and Poughkeepsie.

The opt-in belongs to the City of Ithaca. It covers Collegetown, Fall Creek and downtown. An apartment in the Town of Ithaca, Lansing or Cayuga Heights is outside it, and the [City versus town guide](/cornell/housing/city-of-ithaca-vs-town-lansing-cayuga-heights-renters/) shows which side of the line you are on.

## What are the two protections?

**A right to renew.** A covered tenant is entitled to a renewal unless the landlord has a ground the law allows: the landlord or a family member will live there, the building will be demolished, the unit is leaving the rental market, or a proven legal ground such as nonpayment or a lease violation not fixed after 10 days' written notice ([Cornell Law](https://sites.lawschool.cornell.edu/tenants-advocacy/lease-renewal)).

**A test for rent increases.** An annual increase above the lower of 10% or 5% plus the year's inflation figure (CPI) is presumed unreasonable ([NY Homes and Community Renewal](https://hcr.ny.gov/good-cause-eviction)). The landlord can rebut the presumption in court, so this is a test and not a hard cap. The state housing agency publishes the CPI figure, usually by Aug. 1. The agency's August 2026 notice could not be read as text, so the CPI figure behind the 2026 line is unconfirmed. Treat the 10% line as the outer limit and look up the notice on the agency's page.

The two connect in [section 216](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/RPP/216). A tenant who refuses reasonable renewal terms can be removed if the landlord gave written notice 30 to 90 days before the lease ends. Failing to pay an unreasonable increase is not a good cause for eviction.

## What does the rent test mean in dollars?

The test applies at each renewal, to the increase over your current rent. It does not limit your starting rent. The examples use Ithaca Renting's 2027-28 Collegetown per-bed medians ([Ithaca Renting](https://ithacarenting.com/collegetown/)).

| Current rent per bed | 5% increase | 10% increase | Rent at the 10% line |
|---|---|---|---|
| $800 (6BR median) | $40.00 | $80.00 | $880.00 |
| $1,600 (example) | $80.00 | $160.00 | $1,760.00 |
| $2,025 (1BR median) | $101.25 | $202.50 | $2,227.50 |

Source: Ithaca Renting medians; the increases are percentages of each rent. The real line is 5% plus that year's CPI whenever that total is under 10%.

Say a senior pays $2,025 for a one-bedroom in an older City building owned by a large company. The renewal offer is $2,300. That is $275 more, or 13.6%, which is above the line and presumed unreasonable. The senior can counter at or below $2,227.50, ask the landlord to justify the rest, and call the tenant hotline at 607-301-1560 before refusing to pay (Cornell Law Tenants Advocacy).

## Who is exempt, and who is not?

A common belief is that student housing is exempt. The statute exempts only dormitories owned and operated by colleges, which means Cornell's residence halls, not a private Collegetown apartment. It lists no exemption for students or fixed-term student leases ([RPL 214](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/RPP/214)).

| Exemption | Removes student rentals? |
|---|---|
| Certificate of occupancy dated Jan. 1, 2009 or later, exempt for 30 years | Yes, for newer Collegetown buildings; The Ithacan, built in 2023, was cited as exempt until 2053 |
| Landlord owns no more than one unit anywhere in New York | Yes; the state default is 10 units, but Ithaca set one |
| Owner lives in the building and it has 10 or fewer units | Yes |
| Rent above 345% of HUD fair market rent | Yes; the state default is 245% |
| Condos, co-ops and rent-regulated or income-restricted units | Yes |
| Cornell-owned residence halls | Yes |

Source: [RPL 214](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/RPP/214), the [February 2025 state notice](https://hcr.ny.gov/good-cause-eviction-law-notice-february-2025) and [The Cornell Daily Sun](https://cornellsun.com/2024/07/10/landlords-and-advocates-grapple-with-good-cause-eviction-law-at-public-hearing).

To claim the small-landlord exemption, a landlord must disclose its owners and their unit counts ([RPL 214](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/RPP/214)). A landlord of newer Collegetown buildings falls under the 2009 rule, so ask for the certificate of occupancy year in writing.

## How much notice must a landlord give?

A separate state rule, RPL 226-c, requires written notice when a landlord will not renew or offers a renewal with a rent increase of 5% or more. The notice period depends on how long you have lived there: 30 days for under one year, 60 days for one to two years, and 90 days for more than two years ([RPL 226-c](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/RPP/226-C)). A late notice keeps you on your existing terms until the period ends. The notice must also say whether Good Cause covers the unit and, if not, why. The City adds a 120-day rule for re-renting, explained in the [120-day notice guide](/cornell/housing/ithaca-120-day-lease-renewal-notice-rule/).

Most Ithaca leases end July 31, tied to the student calendar, so a 60-day notice is due by June 1 ([The Cornell Daily Sun](https://cornellsun.com/2024/07/10/landlords-and-advocates-grapple-with-good-cause-eviction-law-at-public-hearing)).

## Can a landlord punish you for a complaint?

No refusal to renew and no unreasonable increase is allowed in response to a good-faith complaint made within the past year, and eviction within a year of a complaint can be defended as retaliation. The protection does not apply in owner-occupied buildings with fewer than four units ([Cornell Law](https://sites.lawschool.cornell.edu/tenants-advocacy/lease-renewal)).

```reader "If you're a junior or senior already renting"
Before you answer a renewal offer, find out whether your building is covered. Ask for the certificate of occupancy year in writing, or run the checker above. If you are covered and the offer crosses the line, push back. The costly mistake is signing an increase you could have contested because you assumed student housing is exempt. The [junior and senior renting guide](/cornell/housing/off-campus-housing-cornell-juniors-seniors-renting/) covers the rest of the renewal year.
```

```reader "If you're a graduate or professional student"
You stay several years, which is when renewals compound. Two years of 10% raises on a $1,600 one-bedroom add $336 a month. Ask about coverage before you sign the first lease, not at the first renewal.
```

```reader "If you're a student with a spouse, partner or children"
A right to renew matters most to a household that cannot move every August. A renewal at a higher rent also lets the landlord raise the deposit to the new monthly rent ([New York Attorney General](https://ag.ny.gov/resources/individuals/tenants-homeowners/tenants)), so budget for the difference.
```

```reader "If you're a parent paying the rent"
Good Cause protects your student at renewal, not at the first signing. It does not limit the starting rent. What it changes is the second year, when a covered landlord who wants more must justify it.
```

```reader "If you're an alum who owns or may buy an Ithaca rental"
Own a second unit statewide and the small-landlord exemption is gone, unless another exemption applies. Every non-renewal notice must state whether the unit is covered. At the 2024 hearing, landlords and the Board of Realtors predicted $2,000 to $5,000 in legal costs per non-renewal, which is their claim ([WAMC](https://www.wamc.org/2024-07-12/ithaca-is-the-latest-upstate-city-to-opt-into-good-cause-renter-protections)).
```

## What if you get an eviction notice?

Good Cause does not stop an eviction for nonpayment or a lease violation the tenant did not fix. If you receive a notice or a court paper, read [what to do after an eviction or vacate order](/cornell/housing/ithaca-eviction-vacate-order-what-to-do/) the same day. Read your lease terms with the [lease reading guide](/cornell/housing/how-to-read-ithaca-lease-before-signing/).

## What to do next

1. Find your building's certificate of occupancy year.
2. Mark your renewal notice date on a calendar.
3. Call 607-301-1560 before you answer an offer above the line.
