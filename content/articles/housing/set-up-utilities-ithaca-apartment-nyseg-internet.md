Read your lease first to see which utilities your rent covers, then start the rest with NYSEG, the electric and gas company, through its "Turn On Service" page at nyseg.com ([NYSEG](https://www.nyseg.com/support/moving-help)). Internet is a separate order with a provider you choose. Do both before your lease starts, since Cornell's checklist says to arrange utilities and phone before move-in.

```glance
- Start NYSEG service at nyseg.com or call 800.572.1111, Monday to Friday, 7 a.m. to 7 p.m.
- Check the lease first: some Collegetown and downtown rents already include electric and Wi-Fi.
- Schedule disconnection one week before move-out for every utility you pay.
- Heat shutoffs need 30 days' notice from Nov. 1 to Apr. 15 when your landlord pays.
```

## Which utilities do you have to set up yourself?

Only the ones your lease does not include. Listings vary a lot, so match each one to its own wording:

| Place | What the rent includes |
|---|---|
| 209 Stewart Ave, Collegetown | Heat, hot and cold water, electric and WiFi in $850 per room per month |
| Whiton Hall, 222 S. Aurora St., downtown | Electricity, Wi-Fi, heat, water and trash |
| Colonial Building and Commons Studios, downtown | Electricity, heat, water and trash |
| Dryden South, 205 Dryden Rd. | Heat and hot water; electricity and Wi-Fi not stated |

Source: [209 Stewart Ave listing](https://offcampushousing.ithaca.edu/city/ithaca-college-ny/listing/209-stewart-avenue-235095), [Ithaca Renting downtown page](https://ithacarenting.com/downtown/) and [Dryden South](https://drydensouth.com/common-questions/).

Cornell's checklist tells you to include utilities when calculating what rent you can afford, and lists pets, parking and furniture among the features to check ([Cornell Off-Campus Living](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/living-off-campus/housing-search-process/checklists-and-considerations)). The [lease guide](/cornell/housing/how-to-read-ithaca-lease-before-signing/) covers what else to read before you sign.

## How do you start NYSEG service?

Use whichever route is easier. NYSEG's moving help page lists a "Turn On Service" option at nyseg.com, a Request for Service form at nyseg.com/Moving and the email custserv@nyseg.com ([NYSEG](https://www.nyseg.com/support/moving-help)). The customer line is 800.572.1111, Monday to Friday, 7 a.m. to 7 p.m., except holidays, and an automated line, 800.600.2275, runs 24 hours.

1. Get your move-in date and the unit's exact address from your lease.
2. Request service online, by form or by phone for that date.
3. If the meter is indoors, arrange access with your landlord, because NYSEG says indoor meters need access.
4. Expect a safety inspection if service at the address has been inactive for more than two years.

NYSEG's page is a secondary source here, so confirm the steps when you call. Ask about heating history too. Cornell suggests asking NYSEG for average annual heating bills before you commit, especially for West Hill cottages ([Cornell Off-Campus Living](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/living-off-campus/housing-search-process/ithaca-neighborhoods)).

Rates are in motion. The state Public Service Commission approved a temporary NYSEG electric increase of 3.7% of revenues starting June 1, 2026, and a permanent decision was still pending in November 2026 ([FingerLakes1](https://www.fingerlakes1.com/2026/05/15/nyseg-and-rge-win-temporary-rate-hikes-as-larger-increases-still-loom/)). What a winter bill looks like is in the [utilities cost guide](/cornell/money/ithaca-utilities-cost-winter-heating-bill-apartment-students/).

If your landlord pays for heat, a shutoff needs at least 30 days' notice from Nov. 1 to Apr. 15 ([Cornell Law Tenants Advocacy Program](https://sites.lawschool.cornell.edu/tenants-advocacy/building-issues-maintenance)).

## How do you set up internet?

Order internet yourself unless the lease includes it. No official source names Ithaca's providers. An internet-provider directory lists Spectrum as cheapest at about $30 a month for 100 Mbps, one East Ithaca listing at $49.99 for 1,000 Mbps, and a range of $30 to $120 ([ISP Reports](https://ispreports.org/internet-service-providers-ithaca-ny/)). Those figures mix promotions and regular prices. Ask each provider for the price after the first year.

Splitting a bill among roommates works best if one person owns the account. Agree on who that is before you order. The provider comparison and dorm rules are in the [internet guide](/daily-life/internet-providers-ithaca-compared-cornell-students/).

## How do you stop service when you leave?

One week before move-out, schedule disconnection of electric, gas, phone, internet, cable and garbage for any you pay ([Cornell Off-Campus Living](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/living-off-campus/housing-search-process/moving-and-out)). Unpaid utilities owed to the landlord can come out of your deposit ([Cornell Law Tenants Advocacy Program](https://sites.lawschool.cornell.edu/tenants-advocacy/security-deposits)). See the [deposit guide](/cornell/housing/moving-out-ithaca-apartment-deposit-back/).

```reader "If you're a low-budget renter"
Compare the all-in rent, not the base rent. An $850 room with heat, electric and Wi-Fi can cost less than a cheaper room with three bills. The [monthly cost guide](/cornell/housing/what-a-month-off-campus-costs-cornell/) lays out the totals.
```

## What to do next

Copy the utility lines from your lease, request NYSEG service for your start date and book internet two weeks ahead.
