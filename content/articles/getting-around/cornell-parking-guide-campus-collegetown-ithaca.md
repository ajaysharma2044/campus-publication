```glance
- Cornell sells campus spaces by permit or by ParkMobile; the city runs the streets and garages beyond.
- A resident permit costs $752.98 a year, a commuter permit $359.77, and both sell first come.
- City garages charge $2.25 an hour in 2026, capped at $12 a day downtown and $18 in Collegetown.
- Never leave a car on a city street over break: 72 hours without moving counts as abandoned.
```

Deana Druz had paid several hundred dollars for a permit to park in the Williams Street lot, at the corner of Williams Street and Stewart Avenue. One morning in September 2023 she got there early and found every space taken. She scrambled to a ParkMobile lot near her class, paid extra and arrived late. She called parking at Cornell a "never-ending treasure hunt" ([Cornell Daily Sun](https://www.cornellsun.com/article/2023/09/williams-street-parking-lot-struggles-to-accommodate-students)).

Students told the Sun those permits cost around $700 and sold out within hours that August (Cornell Daily Sun). Druz had a permit and still had nowhere to park.

That is parking at Cornell in miniature. Two different owners run it, and they meet at the edge of campus. Cornell Transportation and Delivery Services, the university office that sells permits, controls the lots on the hill. The City of Ithaca controls the streets, meters and garages below and around it. A student who lives off campus and drives to class pays into both systems. Each one has its own prices, its own winter rules and its own fines.

The stakes are real money. A permit for a car parked near your dorm is $752.98 a year, and a permit for a student who drives in from off campus is $359.77 ([Cornell parking permits](https://fcs.cornell.edu/departments/transportation-delivery-services/parking-campus/parking-students)). Parking without either one costs $50 a ticket on campus ([Cornell, 2023](https://fcs.cornell.edu/announcements/2023-08-01/citation-fines-increasing-september-1)). Off campus, the city raised its garage and street rates on Jan. 5, 2026 ([City of Ithaca](https://www.cityofithacany.gov/CivicAlerts.aspx?AID=1328)).

## Why Cornell makes a car expensive

Cornell's high permit prices are a choice, and Cornell has said so publicly. In 2006 an annual on-campus permit cost $641.10, and David Lieb of Cornell Transportation told The Cornell Daily Sun that permits were priced high to discourage students from bringing cars. He pointed to a walkable, bikeable campus with good transit. Cornell then had about 10,000 parking spaces, and it guarded them for commuting students and faculty ([Cornell Daily Sun, 2006](https://cornellsun.com/2006/11/03/parking-rate-rage-top-spots-break-2k)).

The thinking goes back further. Cornell built a program to move employees out of cars and onto buses and carpools, and its OmniRide and RideShare programs were nearly 20 years old by 2009. The university said they had cut its employee parking needs by about 2,200 spaces, which saved it from building and maintaining them ([Cornell Chronicle, 2009](https://news.cornell.edu/node/273202)).

The price itself has risen slowly. Since 2006 the price of an annual on-campus permit has risen $111.88 from $641.10, or 17.5 percent, in 20 years. What changed more is how Cornell sells the rest of its space. In August 2020 it started Daily Decision, a pilot that let commuters pay by the day through the ParkMobile phone app ([Cornell, 2020](https://fcs.cornell.edu/announcements/2020-10-28/expanded-daily-decision-parking-options)). In 2022 it stopped selling the old SC student commuter permit and moved Crescent Lot to pay-by-the-day parking ([Cornell 2022 parking changes](https://fcs.cornell.edu/departments/transportation-delivery-services/parking/2022-campus-parking-changes)). Since July 1, 2025, a permit holder who charges an electric car pays $3.50 an hour to charge ([Cornell Transportation announcements](https://fcs.cornell.edu/departments/transportation-delivery-services/tds-announcements)).

Enforcement got tougher too. On Sept. 1, 2023, Cornell raised its fines for the first time since 2014. Parking without a permit or payment went from $35 to $50, and Cornell said that violation made up 85 percent of all its tickets. Bridgette Brady, senior director for Transportation and Delivery Services, said the goal was to get drivers to do the right thing and use the permit and pay-by-phone options (Cornell, 2023). Not every driver listens. A senior wrote in a 2023 Sun opinion column that some students collected 10 or 11 tickets in a single semester ([Cornell Daily Sun column, 2023](https://www.cornellsun.com/article/2023/03/kempff-the-unspoken-war-for-campus-parking)).

## Before you bring a car

Start with your plate, not your permit. Cornell requires every student to register a car, motorcycle or scooter with Transportation Services, even to use short-term parking ([Cornell Transportation](https://fcs.cornell.edu/departments/transportation-delivery-services/parking-campus)). You register, buy permits and pay tickets at MyParking.Cornell.edu with your NetID, Cornell's login.

Then ask whether you need the car at all. The quiz below weighs it against buses and shared cars, and the [full-cost calculator](/tools/should-i-bring-a-car-cornell-ithaca-cost/) prices a year of owning one.

## August: the permit sale and move-in

Permits go on sale once a year, first come, first served. Sales run at myparking.cornell.edu, and annual permits are valid through Aug. 15 ([Cornell parking permits](https://fcs.cornell.edu/departments/transportation-delivery-services/parking-campus/parking-students)). The 2026-27 sale opened on Tuesday, Aug. 4, 2026, at 9 a.m., for permits valid from Aug. 16, 2026 ([Cornell permit sale notice](https://fcs.cornell.edu/node/17809)).

That end date grew out of a 2023 crunch. That year, old permits ran to Aug. 31 while new ones went on sale in early August. For a few weeks more permits were out than there were spaces. Brady told the Sun the crowding at Williams Street came from holders of old permits still parking there. She said a move to virtual permits, which use your license plate instead of a hangtag, would end the overlap (Cornell Daily Sun). Annual permits now end Aug. 15, the day before the new ones start (Cornell parking permits).

A permit bought later costs less: resident permits prorate daily from Sept. 1, and commuter permits from Aug. 16. For the step-by-step purchase and eligibility rules, see the [student parking permit guide](/cornell/getting-around/cornell-student-parking-permit-cost-rules/).

Move-in is its own small parking system. On North Campus, the move-in timeslot you pick is for arrival and check-in only, not unloading. You print a Move-In Ticket from the Housing and Dining Portal after Aug. 1, and it names where you park. After you unload, you get 3 hours of short-term parking in that area ([North Campus move-in](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/housing-details/moving-campus/move-instructions/move-instructions-north-campus)). Each area has its own unloading zones, all covered in the [move-in day parking guide](/cornell/getting-around/cornell-move-in-day-parking-unloading/).

```reader "If you're a transfer or arriving in January"
A permit bought after the August sale, while supply lasts, is prorated daily, from Sept. 1 for resident permits and from Aug. 16 for commuter permits ([Cornell parking permits](https://fcs.cornell.edu/departments/transportation-delivery-services/parking-campus/parking-students)). A January arrival lands inside both winter seasons: Cornell's overnight rule, Dec. 1 to April 1, and the city's odd/even street rule, Nov. 1 to April 1. Register your plate before you drive up, buy the permit for your housing area, and park overnight only in your own lot or a listed winter bay. A permit bought in January can still be returned by March 31 for a prorated refund. Overnight parking outside the bays when snow is forecast costs a $50 winter-rules fine ([Cornell Daily Sun, 2023](https://cornellsun.com/2023/09/04/on-campus-parking-violation-fines-increase-for-first-time-since-2014/)).
```

## Living on campus with a permit

A resident permit is narrow. It works in the lot signed for your area and, after 2:30 p.m., in A Lot on North Campus. The ND permit works in A Lot at all hours. The FH permit for Hasbrouck Apartments also carries OmniRide bus privileges. If you move out of university housing, Cornell recalls the permit (Cornell parking permits).

Everywhere else on campus, you pay by phone. ParkMobile lets you pay through its app, by texting PARK to 77223 or by calling the number on the sign. You can buy up to 8 hours or a full day, and most lots allow several days at once. In most lots a parking day ends at 7:30 a.m., but B Lot runs on a 24-hour day (Cornell short-term parking).

The hours matter. Most commuter lots require a permit or payment from 7:30 a.m. to 5 p.m. on weekdays, all year, including breaks and holidays. The resident lots signed FH, ND, SW and WD need a permit or payment at all hours. Some lots stay permit-only on evenings and weekends, so read the sign at the entrance (Cornell short-term parking).

Holidays are the one break in enforcement. When the university closes for an official staff holiday, permit and payment rules pause until the next business day. Fire lanes, accessible spaces, reserved spaces and no-parking zones are enforced every day ([Cornell winter rules](https://fcs.cornell.edu/departments/transportation-delivery-services/parking-campus/winter-break-parking-rules)).

For a car kept at your dorm, the permit is the cheaper choice. Resident lots need a permit or a ParkMobile payment at all hours, so a dorm resident without a permit pays every night (Cornell short-term parking). Spread over 365 days, the resident permit costs $2.06 a day, less than the $3.45 a day an edge lot costs with the fee (Cornell parking map, August 2026).

## Off campus: renting with a car

Students who rent off campus cannot buy resident permits. Their campus option is the B commuter permit, which works in B Lot by the Vet College at all times and in A Lot after 2:30 p.m. (Cornell parking permits). The B lots include TRB, South and North Morrison and BTI. In 2022 one off-campus student told the Sun that street parking at her house was always full, so she parked in a lot about 15 minutes away ([Cornell Daily Sun, 2022](https://cornellsun.com/2022/11/21/dont-let-it-snow-students-express-concerns-over-driving-conditions-as-temperatures-fall/)).

At home, the city's rules take over. Ithaca runs a residential parking permit system on some blocks. The permit costs $45 a year, runs from the day you buy it to July 31, and has no partial-year price. A visitor pass is $10. You apply yourself with your vehicle registration and proof that you live there, such as a lease. Your landlord cannot get the permit for you ([City permit page](https://www.cityofithacany.gov/731/Permit-Issuance)). The permit is tied to one license plate. It goes on the driver's side back window, never the windshield.

The permit is voluntary. Residents of a permit block are not required to buy one, but they still have to follow city parking rules (City permit page). The city posts a permit system map where you can search your address ([City residential permits](https://www.cityofithacany.gov/711/Residential-Parking-Permits)). Mailed applications go to the City Clerk's Office at 108 E. Green St.

That permit was built to keep Cornell cars out of the neighborhoods around it. In 1998 a parking consultant estimated that up to 450 Cornell-related cars parked each day in the Belle Sherman, Bryant Park, Collegetown and Cornell Heights neighborhoods. The city created the permit system in response. It was open only to residents of R-1 and R-2 zones, mostly single-family homes and duplexes. Residents of the denser R-3 zones, including central Collegetown, could not get one ([Cornell Chronicle, 1998](https://news.cornell.edu/node/278473)). The price then was the same as the price today.

State law sets the frame. New York's Vehicle and Traffic Law lets Ithaca run the permit system only on city streets, and it excludes streets inside Cornell. It requires at least 120 minutes of short-term parking in a permit area and keeps at least 20 percent of the spaces open to nonresidents ([NY Vehicle and Traffic Law 1640-e](https://nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/VAT/1640-E*2)).

```reader "If you're renting off campus with a car"
You pay twice: once at home and once on campus. Check your lease for a parking spot before you sign. Without one in central Collegetown, the Dryden Road Garage monthly permit runs $2,088 over nine months. A city permit bought in spring still expires July 31.
```

## Collegetown and downtown, by the hour

The city's garages are the paid fallback for anyone without a spot. Since Jan. 5, 2026, all three open city garages charge $2.25 an hour. The daily cap is $12 at Green Street and Cayuga Street and $18 at Dryden Road. Street parking in ParkMobile and pay-station zones is $2.50 an hour, and coin-only meters farther out are $1.50 an hour (City of Ithaca). Street meters run 9 a.m. to 6 p.m., Monday through Saturday, and Sunday is free ([City parking information](https://www.cityofithacany.gov/705/Parking-Information)).

Saturday used to be free. Until 2026, street and garage parking was free from 6 p.m. Friday to 8 a.m. Monday. The Common Council voted on Nov. 5, 2025, to charge for Saturdays and to raise garage and street rates, which had both been $1.50 an hour. The city expected $322,000 more a year and was closing a budget gap of more than $2 million ([The Ithaca Voice, 2025](https://ithacavoice.org/2025/11/free-saturday-parking-in-ithaca-poised-to-end/)). That is a 50 percent jump in garages and a 67 percent jump on the street. Mayor Robert Cantelmo said higher turnover would free spaces for short-term visitors and local shops (City of Ithaca).

A Worker's Special at $185 a month covers weekdays only, up to 10 hours a day. Monthly permits come in 30-day blocks and do not guarantee a space. Downtown, the choice is now Green Street or Cayuga Street. The Green Street Garage at 120 E. Cayuga St. has more than 300 spaces within a five-minute walk of the Commons. From Nov. 9, 2026, intercity buses stop on the 200 block of South Cayuga Street, beside it ([FingerLakes1](https://www.fingerlakes1.com/2026/11/03/ithaca-and-tcat-will-move-downtown-bus-stops-nov-9/)).

The third downtown garage is gone. On April 10, 2026, debris fell from the third floor of the Seneca Street garage, and the city closed it. A later engineering review confirmed the deterioration, and officials made the closure permanent in May. The garage was 53 years old. Dominick Recckio, then acting city manager, called closing it the most responsible choice for the city's money and operations ([The Ithaca Voice, May 2026](https://ithacavoice.org/2026/05/seneca-street-parking-garage-closed-for-good-officials-weigh-next-steps/)). In September 2026 the Common Council authorized the Ithaca Urban Renewal Agency to start a formal redevelopment process. The city wants housing on the site ([FingerLakes1, Sept. 2026](https://www.fingerlakes1.com/2026/09/05/ithaca-advances-seneca-street-garage-redevelopment-public-safety-facility-alternatives-amid-budget-pressure/)).

Graduate students on assistantships have an option no undergraduate does. A grad student with a full assistantship who works on the Ithaca campus can get Outer Tier A and E permits at no cost. ## Winter: two rules, two start dates

Winter is when the two systems overlap most, and their rules start on different days.

The city's rule comes first. From Nov. 1 to April 1, Ithaca can enforce odd/even parking. On an odd-numbered date, from 7 p.m. to 6 a.m. the next morning, you park on the odd-numbered side of the street. On an even date you park on the even side. The city enforces the rule only when it needs to, such as during a snow event, and posts an alert while enforcement lasts. You can sign up for alerts through TC SIREN at tompkinscountyny.gov/siren or by texting SIREN to 67283 ([City parking rules](https://www.cityofithacany.gov/727/Parking-Regulations-Violations)). Streets marked for 24-hour parking are exempt.

Cornell's rule starts a month later. From Dec. 1 to April 1, when it is snowing or snow is forecast, overnight parking from 5 p.m. to 7:30 a.m. is limited to designated areas so plows can clear the lots. Any registered vehicle can use them (Cornell winter rules). The areas are:

- the West and Mid bays of A Lot on North Campus
- the East and West bays of B Lot at the Vet College
- the north bay of Crescent Lot
- the west bay of the Wilson Lab lot
- the east end of Alumni Field near Wing Drive, on Tower Road
- the Forest Home parking garage, all levels
- the Hoy Road parking garage, all levels except the roof
- the northwest end of the Myron Taylor Hall lot
- the Morrison Hall lot, at Judd Falls and Campus roads

Resident-permit holders in FH, ND, SW and WD lots are exempt and park as usual. B permit holders must follow the overnight rule and use the designated bays in their lot. After a heavy snow, A and B lots are plowed first (Cornell winter rules). The full set of winter rules is in the [winter parking guide](/cornell/getting-around/ithaca-winter-parking-rules-cornell-odd-even/).

The city's rule used to be stricter and harder to follow. In 2014 it applied every night of the season, from 2 a.m. to 6 a.m. The city wrote about 4,200 to 7,300 odd/even tickets a year from 2009 to 2013. In December 2013, Mayor Myrick formed a task force after calling the system confusing, costly and deeply unpopular. Its report described an overnight plow crew that had shrunk to four volunteers. It proposed replacing odd/even with one no-parking day a week on each street ([Odd/Even Parking Task Force, 2014](https://assembly.cornell.edu/sites/default/files/oddeventaskforceinitialreport70314.pdf)). Today the city enforces odd/even only when snow or street work requires it (City parking rules).

Snow causes trouble beyond tickets. In 2022 one student told the Sun her front-wheel-drive car already struggled on Ithaca's hills when they were wet. A Collegetown renter who parked on the street worried that plow banks would box his car in (Cornell Daily Sun, 2022).

## Breaks: where a car can sit

Fall finals end Dec. 19, 2026, and spring classes start Jan. 25, 2027. That is a 37-day gap ([Cornell academic calendar](https://registrar.cornell.edu/academic-calendar)). Where your car spends it decides whether it is still there when you get back.

On campus, students in university housing can leave a car in their permit area over break (Cornell winter rules).

City streets are the danger. A city rule requires every car on the street to move at least once every 24 hours, Monday through Friday. The city treats a car that has not moved in 72 hours as abandoned and can tow it at the owner's cost. City garages are not a fix either. The Dryden Road and Green Street garages also require a move every 24 hours, and the city says its garages offer no long-term parking. For long stays, the city points drivers to Premium Parking on Cayuga Street, across from the Hotel Ithaca, at (607) 256-7275 (City parking rules), the operator its [rate notice](https://www.cityofithacany.gov/CivicAlerts.aspx?AID=1328) names for the Cayuga Street Garage. Cornell's own winter page warns that a car left on a city street over break can be ticketed, towed and impounded (Cornell winter rules). Our guide to [where to leave a car over break](/cornell/getting-around/storing-a-car-ithaca-summer-winter-break-cornell/) compares every option, with costs.

C2C riders have one more choice. Passengers on Cornell's Campus-to-Campus bus to New York can buy temporary B Lot parking for up to 14 days at a time. They can also park for free by filling out the B Lot reservation form (C2C FAQ). Cornell's announcements page says the same free parking works by emailing c2cbus@cornell.edu with your plate number, a rule in effect since Oct. 10, 2024 (Cornell Transportation announcements).

## Spring: refunds and move-out

Spring brings one money decision. If you no longer need your permit, return it by March 31 for a prorated refund, figured from the day you hand it back. After that date there are no refunds, and motorcycle and temporary permits are never refundable. Refunds go back to the account you paid with (Cornell parking permits).

Move-out has its own loading map. Cornell's move-out page names the places where you can stop to load a car. On North Campus they include the Mews, Dickson and Donlon loading docks, the Robert Purcell Community Center lot and the Helen Newman lot, which uses ParkMobile. On West Campus they include marked spaces along West Avenue and Stewart Avenue, and the University Avenue lot is open before or after you load. Cascadilla Hall has no parking between the gorge and the building, so its residents load from the city garage just south of the hall or the Williams Street garage ([Cornell move-out loading](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/housing-details/moving-out/important-loading-and-parking-information)).

## When it goes wrong: tickets and tows

The two systems fine differently. On campus, the most common ticket, for parking without a permit or payment, costs $50, plus $10 if it is unpaid after 10 days (Cornell, 2023). The city raises some fines for repeat offenders within 18 months (City parking rules). Both fine schedules, and how to contest a Cornell or city ticket, are in the [Cornell and Ithaca parking tickets guide](/cornell/getting-around/ithaca-parking-tickets-fines-how-to-appeal-cornell/).

Unpaid city tickets grow teeth. After 20 days the city adds the late penalty. Unanswered tickets can lead the city to certify you to the state as a scofflaw, which blocks registration renewal. More than five unpaid tickets can get a car impounded (City parking rules).

City fines are set to rise. In the 2027 budget it passed in November 2026, the Common Council plans to raise or add fines for many common parking violations. Officials expect about $381,000 more a year from the change. City Manager Dominick Recckio called it a modernization of parking enforcement ([The Ithaca Voice, Nov. 2026](https://ithacavoice.org/2026/11/common-council-approves-2027-budget-city-will-remain-below-tax-cap/)).

## The bigger picture

Three changes will shape parking around Cornell over the next few years.

The first is downtown. With Seneca Street closed, Green Street and Cayuga Street carry the downtown load. The city's plan for the Seneca site is housing, not parking (FingerLakes1, Sept. 2026). The Ithaca Voice reported in May 2026 that the redevelopment could take about 24 months before construction begins (The Ithaca Voice, May 2026).

The second is the city's money. Ithaca leaned on parking in both of its last two budgets: higher rates and paid Saturdays for 2026, higher fines for 2027 (The Ithaca Voice, 2025; The Ithaca Voice, Nov. 2026). Each change makes a car near campus cost more.

The third is Cornell's direction. The university has spent decades pricing student cars high and steering people to buses, and its recent moves point the same way. It sells more spaces by the day than it once did, and its central lots now cost $24.00 a day (Cornell 2022 parking changes; Cornell parking map, August 2026). If you plan to bring a car for 2027-28, budget for higher city fines and check both sets of rates before the August sale.

## Before you bring a car: a checklist

- Register your plate at MyParking.Cornell.edu before you park anywhere on campus.
- Decide on a permit before August, and be online when the sale opens.
- Ask your landlord, in writing, whether your lease includes a parking spot and what it costs.
- Look up your block on the city's residential permit map if you will park on the street.
- Put the ParkMobile app on your phone and save the zones you use.
- Sign up for TC SIREN odd/even alerts by Nov. 1.
- Learn your lot's winter bay before Dec. 1.
- Plan where the car sits over winter break, before finals.
- Pay any ticket within 10 days on campus and 20 days in the city.
- Return an unneeded permit before the spring refund deadline.

For more on keeping a car through the year, see the [car-on-campus starting page](/start/car-on-campus/).
