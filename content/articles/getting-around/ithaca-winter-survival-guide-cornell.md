On Saturday night, Jan. 24, 2026, Cornell's Office of Emergency Management made a call it rarely makes. The Ithaca and AgriTech campuses would close at noon Sunday, with classes, exams and events canceled until noon Monday ([Cornell Emergency Management](https://emergency.cornell.edu/?p=5006)). The National Weather Service was calling for 12 to 18 inches of snow, and Gov. Kathy Hochul had declared a state of emergency ([Cornell Daily Sun](https://cornellsun.com/article/2026/01/cornell-closes-ithaca-agritech-campuses-beginning-sunday-ahead-of-upcoming-snowstorm)).

The storm dropped 7 inches. The cold around it was the real event. From Jan. 23 to Feb. 11, Ithaca went about 19 days without rising above freezing, and the low hit minus 7 on Jan. 30 ([Cornell Daily Sun, March 2026](https://cornellsun.com/article/2026/03/5z6poblqwoea)). A Sun analysis by an atmospheric science student called it Ithaca's coldest winter in over a decade.

An Ithaca winter is not one storm. It is a five-month season, from the first odd/even parking night in November to the last one in early spring. It runs on two rulebooks, Cornell's on the hill and the City of Ithaca's below it. And the cold, the dark and the ice last longer than any snow day.

The stakes are concrete. Ithaca's normal year brings 62.9 inches of snow, and January averages 22.8°F ([Northeast Regional Climate Center](https://www.nrcc.cornell.edu/wxstation/ithaca/normal.html)). A night in the wrong campus lot costs a $50 ticket ([Cornell Daily Sun](https://cornellsun.com/2023/09/04/on-campus-parking-violation-fines-increase-for-first-time-since-2014/)). Staying in a dorm past the December close without approval is billed by the hour. New York's utility regulator puts an average gas-heated household at about $241 a month from November through March ([NY Department of Public Service](https://dps.ny.gov/winter)).

## What an Ithaca winter is actually like

Snow falls in Ithaca from October to April, but four months carry nearly all of it. The 1991-2020 normals, the 30-year averages that weather scientists use as "normal," put snowfall at 12.2 inches in December, 16.6 in January, 14.5 in February and 12.0 in March. Those four months add up to 55.3 inches, or 88 percent of the year's 62.9 inches (Northeast Regional Climate Center).

January is the coldest month, with an average high of 31.0°F and an average low of 14.6°F. The cold drives heating, too. Climate scientists measure heating need in "heating degree days," a count of how far each day's average falls below 65°F. January's normal is 1,308, which is 2.6 times October's 501 (Northeast Regional Climate Center). A heating bill follows that curve.

The dark matters as much as the cold. On Dec. 21, 2026, the sun rises in Ithaca at 7:32 a.m. and sets at 4:36 p.m., a day of 9 hours and 4 minutes ([U.S. Naval Observatory](https://aa.usno.navy.mil/api/rstt/oneday?date=2026-12-21&coords=42.44,-76.50&tz=-5)). By the first week of spring classes the sun sets at 5:11 p.m. ([U.S. Naval Observatory](https://aa.usno.navy.mil/api/rstt/oneday?date=2027-01-25&coords=42.44,-76.50&tz=-5)). Andres Murillo '24 told the Sun that the early sunsets were what he disliked most ([Cornell Daily Sun](https://www.cornellsun.com/article/2023/01/upperclassman-cornellians-share-how-they-cope-with-harsh-ithaca-winters)).

Normals hide the swings. The 2025-26 winter ran 3.1°F below normal, the coldest in over a decade, yet snowfall through March 12 was only 42.1 inches. Early March then brought days up to 20 degrees above average, with highs in the 60s and 70s (Cornell Daily Sun, March 2026). Ellie Gardner '26, who lives in Ithaca year-round, described snow, sleet and sun in a single week ([Cornell Daily Sun](https://www.cornellsun.com/article/2023/01/freshmens-first-winter-a-culture-shock-for-students-from-warm-climates)).

## Why Ithaca winters are gray, cold and long

Lake Ontario sits about 70 miles north of Ithaca, and it shapes the season. In winter the Great Lakes bring heavy snow and near-constant clouds, Drew Montreuil, a meteorologist who runs the Finger Lakes Weather site, told Cornell's alumni magazine. They also keep the region warmer than it would be. Without them, he said, Ithaca would be "a lot more like Minnesota" ([Cornellians](https://alumni.cornell.edu/cornellians/cold-comforts-of-a-big-red-winter)).

The hills add a second layer. Montreuil noted how different the weather can be between downtown, campus and the hills around them, so the forecast for "Ithaca" is not always the weather on your street.

Then there is ice. Salt, the main tool against it, gets weaker as the air gets colder. A pound of salt melts 46 pounds of ice at 30°F but barely five pounds at 10°F, the same magazine reported. That is about nine times less melting on the coldest mornings. Cornell spreads more than 2,500 tons of salt in a typical winter, grounds director Dan Schied told the magazine (Cornellians). The City of Ithaca says salt loses its effectiveness below 20°F ([City of Ithaca DPW](https://cityofithacany.gov/DocumentCenter/View/12559)). On a 10-degree morning, the path that looks cleared is the one to watch.

The long trend points the other way from 2025-26. Ithaca's normal date for the first inch of snow moved from Nov. 20 to Nov. 25 when the normals period shifted to 1991-2020, said Cornell Prof. Art DeGaetano. He expected that date to keep moving later as the climate warms. Warmer is not the same as easier. Prof. Angeline Pendergrass told the Sun she expects more winter precipitation to fall as rain instead of snow ([Cornell Daily Sun, 2021](https://cornellsun.com/2021/11/17/delayed-first-snowfall-in-ithaca-reflects-changing-climate-patterns/)).

## November: dress for it before you need it

The city's odd/even parking season opens as November starts, and the first inch of snow normally falls late in the month. November is the month to buy what you will wear.

Cornell Health recommends warm, loose layers, a water-resistant jacket and boots, and covered hands and ears ([Cornell Health](https://health.cornell.edu/about/news/cold-weather-safety)). International Services gets specific. Layers trap pockets of warm air. Long underwear goes under loose pants. Mittens beat gloves because less of your hand meets the cold. Insulated hiking boots with thick rubber soles and deep tread work well on campus, and all-leather boots need a waterproofing spray (International Services).

Winter gear does not have to be new. Cornell's Graduate School lists new-clothing stores in Ithaca, including REI Co-op, Target, T.J. Maxx and Walmart, and secondhand options such as Goodwill, Plato's Closet, the Salvation Army and the Ithaca ReUse Center. Catholic Charities of Tompkins/Tioga and the Women's Opportunity Center run free clothing closets ([Cornell Graduate School](https://gradschool.cornell.edu/announcements/grad-tips-winter-clothing-resources)). Low-income students can apply to Cornell's Access Fund, run by the Office of the Dean of Students through First-Generation and Low-Income Student Support. It covers up to $100 for boots or up to $150 for a coat, hat and gloves (Cornell Graduate School).

Thanksgiving break runs Nov. 25 to Nov. 29, 2026 ([Cornell Registrar](https://registrar.cornell.edu/calendars-exams/academic-calendar)). TCAT does not run on Thanksgiving Day, Christmas or New Year's Day (Cornell Daily Sun, 2025), so if you stay in Ithaca, plan food and errands around those days.

## December: finals, parking and the building close

December stacks three deadlines on top of finals, and two of them fall on the same day.

On Dec. 1, Cornell's overnight winter parking rule begins. From then until the rule ends in spring, when snow is falling or forecast, cars can park overnight only in nine designated areas, from 5 p.m. to 7:30 a.m. ([Cornell Transportation](https://fcs.cornell.edu/departments/transportation-delivery-services/parking-campus/winter-break-parking-rules)). They include the west and middle bays of A Lot, the east and west bays of B Lot, the Forest Home garage and the Hoy Road garage (not the roof). Students with FH, ND, SW or WD resident permits keep parking in their own lots. After heavy snow, A and B lots are plowed first. A car in the wrong spot gets the winter-rules ticket described above (Cornell Daily Sun). The full list of bays, and how the city's rule fits with Cornell's, is in our guide to [winter parking at Cornell and in Ithaca](/cornell/getting-around/ithaca-winter-parking-rules-cornell-odd-even/).

The same day is the last day to apply for winter break housing.

Study days run Dec. 8 to 10, and finals run Dec. 11 to 19 (Cornell Registrar). Then the buildings close. All undergraduate residences close at 2 p.m. on Sunday, Dec. 20, 2026. Anyone still inside without approval is charged $60 for each hour or part of an hour, with no proration. If you cannot leave by 2 p.m., request a later departure in the Housing and Dining Portal by Monday, Dec. 7, the last day of classes ([Cornell Housing](https://scl.cornell.edu/sub/sub/residential-life/housing/housing-details/winter-closing-notice)).

The fee is steeper than it looks. Nine hours past the close at $60 an hour is $540, more than the $500 for the whole break in winter housing (Cornell Housing; Winter Break Housing). A late flight is a reason to file the late-departure request, not to linger.

Before you leave, the closing checklist asks you to unplug everything except fridges, empty community fridges, lock windows and take valuables, medications and passports. Staff inspect rooms over break and can enter for repairs.

The trip home is its own winter hazard. Cornell's Campus-to-Campus bus books departures through Dec. 23, 2026, on one link and departures from Jan. 6, 2027, on another ([Cornell C2C](https://fcs.cornell.edu/departments/transportation-delivery-services/campus-campus-bus-service)). Our guide to [getting home for winter break](/cornell/getting-around/cornell-winter-break-travel-home-2026/) compares every bus, flight and fare. Storms have wrecked these trips before. When snow hit the Sunday after Thanksgiving in 2019, Eva Zhang's bus from New York City took nine hours, against the usual four to five. ## Over the break: who stays and what still runs

To qualify for winter break housing, you must be returning to campus housing for spring. Apply in the Housing and Dining Portal between Oct. 1 and Dec. 1. The fee is flat: $125 to stay one week after fall term, $125 to arrive one week before spring term, or $500 for the whole break ([Winter Break Housing](https://scl.cornell.edu/sub/sub/residential-life/housing/campus-housing/winter-break-housing)). Students outside an open building can apply only for the full break, in limited flex spaces.

For 2026-27, ten buildings stay open: Ganędagǫ: Hall, High Rise 5, Holland International Living Center/Low Rise 8, Hu Shih Hall, Loving House, Mews Hall, Toni Morrison Hall, the Townhouse Apartments, Cascadilla Hall and Veterans House (Winter Break Housing). Residents of HILC/Low Rise 8 and Loving House stay at no extra cost, thanks to gifts to those communities. Veterans House residents stay free because break is part of their license. For 2027-28, the list adds 301 College Avenue and Jameson Hall and drops High Rise 5 and Cascadilla.

Food is the hard part. Meal plans end with brunch on closing day and resume with lunch the day the halls reopen (Cornell Housing). Students can use BRBs at open dining rooms. Cornell Dining traditionally closes everything during the week the university itself is closed, and students can then use City Bucks at local restaurants that are open. Mail and packages stop after Dec. 23 and resume Jan. 7, so order what you need early (Winter Break Housing).

## January and February: the deep cold

Residence halls reopen at 9 a.m. Tuesday, Jan. 19, 2027. The registrar lists Monday, Jan. 25, as the first day of spring term. Housing's closing notice says classes begin Tuesday, Jan. 26. Go by the registrar's calendar for your class schedule (Cornell Registrar; Cornell Housing).

Students come back to the heart of winter. January is both the coldest and the snowiest month on average. In 2026, nine days in a row, Jan. 25 through Feb. 2, averaged below 10°F (Cornell Daily Sun, March 2026). February break, Feb. 13 to 16, 2027, comes in the middle of it (Cornell Registrar).

Cold this deep is a health issue, not a comfort issue. Frostbite freezes skin and the tissue under it. Frostnip is a milder form that does not cause permanent damage. Hypothermia is a dangerously low body temperature. The early signs of frostbite are cold, prickling skin, then numbness, then skin that turns red, white, bluish-white or grayish-yellow. If you suspect frostbite, get medical care right away, and do not rub the area or warm it with direct heat. Cornell Health's 24/7 advice line is 607-255-5155 (Cornell Health).

Wind makes it worse. Wind chill is how cold the air feels on bare skin once wind is counted. At 0°F with a 15 mph wind, the wind chill is minus 19, and exposed skin can freeze in 30 minutes ([National Weather Service](https://www.weather.gov/safety/cold-wind-chill-chart)). Cornell Health also warns that wet clothes and sweaty boots speed heat loss, and that alcohol makes skin lose heat faster (Cornell Health).

## Getting around campus on snow and ice

Cornell's Grounds staff clear 15 miles of roads, 61 miles of walkways and 114 acres of parking lots after a storm. Building Care staff salt and grit entrances, steps and ramps. Some stairs and walks are posted as not getting winter maintenance, and those are not cleared ([Cornell snow removal](https://fcs.cornell.edu/services/repair-maintenance/snow-removal)). Response times depend on how fast the snow falls and which roads and walkways are priorities. Report an unsafe or slippery spot to Customer Service at 607-255-5322.

Students add their own rules. Don't walk down Libe Slope while it is snowing, Taylor Brown '25 told the Sun, and avoid the metal panels on bridges (Cornell Daily Sun, 2023).

TCAT is the fallback on bad days, and storms bend it too. After the January 2026 storm, TCAT delayed Monday service until 10 a.m. because of road conditions ([Cornell Daily Sun](https://cornellsun.com/article/2026/01/cornell-announces-ithaca-and-agritech-campuses-will-reopen-at-noon-on-monday)). Who rides free depends on your year. First-year degree students ride free at all hours. PhD students and graduate assistants get free OmniRide passes. Other students who have been enrolled a year or more ride free after 6 p.m. on weekdays and all weekend, and pay $1.50 a daytime ride (Cornell Transportation).

Cyclists can keep riding, with care. Every TCAT bus has a front bike rack at no extra fare, so you can ride down the hill and bus up it, according to Reed Huegerich, Cornell's assistant director of transportation ([Cornell Daily Sun](https://cornellsun.com/article/2025/11/guest-room-from-bikes-to-buses-how-to-sustainably-navigate-campus-this-fall)). Cornell publishes no winter cycling guide. Our guide to [biking in Ithaca in winter](/cornell/getting-around/biking-in-ithaca-winter-cornell/) collects the gear, locking and route advice riders have given.

## When Cornell closes for snow

Cornell rarely closes. It canceled classes for snow in March 2017, during Winter Storm Stella, for the first time since 1993 ([Cornell Daily Sun, 2020](https://cornellsun.com/2020/12/02/snow-days-no-more-students-not-warming-to-cornells-new-policy-for-the-coldest-days/)). Stella dropped over a foot of snow and closed campus for 28 hours (Cornell Daily Sun, January 2026). The closure before that, in 1993, came during a blizzard that dropped 30 inches on East Hill; dining stayed open while the university shut (Cornellians). Since Stella, closures have come more often. Cornell closed for snow in March 2018, twice in 2019-20, on March 14, 2023 ([Cornell Daily Sun, 2023](https://cornellsun.com/2023/03/13/breaking-news-university-to-close-tuesday-due-to-winter-storm/)) and in January 2026.

The rules for a closed day have changed. In December 2020, with most classes online during the pandemic, Executive Vice President Joanne DeStefano announced that students could be required to take an online version of a missed class. The Sun reported that this effectively ended snow days, and students objected (Cornell Daily Sun, 2020). Cornell's current inclement weather policy takes the opposite line. Under Policy 8.2, when the university closes, classes are canceled, and students are not expected to attend virtually or do extra coursework for that day ([Cornell Policy 8.2](https://hr.cornell.edu/about/employment-policy-practice/employment-policies/inclement-weather)).

When Cornell closes, residence halls stay open, while libraries, The Cornell Store and campus shops close (Cornell Daily Sun, January 2026). Some dining locations closed in the January 2026 storm too. Check Cornell's operating status at emergency.cornell.edu, or call the inclement weather line at 607-255-3377 (Cornell Emergency Management). Closures are announced through CornellALERT. Until one arrives, plan as if classes will meet.

## Off campus: heat, bills and the sidewalk out front

Living off campus moves three winter jobs onto you or your landlord: heat, the heating bill and the sidewalk.

Heat is the landlord's legal duty. In Ithaca the heating season runs Sept. 15 to May 31, longer than the state's Oct. 1 start. From 6 a.m. to 10 p.m., when it is below 55°F outside, your apartment must be at least 68°F. From 10 p.m. to 6 a.m., it must be at least 62°F whatever the weather. If a landlord who pays for heat falls behind, the utility must give tenants at least 30 days' notice before cutting heat between the start of November and April 15 ([Cornell Tenants Advocacy Program](https://sites.lawschool.cornell.edu/tenants-advocacy/building-issues-maintenance)). Put repair requests in writing and keep copies. If the landlord ignores them, the City of Ithaca Building Division is at 607-274-6508. Our guide to [what to do when a landlord won't fix the heat](/cornell/housing/ithaca-landlord-wont-fix-heat-mold-bed-bugs/) walks through each step.

If the heat is on your bill, budget for it before the lease starts. New York's utility regulator estimates that a household using 140 therms of natural gas a month will pay about $241 a month from November through March in 2026-27, down 3.2 percent from a year earlier (NY Department of Public Service). A therm is the unit gas is billed in. Five months at that rate is $1,205. Split four ways, that is $301.25 a person for the season. The state warns that real bills vary widely with weather and usage, and that a colder-than-normal winter raises them.

Rates could rise. NYSEG, the utility that serves Ithaca, asked the state for increases that would have added about $33 a month to a typical gas bill. In May 2026 the Public Service Commission approved smaller temporary increases, effective June 1, with an expected 1.7 percent rise in typical NYSEG gas bills, while it decided the full request ([FingerLakes1](https://www.fingerlakes1.com/2026/05/15/nyseg-and-rge-win-temporary-rate-hikes-as-larger-increases-still-loom/)). Our guide to [Ithaca utility and heating costs](/cornell/money/ithaca-utilities-cost-winter-heating-bill-apartment-students/) shows how heat type changes the bill and what to ask a landlord. The state's Home Energy Assistance Program offers heating grants to income-eligible households; its hotline is 1-800-342-3009 (NY Department of Public Service).

The sidewalk is the third job. City code requires all snow and ice to be removed from the sidewalk in front of a property within 24 hours after snow starts ([City of Ithaca](https://www.cityofithacany.gov/136/Exterior-Property-Maintenance)). The code puts that duty on the property owner, and each day of a violation counts separately. If a plow pushes snow back onto a cleared walk, the owner clears it again. Corner owners clear both sides (City of Ithaca DPW). A lease can hand the shoveling to tenants, so read yours. Our guide to [Ithaca's trash, snow and noise rules for renters](/cornell/housing/ithaca-trash-tags-snow-noise-rules-renters/) covers who owes what.

The street itself is the city's job, in a set order. Plows open lanes for police, fire and ambulances first. The busiest roads come next, along with steep hills where cars can slide. Small residential streets come last. With back-to-back storms and long cold spells, getting parking lanes back can take weeks. The city has about 65 miles of road, or nearly 150 miles of lanes to plow (City of Ithaca DPW).

That is why odd/even parking exists. In narrow, dense neighborhoods such as Collegetown, Fall Creek, Southside and South Hill, one or two cars on the wrong side can keep a plow from clearing to the curb. The rule runs from Nov. 1 to April 1. It used to apply every night of that season, but the city now turns it on only when it needs to plow (City of Ithaca DPW). On those nights, from 7 p.m. to 6 a.m., cars park on the odd side on odd dates and the even side on even dates. Sign up for alerts through TC SIREN or by texting "SIREN" to 67283. Fines climb from $15 to $30 to $45 for repeat violations within 18 months, so three missed nights cost $90 (City parking rules). In the Dryden Road Garage, the uncovered 4th and 5th floors are closed from 2 a.m. to 8 a.m., November through April ([Dryden Road Garage](https://www.cityofithacany.gov/718/Dryden-Road-Garage)).

The sidewalks are a live fight in Ithaca. Advocates for people with disabilities, older residents and walkers have pushed the city to clear sidewalks and curb cuts itself, as Syracuse does on about 125 miles of busy sidewalk with a $650,000 yearly budget. In September 2023, Superintendent of Public Works Mike Thorne told a Common Council committee the city lacked the staff, and put the cost near $4,000 a mile each winter. He argued the money would be wasted once the snow melted. Armin Heurich, a snow-removal advocate and Ithaca High School librarian, answered that a city that calls itself walkable has to be walkable in winter, too ([The Ithaca Voice](https://ithacavoice.org/2023/09/snow-removal-or-no-removal/)). In February 2026 the city was still reminding owners to clear their walks and warning that it can do the job and bill them ([FingerLakes1](https://www.fingerlakes1.com/2026/02/14/ithaca-sidewalk-snow-rules-in-effect/)).

## The winter blues

Short days wear on people. Seasonal affective disorder, or SAD, is a type of depression that follows the seasons, returning between fall and spring. A Cornell Health psychiatric nurse practitioner, Güney Acipayamli, recommends light therapy for it: a light box of up to 10,000 lux, used for 30 minutes soon after waking, ideally before noon, placed 12 to 24 inches away. Light boxes sell for $20 at the Cornell Health Pharmacy. Check with a clinician first if you have bipolar disorder, certain eye conditions or take light-sensitizing medicines ([Cornell Daily Sun](https://www.cornellsun.com/article/2026/02/winter-blues-sad-and-the-benefits-of-light-therapy)). Cornell Health offers appointments with medical and mental health providers, and "Let's Talk" drop-in consultations with counselors.

Students have their own fixes. Ellie Gardner '26 suggested an outdoor hobby, such as skiing, hiking the gorges or visiting Taughannock Falls State Park (Cornell Daily Sun, 2023). Murillo said to keep busy with friends and clubs. Fátima Martínez '24 said students who feel low in winter tend to write it off as laziness (Cornell Daily Sun, 2023).

Winter used to be a campus sport. Beebe Lake hosted ice carnivals, toboggan runs and hockey games until Lynah Rink opened in 1957, and in the winter of 1911-12 Cayuga Lake froze over completely (Cornellians). Greek Peak, a ski area near Ithaca, hosts Cornell's skiing physical education course and offered college discounts on Monday nights in 2023 ([Cornell Daily Sun](https://www.cornellsun.com/article/2023/01/winter-sports-organizations-aim-to-engage-cornellians-in-cold-weather-activities)).

## March and the slow thaw

March still brings 12 inches of snow in a normal year, and April adds almost three more (Northeast Regional Climate Center). Spring break runs March 27 to April 4, 2027 (Cornell Registrar). Then the rulebooks close. Cornell's overnight winter parking rule and the city's odd/even season both end as April begins (Cornell Transportation; City parking rules). The heat rules in rentals run to the end of May.

## The bigger picture

Winter at Cornell is shaped by forces that will keep moving after this season.

The first is the climate. Ithaca's normal first snow has already slid later, and DeGaetano expects it to keep sliding (Cornell Daily Sun, 2021). But 2025-26 showed that a warming trend still allows a long, brutal cold spell (Cornell Daily Sun, March 2026). Plan for both.

The second is cost. The state's final decision on NYSEG's larger rate request will set what a heated Ithaca apartment costs in winters to come (FingerLakes1). Anyone signing a lease for 2027-28 should ask the landlord, in writing, who pays for heat and what the unit used last winter.

The third is the sidewalk. Ithaca's walks stay the job of thousands of individual owners and tenants. Whether the city ever takes on a Syracuse-style program is up to City Hall and its budget. Until it does, the walk in front of your house is clear only if someone on your block clears it.

## Before the first snow: a checklist

- Buy a warm coat, insulated waterproof boots with deep tread, a hat, mittens and a scarf by mid-November.
- If cost is a barrier, apply to the Access Fund through the Dean of Students office.
- Save Cornell Health's 24/7 line (607-255-5155) and the weather line (607-255-3377) in your phone.
- If you park in the city, sign up for TC SIREN odd/even alerts.
- Decide before the housing deadline whether you will stay for winter break, and apply if so.
- Learn your car's winter bay on campus before the campus winter rule starts.
- Book your trip home before finals, and file a late-departure request by the last day of classes if you need one.
- Off campus, read your lease for heat and snow removal, and buy a shovel and salt before the first storm.
- Order anything you need shipped to campus to arrive before campus mail pauses for break.
- Plan a light box, a winter activity or a standing plan with friends for January.
