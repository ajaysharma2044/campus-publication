```glance
- Resident permit (North, West, South, Hasbrouck): $752.98 a year, about $2.06 a day. Commuter B and accessible AP: $359.77. Motorcycle: $120.01.
- First-years may bring a car: Cornell discourages it, but limited permits exist, and no permit rule bars first-years.
- Sales are first come, first served: 2026-27 permits went on sale Tue Aug 4, 2026 at 9 a.m. at myparking.cornell.edu.
- Register your plate in MyParking first. It is required even for short-term parking.
- Permits run Aug 16 to Aug 15. Return one by Mar 31 for a prorated refund; after that, nothing back.
- Fines run $50 (no permit or payment) to $150 plus a tow (fire lane or accessible space).
```

Yes, a freshman can have a car at Cornell, but Cornell's families page says first-years are [discouraged from bringing one, though limited permits are available](https://scl.cornell.edu/families/cornell-family-resource-hub/parking-transportation). A permit for your residence area costs [$752.98 a year, and a commuter permit $359.77](https://fcs.cornell.edu/departments/transportation-delivery-services/parking-campus/parking-students). Most first-years should skip it: they ride TCAT free at all hours, and permits sell out after the August sale opens.

```reader "If you're a first-year"
You ride TCAT free at any hour with your Cornell ID [until Aug 31, 2027](https://fcs.cornell.edu/departments/transportation-delivery-services/sustainable-transportation-options/bus-services-privileges-passes), so a car mostly buys trips home, for $752.98 a resident permit, or $2.06 a day by our math. The North Campus (ND) permit is [extremely limited, capped and for North Campus residents only](https://fcs.cornell.edu/departments/transportation-delivery-services/parking-campus/parking-students). If you still want one, register your plate and be online at 9 a.m. on the August sale day. If ND is gone, ParkMobile by the day is the fallback. The mistake that costs most: parking without a permit or payment, at [$50 a ticket plus $10 if unpaid after 10 days](https://fcs.cornell.edu/announcements/2023-08-01/citation-fines-increasing-september-1).
```

```reader "If you're keeping a car in university housing"
Buy the permit for your area: FH (Hasbrouck), ND (North), SW (South) or WD (West), $752.98 a year. It also works in North Campus A Lot after 2:30 p.m. Anywhere else, pay by ParkMobile, with a [$0.45 fee per transaction](https://fcs.cornell.edu/departments/transportation-delivery-services/parking-campus/short-term-parking-options). In winter, resident-permit holders [park in their own lots as usual](https://fcs.cornell.edu/departments/transportation-delivery-services/parking-campus/winter-break-parking-rules), and the car can stay in your permit area over break. The costly mistake: missing the sale morning. In 2023 the Williams Street lot [sold out within hours](https://www.cornellsun.com/article/2023/09/williams-street-parking-lot-struggles-to-accommodate-students).
```

```reader "If you're renting off campus"
You cannot buy a resident permit. Your options are the student commuter (B) permit at $359.77, about $30 a month, or paying by the day. The B permit works in B Lot at the Vet College at all times and in A Lot after 2:30 p.m. Daily Decision parking in outer lots is advertised from [about $1 a day](https://fcs.cornell.edu/departments/transportation-delivery-services/parking/getting-around/commuter-support-services). At home, the City of Ithaca sells a [$45 residential permit](https://www.cityofithacany.gov/731/Permit-Issuance) valid to July 31, but only to residents: a landlord cannot get it for you.
```

```reader "If you're a grad student"
Check Workday first. With a full assistantship on the Ithaca campus, you get [free Outer Tier A and E permits](https://fcs.cornell.edu/departments/transportation-delivery-services/parking-campus/parking-students) (E needs work in its zone). You may also buy faculty and staff permits. Grads living in university housing are not eligible for the free permits. Without an assistantship, the commuter B permit is $359.77. The costly mistake is paying that $359.77 when your appointment makes a permit free.
```

```reader "If you need an accessible permit"
The accessible (AP) permit costs [$359.77](https://fcs.cornell.edu/departments/transportation-delivery-services/parking-campus/parking-students), the same as a commuter permit, and works in permit-restricted spaces except reserved and MT spaces. Contact Student Disability Services before the early-August sale, not after. If a friend or relative drives your car into an accessible space without the permit, the fine is [$150 with automatic towing](https://cornellsun.com/2023/09/04/on-campus-parking-violation-fines-increase-for-first-time-since-2014/).
```

```reader "If you're a transfer or starting in spring"
No transfer-specific permit rule appears on Cornell's permit page. A permit bought mid-year is [prorated daily](https://fcs.cornell.edu/departments/transportation-delivery-services/parking-campus/parking-students): from Aug 16 for commuter permits and from Sept 1 for resident permits. A January arrival lands inside the winter rules. From [Dec 1 to Apr 1](https://fcs.cornell.edu/departments/transportation-delivery-services/parking-campus/winter-break-parking-rules), when snow is forecast, overnight parking is limited to listed lots. Return the permit by Mar 31 if you leave or sell the car; after that date there is no refund.
```

## What each student permit costs and where it works

Cornell sells campus parking two ways: an annual permit, sold [first come, first served while space lasts](https://fcs.cornell.edu/departments/transportation-delivery-services/parking-campus), or payment by the hour or day through ParkMobile. Prices below are from Cornell's [student parking page](https://fcs.cornell.edu/departments/transportation-delivery-services/parking-campus/parking-students), which gives them no year: it sets rates as of July 1 and labels its map 2025-26.

| Permit | Who can buy it | Price | Where it works | Proration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Resident (FH, ND, SW, WD) | Students in university housing in that area | $752.98 a year | Your signed area, plus A Lot after 2:30 p.m. (ND: A Lot at all times) | Daily, from Sept 1 |
| ND (North) | North Campus residents of university housing only; extremely limited and capped | $752.98 a year | Your signed area, plus A Lot at all times | Daily, from Sept 1 |
| Student commuter (B) | Commuters only | $359.77 a year | B Lot always, A Lot after 2:30 p.m. | Daily, from Aug 16 |
| Accessible (AP) | Through Student Disability Services first | $359.77 a year | Permit-restricted spaces except reserved and MT | Not stated |
| Motorcycle (M) | Motorcycles, scooters, mopeds | $120.01 a year | Not stated | Monthly |
| Outer Tier A and E | Graduate students with a full assistantship; not resident students | Free | Outer lots; E needs work in its zone | Not applicable |

The commuter lots are Vet College B, TRB, South and North Morrison, and BTI. A second Cornell page gives the motorcycle permit as [$119.94](https://fcs.cornell.edu/node/1456); trust the $120.01 on the main student page. Every permit is valid through Aug 15. Resident and commuter permits are limited, and Cornell recalls a permit if you lose eligibility.

The resident price has barely moved. The Cornell Sun printed it at [$752.86 in 2023](https://cornellsun.com/2023/09/04/on-campus-parking-violation-fines-increase-for-first-time-since-2014/), 12 cents less than today, and a 2016 forum poster put it at about $750.

## Can first-years have a car?

Yes. The student permit page lists no first-year permit and no first-year ban; the [families page](https://scl.cornell.edu/families/cornell-family-resource-hub/parking-transportation) only discourages the car and calls permits limited. A first-year in a residence hall buys the resident permit for that area. On North Campus, that is the capped ND permit.

Not confirmed: whether a first-year is ever refused a permit for class year alone. Ask Transportation and Delivery Services at [607-255-4600](https://fcs.cornell.edu/departments/transportation-delivery-services), open Mon to Fri 7:30 a.m. to 12 p.m., before you drive the car to Ithaca.

Students who answered the question say skip it. In a 2016 [College Confidential thread](https://talk.collegeconfidential.com/t/should-i-bring-a-car/1811430) with 8 to 10 posters, two-thirds to four-fifths of the arguments (our two counts) told an incoming freshman to leave the car home. Their reasons: scarce, pricey parking, classroom buildings with no parking, and TCAT and walking for daily life. One parent said their younger daughter, who doesn't drive, got rides from friends and took a taxi when none could drive her. It is one old thread, not a survey.

Search results for this question mix in Cornell College in Iowa, a different school with different permits. Cornell University's rules live on fcs.cornell.edu.

## How to get a permit, step by step

```figure
kind: steps
title: Getting a Cornell student parking permit
rows:
  - label: Register your plate in MyParking.Cornell.edu
  - label: Check which permit your housing or status allows
  - label: Accessible permit only - contact Student Disability Services first
  - label: Be online when sales open (Tue Aug 4, 2026, 9 a.m.)
  - label: Pay at myparking.cornell.edu; permits valid from Aug 16
  - label: Return it by Mar 31 if you no longer need it
```

1. **Register your plate.** Students must [register cars, motorcycles and scooters](https://fcs.cornell.edu/departments/transportation-delivery-services/parking-campus) in MyParking.Cornell.edu. This applies to short-term parking too, so do it even if you never buy a permit.
2. **Check eligibility.** Resident permits follow your housing area, B is for commuters, AP starts with Student Disability Services, and grad assistants check their appointment in Workday.
3. **Be online at sale time.** The 2026-27 sale [opened Tue Aug 4, 2026 at 9 a.m.](https://fcs.cornell.edu/node/17809), first come, first served, at myparking.cornell.edu. Those permits are valid Aug 16, 2026 to Aug 15, 2027. The 2027-28 sale date is not posted yet. In fall 2023, Williams Street lot permits, about $700, [sold out within hours](https://www.cornellsun.com/article/2023/09/williams-street-parking-lot-struggles-to-accommodate-students).
4. **Pay.** Prices drop daily from Sept 1 (resident) or Aug 16 (commuter); the motorcycle permit prorates monthly.
5. **Know when to quit.** Return the permit by Mar 31 for a prorated refund. Motorcycle and temporary permits get no refund, and no refund is paid in cash.

Example: a spring transfer moving into Collegetown buys a B permit on Jan 15. That leaves 213 of the permit year's 365 days. If proration is a straight daily share, the price is about $210 ($359.77 x 213 / 365, our math). Cornell does not publish its formula, so confirm the figure at checkout.

## Parking without a permit

Lots in the resident areas need a permit or [ParkMobile payment 24 hours a day](https://fcs.cornell.edu/departments/transportation-delivery-services/parking-campus/short-term-parking-options). Commuter lots charge Mon to Fri 7:30 a.m. to 5 p.m., year-round, including holidays and breaks. ParkMobile adds $0.45 per transaction, extensions included. You pay by text (PARK to 77223) or in the app, for at least 1 hour.

Daily Decision is Cornell's demand-priced option in outer lots, open to all. Cornell advertises it at [as little as $1 a day](https://fcs.cornell.edu/departments/transportation-delivery-services/parking/getting-around/commuter-support-services) ($0.65 plus the $0.35 ParkMobile fee) in select zones. Short-term rates changed Aug 1, 2026, so check the zone price in the app.

Enforcement runs whenever the university is open and pauses only on official closed staff holidays, until the next business day. No-parking, fire-lane, reserved, service and accessible rules are never suspended.

## Is a permit worth it?

By our math, the resident permit works out to $62.75 a month over 12 months, or $2.06 a day over 365. That equals about 502 TCAT cash rides at $1.50. The commuter permit is $29.98 a month, under $1 a day. Those figures cover the space only, before gas, insurance and tickets.

Compare a shared car. Ithaca Carshare's [It's My Car plan](https://www.ithacacarshare.org/rates) for students costs $310 a year plus $6 an hour and a $20 sign-up. The money for a resident permit, after that plan's fees, buys about 59 to 70 hours of driving, by our math, depending on whether usage taxes apply. Our [Carshare guide](/getting-around/ithaca-carshare-cornell-students-cost/) runs the numbers by plan.

Example: a first-year on North Campus gets an ND permit for $752.98 and collects two $50 tickets parking outside the lot. The year costs $852.98 before gas and insurance. The same student's TCAT rides cost nothing.

A permit buys a lot, not a spot. In the 2023 Sun story, Williams Street permit holder Deana Druz '24 called finding a space a daily "treasure hunt." When the lot was full one morning, she paid for a ParkMobile lot and was late. Sasha Planinsic '25 told the Sun she plans errands around finding a spot when she returns. In a [2022 Sun story](https://cornellsun.com/2022/11/21/dont-let-it-snow-students-express-concerns-over-driving-conditions-as-temperatures-fall/), one student lived about 15 minutes from the off-campus lot where she parked, a front-wheel-drive car struggled on wet hills, and another student worried snowbanks would build up around his car after plows cleared Collegetown roads.

## Common mistakes

- **Missing the sale morning.** Permits are limited and sold first come; the Williams Street lot sold out within hours in 2023. Cost: no permit for the year, and $50 a ticket if you park anyway.
- **Bringing a first-year car without a plan.** The capped ND permit is $752.98, paid for trips TCAT already covers free.
- **Letting a ticket sit.** The $50 fine grows by $10 after 10 days.
- **Returning the permit after Mar 31.** No refund at all, on a permit that cost up to $752.98.
- **Paying for a B permit with a full assistantship.** Grad assistants get Outer Tier A and E free; the mistake costs $359.77.
- **Parking a visitor's car in an accessible space or fire lane.** $150 and an automatic tow.
- **Assuming a resident permit works everywhere.** It covers your area and A Lot after 2:30 p.m.; elsewhere you pay ParkMobile plus the $0.45 fee, or risk $50.

## FAQ

**Can I buy a permit after the August sale?** Yes, while any are left. Prices prorate daily after Sept 1 (resident) or Aug 16 (commuter), per the [student parking page](https://fcs.cornell.edu/departments/transportation-delivery-services/parking-campus/parking-students).

**Does a Cornell permit cover parking in Collegetown?** Not confirmed. City streets follow city rules; see our [Collegetown and downtown parking guide](/getting-around/parking-collegetown-downtown-ithaca-garages-permits/) for garages and the $45 city permit.

**Can I park at Cornell for a day without a permit?** Yes. Register the plate, then pay by ParkMobile; the [Tang Welcome Center lot](https://fcs.cornell.edu/departments/transportation-delivery-services/parking-campus/short-term-parking-options) charges $1.50 an hour or $10 a day.

**Can I leave my car on campus over winter break?** Students in university housing may leave it in their permit area, per the [winter rules page](https://fcs.cornell.edu/departments/transportation-delivery-services/parking-campus/winter-break-parking-rules). Never leave it on a city street.

**Who do I call about a permit?** Transportation and Delivery Services, 116 Maple Avenue, 607-255-4600, Mon to Fri 7:30 a.m. to 12 p.m. ([TDS](https://fcs.cornell.edu/departments/transportation-delivery-services)).
