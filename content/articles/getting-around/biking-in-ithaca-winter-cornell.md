You can bike in Ithaca all winter. The setup that works: a bike you don't mind salting, good brakes, warm layers, and a bus backup, since [every TCAT bus carries a front bike rack at no extra fare](https://cornellsun.com/article/2025/11/guest-room-from-bikes-to-buses-how-to-sustainably-navigate-campus-this-fall). Ride when the roads are bare and put the bike on the bus for the climbs and the storms.

```reader "If you're a year-round cyclist"
Your gear decides the season. In a 2007 Cornell Daily Sun story, a rider advised a separate winter bike because snow, ice and salt wear a bike down, and another said good brakes keep stopping distance steady. Lock frame and wheel to a rack with a U-lock: in fall 2024 campus police logged 51 bike and scooter thefts worth at least $41,177, with four bikes recovered intact. A rack buried in snow is still the only legal spot; bikes on railings, trees or poles can be impounded. On an e-bike, never charge the battery in your dorm room, office or an academic building. The costliest mistake is a cheap lock on a good bike.
```

```reader "If you're an upper-year without a car"
The bus backup is not free all day for you. After first year, TCAT is free only after 6 p.m. on weekdays and all weekend; a weekday daytime ride costs $1.50, or $200 a year for OmniRide. A snowy weekday with a bus each way costs $3.00. Our break-even: below about 134 paid daytime rides a year, paying per ride beats the pass, so winter-only bus days rarely justify it. The Sun puts the walk between North Campus and Collegetown at 15 to 20 minutes each way. The mistake that costs most is assuming the free ride covers your 10 a.m. trip in a storm.
```

```reader "If you're a grad student"
If you are a PhD student or graduate assistant, your OmniRide pass is free, so the ride-down, bus-up plan costs nothing at any hour. Put the bike on the front rack and ride the bus up the hill. Master's and professional students without an assistantship pay $1.50 a daytime weekday ride after their first year. If you are at Johnson, Sage Hall has showers at classroom level for riders. If you live near the Commons, about 2 miles from campus, the climb home is long. The costly mistake is buying a $200 pass when your PhD or assistantship already covers it.
```

```reader "If you're renting off campus"
Your commute starts off campus, so the hill home is the hard part. Students in a 2022 Sun story worried about icy hills and snowbanks piling up around a Collegetown street-parked car. The Commons is about 2 miles from campus; the Sun puts the North Campus to Collegetown walk at 15 to 20 minutes. Ride downhill only on bare pavement, and use the free TCAT front rack for the climb. City plows push snow from the center of the road toward the curb, and after back-to-back storms in a cold spell, clearing out to the curb can take several weeks. Before 6 p.m. on weekdays the bus is $1.50 unless you are a first-year, PhD student or graduate assistant. The worst mistake is leaving a good bike outside all winter, where salt and thieves both get to it.
```

## What winter riding in Ithaca is like

Ithaca's problem is not the cold alone. It is hills, ice and snow piled where a bike lane should be. Cornell's campus has [more than 1,000 bike parking spots, about 7 miles of bike lanes and 2 miles of shared paths](https://cornellsun.com/article/2025/11/guest-room-from-bikes-to-buses-how-to-sustainably-navigate-campus-this-fall), according to Cornell's assistant director of transportation in a 2025 opinion column.

The steepest risk is downhill on ice. Climbs are slow but safe; descents are where a slide happens. Students in a [2022 Sun story](https://cornellsun.com/2022/11/21/dont-let-it-snow-students-express-concerns-over-driving-conditions-as-temperatures-fall/) worried about icy hills and about snowbanks piling up around a car parked on a Collegetown street. That story was about driving, but the same hills and the same snowbanks face a cyclist.

On campus, judging the conditions is a rule, not only advice. Cornell's [2023-24 traffic and parking regulations](https://fcs.cornell.edu/sites/default/files/2023-12/Control%20of%20Traffic%20and%20Parking_complete-2023-24.pdf) bar riding a bike in a way that is unreasonable for the conditions.

In a [2007 Cornell Daily Sun story](https://cornellsun.com/2007/11/12/cyclists-persevere-through-wintry-weather), rider Mike Wine said good brakes keep stopping distance from suffering in bad weather, and rider Lois Chaplin advised warm, synthetic gear and a second bike for winter, because snow, ice and salt wear down a bike ridden year-round. The advice is from 2007, but the hills and the salt have not changed.

A 2023 Sun story on coping with Ithaca winters carries two tips from Taylor Brown '25: [avoid the Slope while it is snowing, and stay off metal bridge panels](https://www.cornellsun.com/article/2023/01/upperclassman-cornellians-share-how-they-cope-with-harsh-ithaca-winters). The tips were for getting around campus on foot, and they apply even more on two wheels. On a bike, Libe Slope's walkways are off limits in every season: Cornell's [regulations](https://fcs.cornell.edu/sites/default/files/2023-12/Control%20of%20Traffic%20and%20Parking_complete-2023-24.pdf) ban riding on them.

## Who clears what, and when

Cornell's [Snow Removal page](https://fcs.cornell.edu/services/repair-maintenance/snow-removal) says Grounds clears roads, parking lots, steps and sidewalks. It covers 15 miles of roads, 61 miles of walkways and 114 acres of parking lots, and response times vary with the rate of snowfall and the status of priority roads and walkways. When snow falls overnight, crews start plowing roads and parking lots at 2 a.m., and the sidewalk and stairway crews start around 4 a.m. The main areas take about four hours; in wet, heavy snow, clearing everything can take seven hours or more ([Cornellians, 2025](https://alumni.cornell.edu/cornellians/heat-plow-campus)).

Cornell's campus master plan, [approved by the trustees in March 2008](https://fcs.cornell.edu/cornell-master-plan-ithaca-campus-2), says [on-street bike lanes should be cleared of snow](https://fcs.cornell.edu/sites/default/files/masterplan/transportation_circulation.pdf) and shared walk-and-bike paths kept clear in winter. It is a long-range plan, meant to guide campus development over 30 to 60 years.

On City of Ithaca streets, plow crews first clear travel lanes wide enough for emergency vehicles, starting with the busiest roads. Steep roads are also a priority, and small residential streets come last. Plows push snow from the center of the road out toward the curb, and after back-to-back storms in a cold spell, clearing out to the curb can take several weeks. Salt loses effectiveness below 20°F ([City of Ithaca DPW](https://cityofithacany.gov/DocumentCenter/View/12559)).

## How to set up for a winter of riding

```figure

  - {label: "Check the brakes before the first snow"}
  - {label: "Pick a bike you can let the salt wear down"}
  - {label: "Find the TCAT route that climbs your hill"}
  - {label: "Plan indoor storage: a bike room or written permission"}
```

1. **Register it.** Registration with Cornell is [free](https://fcs.cornell.edu/departments/transportation-delivery-services/alternative-transportation-options/cycling-campus). The page urges it for bikes, e-bikes and scooters.
2. **Lock to a rack.** Park only at designated racks and lock frame and wheel. Bikes locked to railings, signposts, trees or poles can be impounded, per the [same page](https://fcs.cornell.edu/departments/transportation-delivery-services/alternative-transportation-options/cycling-campus). A snow-covered rack does not change that.
3. **Brakes and gear.** Riders in the [2007 Sun story](https://cornellsun.com/2007/11/12/cyclists-persevere-through-wintry-weather) stressed good brakes and winter gear. Our advice: ask a local bike shop about winter tires before the first snow.
4. **A bike for the salt.** A rider in the same story advised a second bike for winter because salt wears a year-round bike. Our advice: if you own one good bike, store it and ride an older one.
5. **Store it indoors, legally.** Cornell housing requires [external racks or sprinklered bike rooms](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/housing-details/rates-amenities-licenses/bicycle-registration-storage) for bike storage. In other campus buildings, indoor storage needs [written permission from the building coordinator](https://fcs.cornell.edu/departments/transportation-delivery-services/alternative-transportation-options/cycling-campus).
6. **E-bike batteries.** Cornell's [guidance document](https://fcs.cornell.edu/sites/default/files/2025-02/Micro-Mobility%20E-Bikes_Litium-Ion%20Batteries%20Guidance%20Document%20v.2%20final%2002.09.2024.pdf) says not to charge e-bike or e-scooter batteries in residential rooms, offices or academic buildings. Cold days do not change that. An e-bike is one with a motor of [no more than 750 W, no wider than 36 inches, with working pedals](https://fcs.cornell.edu/departments/transportation-delivery-services/sustainable-transportation-options/cycling-campus).

Path rules hold in winter too. Parts of the Botanic Gardens and Six Mile Gorge are [closed to bikes](https://fcs.cornell.edu/departments/transportation-delivery-services/sustainable-transportation-options/cycling-campus).

## Using TCAT as your winter backup

[Every TCAT bus has a front bike rack at no extra fare](https://cornellsun.com/article/2025/11/guest-room-from-bikes-to-buses-how-to-sustainably-navigate-campus-this-fall). That makes a split commute possible: ride down when the road is clear, then load the bike and ride the bus up.

What the bus costs depends on who you are. Per [Cornell Transportation Services](https://fcs.cornell.edu/departments/transportation-delivery-services/sustainable-transportation-options/bus-services-privileges-passes), new first-years ride free at all hours. PhD students and graduate assistants get a free OmniRide pass. After first year, other students ride free after 6 p.m. on weekdays and all day Saturday and Sunday. Otherwise it is $1.50 a ride, or $200 a year for OmniRide.

Snow changes the bus routes too. TCAT uses snow routes chosen by dispatch; in a 2019 storm, [routes 51, 92 and 93 detoured around parts of University, Stewart and Thurston Avenues](https://www.cornellsun.com/article/2019/01/tcat-prepares-to-swerve-away-winter-storm-conditions). Riders were told to check Bus Tracker and the Transit or MyStop apps. At night, the Route 92 Blue Light bus runs [6:30 p.m. to 2:30 a.m., year-round](https://scl.cornell.edu/families/cornell-family-resource-hub/parking-transportation).

Example: a junior with no car lives in Collegetown and has a 10:10 a.m. class on a snowy Tuesday. The Sun puts the North Campus to Collegetown walk at [15 to 20 minutes each way](https://cornellsun.com/article/2025/10/embrace-your-inner-intellectual-go-on-a-midnight-walk), and the way back is uphill. The bus is $1.50 each way before 6 p.m., so $3.00 for the day. A 10-minute bikeshare trip is $4.00 before tax. On ice, walking wins; on bare pavement, the junior's own bike wins.

Example: a PhD student lives near the Commons, about 2 miles from campus. Mornings are downhill: ride if the pavement is bare. Evenings are a climb in the dark: put the bike on the front rack. The pass is free, so the split commute costs nothing.

## Ithaca Bikeshare in winter

Ithaca Bikeshare runs through the winter: the Ithaca Times reported in November 2024 that it offers [year-round, 24-hour access to its e-bikes](https://www.ithaca.com/news/ithaca/ithaca-moves-to-support-bikeshare-eyes-multi-entity-funding-model/article_1d8a2850-9c75-11ef-bbd2-ab6266bd0b59.html). [Ithaca Bikeshare](https://www.ithacabikeshare.org) charges $1.50 to start a ride plus $0.25 a minute plus tax, a $25 day pass or a $60 monthly plan. A trip that does not end at a hub adds $1.50. Our math: a 20-minute ride is $6.50 before tax.

In July 2023 the system had [85 e-bikes, 250 to 275 trips a day in good weather and no student discount](https://betterbikeshare.org/2023/07/10/ithaca-stands-by-bike-share/). Prices from 2023 Sun articles are out of date.

## Theft does not take the winter off

In fall 2024 campus police counted [51 bike and scooter thefts worth at least $41,177](https://cornellsun.com/2024/11/14/this-happens-all-the-time-campus-police-report-more-than-40000-in-bike-and-scooter-thefts-this-semester-so-far), with one arrest and four bikes recovered intact. The stories in that article:

| What was stolen | Where and how | The lesson |
|---|---|---|
| A $600 bike (Liv Licursi '25) | Collegetown, left without a lock for about ten minutes | Lock it every time |
| A $2,025 e-bike (Samantha Davies, PhD) | A rack, despite a cheap lock | Match the lock to the bike |
| A $400 bike (Reid Fleishman '25) | A rack at Carpenter | The owner now uses a cable, a U-lock and two AirTags |

Cornell's advice in the same article: register the bike and use a U-lock.

## Common mistakes

- **Riding downhill on fresh snow or ice.** The descent is where a slide happens. Walk the bike or take the bus instead; the bus costs $1.50 at most.
- **Riding on Libe Slope.** Cornell bans riding on its walkways all year, snow or not.
- **Locking to a railing because the rack is buried.** That is improper parking: a fine of up to $50, or $60 if paid after ten days, and Cornell can impound the bike at your expense ([regulations](https://fcs.cornell.edu/sites/default/files/2023-12/Control%20of%20Traffic%20and%20Parking_complete-2023-24.pdf)).
- **Riding your one good bike all winter.** Salt wears it down, per a 2007 rider; a $600 or $2,025 bike outside is also what thieves took in 2024.
- **Charging an e-bike in your room to keep the battery warm.** Cornell's guidance forbids charging in residential rooms, offices and academic buildings.
- **Counting on a free daytime bus after first year.** Upper-years without OmniRide pay $1.50 a ride before 6 p.m. on weekdays.

## FAQ

**Can I take my bike on the C2C bus home for break?** Only packed in a bike box, which [counts as one of your two suitcases](https://fcs.cornell.edu/departments/transportation-delivery-services/campus-campus-bus-service/c2c-policies-regulations).

**Can I store my bike in my dorm room over winter?** Cornell housing requires [external racks or sprinklered bike rooms](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/housing-details/rates-amenities-licenses/bicycle-registration-storage). Ask your building about its bike room.

**Who do I call about an icy path on campus?** Customer Service/EMCS at [607-255-5322](https://fcs.cornell.edu/services/repair-maintenance/snow-removal), for unsafe or slippery conditions anywhere on campus.

**Does the TCAT bike rack cost extra?** No. [Every TCAT bus has a front rack at no extra fare](https://cornellsun.com/article/2025/11/guest-room-from-bikes-to-buses-how-to-sustainably-navigate-campus-this-fall); you pay only the normal fare, if any.

**Are there showers for bike commuters?** At Johnson, [Sage Hall has showers at classroom level](https://business.cornell.edu/students/by-program/mba-students/living-in-ithaca/). Cornell Recreation lists [locker rooms with showers and day-use lockers](https://scl.cornell.edu/recreation/recreation/recreation-locations/restroom-changing-facilities) at Helen Newman Hall and Teagle Hall, and a single shower in the lower-level restrooms at Noyes Community Recreation Center.
