```glance
- Registration is free. Cornell's campus rules encourage it for bikes, e-bikes and scooters; Housing requires it, and bike room access comes after it.
- An e-bike on campus: motor of 750 W or less, 36 in wide or less, working pedals. An e-scooter: under 100 lb, top speed 20 mph.
- Never charge an e-bike or scooter battery in a dorm room, office or academic building. Residence hall bike rooms are where Housing allows it.
- Lock to a designated rack, frame and wheel. A bike on a railing, signpost, tree or pole can draw a fine of up to $50 and an impound at your expense.
- Fall 2024 saw 51 bike and scooter thefts worth at least $41,177; four bikes came back intact. Use a U-lock, and add a tracker.
- Every TCAT bus has a free front bike rack; C2C takes a bike only boxed, as one of your two suitcases.
```

Cornell lets you ride a bike, an e-bike of up to [750 W and 36 in wide, or an e-scooter under 100 lb that tops out at 20 mph](https://fcs.cornell.edu/departments/transportation-delivery-services/sustainable-transportation-options/cycling-campus), as long as you park at racks and never charge the battery in a room, office or academic building. Registration is [free and encouraged across campus](https://fcs.cornell.edu/departments/transportation-delivery-services/alternative-transportation-options/cycling-campus), and [required if you live in a residence hall](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/housing-details/rates-amenities-licenses/bicycle-registration-storage). The bigger risk is theft: [51 bikes and scooters worth at least $41,177](https://cornellsun.com/2024/11/14/this-happens-all-the-time-campus-police-report-more-than-40000-in-bike-and-scooter-thefts-this-semester-so-far) were stolen in fall 2024, so buy a U-lock before you buy anything else.

```reader "If you're riding an e-bike or e-scooter"
Check the machine against Cornell's limits first. An e-bike needs a motor of 750 W or less, a width of 36 in or less, working pedals and one of New York's three classes. A scooter must weigh under 100 lb and top out at 20 mph ([Cornell](https://fcs.cornell.edu/departments/transportation-delivery-services/sustainable-transportation-options/cycling-campus)). Never charge it in a residence room, office or academic building ([guidance](https://fcs.cornell.edu/sites/default/files/2025-02/Micro-Mobility%20E-Bikes_Litium-Ion%20Batteries%20Guidance%20Document%20v.2%20final%2002.09.2024.pdf)); if you live on campus, the residence hall bike room is the charging spot ([Housing](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/housing-details/rates-amenities-licenses/bicycle-registration-storage)). The costly mistake is a cheap lock: a $2,025 e-bike was taken from a campus rack in 2024 despite one ([Sun](https://cornellsun.com/2024/11/14/this-happens-all-the-time-campus-police-report-more-than-40000-in-bike-and-scooter-thefts-this-semester-so-far)). A gas moped or motor scooter is a vehicle instead: register the plate and buy the $120.01 motorcycle permit ([parking](https://fcs.cornell.edu/departments/transportation-delivery-services/parking-campus/parking-students)).
```

```reader "If you're a first-year in a residence hall"
Bring a U-lock and register the bike free before you arrive: Housing requires it, and the bike room access request comes after it ([Housing](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/housing-details/rates-amenities-licenses/bicycle-registration-storage)). Bikes go on outside racks or in your hall's bike room. Only a fold-up bike that does not block the way out may stay in your room, and an e-bike charges only in the bike room. You ride TCAT free at all hours until Aug 31 of next year ([bus passes](https://fcs.cornell.edu/departments/transportation-delivery-services/sustainable-transportation-options/bus-services-privileges-passes)), and every bus has a front rack, so you can ride down and bus back up. The mistake that costs most: locking to a railing when the rack is full, which can bring a fine of up to $50 and an impound.
```

```reader "If you're an upper-year without a car"
Your bike plus TCAT covers most trips, but the bus is free for you only after 6 p.m. on weekdays and all weekend; a daytime weekday ride is $1.50 ([bus passes](https://fcs.cornell.edu/departments/transportation-delivery-services/sustainable-transportation-options/bus-services-privileges-passes)). The front rack costs nothing extra. If you only ride now and then, Ithaca Bikeshare is $1.50 to start a ride plus $0.25 a minute, so a 20-minute ride is $6.50 before tax by our arithmetic ([Ithaca Bikeshare](https://www.ithacabikeshare.org)). Owning a bike pays off fast at that rate, provided it is not stolen: a $400 bike vanished from a Carpenter Hall rack in 2024 ([Sun](https://cornellsun.com/2024/11/14/this-happens-all-the-time-campus-police-report-more-than-40000-in-bike-and-scooter-thefts-this-semester-so-far)).
```

```reader "If you're a grad student"
PhD students and graduate assistants get a free TCAT OmniRide pass ([bus passes](https://fcs.cornell.edu/departments/transportation-delivery-services/sustainable-transportation-options/bus-services-privileges-passes)), so the bus rack works for you at any hour, including the climb from downtown. The charging ban covers offices and academic buildings, not only dorms, so a lab or office outlet is off limits for an e-bike battery ([guidance](https://fcs.cornell.edu/sites/default/files/2025-02/Micro-Mobility%20E-Bikes_Litium-Ion%20Batteries%20Guidance%20Document%20v.2%20final%2002.09.2024.pdf)). Keeping a bike inside a campus building needs the building coordinator's written permission. Johnson students can shower at Sage Hall, classroom level ([Johnson](https://business.cornell.edu/students/by-program/mba-students/living-in-ithaca/)). The PhD student in the 2024 theft story lost a $2,025 e-bike.
```

```reader "If you're renting off campus"
Register the bike free with Cornell and with your city or town too; Cornell's page urges both ([Cornell](https://fcs.cornell.edu/departments/transportation-delivery-services/alternative-transportation-options/cycling-campus)). Collegetown is where a $600 bike disappeared after about ten minutes without a lock in 2024 ([Sun](https://cornellsun.com/2024/11/14/this-happens-all-the-time-campus-police-report-more-than-40000-in-bike-and-scooter-thefts-this-semester-so-far)), so lock even for a quick stop. On campus, the racks-only rule applies to you as much as to residents. From downtown, put the bike on a TCAT front rack for the hill ([Sun 2025](https://cornellsun.com/article/2025/11/guest-room-from-bikes-to-buses-how-to-sustainably-navigate-campus-this-fall)); you ride free after 6 p.m. on weekdays and all weekend, or pay $1.50.
```

## Do you have to register your bike at Cornell?

Yes if you live in a residence hall, and it is free and worth doing either way. Cornell's [traffic and parking regulations](https://fcs.cornell.edu/sites/default/files/2023-12/Control%20of%20Traffic%20and%20Parking_complete-2023-24.pdf) encourage every community member who parks a bike on campus to register it with the Transportation Office. The sticker goes on the frame, where it shows when the bike is locked. A registration does not transfer with the bike, and a stolen bike should be reported to the police right away. The [cycling page](https://fcs.cornell.edu/departments/transportation-delivery-services/alternative-transportation-options/cycling-campus) urges you to register bikes, e-bikes and scooters with Cornell Commuter and Parking Services and with your local municipality, free.

Two Cornell offices go further. Housing says [all devices must be registered](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/housing-details/rates-amenities-licenses/bicycle-registration-storage) through the FCS registration tool, and its bike room access request comes after registration. The [Weill Hall facilities page](https://facilities.research.cornell.edu/weill-hall-facilities-services/about-weill-hall/rules-for-cycling-electric-mobility-devices) says all bicycles must be registered with the university. Anyone breaking its rules risks losing building access or having the bike or scooter impounded. After the fall 2024 thefts, Cornell advised [registering and using a U-lock](https://cornellsun.com/2024/11/14/this-happens-all-the-time-campus-police-report-more-than-40000-in-bike-and-scooter-thefts-this-semester-so-far).

## What counts as an e-bike or e-scooter on campus

Cornell sets its own limits on [its e-mobility page](https://fcs.cornell.edu/departments/transportation-delivery-services/sustainable-transportation-options/cycling-campus). A machine outside them is not an e-bike or e-scooter by Cornell's definition.

| Device | Cornell's limits | Where it parks | Permit |
|---|---|---|---|
| Bicycle | None beyond the path rules | Designated bike racks | None; free registration urged |
| E-bike | Motor 750 W or less; 36 in wide or less; working pedals; one of New York's three e-bike classes | Designated bike racks | None; free registration urged |
| E-scooter | Under 100 lb; top speed 20 mph | Designated bike racks | None; free registration urged |
| Moped, motorcycle or motor scooter with a plate | A motor vehicle | Where the motorcycle permit allows | Plate registered on MyParking.Cornell.edu; motorcycle permit $120.01, prorated monthly |

The last row trips people up. Cornell requires students to [register plates for cars, motorcycles and scooters](https://fcs.cornell.edu/departments/transportation-delivery-services/parking-campus), and its motorcycle permit covers [motorcycles, scooters and mopeds at $120.01](https://fcs.cornell.edu/departments/transportation-delivery-services/parking-campus/parking-students). A stand-up e-scooter under Cornell's limits is the bike-rack kind.

Off campus, New York State law applies, as the [DMV](https://dmv.ny.gov/registration/electric-scooters-and-bicycles-and-other-unregistered-vehicles) explains it. E-bikes and e-scooters may ride on roads with a posted speed limit of 30 mph or less. They stay off sidewalks unless a local law allows them there, and a municipality can add its own limits on when, where and how they are used. Class 1 and Class 2 e-bikes stop assisting at 20 mph. Class 3 assists to 25 mph and is legal only in a city of one million or more people, so not in Ithaca. An e-scooter's motor may top out at 20 mph, but riding one faster than 15 mph is illegal. On campus roads, Cornell's [regulations](https://fcs.cornell.edu/sites/default/files/2023-12/Control%20of%20Traffic%20and%20Parking_complete-2023-24.pdf) put e-scooters and motorized skateboards in the bike lane or near the right-hand curb, never on a sidewalk.

## Where you can ride, and where you must walk

Campus paths are color-coded, per Cornell's [cycling rules](https://fcs.cornell.edu/departments/transportation-delivery-services/alternative-transportation-options/cycling/cycling-rules-regulations):

- **White:** a bike lane. Ride.
- **Yellow:** a shared walkway. Ride, but yield to people on foot.
- **Red or unmarked:** dismount and walk.

Cornell's [regulations](https://fcs.cornell.edu/sites/default/files/2023-12/Control%20of%20Traffic%20and%20Parking_complete-2023-24.pdf) add places where no one rides. The list covers the inside of any building, parking garages, stairs, landings and handrails, ramps built for people with disabilities, and every walkway on Libe Slope.

Parts of the Botanic Gardens and Six Mile Gorge are [closed to bikes](https://fcs.cornell.edu/departments/transportation-delivery-services/sustainable-transportation-options/cycling-campus). The campus network is small: more than 1,000 bike parking spots, about 7 miles of bike lanes and 2 miles of shared paths, per Cornell's assistant director of transportation in a [November 2025 Sun column](https://cornellsun.com/article/2025/11/guest-room-from-bikes-to-buses-how-to-sustainably-navigate-campus-this-fall).

## Parking, storage and charging

Lock only at designated bike racks. Cornell says a bike locked to [railings, signposts, trees or anything else that is not a bike rack can be impounded](https://fcs.cornell.edu/departments/transportation-delivery-services/alternative-transportation-options/cycling-campus). So can one that creates a hazard, blocks a path or looks abandoned. Lock the frame and a wheel, not the wheel alone.

The price of a bad lock-up is set in Cornell's [2023-24 traffic and parking regulations](https://fcs.cornell.edu/sites/default/files/2023-12/Control%20of%20Traffic%20and%20Parking_complete-2023-24.pdf):

```figure

title: Cornell bike parking fines, 2023-24 regulations

  - {label: "Improper bike parking, paid later", value: "$60"}
  - {label: "Fire lane, hydrant or other life safety zone, paid within 10 calendar days", value: "$50"}
  - {label: "Fire lane, hydrant or other life safety zone, paid later", value: "$60"}
```

The university can also impound or boot the bike at your expense. It may cut the lock to do so, and it does not pay for the lock. Impounded bikes are stored at Cornell University Police, and release takes proof of ownership and the release form from the designated campus office. The same fines and impound rules cover skateboards and similar wheeled devices, in the section of the regulations that also covers e-scooters. Questions go to Transportation Services at [607-255-4600](https://fcs.cornell.edu/departments/transportation-delivery-services/alternative-transportation-options/cycling-campus).

Indoors, the rules are tight:

- **Residence halls:** outside racks or the hall's bike room, which you request after registering. Nothing goes in your room except a fold-up bike that does not block the way out. A bike left in an alcove, hallway, stairwell or common area is confiscated and handed to Cornell Police. A bike still in a bike room or on a rack after Commencement is removed, lock cut if needed, and taken to the Lost and Found in Barton Hall. If it is registered, Cornell Police send a registered letter to the owner's permanent address ([Housing](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/housing-details/rates-amenities-licenses/bicycle-registration-storage)).
- **Other campus buildings:** only with the [written permission of the building coordinator](https://fcs.cornell.edu/departments/transportation-delivery-services/alternative-transportation-options/cycling-campus), and never in a hallway or stairwell ([regulations](https://fcs.cornell.edu/sites/default/files/2023-12/Control%20of%20Traffic%20and%20Parking_complete-2023-24.pdf)).
- **Batteries:** Cornell's lithium-ion guidance says not to charge e-bike or e-scooter batteries in [residential rooms, offices or academic buildings](https://fcs.cornell.edu/sites/default/files/2025-02/Micro-Mobility%20E-Bikes_Litium-Ion%20Batteries%20Guidance%20Document%20v.2%20final%2002.09.2024.pdf). It requires external bike racks or bike rooms protected by an automatic sprinkler system. Plug the charger straight into a wall outlet, never an extension cord or power strip, and unplug it once the battery is full. Housing's rule matches: e-bikes, e-scooters, e-skateboards and hoverboards may be stored or charged in a residence only in its bike room ([Housing](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/housing-details/rates-amenities-licenses/bicycle-registration-storage)). If a battery overheats, smokes, smells or changes shape, call Cornell Public Safety at [607-255-1111](https://fcs.cornell.edu/sites/default/files/2025-02/Micro-Mobility%20E-Bikes_Litium-Ion%20Batteries%20Guidance%20Document%20v.2%20final%2002.09.2024.pdf).

Ithaca Bikeshare's e-bikes follow the racks rule too. Cornell's [cycling rules page](https://fcs.cornell.edu/departments/transportation-delivery-services/alternative-transportation-options/cycling/cycling-rules-regulations) says Cornell requires that bikeshare bikes be parked at a bike rack. The service itself lets you leave a bike anywhere in its zone, per the [Sun](https://cornellsun.com/2023/01/24/ithaca-bikeshare-a-comprehensive-guide-to-ithacas-newest-transportation-option/), now for a $1.50 fee when the trip ends outside a hub ([Ithaca Bikeshare](https://www.ithacabikeshare.org)).

## How bikes get stolen at Cornell, and how to stop it

In fall 2024 campus police counted [51 bike and scooter thefts, at least $41,177 in losses, one arrest and four bikes recovered intact](https://cornellsun.com/2024/11/14/this-happens-all-the-time-campus-police-report-more-than-40000-in-bike-and-scooter-thefts-this-semester-so-far). The same Sun story told three students' losses:

```figure
  - {label: "E-bike on a campus rack, cheap lock", value: "$2,025"}
```

Liv Licursi '25 lost the $600 bike. Samantha Davies, a PhD student, lost the $2,025 e-bike. Reid Fleishman '25 lost the $400 bike and now locks with a cable, a U-lock and two AirTags.

The fix is cheap next to those numbers:

```figure
kind: steps
title: Theft-proofing a bike in your first week
rows:
  - {label: "Register the bike, e-bike or scooter free with Cornell and your town"}
  - {label: "Buy a U-lock; add a cable for the second wheel"}
  - {label: "Hide a tracker such as an AirTag on the frame"}
  - {label: "Lock frame and wheel to a designated rack, every stop"}
```

Example: a sophomore with a $600 bike stops at a Collegetown shop for ten minutes. Left without a lock, that is the exact setup that cost a student a $600 bike in 2024. Locked frame and wheel to a rack with a U-lock, registered, with a tracker on the frame, it is a harder target, and a findable one.

## Bikes on the bus, and bringing a bike home

Every TCAT bus carries a [front bike rack at no extra fare](https://tompkinscountyny.gov/files/assets/county/v/1/itctc/documents/ithacatompkinsbikemap_2024sm.pdf). The usual use: ride downhill, then put the bike on the bus for the climb back. What the ride costs depends on who you are, per Cornell's [bus pass page](https://fcs.cornell.edu/departments/transportation-delivery-services/sustainable-transportation-options/bus-services-privileges-passes):

| Rider | TCAT fare with a bike on the rack |
|---|---|
| First-year or new student | Free at all hours until Aug 31 of the next year (spring entrants: Dec 31) |
| PhD student or graduate assistant | Free (OmniRide pass) |
| Other students | Free after 6 p.m. weekdays and all weekend; otherwise $1.50, or $200 a year for OmniRide |

Going home on Cornell's Campus-to-Campus bus to New York City, a bike rides only [in a bike box, counted as one suitcase](https://fcs.cornell.edu/departments/transportation-delivery-services/campus-campus-bus-service/c2c-policies-regulations). Each passenger gets two suitcases below deck and two small carry-ons, so a boxed bike leaves room for one bag.

## E-bike help and repairs

For repairs, Cornell's cycling page [points riders to a local bike shop](https://fcs.cornell.edu/departments/transportation-delivery-services/alternative-transportation-options/cycling-campus) to get the bike adjusted so it is safe and fits. Its battery guidance sends e-bike and scooter repairs to a [qualified professional](https://fcs.cornell.edu/sites/default/files/2025-02/Micro-Mobility%20E-Bikes_Litium-Ion%20Batteries%20Guidance%20Document%20v.2%20final%2002.09.2024.pdf). Bike Walk Tompkins runs [e-bike classes at Swamp City Community Cycles, 803 Cascadilla St](https://www.bikewalktompkins.org/e-bikes); you must pre-register. For a feel of a legal e-bike before buying, Ithaca Bikeshare's fleet is Class 1 pedal-assist with a top speed of about 16 mph, per the same page.

## Common mistakes

- **A cheap lock, or none.** The 2024 losses ran from $400 to $2,025 per bike ([Sun](https://cornellsun.com/2024/11/14/this-happens-all-the-time-campus-police-report-more-than-40000-in-bike-and-scooter-thefts-this-semester-so-far)).
- **"Just ten minutes" without a lock.** That is how the $600 Collegetown bike went.
- **Locking to a railing, signpost, tree or pole.** A fine of up to $50, $60 after ten days, and the bike can be impounded or booted at your expense ([regulations](https://fcs.cornell.edu/sites/default/files/2023-12/Control%20of%20Traffic%20and%20Parking_complete-2023-24.pdf)).
- **Charging a battery in your room, office or lab.** It breaks Cornell's battery guidance and Housing's rules; the bike room is the place.
- **Skipping registration.** Only four of 51 stolen bikes came back intact in fall 2024, Cornell's advice afterward was to register, and Housing requires it anyway.
- **Riding red or unmarked paths.** Cornell's rule there is dismount.
- **Bringing an e-bike over 750 W.** Cornell does not count it as an e-bike.

## FAQ

**Can I keep my bike in my dorm room?** Only a fold-up bike that does not block the way out. Everything else goes on an outside rack or in the hall's bike room, requested after you register ([Housing](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/housing-details/rates-amenities-licenses/bicycle-registration-storage)). In other campus buildings you need the building coordinator's written permission.

**Is Ithaca Bikeshare free for Cornell students?** No. Its price page lists $1.50 to start a ride plus $0.25 a minute, $25 a day or $60 a month, all plus tax. A trip that does not end at a hub costs $1.50 more ([Ithaca Bikeshare](https://www.ithacabikeshare.org)). A reduced-cost option, Easy Access, is for GO ITHACA members with income at or below 150% of the federal poverty guideline.

**Can I ride in the snow?** The campus rule is to ride in a way that is reasonable for the conditions, and never on a walkway on Libe Slope ([regulations](https://fcs.cornell.edu/sites/default/files/2023-12/Control%20of%20Traffic%20and%20Parking_complete-2023-24.pdf)). A 2023 student tip was to avoid the Slope while it is snowing and stay off metal bridge panels ([Sun 2023](https://www.cornellsun.com/article/2023/01/upperclassman-cornellians-share-how-they-cope-with-harsh-ithaca-winters)); the winter guide below has more.

**Is there anywhere to shower after riding in?** Johnson lists showers at Sage Hall, classroom level ([Johnson](https://business.cornell.edu/students/by-program/mba-students/living-in-ithaca/)). Cornell Recreation's locker rooms at Helen Newman Hall and Teagle Hall have showers and day-use lockers. Noyes Community Recreation Center has a single shower in its lower-level restrooms ([Recreation](https://scl.cornell.edu/recreation/recreation/recreation-locations/restroom-changing-facilities)).

**Who do I call about an impounded bike?** Impounded bikes are stored at Cornell University Police, and release takes proof of ownership and the release form ([regulations](https://fcs.cornell.edu/sites/default/files/2023-12/Control%20of%20Traffic%20and%20Parking_complete-2023-24.pdf)). Transportation Services is at 607-255-4600 ([Cornell](https://fcs.cornell.edu/departments/transportation-delivery-services/alternative-transportation-options/cycling-campus)).
