```glance
- Cornell's aid is need-based only: no merit, athletic or talent scholarships, for transfers or anyone else.
- Cornell meets 100% of demonstrated need for admitted transfers; US admission is need-blind, international is need-aware.
- File the CSS Profile (code 2098) and FAFSA (002711) by March 15, 2027; New York residents add HESC (0174 or 0175).
- 2026-27: family income up to $125,000 with typical assets, grants cover tuition; up to $75,000, the full cost of attendance.
- An Aid Estimate comes soon after admission; official offers go out tentatively in early April 2027.
- International applicants who skip the March 15 deadline can never get Cornell aid as undergraduates.
```

Yes, but only need-based aid: Cornell meets [100% of demonstrated need for admitted transfers](https://admissions.cornell.edu/apply/transfer-applicants) with grants, work-study and reasonable loans, and it offers [no merit, athletic or talent aid](https://finaid.cornell.edu/first-year-and-transfer-students-international). For 2026-27, grants cover tuition for families earning up to $125,000 with typical assets, and the [full cost of attendance up to $75,000](https://news.cornell.edu/stories/2026/03/board-trustees-approves-2026-27-budget-parameters). Fall 2027 transfers file the CSS Profile and FAFSA by [March 15, 2027](https://finaid.cornell.edu/apply-aid/prospective-students-or-first-time-aid-applicants).

```reader "If you're a sophomore transfer from a four-year college"
Cornell runs its own need analysis from your CSS Profile and FAFSA. Transfers follow the same aid process and policies as first-years, per CALS (https://cals.cornell.edu/education/admissions/undergraduate-admissions/transfer-applicants). File the CSS Profile (2098) and FAFSA (002711) by March 15, 2027, the same day your application is due, not April 15 with your transcripts. The required on-campus year bills $96,318 for a hall double with the Unlimited plan at endowed tuition in 2026-27 (https://finaid.cornell.edu/cost-attend), so the aid answer decides the move. The costly mistake is filing late: your estimate then arrives after you must commit.
```

```reader "If you're transferring on a tight budget"
Apply for aid even if Cornell looks impossible. Under $75,000 family income with typical assets, grants, scholarships and work-study cover the full cost of attendance in 2026-27 (https://news.cornell.edu/stories/2026/03/board-trustees-approves-2026-27-budget-parameters). The $85 application fee is waived automatically if it is a burden and you plan to apply for aid (https://admissions.cornell.edu/apply/transfer-applicants). The CSS Profile fee is waived automatically up to $100,000 adjusted gross income (https://finaid.cornell.edu/apply-aid/prospective-students-or-first-time-aid-applicants). Undocumented applicants who graduated from a US high school can get Cornell aid, though not federal aid. Opportunity program (EOP and HEOP) applicants add one step, explained below.
```

```reader "If you're the parent of a transfer"
You file the CSS Profile and FAFSA with your student by March 15, 2027 (https://finaid.cornell.edu/apply-aid/prospective-students-or-first-time-aid-applicants). Expect a Cornell Aid Estimate soon after admission, then the official offer. Bills come through Cashnet, and only parents the student invites see them; fall tuition is billed in early July (https://scl.cornell.edu/get-involved/tatkon-center-new-students/new-students-cornell/transfer/finances). Do not wait for merit money: Cornell gives none. If your income drops after the offer, appeal through finaid@cornell.edu.
```

```reader "If you're an international transfer"
Aid is now or never. File the CSS Profile (2098) by March 15, 2027; there is generally no fee waiver (https://finaid.cornell.edu/first-year-and-transfer-students-international). If you do not apply by the deadline, you cannot receive undergraduate aid later (https://admissions.cornell.edu/how-to-apply/transfer-international-applicants). Asking for aid makes your admission need-aware, and Cornell says it meets 100% of need for admitted students who applied. Aid above tuition and fees is taxable, with 14% withheld unless a tax treaty applies. You pay endowed tuition, $73,946 in 2026-27, at any college.
```

```reader "If you're a New York resident aiming at a contract college"
File with HESC at hesc.ny.gov as well as the CSS Profile and FAFSA: code 0174 for a contract college, 0175 for an endowed one (https://finaid.cornell.edu/apply-aid/prospective-students-or-first-time-aid-applicants). At CALS, Human Ecology, ILR, Brooks or Dyson, your 2026-27 tuition is $49,816, not $73,946 (https://news.cornell.edu/stories/2026/03/board-trustees-approves-2026-27-budget-parameters). HEOP applicants to those five colleges email stateprograms@cornell.edu instead of sending the certification form (https://oadi.cornell.edu/node/50). Skipping HESC means state aid is not considered.
```

```reader "If you're transferring from a community college"
Do not rule Cornell out on price before you see an estimate. Four community college graduates Cornell profiled in 2024 had assumed it was too expensive (https://www.news.cornell.edu/stories/2024/05/community-college-transfers-flourish-cornell). Under $125,000 family income with typical assets, grants cover tuition in 2026-27. In fall 2021, transfers who got Cornell need-based grants averaged $44,712 (https://irp.cornell.edu/wp-content/uploads/2021/08/Profile2021-Fall-Transfers.pdf). Your application fee and CSS fee can both be waived. File by March 15, 2027 like everyone else.
```

## Merit scholarships: there are none

Cornell does not award merit, athletic or talent aid. Its [international aid page](https://finaid.cornell.edu/first-year-and-transfer-students-international) says so directly, and every grant Cornell gives is based on need. Transfers get the same deal as first-years: CALS says transfers follow the [same aid process and policies](https://cals.cornell.edu/education/admissions/undergraduate-admissions/transfer-applicants).

If a search for "Cornell transfer scholarship" shows a $33,000 transfer scholarship, you are on the wrong school's site. That award belongs to [Cornell College in Mount Vernon, Iowa](https://www.cornellcollege.edu/financial-assistance/scholarships-awards/transfer-scholarships.shtml). So does FAFSA code 001856. Cornell University's FAFSA code is 002711.

Cornell's aid page for US transfers is titled ["First-Year and Transfer Students, U.S."](https://finaid.cornell.edu/apply-aid/prospective-students-or-first-time-aid-applicants).

## What need-based aid covers in 2026-27

Cornell meets [100% of demonstrated need](https://admissions.cornell.edu/apply/transfer-applicants) with a mix of grants, work-study and reasonable loans. For US applicants, admission is need-blind: asking for aid does not affect the decision. For international applicants it is [need-aware](https://admissions.cornell.edu/how-to-apply/transfer-international-applicants).

The trustees set two income lines for 2026-27, both for families [with typical assets](https://news.cornell.edu/stories/2026/03/board-trustees-approves-2026-27-budget-parameters):

| Family income, typical assets | What Cornell's aid covers (2026-27) |
|---|---|
| Up to $75,000 | Full cost of attendance, through grants, scholarships and work-study |
| Up to $125,000 | Tuition, through grants |
| Above $125,000 | Your demonstrated need, as Cornell calculates it |

The same story says nearly 50% of undergraduates, more than 16,000 in all by its count, receive grant aid. The [Common Data Set for 2025-26](https://irp.cornell.edu/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/Cornell-Common-Data-Set-2025-26.pdf) shows 8,498 full-time undergraduates applied for need-based aid, 7,489 had need, and all 7,489 got aid. The average need-based grant was $63,827, and the average need-based loan $3,512.

Those are prices before aid:

| 2026-27, one year | Endowed college or non-NY resident | NY resident at a contract college |
|---|---|---|
| Tuition | $73,946 | $49,816 |
| Billed: tuition, fees, double room, Unlimited plan | $96,318 | $72,188 |
| Full cost of attendance (adds books and personal) | $99,734 | $75,604 |

Tuition figures come from the [trustees' 2026-27 budget story](https://news.cornell.edu/stories/2026/03/board-trustees-approves-2026-27-budget-parameters); the billed and total figures from Cornell's [cost of attendance page](https://finaid.cornell.edu/cost-attend). Fall 2027 entrants pay 2027-28 prices, which Cornell has not yet announced.

## How to apply: forms, codes and dates

| Form | Who files | Code | Due for fall 2027 |
|---|---|---|---|
| CSS Profile | All aid applicants, US and international | 2098 | March 15, 2027 |
| FAFSA | US applicants | 002711 | March 15, 2027 |
| HESC (hesc.ny.gov) | New York residents | 0174 contract college, 0175 endowed | With the others |
| IDOC uploads | Anyone Cornell asks | none | Only when requested |

All codes and dates are from the [US aid page](https://finaid.cornell.edu/apply-aid/prospective-students-or-first-time-aid-applicants) and the [international aid page](https://finaid.cornell.edu/first-year-and-transfer-students-international). The FAFSA opened October 1, 2026, and Cornell asks you to submit as soon as possible.

```figure
kind: timeline
title: Aid timeline for fall 2027 transfers
rows:
  - {label: FAFSA opens; file the CSS Profile early, date: "October 1, 2026"}
  - {label: CSS Profile, FAFSA and HESC due, date: "March 15, 2027"}
  - {label: Official aid offers go out (tentative), date: "Early April 2027"}
  - {label: Other application materials due, date: "April 15, 2027"}
  - {label: Rolling admission decisions, date: "Late April to June 2027"}
  - {label: Fall tuition billed, date: "Early July 2027"}
```

1. **File the CSS Profile and FAFSA by March 15.** The application deadline is the same day. The April 15 deadline is only for transcripts and reports, not aid.
2. **Answer document requests fast.** Upload to IDOC only what Cornell asks for.
3. **Read the Cornell Aid Estimate.** It arrives soon after admission. Cornell asks you to answer requests quickly so the estimate arrives before your deposit deadline.
4. **Read the official offer.** Offers go out tentatively in early April. Transfer decisions roll from late April through June, per the [admissions page](https://admissions.cornell.edu/apply/transfer-applicants), so expect your estimate soon after your decision.
5. **Appeal if your finances changed.** Email finaid@cornell.edu with your name and admissions ID.

## EOP and HEOP applicants

The extra step depends on your college, per the [Office of Academic Discovery and Impact](https://oadi.cornell.edu/node/50). You file the same CSS Profile and FAFSA, send tax documents through IDOC, and add a non-filer statement or non-custodial waiver where one applies. Fall 2027 applicants submit 2025 financial documents.

- **AAP, Arts and Sciences, Engineering, or SC Johnson with the Nolan Hotel School:** submit the HEOP Transfer Certification form.
- **CALS, Human Ecology, ILR, Brooks or Dyson:** email stateprograms@cornell.edu instead.

The office is in Suite 200 of the Computing and Communications Center, 235 Garden Ave. Call 607.255.3841 or 607.255.6384, weekdays 8 to 4:30.

## Costs aid does not touch

- **Health insurance.** The Student Health Plan sits outside the cost of attendance. A fall 2026 entrant paid [$1,705 for fall and $2,046 for spring](https://studenthealthbenefits.cornell.edu/shp-costs-billing-important-dates/shp-costs-dates-2026-27), unless they waived it with comparable coverage.
- **Taxes on grants.** Grant aid above tuition and mandatory fees is taxable and reported on a 1098-T, per the [US aid page](https://finaid.cornell.edu/apply-aid/prospective-students-or-first-time-aid-applicants). International students have 14% withheld unless a treaty applies.
- **Transportation.** Cornell's [cost of attendance](https://finaid.cornell.edu/cost-attend) lists it as "varies."

One cost aid does cover: the Unlimited meal plan, which is [fully covered by financial aid for eligible students](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/dining/meal-plans-rates/undergraduate-meal-plans).

## What went wrong for transfers before

In spring 2021, admitted first-years and transfers had to commit by May 3, before many aid offers arrived, [the Cornell Daily Sun reported](https://cornellsun.com/2021/05/14/admitted-students-await-financial-aid-offers-weeks-after-commitment-deadlines/). Some students waited on the phone for more than two hours. One student described her offer as mostly loans. The vice provost for enrollment pointed to a higher volume of students and changed finances in the pandemic, and late applicants were offered extensions. The lesson holds: file early so your estimate lands before you commit.

In February 2017 the Sun reported that a Cornell working group had listed [need-aware transfer admission](https://cornellsun.com/2017/02/26/cornell-considering-need-aware-admissions-for-transfers-additional-controversial-measures/) among its contingency options. A transfer on aid called it a "terrible policy." Cornell's current page says US admission is need-blind.

## Common mistakes

- **Trusting Iowa search results.** Cornell College's $33,000 scholarship and FAFSA code 001856 belong to a different school in Iowa. Cornell University's code is 002711.
- **Filing aid forms with the April 15 materials.** Aid is due March 15. The 2021 commitment crunch shows what a late offer costs: a decision made without your number.
- **International applicants skipping the CSS Profile.** Miss March 15 and Cornell aid is closed for your whole undergraduate career.
- **New Yorkers skipping HESC.** State aid is not considered without it.
- **Not waiving the health plan.** It bills $1,705 for a first fall if you have comparable coverage and do not waive.
- **Not appealing after a change.** If your family's finances change after the offer, appeal through finaid@cornell.edu with your name and admissions ID.

## FAQ

**Is Cornell need-blind for transfers?** For US citizens, permanent residents and undocumented applicants, yes; for international applicants, no ([admissions](https://admissions.cornell.edu/apply/transfer-applicants)).

**Can undocumented transfers get aid?** Yes, Cornell aid if you graduated from a US high school, but not federal aid ([admissions](https://admissions.cornell.edu/apply/transfer-applicants)).

**Does my current college's aid package carry over?** Cornell sets your aid from your own CSS Profile and FAFSA, under the same process as first-years ([CALS](https://cals.cornell.edu/education/admissions/undergraduate-admissions/transfer-applicants)).

**Will I have to take loans?** Cornell meets need with grants, work-study and reasonable loans. The 2025-26 average need-based loan was $3,512 across undergraduates ([CDS](https://irp.cornell.edu/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/Cornell-Common-Data-Set-2025-26.pdf)).

**Can I pay the bill in installments?** Yes, through the Cornell Installment Plan ([Tatkon finances page](https://scl.cornell.edu/get-involved/tatkon-center-new-students/new-students-cornell/transfer/finances)).
