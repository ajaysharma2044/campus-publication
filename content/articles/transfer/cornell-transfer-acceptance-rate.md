```glance
- Fall 2025: 7,381 transfer applicants, 864 admitted (11.7%), 625 enrolled, per Cornell's Common Data Set.
- First-year applicants that fall were admitted at 8.4%, so transfer odds ran about 1.4 times higher.
- 72.3% of admitted transfers enrolled; transfers were 14.0% of the fall 2025 new class.
- Cornell publishes no transfer admit rate by college or major; in fall 2021, 36.1% of transfers entered CALS.
- Admitted transfers "generally" have a 3.3 GPA or better; your college's own floor matters more.
- For fall 2027: apply by March 15, 2027; decisions roll from late April through June.
```

Cornell admitted 864 of 7,381 transfer applicants for fall 2025, an acceptance rate of 11.7%, and 625 of them enrolled, per the [Common Data Set 2025-26](https://irp.cornell.edu/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/Cornell-Common-Data-Set-2025-26.pdf). That is harder than most colleges and easier than Cornell's own first-year round, which admitted 8.4% that fall. Your real odds depend on the college you apply to, the courses you have taken for it and your grades.

```reader "If you're a sophomore transfer from a four-year college"
You are the typical applicant: in fall 2021, 578 of 674 incoming transfers came from four-year schools, per Cornell's [2021 transfer profile](https://irp.cornell.edu/wp-content/uploads/2021/08/Profile2021-Fall-Transfers.pdf). Apply in the spring of your first year; Cornell takes students with [two to four semesters done](https://faq.enrollment.cornell.edu/kb/article/330-when-should-i-apply-to-cornell-as-a-transfer/) and none in their first semester. The 11.7% rate is an average across all of Cornell's colleges, and you apply to [one college](https://admissions.cornell.edu/apply/transfer-applicants), usually one major. What moves your file is the target college's prerequisites, a GPA near or above its signal, and an essay about that program. The costly mistake is applying to a college whose required courses you have not taken: Engineering will not review a file missing its math, physics and chemistry block.
```

```reader "If you're a junior-entry transfer"
Cornell publishes no admit rate by class standing, but its own advice is blunt. The admissions FAQ [discourages transfer after the junior year at a four-year school](https://faq.enrollment.cornell.edu/kb/article/323-can-i-transfer-to-cornell-in-my-junior-year-from-another-college/) because most colleges cap transfer credit near 60. Some doors are shut: [CALS](https://cals.cornell.edu/education/admissions/undergraduate-admissions/transfer-applicants) bars anyone with more than four semesters at a four-year school. Engineering takes juniors only in their first semester of junior standing, per its [requirements page](https://www.duffield.cornell.edu/admissions/transfer-applicants/requirements). Check eligibility before you weigh the odds; an ineligible application has no odds at all.
```

```reader "If you're a community college transfer"
Cornell states [no preference by school type](https://faq.enrollment.cornell.edu/kb/article/325-does-cornell-prefer-transfer-applicants-from-certain-colleges-or-types-of-colleges/) and says about a quarter of its transfers come from community colleges. The only official count is lower: 96 of 674 fall 2021 transfers, 14.2%, in the [2021 profile](https://irp.cornell.edu/wp-content/uploads/2021/08/Profile2021-Fall-Transfers.pdf). The Chronicle reported in 2024 that Cornell admits [about 100 community college students a year](https://www.news.cornell.edu/stories/2024/05/community-college-transfers-flourish-cornell). The costly mistake is treating a pathway as a guarantee. The TC3 to CALS agreement of 2025 gives ["priority consideration"](https://cals.cornell.edu/news/2025/08/cornell-cals-and-tc3-establish-transfer-pathway-for-agriculture-students), not admission, so apply elsewhere too.
```

```reader "If you're an international transfer"
The 11.7% rate covers everyone, but your admission works differently. Cornell is need-blind for domestic applicants and need-aware for international ones, per the [transfer admissions page](https://admissions.cornell.edu/apply/transfer-applicants). If you might need aid, file the CSS Profile by March 15, 2027: Cornell's [international aid page](https://finaid.cornell.edu/first-year-and-transfer-students-international) says an applicant who skips that deadline cannot get undergraduate aid later. Skipping aid to look stronger closes the door for your whole degree. In fall 2021, 86 transfers (12.8%) were international, per the [2021 profile](https://irp.cornell.edu/wp-content/uploads/2021/08/Profile2021-Fall-Transfers.pdf).
```

```reader "If you're a parent of a transfer"
Plan as if admission is uncertain until late April to June, when [decisions roll out](https://admissions.cornell.edu/apply/transfer-applicants). About one applicant in 8.5 was admitted in fall 2025 (7,381 applied, 864 admitted, per the [Common Data Set](https://irp.cornell.edu/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/Cornell-Common-Data-Set-2025-26.pdf)). Your student should keep a backup: staying at the current school or another transfer offer. Aid is need-based only, and the [CSS Profile and FAFSA](https://finaid.cornell.edu/apply-aid/prospective-students-or-first-time-aid-applicants) are due March 15, 2027, with the application. The Common Data Set lists a reply date of July 1, so an offer leaves weeks, not months, to decide. Most families say yes: 72.3% of fall 2025 admits enrolled.
```

## Cornell transfer vs first-year admission, fall 2025

Both columns come from Cornell's [Common Data Set 2025-26](https://irp.cornell.edu/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/Cornell-Common-Data-Set-2025-26.pdf), census date October 15, 2025. The rates, yield and ratios are our math from its counts.

| Fall 2025 | Transfer | First-year |
|---|---|---|
| Applied | 7,381 | 72,523 |
| Admitted | 864 | 6,077 |
| Enrolled | 625 | 3,827 |
| Admit rate | 11.7% | 8.4% |
| Applicants per admit | 8.5 | 11.9 |
| Yield (enrolled / admitted) | 72.3% | 63.0% |

Transfer odds were about 1.4 times first-year odds (11.7 divided by 8.4). The yield stands out: nearly three in four admitted transfers came, against under two in three admitted first-years.

Transfers were 14.0% of the fall 2025 new class (625 of 4,452 new students). Cornell's housing office puts the share at ["almost 15%"](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/housing-details/apply-housing-campus/new-undergrad-application-process), which matches.

## The rate year by year, and which numbers to trust

Cornell's Common Data Set is Cornell's own report of the transfer admit rate. The older rows below are third-party estimates from admissions blogs, so read them as approximate.

| Fall | Applied | Admitted | Rate | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2025 | 7,381 | 864 | 11.7% | Cornell [CDS 2025-26](https://irp.cornell.edu/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/Cornell-Common-Data-Set-2025-26.pdf) |
| 2024 | 7,218 | 670 | 9.3% | Third-party: [lingoleap](https://lingoleap.ai/universities/cornell), [AdmissionSight](https://admissionsight.com/cornell-acceptance-rate/) |
| 2023 | 6,581 | 834 | 12.7% | Third-party: [College Transitions](https://www.collegetransitions.com/blog/cornell-transfer-acceptance-rate-requirements-application-deadlines/) |
| 2022 | | | 13.8% | Third-party: College Transitions |
| 2021 | | | 15.7% | Third-party: College Transitions |
| 2020 | | | 18% | Third-party: College Transitions |
| 2019 | | | 13% (17.09% on another site) | Third-party: College Transitions |

On the third-party figures, the rate fell 3.4 points in one year, from 12.7% in 2023 to 9.3% in 2024, then rose to 11.7% in 2025. Applicants grew from 6,581 to 7,381 between 2023 and 2025 (12.2%, our math), while admits rose by 30. The safe reading: about one in eight to one in ten, with applicants climbing. Quote a single "stable" rate and you will be wrong within a year.

Cornell publishes no admit rate by college or by major.

## Where transfers go: the college mix

No college-level admit rate is published, but where transfers land shows where the seats are. The newest official breakdown is the fall 2021 profile, built from July deposit data in a pandemic year.

| College | Fall 2021 transfers | Share (our math) |
|---|---|---|
| Agriculture and Life Sciences (CALS) | 243 | 36.1% |
| Industrial and Labor Relations (ILR) | 116 | 17.2% |
| Arts and Sciences | 102 | 15.1% |
| Human Ecology | 59 | 8.8% |
| Dyson (Applied Economics and Management) | 54 | 8.0% |
| Hotel Administration | 48 | 7.1% |
| Engineering | 37 | 5.5% |
| Architecture, Art and Planning | 15 | 2.2% |
| All colleges | 674 | 100% |

Source: [IRP 2021 Fall Transfers profile](https://irp.cornell.edu/wp-content/uploads/2021/08/Profile2021-Fall-Transfers.pdf). The four contract colleges (CALS, Dyson, Human Ecology, ILR) took 472 of 674, or 70.0%.

Two colleges say transfers are a large part of their students today. [CALS](https://cals.cornell.edu/education/admissions/undergraduate-admissions/transfer-applicants) says transfers are nearly one-third of its students. ILR's [fall 2026 guidelines](https://www.ilr.cornell.edu/sites/default/files-d8/2026-02/transfer-guidelines-fa-26-final.pdf) say about one-third of its graduates transferred in. A large share of seats is not the same as an easy admit, since more applicants aim there too.

Example: a sophomore choosing between ILR and Engineering. In 2021, ILR enrolled 116 transfers and Engineering 37. That suggests ILR has more seats, not better odds. Engineering also screens out any file missing its required math, physics and chemistry courses, per its [requirements page](https://www.duffield.cornell.edu/admissions/transfer-applicants/requirements). The applicant who has those courses and wants engineering should apply to Engineering; seat counts should not pick a major.

## What decides your odds more than the rate

Cornell's [admissions FAQ](https://faq.enrollment.cornell.edu/kb/article/325-does-cornell-prefer-transfer-applicants-from-certain-colleges-or-types-of-colleges/) names what matters most: coursework and essays. That means the Writing Supplement showing interest in the program, grades at your current school and professor recommendations. Extracurriculars and the high school record are read too. No SAT or ACT is needed, per [FAQ 322](https://faq.enrollment.cornell.edu/kb/article/322-are-standardized-tests-required-for-transfer-applicants/).

Admitted transfers are "generally" at 3.3 or better, per the same FAQ, which calls the process "selective and highly competitive." Each college sets its own signal:

| College | GPA signal | Kind |
|---|---|---|
| CALS | 3.0 (3.5 for Biological Sciences) | Minimum |
| ILR | 3.4 | Typical of admits |
| Human Ecology | 3.5 | Competitive |
| Engineering | No GPA floor published | Required courses decide review |
| All colleges | 3.3 | "Generally," per the FAQ |

Sources: [CALS](https://cals.cornell.edu/education/admissions/undergraduate-admissions/transfer-applicants), [ILR guidelines](https://www.ilr.cornell.edu/sites/default/files-d8/2026-02/transfer-guidelines-fa-26-final.pdf), [Human Ecology](https://www.human.cornell.edu/admissions/transfer), [Engineering](https://www.duffield.cornell.edu/admissions/transfer-applicants/requirements). Every college's number is on our [GPA by college page](/transfer/cornell-transfer-gpa-requirements-by-college/).

Eligibility comes before odds. You need at least 12 semester hours of college credit after high school, or full-time enrollment elsewhere, and you cannot already hold a bachelor's degree, per the [admissions page](https://admissions.cornell.edu/apply/transfer-applicants). First-year applicants denied in Regular Decision [may reapply as transfers](https://admissions.cornell.edu/resources/transfer-option-instructions) in a later cycle.

## Common mistakes

- **Quoting an old rate.** The 18% (2020) and 15.7% (2021) figures still circulate. Planning on them overstates your odds by a third to a half against the 11.7% of fall 2025.
- **Reading a big college as an easy college.** CALS took 36.1% of fall 2021 transfers, but it has a 3.0 floor and a 3.5 floor for Biological Sciences. Applying below the floor wastes the [$85 fee](https://admissions.cornell.edu/apply/transfer-applicants).
- **Applying without the prerequisites.** Engineering does not review a file missing its required courses. That is a lost cycle, a full year.
- **Treating a pathway as admission.** TC3's agreement gives priority consideration, and the Transfer Option is an invitation to apply. A student with no backup applications can end up with nothing for fall.
- **Waiting until junior year.** Most colleges cap transfer credit near 60; a student arriving with 90 credits loses 30 and about a year of tuition.
- **Planning for spring.** Cornell takes external transfers for fall only, so a January plan costs a full cycle.

## FAQ

**Is it easier to transfer to Cornell than to get in as a first-year?** On the fall 2025 numbers, yes: 11.7% against 8.4%, per the [Common Data Set](https://irp.cornell.edu/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/Cornell-Common-Data-Set-2025-26.pdf). The transfer pool is also smaller and self-selected, so it is not an easy back door.

**Does applying for financial aid hurt my chances?** Not for domestic applicants: admission is need-blind for them and need-aware for international applicants, per the [admissions page](https://admissions.cornell.edu/apply/transfer-applicants). Cornell meets 100% of demonstrated need for those it admits.

**Which Cornell college is easiest to transfer into?** Cornell publishes no admit rate by college. The fall 2021 profile shows CALS, ILR and Arts and Sciences enrolled the most transfers, which reflects seats, not odds.

**Do I need test scores?** No. Transfer applicants do not need the SAT or ACT, per [FAQ 322](https://faq.enrollment.cornell.edu/kb/article/322-are-standardized-tests-required-for-transfer-applicants/).

**When will I hear back?** Decisions roll from late April through June, per the [admissions page](https://admissions.cornell.edu/apply/transfer-applicants). Our [deadlines page](/transfer/cornell-transfer-deadlines-decision-dates/) has every date for fall 2027.
