```glance
- Apply to one of two Brooks majors: Public Policy or Health Care Policy, fall entry only.
- Required before you enroll: 15 letter-graded credits (2 writing courses, intro micro, statistics or calculus, intro sociology).
- No GPA minimum; a 3.5 or higher with As and B+s in those courses makes you more competitive.
- Deadlines: March 15, 2027 for the application and $85 fee; April 15, 2027 for everything else.
- Brooks accepts up to 60 transfer credits and requires four semesters in residence in Ithaca.
- No articulation agreements: every applicant, including community college students, applies the same way.
```

To transfer into Cornell's Brooks School of Public Policy, you apply through the Transfer Common App to the Public Policy or Health Care Policy major for fall 2027, with the application due [March 15, 2027](https://admissions.cornell.edu/apply/transfer-applicants). Before you enroll you need [15 credits of letter-graded prep](https://publicpolicy.cornell.edu/undergraduate/apply/transfer-applicants/external-transfers/): two writing courses, introductory microeconomics, statistics or calculus, and introductory sociology. Brooks sets no GPA floor, but a 3.5 or higher with As and B+s in those courses raises your odds.

```reader "If you're entering as a junior"
Finish the four required courses plus the starred ones Brooks [strongly recommends for junior entrants](https://publicpolicy.cornell.edu/undergraduate/apply/transfer-applicants/external-transfers/): intermediate microeconomics, statistics and a second quantitative course, done by the end of sophomore year. Count your credits first. Brooks takes at most 60 and requires four semesters in Ithaca, so a student arriving with 90 credits loses 30 and spends ten semesters in college. Cornell's admissions office [discourages junior-year transfer](https://faq.enrollment.cornell.edu/kb/article/323-can-i-transfer-to-cornell-in-my-junior-year-from-another-college/) from four-year schools for that reason. Juniors are [exempt from the on-campus housing year](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/campus-housing/new-transfer-undergraduates), so start a lease search the day you are admitted.
```

```reader "If you're at a community college"
Brooks has [no articulation agreements](https://publicpolicy.cornell.edu/undergraduate/apply/transfer-applicants/external-transfers/) with any college, so there is no pathway to wait for: you apply in the open pool. That pool does not penalize you. Cornell has [no preference by type of school](https://faq.enrollment.cornell.edu/kb/article/325-does-cornell-prefer-transfer-applicants-from-certain-colleges-or-types-of-colleges/), and a course [cannot be refused credit](https://registrar.cornell.edu/students/credit-evaluation-services) because it was taken at a community college or online. Take all four prep courses for letter grades with a C or better. Check that your math is calculus or statistics: Brooks does not accept math below Calculus I. An associate's degree usually means junior entry, so the 60-credit cap matters to you.
```

```reader "If you're hoping to start in spring"
Brooks takes external transfers for fall only. Cornell's admissions page says applications are [considered for the fall semester only](https://admissions.cornell.edu/apply/transfer-applicants), so there is no January 2027 start. Spring entry did exist: [72 transfers started in spring 2023](https://cornellsun.com/2023/01/26/spring-transfers-start-cornell-journey-mid-year-and-prepare-for-challenges-ahead/), which is why older pages still mention it. The costly mistake is waiting for a spring deadline that never opens and missing March 15, 2027. Use the extra semester at your current school to finish the Brooks prep courses, and watch that the added credits do not push you past the 60-credit cap.
```

## Which major you apply to

Brooks has two undergraduate majors open to external transfers. On the Common App you pick "Brooks School of Public Policy" as your Cornell program and list [Public Policy or Health Care Policy](https://publicpolicy.cornell.edu/undergraduate/apply/transfer-applicants/external-transfers/) as your major of interest.

Cornell's enrollment FAQ says Brooks looks for [demonstrated interest in public or health care policy](https://faq.enrollment.cornell.edu/kb/article/441-how-can-i-prepare-as-a-prospective-transfer-applicant-to-the-cornell-jeb-e-brooks-school-of-public-policy/), shown through coursework and activities, and a mix of social science, quantitative and writing-intensive courses. Your Writing Supplement is where that interest gets argued. Cornell ranks the [essay on interest in the program](https://faq.enrollment.cornell.edu/kb/article/325-does-cornell-prefer-transfer-applicants-from-certain-colleges-or-types-of-colleges/) among the most important factors, alongside your college grades and professor recommendations.

## The required courses

Every Brooks applicant needs the four courses below, completed [with a letter grade](https://publicpolicy.cornell.edu/undergraduate/apply/transfer-applicants/external-transfers/) at the current school. The writing credits can come from any discipline, so a writing-intensive history or political science course counts.

| Requirement | Credits | Courses | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| English composition or writing-intensive | 6 | 2 | Any discipline |
| Introductory microeconomics | 3 | 1 | Required |
| Statistics and/or calculus | 3 | 1 | Statistics recommended for juniors |
| Introductory sociology | 3 | 1 | Required |
| **Total** | **15** | **5** | Letter grades only |

Brooks also suggests intermediate microeconomics, introductory government or U.S. health care systems, a second quantitative course, and natural science coursework. It marks intermediate micro, statistics and the second quantitative course as strongly recommended for junior entrants, finished by the end of sophomore year.

To check whether a specific course will count, Brooks asks you to send its syllabus to Brookscrediteval@cornell.edu. The general test is [Cornell's university rule](https://registrar.cornell.edu/students/credit-evaluation-services): about 80% similar in scope and content, a grade of C or better, from a regionally accredited school.

## What GPA Brooks expects

Brooks sets no minimum. Its page says a [GPA of 3.5 or above, with As and B+s](https://publicpolicy.cornell.edu/undergraduate/apply/transfer-applicants/external-transfers/) in the prep courses, increases an applicant's competitiveness, and that meeting the requirements does not guarantee admission. Across Cornell, admitted transfers are [generally 3.3 or better](https://faq.enrollment.cornell.edu/kb/article/325-does-cornell-prefer-transfer-applicants-from-certain-colleges-or-types-of-colleges/).

Read those numbers as layers. A 3.3 is the university-wide norm; a 3.5 with As and B+s in the prep courses is the Brooks target. Our advice: protect your grades in micro, statistics, sociology and writing first, since those are the courses Brooks names. For how other colleges set their bar, see [GPA requirements by college](/transfer/cornell-transfer-gpa-requirements-by-college/).

## When to apply

You can apply after two to four semesters elsewhere; [first-semester students are not eligible](https://faq.enrollment.cornell.edu/kb/article/330-when-should-i-apply-to-cornell-as-a-transfer/). Brooks reviews files on a rolling basis, and a late midterm grade can delay your decision. The central Cornell deadlines apply.

1. **By March 15, 2027:** the Transfer Common App, the [$85 fee or fee waiver](https://admissions.cornell.edu/apply/transfer-applicants), the 250 to 650 word essay and the Writing Supplement. US aid forms, the [CSS Profile and FAFSA](https://finaid.cornell.edu/apply-aid/prospective-students-or-first-time-aid-applicants), are due the same day; the FAFSA opens October 1, 2026.
2. **By April 15, 2027:** the College Report (your school's official fills it in), the Academic Evaluation from an instructor, the Mid-Term Report (you fill it in) and every transcript.
3. **Late April through June:** decisions [roll out](https://admissions.cornell.edu/apply/transfer-applicants) in the Applicant Portal. If another school's deadline is coming, Brooks asks you to tell admissions.
4. **After acceptance:** Brooks sends a credit evaluation one to three weeks after the letter. In the 2024 cycle, Brooks and other colleges [emailed admitted transfers early to mid-June](https://scl.cornell.edu/get-involved/tatkon-center-new-students/new-students-cornell/transfer/course-enrollment/college-school-links) about enrollment.

## How many credits transfer

Brooks accepts up to 60 credits of college-level work and requires at least four semesters in residence on the Ithaca campus. Physical education credits and math below Calculus I do not transfer. Under [university policy](https://registrar.cornell.edu/students/credit-evaluation-services), credits over the cap are simply lost, and transfer grades do not enter your Cornell GPA.

| Credits earned before Cornell | Credits Brooks accepts | Credits lost | Total semesters of college |
|---|---|---|---|
| 30 (one year) | 30 | 0 | 8 |
| 60 (two years) | 60 | 0 | 8 |
| 75 | 60 | 15 | 9 |
| 90 | 60 | 30 | 10 |

The semester counts are our arithmetic at 15 credits a semester: you still need four Cornell semesters, whatever you bring.

Exam credit has its own rules. Brooks's [pre-college credit page](https://publicpolicy.cornell.edu/current-students/undergraduates/course-enrollment-information/pre-college-credits/) counts IB Higher Level exams only, and AP or IB Statistics earns no credit. If you take a Cornell course that overlaps an exam, you lose the exam credit. To claim any exam credit, send scores and a written request to the college registrar by the [last day of instruction of your first Cornell semester](https://registrar.cornell.edu/students/credit-evaluation-services).

## What it costs

Brooks is a contract college, so New York residents pay [$49,816 in tuition for 2026-27](https://news.cornell.edu/stories/2026/03/board-trustees-approves-2026-27-budget-parameters) against $73,946 for everyone else. Mandatory fees add [$1,024 a year](https://finaid.cornell.edu/cost-attend). Prices for 2027-28 come out in late March 2027.

Cornell aid is need-based only. For 2026-27, families earning up to $125,000 with typical assets get [grants that cover tuition](https://news.cornell.edu/stories/2026/03/board-trustees-approves-2026-27-budget-parameters), and families up to $75,000 get the full cost covered. EOP and HEOP applicants to Brooks [email stateprograms@cornell.edu](https://oadi.cornell.edu/node/50) instead of sending the HEOP certification form.

Example: a New York community college student enters Brooks as a junior with 75 credits. Brooks takes 60, so 15 are lost and college runs nine semesters. At 2026-27 prices that extra semester is about $25,420 in tuition and fees (our math: half of $50,840 in yearly tuition and fees), before aid. Stopping at 60 credits avoids it.

## Common mistakes

- **Taking a prep course pass/fail.** Brooks requires letter grades, so the course does not count and you may have to retake it, a full semester's slot.
- **Using precalculus as the math course.** Math below Calculus I does not transfer; take calculus or statistics.
- **Waiting for a spring start.** Entry is fall only; missing March 15, 2027 costs a full year.
- **Arriving with 75 to 90 credits.** Each 15 credits over 60 adds a semester, about $25,420 for a New York resident at 2026-27 rates (our math) and more for others.
- **Counting on AP Statistics.** It earns no Brooks credit; you still need a college statistics or calculus course.
- **Sending only the March 15 pieces.** The College Report, Academic Evaluation and Mid-Term Report are due April 15, and a missing midterm grade delays a rolling decision.

## FAQ

**Is a Brooks transfer guaranteed if I meet the requirements?** No. Brooks says meeting them [does not guarantee admission](https://publicpolicy.cornell.edu/undergraduate/apply/transfer-applicants/external-transfers/).

**Can I get a course pre-approved before I apply?** You can send a syllabus to Brookscrediteval@cornell.edu for review. The official evaluation arrives one to three weeks after acceptance.

**Do I have to live on campus?** Sophomore entrants must live on campus for one academic year; [juniors are exempt](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/campus-housing/new-transfer-undergraduates), as are students who lived on campus two years elsewhere.

**Who do I contact at Brooks?** Admissions questions go to BROOKS_ADMISSIONS@CORNELL.EDU or 607-254-3451. After admission, student services is [brooks-studentservices@cornell.edu](https://scl.cornell.edu/get-involved/tatkon-center-new-students/new-students-cornell/transfer/course-enrollment/college-school-links).

**Do community college transfers get into Cornell?** Yes. Cornell says [about a quarter of its transfers](https://faq.enrollment.cornell.edu/kb/article/325-does-cornell-prefer-transfer-applicants-from-certain-colleges-or-types-of-colleges/) come from community colleges, and it [admits about 100 a year](https://www.news.cornell.edu/stories/2024/05/community-college-transfers-flourish-cornell) on average.
