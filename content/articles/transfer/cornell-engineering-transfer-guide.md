```glance
- Four courses decide whether Engineering reads your file: MATH 1910, MATH 1920, PHYS 1112 with the PHYS 1110 lab, CHEM 2090 with the CHEM 2091 lab.
- Each must be done, in progress or planned by the March 15, 2027 deadline; there is no stated GPA minimum.
- You apply to one of 13 majors and must stay in it at least one academic year; no undecided option.
- Credit: up to 33 per full-time year and 66 in all, AP included; a C counts, a C- does not; AP needs a 5.
- CS comes through the Engineering B.S. or the Arts and Sciences B.A.
- Engineering tuition is $73,946 for 2026-27 for everyone, New York residents included.
```

Cornell Engineering reviews a transfer application only if four courses are completed, in progress or planned: MATH 1910, MATH 1920, PHYS 1112 with PHYS 1110, and CHEM 2090 with CHEM 2091, per the [Duffield requirements page](https://www.duffield.cornell.edu/admissions/transfer-applicants/requirements). The deadline is March 15, 2027, the page states no GPA minimum, and up to 66 credits can transfer. Computer science comes through that Engineering degree or through the [Arts and Sciences B.A.](https://www.cs.cornell.edu/undergrad/csmajor/transfercredits)

```reader "If you're applying to Engineering or CS"
Line up the four courses first: MATH 1910 and 1920, PHYS 1112 with the 1110 lab, CHEM 2090 with the 2091 lab. [Missing one means no review](https://www.duffield.cornell.edu/admissions/transfer-applicants/requirements). Apply in the spring term of your current school, by March 15, 2027. Pick your major with care: you enter it directly and [must keep it at least one academic year](https://faq.enrollment.cornell.edu/kb/article/150-as-a-transfer-student-does-it-matter-which-engineering-major-i-apply-to/). Programming (CS 1110 or 1112) sits in individual major lists, such as Computer Science, not in the four-course block. Keep a detailed syllabus for every course. The costly mistake: counting on an AP 4 in Calculus BC, Chemistry or Physics C Mechanics. Only a 5 earns credit.
```

```reader "If you're at a community college"
Cornell [cannot deny equivalency](https://registrar.cornell.edu/students/credit-evaluation-services) because a course was at a community college or online. Each course needs a C or better and about 80% overlap with the Cornell course. Two full-time years can bring up to 66 credits, Engineering's ceiling. Aiming at Chemical Engineering? Apply as a sophomore if you can: some of its sophomore courses are not offered at most two-year colleges. HEOP applicants to Engineering file a [HEOP Transfer Certification form](https://oadi.cornell.edu/node/50). The costly mistake: a C- in calculus or physics, which earns no credit, or a physics or chemistry course without its lab.
```

```reader "If you're an international applicant"
[Same dates as everyone](https://admissions.cornell.edu/how-to-apply/transfer-international-applicants): March 15, 2027 for the application and $85 fee, April 15 for the rest. The aid deadline is the hard one. File the [CSS Profile (code 2098) by March 15, 2027](https://finaid.cornell.edu/first-year-and-transfer-students-international), or you can never get Cornell aid as an undergraduate. Asking for aid makes admission need-aware. Courses from Ministry-of-Education-recognized institutions are generally accepted; ESL and remedial courses are not. Non-English documents need official English translations. Engineering tuition is $73,946 for 2026-27. The costly mistake: skipping the CSS Profile to look stronger, then needing aid later.
```

```reader "If you'd enter as a junior"
Engineering admits juniors only into the first semester of junior year. The 66-credit ceiling equals two full-time years at 33 a year, so a third year elsewhere adds no credit. Cornell's admissions office [discourages junior-year transfer](https://faq.enrollment.cornell.edu/kb/article/323-can-i-transfer-to-cornell-in-my-junior-year-from-another-college/) from a four-year school because credits are lost. One upside: a student who matriculates as a junior is [exempt from the housing rule](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/campus-housing/new-transfer-undergraduates). The costly mistake: staying a third year to finish the block, then losing every credit above 66.
```

```reader "If you're a New York resident"
Engineering is an endowed college, so you pay the [same $73,946 tuition](https://news.cornell.edu/stories/2026/03/board-trustees-approves-2026-27-budget-parameters) for 2026-27 as a student from anywhere. The $49,816 New York rate applies only at the contract colleges (CALS, Human Ecology, ILR, Brooks, Dyson). That gap is $24,130 a year by our subtraction. The Arts and Sciences route to CS is endowed too. Aid narrows it: families earning up to $125,000 with typical assets get tuition covered by grants. File with HESC using [code 0175](https://finaid.cornell.edu/apply-aid/prospective-students-or-first-time-aid-applicants), the endowed code.
```

## The four required courses

Engineering's page names four basic components. If any one is missing from your record or your plan, the application [would not be reviewed](https://www.duffield.cornell.edu/admissions/transfer-applicants/requirements). "Planned" counts, so a spring course you will take after March 15 still qualifies.

| Component | Cornell course | Lab | Exam that substitutes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Calculus I | MATH 1910 | none | AP Calculus BC, score of 5 |
| Calculus II | MATH 1920 | none | none |
| Physics (mechanics) | PHYS 1112 | PHYS 1110 required | AP Physics C Mechanics 5 earns 1112; 1110 still required |
| Chemistry | CHEM 2090 | CHEM 2091 required | AP Chemistry, score of 5 |

Three details trip people up:

1. **The labs are separate.** A lecture-only physics or chemistry course leaves a hole in the block.
2. **Physics must be calculus-based.** Cornell's physics department tells outside students to look for "calculus-based" in the [course description](https://www.physics.cornell.edu/transfer-credit).
3. **Programming is not one of the four.** CS 1110 or CS 1112 shows up in individual major lists, Computer Science among them. Take it if your major's list names it.

Beyond the block, Engineering publishes sophomore and junior course lists for 13 majors: Biological, Biomedical, Chemical, Civil, Computer Science, Earth and Climate Sciences, Electrical and Computer, Engineering Physics, Environmental, Information Science Systems and Technology, Materials, Mechanical, and Operations Research and Engineering. Match your second-year schedule to your major's list.

## GPA, odds and what admissions reads

Engineering's page states [no minimum GPA](https://www.duffield.cornell.edu/admissions/transfer-applicants/requirements). A 3.7 cutoff circulates online; it is not on Cornell's page. University-wide, admitted transfers are ["generally" 3.3 or better](https://faq.enrollment.cornell.edu/kb/article/325-does-cornell-prefer-transfer-applicants-from-certain-colleges-or-types-of-colleges/). The same FAQ says the essay, your current coursework and professor recommendations weigh most. Cornell has no preference by school type, and about a quarter of its transfers come from community colleges.

For scale: 7,381 people applied to transfer in fall 2025 and 864 were admitted, an 11.7% rate by our math from the [Common Data Set](https://irp.cornell.edu/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/Cornell-Common-Data-Set-2025-26.pdf). That rate covers every college. Engineering takes a small share: [37 of 674](https://irp.cornell.edu/wp-content/uploads/2021/08/Profile2021-Fall-Transfers.pdf) fall 2021 transfers entered it, in Cornell's 2021 by-college profile.

SAT and ACT scores are [neither required nor expected](https://faq.enrollment.cornell.edu/kb/article/322-are-standardized-tests-required-for-transfer-applicants/). Self-report AP and IB scores in the Common App.

## Your major and your standing

You are admitted [directly into one major](https://faq.enrollment.cornell.edu/kb/article/150-as-a-transfer-student-does-it-matter-which-engineering-major-i-apply-to/). You cannot apply undecided or to the Independent Major, and you must stay in your major for at least one academic year.

Cornell takes transfers who have finished [two to four semesters](https://faq.enrollment.cornell.edu/kb/article/330-when-should-i-apply-to-cornell-as-a-transfer/) elsewhere; first-semester college students cannot apply. No associate's degree is needed. Standing is set after the offer. Engineering takes [sophomores in either semester and juniors in the first semester](https://www.duffield.cornell.edu/admissions/transfer-applicants/requirements) only. Chemical Engineering asks applicants to come in as sophomores if possible. Engineering does not pre-evaluate courses before you apply.

## Computer science: two routes

CS majors come in through Engineering's B.S., admitted straight into the major, or through Arts and Sciences' B.A., where you request CS. The CS department [reviews CS transfer credit](https://www.cs.cornell.edu/undergrad/csmajor/transfercredits) itself. The demand is real: computer and information sciences made up 18.80% of Cornell degrees awarded in 2024-25, the largest field in the [Common Data Set](https://irp.cornell.edu/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/Cornell-Common-Data-Set-2025-26.pdf).

| | Engineering B.S. | Arts and Sciences B.A. |
|---|---|---|
| What you apply to | One major, kept for at least a year | The college; junior applicants need the major's prerequisites |
| Courses needed to be reviewed | MATH 1910, 1920, PHYS 1112/1110, CHEM 2090/2091 | Prerequisites for the intended major |
| Transfer credit cap | 66 in all, 33 per full-time year | 60 |
| Required at Cornell | At least 60 credits | At least 60 credits and four semesters |
| Credits to graduate | 123 to 137 | 120, of which 100 are Arts and Sciences credits |
| Tuition, 2026-27 | $73,946 | $73,946 |

Sources: [Duffield requirements](https://www.duffield.cornell.edu/admissions/transfer-applicants/requirements), [Arts and Sciences admissions](https://as.cornell.edu/admissions/admissions-requirements-and-process), [Registrar](https://registrar.cornell.edu/students/credit-evaluation-services), [Chronicle tuition story](https://news.cornell.edu/stories/2026/03/board-trustees-approves-2026-27-budget-parameters).

Our advice: if you already have the chemistry and physics block, the Engineering route puts it to use. If you lack chemistry or physics, check the Arts and Sciences CS prerequisites before you pick that route. Arts and Sciences takes transfer questions at as-transfer@cornell.edu.

## How credit transfers

Credit is evaluated after admission, course by course. Most Cornell colleges cap transfer credit at 60; Engineering's rules differ.

| Rule | Engineering |
|---|---|
| Per year | Up to 33 credits per year of full-time study |
| Total | 66, including AP, IB, GCE and CASE credit |
| At Cornell | At least 60 credits |
| Grade | C or better; a C- earns nothing; departments may ask more |
| Match | About 80% similar in scope and rigor |
| Proof | Detailed syllabus and certified official transcript |
| Never counts | MOOCs, non-degree courses, high school cooperative courses, technical skills, life, work or military experience |
| Outside the 66 | Cornell summer, winter and study abroad credit |

Source: [Duffield requirements page](https://www.duffield.cornell.edu/admissions/transfer-applicants/requirements).

Under university policy, the Cornell department that owns the matching course [decides from the syllabus](https://registrar.cornell.edu/students/credit-evaluation-services). It cannot refuse a course for being from a community college or online. Courses seven or more years old are generally not eligible. Your transfer grades stay off your Cornell GPA; the transcript shows only the school's name and total credits.

Example: a student finishes three full-time years elsewhere with 90 credits. At 33 a year, the cap would allow 99, but the 66 total ceiling applies, so at least 24 credits are lost. Engineering would also admit this student no higher than first-semester junior, with at least 60 credits still to earn at Cornell.

Example: a student with two years at 64 credits stays under both caps. If every course clears a C and the 80% test, all 64 can count, leaving at least 60 Cornell credits toward a degree of 123 to 137.

## AP scores and the MATH CASE

AP credit your first school accepted does not carry over [automatically](https://faq.enrollment.cornell.edu/kb/article/332-as-a-transfer-student-will-i-automatically-receive-ap-ib-or-a-level-examinations-credit-if-it-was-accepted-at-my-previous-institution/). Engineering accepts only these scores:

- AP Calculus BC: 5, which meets MATH 1910.
- AP Chemistry: 5.
- AP Physics C Mechanics: 5, which earns PHYS 1112. You still take the PHYS 1110 lab.

A 4 earns nothing. If you have a 4 on Calculus BC, Engineering strongly encourages the optional MATH CASE exam. In 2026 it ran [Thursday, August 20, 9 to 11 a.m. in Olin Hall 155](https://www.duffield.cornell.edu/advising/new-engineering-students/orientation/transfer-student-orientation/), with no calculators. Pass it, or plan to retake the course.

Exam credit is a separate request. Send scores with College Board [code 2098](https://scl.cornell.edu/get-involved/tatkon-center-new-students/new-students-cornell/transfer/course-enrollment), then ask the college registrar in writing by the [last day of instruction](https://registrar.cornell.edu/students/credit-evaluation-services) of your first Cornell semester. IB and A-Level transcripts go to the college registrar too. Where an exam and a transfer course overlap, only the course counts.

## Graduation rules that apply to transfers

Transfers who enter fall 2025 or later complete [two PE courses and the water competency](https://www.duffield.cornell.edu/admissions/transfer-applicants/requirements), the swim test. You need 123 to 137 credits to graduate.

Once enrolled, outside courses get tight. You earn no credit for an outside course taken during a Cornell fall or spring term you are enrolled in. Winter credit counts only if you take fewer than 6 Cornell credits that term; summer, fewer than 12. Physics wants transfer credit [requested before you enroll](https://www.physics.cornell.edu/transfer-credit) in the outside course.

## What the year costs

Engineering is an endowed college, so tuition is [$73,946 for 2026-27](https://news.cornell.edu/stories/2026/03/board-trustees-approves-2026-27-budget-parameters) whatever your state. New transfers live on campus for [one academic year](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/campus-housing/new-transfer-undergraduates), unless they lived on campus two years elsewhere or enter as juniors.

Fees are [$1,024](https://finaid.cornell.edu/cost-attend), a double is [$13,744 a year](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/housing-details/rates-amenities-licenses/housing-rates), and the Unlimited plan is [$7,604](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/dining/meal-plans-rates/undergraduate-meal-plans). That bills $96,318 by our addition. Cornell's own cost of attendance adds $1,234 for books and $2,182 for personal costs, for $99,734. The Student Health Plan comes on top unless you waive it: $1,705 for a new student's fall and $2,046 for spring in [2026-27](https://studenthealthbenefits.cornell.edu/shp-costs-billing-important-dates/shp-costs-dates-2026-27). Fall 2027 entrants will pay 2027-28 prices, not yet set.

Aid is need-based. Cornell meets [100% of demonstrated need](https://admissions.cornell.edu/apply/transfer-applicants), and domestic admission is need-blind. Families earning up to $125,000 with typical assets get tuition covered by grants; up to $75,000, the full cost. US applicants file the CSS Profile and FAFSA by [March 15, 2027](https://finaid.cornell.edu/apply-aid/prospective-students-or-first-time-aid-applicants).

Don't rule Cornell out on sticker price. Four 2024 community college graduates profiled by the [Cornell Chronicle](https://www.news.cornell.edu/stories/2024/05/community-college-transfers-flourish-cornell) said they had assumed Cornell was too expensive; a counselor in the story called that a common misconception.

## Common mistakes

- **Leaving one course or lab off the plan.** The application is not reviewed at all, whatever your grades.
- **Counting on an AP 4.** No credit in calculus, chemistry or physics. Skip the MATH CASE and you retake the course.
- **Settling for a C-.** It earns no credit, even where your old school passed it.
- **Choosing a major loosely.** You stay in it for at least one academic year.
- **Staying a third year to get ahead.** Engineering takes no more than 66 credits; at 90 earned, at least 24 are lost.
- **Taking a summer or winter course after enrolling without checking.** It counts only with fewer than 12 (summer) or 6 (winter) Cornell credits that term.
- **International applicants skipping the CSS Profile.** Miss March 15, 2027 and Cornell aid is [closed for good](https://admissions.cornell.edu/how-to-apply/transfer-international-applicants).

## FAQ

**Will Engineering tell me before I apply which courses will count?** No. Engineering does not offer [pre-evaluation](https://www.duffield.cornell.edu/admissions/transfer-applicants/requirements); credit is decided after admission from your syllabi.

**Can a pass/fail course count?** University policy accepts pass/fail only with a written statement that the work was a [C or better](https://registrar.cornell.edu/students/credit-evaluation-services). For the four required courses, take a letter grade.

**Can I move to another Cornell college later, say to the Arts and Sciences CS major?** Not right away. You keep your Engineering major for at least a year, and Arts and Sciences says external transfers may apply to another college only [after two semesters at Cornell](https://as.cornell.edu/admissions/admissions-requirements-and-process). Pick the route you want before you apply.

**Who do I ask about credit once I'm admitted?** The Office of Engineering Advising: adv_engineering@cornell.edu, (607) 255-7414, per Cornell's [college contact list](https://scl.cornell.edu/get-involved/tatkon-center-new-students/new-students-cornell/transfer/course-enrollment/college-school-links). Before admission, questions go to Undergraduate Admissions.

**Do international students get aid above tuition taxed?** Yes. Aid above tuition and fees is taxable, with 14% withheld unless a [tax treaty](https://finaid.cornell.edu/first-year-and-transfer-students-international) applies.

**Is there a separate application for EOP or HEOP?** Engineering applicants to HEOP file a [HEOP Transfer Certification form](https://oadi.cornell.edu/node/50) along with the usual forms. Fall 2027 applicants use 2025 tax documents.
