```glance
- Cap: up to 60 transfer credits in most colleges, exam credit included; Engineering 66 (33 per full-time year), Architecture 64.
- Floor: at least 60 credits at Cornell, and four semesters in Arts and Sciences, ILR and Brooks; Architecture needs 90.
- Every credit past 60 is lost: 90 credits in means 30 lost and ten semesters of college at 15 a semester.
- A course counts with a C or better and about 80% overlap with a Cornell course; no MOOCs, remedial or military credit.
- Evaluation happens only after admission, from your syllabi; nothing is pre-approved.
- AP and IB credit is a separate written request, due by the last day of classes in your first Cornell semester.
```

Most Cornell colleges accept [up to 60 transfer credits, exam credit included](https://registrar.cornell.edu/students/credit-evaluation-services); Engineering takes up to 66 and Architecture 64. Each college judges your courses one by one after you are admitted, and a course counts only with a C or better and about 80% overlap with a Cornell course. Arrive with 60 or fewer credits and nothing is lost; past 60, every 15 credits adds a semester to your total time in college.

```reader "If you're a sophomore transfer from a four-year college"
You are the reader the 60-credit cap was built for: with two semesters done you will lose nothing to the cap. The risk is grades and format. Take your remaining courses for letter grades, because [CALS](https://cals.cornell.edu/education/admissions/undergraduate-admissions/transfer-applicants) and [Human Ecology](https://www.human.cornell.edu/admissions/transfer) refuse pass/fail outright. Save every syllabus now, while you can still download it. If your old school gave you AP credit, Cornell will not carry it over on its own: send scores to code 2098 and file the [written request](https://registrar.cornell.edu/students/credit-evaluation-services) by the last day of fall classes ([Dec 7 in 2026](https://registrar.cornell.edu/calendars-exams/academic-calendar)).
```

```reader "If you're a junior transfer"
Do the arithmetic before you apply. At 15 credits a semester, 90 credits elsewhere means 60 accepted, 30 lost and ten semesters of college, because [Arts and Sciences](https://as.cornell.edu/registrar/transferring-credits) still wants 60 Cornell credits and four semesters. Overloading cannot shorten it. With 60 or fewer, you still finish in eight semesters total. Cornell [discourages junior transfer from four-year schools](https://faq.enrollment.cornell.edu/kb/article/323-can-i-transfer-to-cornell-in-my-junior-year-from-another-college/), and CALS bars anyone with more than four semesters at one. AP credit sits inside the cap, so it fills your 60 faster. Our estimate of the cost of the extra year: $74,970 in endowed tuition and fees at 2026-27 rates, before housing and aid.
```

```reader "If you're transferring from a community college"
Cornell cannot refuse equivalency [because a course was taken at a community college or online](https://registrar.cornell.edu/students/credit-evaluation-services). The course still has to come from a regionally accredited school, match about 80% of a Cornell course and carry a C or better. An associate's degree puts you at junior standing in [CALS](https://cals.cornell.edu/education/admissions/undergraduate-admissions/transfer-applicants), with no option to enter as a sophomore. Math below precalculus, study skills, orientation and remedial courses never transfer, so do not count those credits toward your 60. The costly mistake: pass/fail or a C- in a course you need, which earns nothing in CALS, Human Ecology or Engineering.
```

```reader "If you're applying to Engineering or computer science"
[Duffield](https://www.duffield.cornell.edu/admissions/transfer-applicants/requirements) caps transfer credit at 33 per full-time year and 66 in all, AP, IB, GCE and CASE credit included. A C- earns nothing; you need a C, and departments may set the bar higher. Courses must meet undergraduate engineering requirements, and Duffield wants a detailed syllabus plus a certified transcript for each. Pre-evaluation is not offered. A 4 on AP Calculus BC, Chemistry or Physics C Mechanics gives no credit; Duffield needs a 5. MOOCs, high school cooperative courses, technical skills and life, work or military experience are all excluded.
```

```reader "If you're an international transfer"
Courses from a university abroad are [generally accepted](https://registrar.cornell.edu/students/credit-evaluation-services) when the school is recognized by its Ministry of Education. Remedial courses, ESL courses and international military courses never transfer. English courses go to the Literatures in English department for review. Non-English documents need [official English translations](https://admissions.cornell.edu/apply/transfer-applicants), which a teacher or school official may provide. Once admitted, send A-Level and IB transcripts to your [college registrar](https://scl.cornell.edu/get-involved/tatkon-center-new-students/new-students-cornell/transfer/course-enrollment). The rest of the rules on this page, from the 60-credit cap to the C floor, apply to you the same way.
```

```reader "If you're a veteran or returning after years away"
Cornell [generally cannot award credit for military training](https://admissions.cornell.edu/veterans), and ILR and Engineering exclude military and work experience by name. Courses [seven or more years old](https://registrar.cornell.edu/students/credit-evaluation-services) are generally not eligible, though the department that owns the matching Cornell course may decide otherwise. Plan for more semesters at Cornell than your transcript total suggests. If you have been out of school two or more years, you may [substitute a personal or employer recommendation](https://admissions.cornell.edu/apply/transfer-applicants) for the Academic Evaluation.
```

## The caps and minimums, college by college

Cornell has no single transfer credit number. The [Enrollment FAQ](https://faq.enrollment.cornell.edu/kb/article/327-how-are-my-transfer-credits-evaluated/) says most colleges allow up to 60 semester credits; the [registrar](https://registrar.cornell.edu/students/credit-evaluation-services) sets the exceptions. Two numbers matter for each college: the most it accepts, and the least you must earn at Cornell.

| College | Most transfer credit accepted | AP, IB and exam credit inside that cap | Minimum at Cornell |
|---|---|---|---|
| Arts and Sciences | [60](https://as.cornell.edu/registrar/transferring-credits) | counts toward 60 | [60 credits and four semesters](https://as.cornell.edu/admissions/admissions-requirements-and-process) |
| CALS | [60, test plus transfer](https://cals.cornell.edu/education/admissions/undergraduate-admissions/transfer-applicants) | AP up to 15 | 60 credits (university rule) |
| Engineering | [66 total, 33 per full-time year](https://www.duffield.cornell.edu/admissions/transfer-applicants/requirements) | AP, IB, GCE and CASE count toward 66 | 60 credits |
| ILR | [60](https://www.ilr.cornell.edu/sites/default/files-d8/2026-02/transfer-guidelines-fa-26-final.pdf) | AP up to 12 | 60 credits and four full-time semesters |
| Hotel | [60](https://business.cornell.edu/admissions/undergraduate/bs-hotel-administration/application-requirements/) | AP, IB and GCE up to 15 | 60 credits (university rule) |
| Human Ecology | 60 (university rule) | [15 pre-college credits at most](https://www.human.cornell.edu/admissions/transfer) | 60 credits (university rule) |
| Brooks | [60](https://publicpolicy.cornell.edu/undergraduate/apply/transfer-applicants/external-transfers/) | counts toward 60 | four semesters on the Ithaca campus |
| Architecture | [64](https://registrar.cornell.edu/students/credit-evaluation-services) | counts toward 64 | 90 credits; 154 to graduate over five years |

The university-wide floor is [60 credits at Cornell](https://registrar.cornell.edu/students/credit-evaluation-services), 90 in Architecture. Engineering students need 123 to 137 credits to graduate, depending on major.

Engineering also limits credit per year. Example: a student with one and a half full-time years elsewhere can bring at most 49.5 credits (1.5 times 33). Two full years reach 66, the total cap. A third year elsewhere adds nothing.

## Credits you lose past the cap

The cap and the residence floor work together. Credits above the cap vanish, and the four-semester floor means you cannot make the time back by taking more courses. Our model below uses the Arts and Sciences rules (60 credits in, 120 to graduate, four semesters minimum) and assumes 15 credits a semester and every course accepted.

| Credits before Cornell | Semesters there | Accepted | Lost | Semesters at Cornell | Total semesters |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 30 | 2 | 30 | 0 | 6 | 8 |
| 45 | 3 | 45 | 0 | 5 | 8 |
| 60 | 4 | 60 | 0 | 4 | 8 |
| 75 | 5 | 60 | 15 | 4 | 9 |
| 90 | 6 | 60 | 30 | 4 | 10 |
| 105 | 7 | 60 | 45 | 4 | 11 |

Example: a transfer with 90 credits gets 60 accepted and loses 30. That leaves 60 credits, or four semesters, at Cornell. Six semesters elsewhere plus four at Cornell is ten, a year longer than usual. At 2026-27 rates, that year is $73,946 in [endowed tuition](https://news.cornell.edu/stories/2026/03/board-trustees-approves-2026-27-budget-parameters) plus $1,024 in [mandatory fees](https://finaid.cornell.edu/cost-attend), our total of $74,970 before housing and aid.

The model is generous. A course that transfers only as an elective can still leave a requirement open. And exam credit counts inside the cap in most colleges, so a student with AP credit reaches 60 sooner.

## Quarter credits

Cornell multiplies quarter credits by [two-thirds](https://registrar.cornell.edu/students/credit-evaluation-services). Human Ecology states the same rule as 0.67.

| Quarter credits | Semester credits |
|---|---|
| 4 | 2.67 |
| 5 | 3.33 |
| 90 | 60 (the cap) |
| 180 | 120 |

Arts and Sciences adds a catch: only quarter courses worth [4 or more credits](https://as.cornell.edu/registrar/transferring-credits) can meet its requirements. A 3-quarter-credit course can still count toward the total.

## How a course is judged

The college that admits you evaluates each course [case by case](https://faq.enrollment.cornell.edu/kb/article/327-how-are-my-transfer-credits-evaluated/). Accepted credit usually lands on distribution requirements or electives. The [registrar's rules](https://registrar.cornell.edu/students/credit-evaluation-services) set the test:

- **Match.** The course must be about 80% similar in scope, content and learning outcomes to a Cornell course. The Cornell department that owns that course decides, from your syllabus. Two outside courses can combine to equal one Cornell course.
- **Grade.** C or better. A college or major may require up to a B for major courses. Engineering states plainly that a C- does not count.
- **Pass/fail.** Accepted with a written statement that the grade was a C or better. CALS and Human Ecology accept no pass/fail at all.
- **School.** A US school must be regionally accredited. Online courses and community college courses cannot be refused for being either.
- **Age.** Courses seven or more years old are generally not eligible.
- **No equivalent.** If Cornell has no matching course, your college or program decides how it counts.
- **Retakes.** You may retake an equivalent course at Cornell, but you get credit for only one of the two.

Arts and Sciences asks for [substantial equivalence](https://as.cornell.edu/registrar/transferring-credits) in content and rigor and compares content hours, assessments and textbooks. Full-time liberal arts credits can meet requirements, including the First-Year Writing Seminar and language. They also count toward the 100 Arts and Sciences credits you need out of 120. Part-time and high school courses, and courses taken after your offer, fall under current-student rules and cannot fill requirements.

## What never transfers

| Never counts | Where the rule comes from |
|---|---|
| Orientation, study skills and career development courses | [Registrar](https://registrar.cornell.edu/students/credit-evaluation-services) |
| Math below precalculus; non-credit remedial courses | Registrar |
| ESL courses and international military courses | Registrar |
| MOOCs (colleges may allow exceptions) | Registrar; Engineering and ILR exclude them outright |
| Military training and work or life experience | [Admissions](https://admissions.cornell.edu/veterans); Engineering; ILR |
| Internships outside an approved program | Registrar |
| Credit your old school gave for its own departmental exam | [Enrollment FAQ](https://faq.enrollment.cornell.edu/kb/article/326-does-the-credit-i-received-by-examination-at-my-previous-college-or-university-transfer/) |
| CLEP | [Arts and Sciences](https://as.cornell.edu/registrar/transferring-credits) |

AP, IB and A-Level credit is a separate case. It is [not automatic](https://faq.enrollment.cornell.edu/kb/article/332-as-a-transfer-student-will-i-automatically-receive-ap-ib-or-a-level-examinations-credit-if-it-was-accepted-at-my-previous-institution/), even if your old school accepted it, and where an exam and a transfer course overlap, only the course counts. Our [AP and IB guide](/transfer/cornell-transfer-ap-ib-exam-credit/) has the score tables by college.

## What your Cornell transcript shows

Your transfer courses, credits and grades do [not appear on the Cornell transcript](https://registrar.cornell.edu/students/credit-evaluation-services). It lists only the name of your old school and the total transfer credits. Your transfer GPA is not part of your Cornell GPA, so you start at Cornell with a clean average.

## After you're admitted: the steps

No college evaluates credit before admission. CALS states that it [cannot pre-approve schedules](https://cals.cornell.edu/education/admissions/undergraduate-admissions/transfer-applicants), and Engineering offers no pre-evaluation.

```figure
kind: steps
title: Transfer credit, from admission to a correct degree audit
rows:
  - {label: "Accept and deposit; Arts and Sciences sends a tentative evaluation only after both"}
  - {label: "Send what your college asks for: syllabi, a certified transcript, Engineering's course description form"}
  - {label: "Send your final spring transcript; evaluations finish only after it arrives"}
  - {label: "Enroll in classes during add/drop (the add deadline was Sep 8 in 2026)"}
  - {label: "Request AP, IB or A-Level credit in writing by the last day of first-semester classes (Dec 7 in 2026)"}
  - {label: "Check the degree audit until every accepted course shows; keep the syllabi until it does"}
```

1. **Accept and deposit.** Arts and Sciences issues its [tentative credit evaluation](https://as.cornell.edu/registrar/transferring-credits) only after both.
2. **Send what your college asks for.** Engineering wants the [course description form](https://admissions.cornell.edu/apply/transfer-applicants) with your Enrollment Response Form, plus detailed syllabi and a certified transcript. AAP gives admitted students [two weeks](https://aap.cornell.edu/undergraduate-admissions/transfer-applicants/) to submit syllabi and coursework, with a portfolio for studio courses.
3. **Send the final transcript.** Evaluations finish only after your spring grades post. That is why transfers usually build schedules during add/drop; the add deadline was [Sep 8 in 2026](https://registrar.cornell.edu/calendars-exams/academic-calendar).
4. **Request exam credit.** Have scores sent (College Board code 2098) and write to your college registrar by the [last day of instruction](https://registrar.cornell.edu/students/credit-evaluation-services) of your first semester, which was Dec 7 in 2026.
5. **Check the degree audit.** In a [2023 Cornell Daily Sun story](https://cornellsun.com/2023/01/26/spring-transfers-start-cornell-journey-mid-year-and-prepare-for-challenges-ahead/), spring transfer Abbey Yang '25 said her advisor told her biology credits should count, but her degree audit did not yet show them. Keep every syllabus until the audit is right.

Before you are admitted, credit questions go to Undergraduate Admissions. Once enrolled, ask your [college registrar](https://scl.cornell.edu/get-involved/tatkon-center-new-students/new-students-cornell/transfer/course-enrollment).

## Common mistakes

- **Arriving with 90 credits without doing the math.** Thirty credits vanish and college runs ten semesters, our estimate $74,970 more in 2026-27 tuition and fees.
- **Taking a needed course pass/fail.** No credit in CALS or Human Ecology, and elsewhere only with a written statement of C or better.
- **Treating a C- as passing.** Engineering gives it nothing, which can mean taking the course again at Cornell.
- **Throwing away syllabi.** Cornell decides from the syllabus. A 2016 College Confidential poster who moved from UVA said Cornell [did not ask for theirs](https://talk.collegeconfidential.com/t/cornell-transfer-courses/1840413) because it knew the school, but that was one secondhand account; Engineering requires syllabi for every course.
- **Assuming AP credit carries over.** It does not on its own, and missing the first-semester written request forfeits it.
- **Counting military, work or MOOC credit toward your 60.** None transfers, so you need more semesters at Cornell.

## FAQ

**Can I get transfer credit approved before I apply?** No. Evaluation happens after admission; [CALS](https://cals.cornell.edu/education/admissions/undergraduate-admissions/transfer-applicants) says it cannot give official credit evaluations beforehand, and Engineering offers no pre-evaluation.

**Can I take summer courses elsewhere once I'm at Cornell?** Yes, with [prior approval](https://registrar.cornell.edu/students/credit-evaluation-services). Arts and Sciences allows up to 12 summer credits, and Engineering allows summer transfer credit with fewer than 12 Cornell credits that term.

**Do study abroad credits count against my 60?** Credit from fall or spring programs approved by the Office of Global Learning does [not count toward the cap](https://registrar.cornell.edu/students/credit-evaluation-services).

**Can I take a course at another school during a Cornell semester?** Not for credit in Engineering, which gives [no credit](https://www.duffield.cornell.edu/admissions/transfer-applicants/requirements) for outside courses during a Cornell fall or spring term. Arts and Sciences also bars simultaneous outside enrollment in fall or spring.

**Will a writing course replace the First-Year Writing Seminar?** It can in Arts and Sciences, if it was a [full-time liberal arts course](https://as.cornell.edu/registrar/transferring-credits) that matches. The 2016 College Confidential poster warned that writing seminars were harder to transfer than intro biology or psychology.
