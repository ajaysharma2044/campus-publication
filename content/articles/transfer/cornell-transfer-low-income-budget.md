```glance
- Family income up to $75,000: grants, scholarships and work-study cover the full cost of attendance (2026-27).
- Up to $125,000 with typical assets: grants cover tuition. There is no merit aid; everything is need-based.
- Fall 2027: the application, the $85 fee or its automatic waiver, the CSS Profile and the FAFSA are all due March 15, 2027.
- The CSS Profile fee is waived automatically up to $100,000 adjusted gross income.
- A co-op double costs $6,974 to $9,962 a year against $13,744 for a hall double (2026-27).
- Outside aid's budget: the student health plan, $1,705 for fall and $2,046 for spring, unless you waive it.
```

Yes, for most families with little money: for 2026-27, Cornell's grants cover tuition for families earning up to $125,000 with typical assets, and grants, scholarships and work-study cover the [full cost of attendance up to $75,000](https://news.cornell.edu/stories/2026/03/board-trustees-approves-2026-27-budget-parameters). Cornell [meets 100% of demonstrated need for admitted transfers, and US admission is need-blind](https://admissions.cornell.edu/apply/transfer-applicants). Your job is to file the CSS Profile and FAFSA by March 15, 2027 and to plan for the one big cost aid leaves out, the student health plan.

## What aid covers at each income level

Cornell's aid is need-based only. There are no merit scholarships, so a strong GPA does not change the price. The [2026-27 budget story](https://news.cornell.edu/stories/2026/03/board-trustees-approves-2026-27-budget-parameters) sets two income lines and says nearly 50% of undergraduates receive grants.

| Family income (2026-27) | What Cornell says aid covers | What is left to plan for |
|---|---|---|
| Up to $75,000 | Grants, scholarships and work-study cover the full cost of attendance | The health plan if not waived; travel |
| Up to $125,000, typical assets | Grants cover tuition | Housing, food, fees, books and personal costs, set by your aid offer |
| Above $125,000 | Aid still meets 100% of demonstrated need | Your calculated family share |

Cornell [meets 100% of demonstrated need](https://admissions.cornell.edu/apply/transfer-applicants) with grants, work-study and reasonable loans at every income. The two lines tell you, before any offer arrives, what is already covered.

Example: a sophomore transfer to Arts and Sciences from a family earning $110,000 with typical assets. Tuition, $73,946 for 2026-27, is covered by grants. The rest of the [$99,734 cost of attendance](https://finaid.cornell.edu/cost-attend) is $25,788 by our subtraction: fees, a hall double, the Unlimited meal plan, books and personal costs. That $25,788 is what the aid offer splits between more grant, work-study, any loan and the family's share.

Example: a New York resident entering CALS from a family earning $60,000. Cornell's [billed cost for a hall double with the Unlimited plan is $72,188](https://finaid.cornell.edu/cost-attend), and the full cost of attendance is $75,604. Under $75,000, grants, scholarships and work-study cover all of it. What remains is the health plan if the student has no other coverage.

The numbers move every year. Fall 2027 transfers pay 2027-28 prices, which Cornell confirms [before the early April aid offers](https://finaid.cornell.edu/apply-aid/prospective-students-or-first-time-aid-applicants).

## How to apply without paying fees

1. **The application fee.** It is [$85, and Cornell applies the waiver automatically](https://admissions.cornell.edu/apply/transfer-applicants) when the fee is a burden and you plan to apply for aid. You answer the Common App's waiver questions and Cornell's own; you send no documents. The waiver is due with the application on March 15, 2027.
2. **The CSS Profile.** Code 2098. The College Board fee is [waived automatically for US families with adjusted gross income up to $100,000](https://finaid.cornell.edu/apply-aid/prospective-students-or-first-time-aid-applicants).
3. **The FAFSA.** Code 002711. It opened October 1, 2026; Cornell asks you to file as soon as possible.
4. **HESC, for New York residents.** File at hesc.ny.gov with code 0174 for a contract college or 0175 for an endowed college.
5. **IDOC documents.** Upload tax documents through College Board IDOC only when Cornell asks for them.

All of the aid forms share the March 15, 2027 deadline with the application. The rest of the application (reports, transcripts, the Academic Evaluation) is due April 15.

## EOP and HEOP: the extra step by college

Students applying through Cornell's Educational Opportunity Program (EOP) or New York's Higher Education Opportunity Program (HEOP) file the same transfer application and aid forms. The [Office of Academic Discovery and Impact](https://oadi.cornell.edu/node/50) then needs one more thing, and which thing depends on your college.

| College you apply to | HEOP step |
|---|---|
| Architecture, Art and Planning; Arts and Sciences; Engineering; SC Johnson College of Business with the Nolan School (Hotel) | Submit the HEOP Transfer Certification form |
| CALS; Human Ecology; ILR; Brooks School of Public Policy; Dyson | Email stateprograms@cornell.edu |

## Undocumented and DACA applicants

Undocumented students, with or without DACA, are [treated as domestic applicants for admission and aid](https://admissions.cornell.edu/apply/transfer-applicants). That means need-blind review. You are eligible for Cornell aid if you graduated from a US high school, but not for federal aid, so Cornell's own grants carry the package.

## Where the housing money goes

New transfers must [live on campus for one academic year](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/campus-housing/new-transfer-undergraduates). You are exempt only if you lived on campus two years at your last school or enter as a junior or senior. The room is the biggest bill after tuition, and the license [cannot be cancelled](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/housing-details/rates-amenities-licenses/12-month-undergraduate-housing-license-2026-2027) by a new transfer unless you leave Cornell or win an exemption.

The cheapest rooms are in the eight cooperative houses. The transfer housing page lists co-ops among the upper-level areas where transfers live. Co-op rates are set by each house's operating costs, per the [2026-27 rate page](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/housing-details/rates-amenities-licenses/housing-rates).

A co-op double costs $6,974 to $9,962 a year against [$13,744 for a hall double](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/housing-details/rates-amenities-licenses/housing-rates). That saves $3,782 to $6,770 on the room alone, by our math (each house's per-semester rate times two). These are room rates; food is separate. Hall rates include gas, electricity, garbage and internet up to 200 GB a month. There is no application fee and no damage deposit.

Hall rooms are [assigned by random auto-allocation](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/housing-details/apply-housing-campus/new-undergrad-application-process) from your preferences, so a cheap room only happens on purpose. Apply within ten days of getting your NetID. Before you rank preferences, call Housing and Residential Life at 607-255-5368 and ask how a new transfer gets a co-op place.

## Food: the Unlimited plan and aid

Transfers in hall housing are enrolled automatically in a meal plan, set by the hall. The Unlimited plan costs [$3,802 per semester or $7,604 a year](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/dining/meal-plans-rates/undergraduate-meal-plans) for 2026-27, with $400 Big Red Bucks a semester. Cornell Dining calls it "fully covered by financial aid for eligible students." The West Campus houses require the House Meal Plan at the same price. Cascadilla, Schuyler, Sheldon Court and the other named South Campus halls carry the Collegetown plan at $7,056 a year. Every plan adds a $50 administrative fee once a year.

## The health plan: the bill aid leaves out

The Student Health Plan sits [outside the cost of attendance](https://finaid.cornell.edu/cost-attend), so aid does not budget for it. The $600 Cornell Health fee in your mandatory fees is a different charge.

Full-time students must [enroll or waive with proof of comparable coverage](https://studenthealthbenefits.cornell.edu/). Students who do nothing are enrolled and billed. A new fall 2026 student paid [$1,705 for fall and $2,046 for spring](https://studenthealthbenefits.cornell.edu/shp-costs-billing-important-dates/shp-costs-dates-2026-27), $3,751 for the first year by our addition. The waiver deadline for fall 2026 admits was [July 31, 2026](https://studenthealthbenefits.cornell.edu/node/179); after it, waivers lock. Waivers are checked again each semester.

The [Tatkon Center's finance page](https://scl.cornell.edu/get-involved/tatkon-center-new-students/new-students-cornell/transfer/finances) sets out the billing rhythm. Fall tuition is billed in early July and spring in January, due before registration. Fall housing appears on the July or August bill and spring housing in December; meal plans bill in July and January. E-bills come through Cashnet, and parents see them only if the student invites them.

Three tools soften the bill: the Cornell Installment Plan, the elective Tuition Refund Plan and CornellCard. If your package includes work-study, any campus job needs an I-9: student workers show original identity and work-eligibility documents. Pack the originals when you move to Ithaca.

Grant aid beyond tuition and mandatory fees is [taxable and reported on the 1098-T](https://finaid.cornell.edu/apply-aid/prospective-students-or-first-time-aid-applicants). A student whose grants also pay for housing and food should expect that part to count as income at tax time.

## What other transfers ran into

In spring 2021, [The Cornell Daily Sun reported](https://cornellsun.com/2021/05/14/admitted-students-await-financial-aid-offers-weeks-after-commitment-deadlines/) that admitted first-years and transfers had to commit by May 3 before many aid offers arrived. Some students waited on the phone more than two hours, and one student described her offer as mostly loans. The vice provost for enrollment cited higher volume and changing finances during the pandemic. Today's process sends a Cornell Aid Estimate soon after admission; answer document requests fast so it arrives before you must commit.

A [2024 Cornell Chronicle story](https://www.news.cornell.edu/stories/2024/05/community-college-transfers-flourish-cornell) on community college transfers reported that students assumed Cornell was too expensive. The income lines above say otherwise for most of them.

In February 2017, the Sun reported that an admissions and aid working group listed [need-aware transfer admission](https://cornellsun.com/2017/02/26/cornell-considering-need-aware-admissions-for-transfers-additional-controversial-measures/) among contingency options, and a transfer on aid called it a "terrible policy." Current admissions pages say US admission is need-blind.

## Common mistakes

- **Trusting the wrong Cornell.** Searches for "Cornell transfer scholarship" surface [Cornell College in Mount Vernon, Iowa](https://www.cornellcollege.edu/financial-assistance/scholarships-awards/transfer-scholarships.shtml), with a $33,000 transfer scholarship and FAFSA code 001856. Neither applies to Ithaca; listing 001856 sends your FAFSA to the wrong school.
- **Filing aid forms after March 15, 2027.** Your aid then lags your decision. In 2021, students committed before their offers arrived.
- **Skipping aid because the sticker price looks impossible.** In fall 2021, 32.7% of transfers got [Cornell need-based grants averaging $44,712](https://irp.cornell.edu/wp-content/uploads/2021/08/Profile2021-Fall-Transfers.pdf), out of 38.9% who applied.
- **Forgetting the health plan.** No waiver by the deadline puts $1,705 on the fall bill.
- **Leaving changed finances unreported.** If your family's finances change after you file, appeal at finaid@cornell.edu with your name and admissions ID.
- **Missing HESC as a New Yorker.** New York State aid then stays out of your package.

## FAQ

**Does Cornell give merit scholarships to transfers?** No. Cornell's aid is need-based only, for transfers as for first-years, and [meets 100% of demonstrated need](https://admissions.cornell.edu/apply/transfer-applicants).

**Will my aid offer arrive before I have to commit?** Cornell sends an Aid Estimate soon after admission and the official offer [tentatively in early April](https://finaid.cornell.edu/apply-aid/prospective-students-or-first-time-aid-applicants) for students already admitted. Answer document requests quickly so the estimate is in hand before your deposit.

**Do New York residents pay less at Arts and Sciences or Engineering?** No. Those are endowed colleges, where tuition is [$73,946 for 2026-27](https://news.cornell.edu/stories/2026/03/board-trustees-approves-2026-27-budget-parameters) for everyone; the $49,816 rate applies only at CALS, Human Ecology, ILR, Brooks and Dyson. File HESC with code 0175 for an endowed college.

**Is Cornell need-aware for transfers?** Not for US applicants: domestic admission is [need-blind](https://admissions.cornell.edu/apply/transfer-applicants). Undocumented applicants count as domestic.

**Do I have to live on campus if a cheaper apartment exists?** Yes, for one academic year, unless you lived on campus two years at your last school or [enter as a junior or senior](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/campus-housing/new-transfer-undergraduates).
