```glance
- Cornell gives no preference by school type; about a quarter of its transfers come from community colleges.
- Admitted transfers generally have a 3.3 college GPA or better; CALS sets a 3.0 floor (3.5 for Biological Sciences).
- Fall 2027 deadlines: application, fee and aid forms March 15, 2027; everything else April 15.
- A course counts if it is about 80% like the Cornell course and earns a C or better; most colleges take up to 60 credits.
- No community college has a guarantee: TC3 to CALS gives "priority consideration" only.
- Families earning up to $125,000 with typical assets get tuition covered by grants (2026-27).
```

Cornell [gives no preference by school type](https://faq.enrollment.cornell.edu/kb/article/325-does-cornell-prefer-transfer-applicants-from-certain-colleges-or-types-of-colleges/), says about a quarter of its transfers come from community colleges, and reports admitted transfers generally at 3.3 or better. You apply like every transfer: to one Cornell college, through the Transfer Common App, by [March 15, 2027](https://admissions.cornell.edu/apply/transfer-applicants), with the rest of your file by April 15. Credit [cannot be denied because a course was at a community college or online](https://registrar.cornell.edu/students/credit-evaluation-services), so the real work is picking courses that match your target college's list and earning a C or better in each.

```reader "If you're at a community college now"
Pick the Cornell college first, then take its listed courses for letter grades. A C or better counts if the course is about 80% like the Cornell one; pass/fail earns nothing in CALS or Human Ecology. Aim for a 3.3 or better. If you finish an associate's degree, CALS places you as a junior and you cannot ask for sophomore entry. Ask one instructor of a full-credit course for the Academic Evaluation. The costliest mistake: treating a pathway like TC3's as a guarantee and filing no backup applications.
```

```reader "If you're transferring on a tight budget"
Apply for aid. Cornell meets 100% of demonstrated need, and domestic admission is need-blind. Up to $125,000 family income with typical assets, grants cover tuition; up to $75,000, they cover the full cost of attendance (2026-27). The $85 application fee is waived automatically if it is a burden and you apply for aid. The CSS Profile fee is waived for adjusted gross income up to $100,000. File the CSS Profile (code 2098) and FAFSA (002711) by March 15, 2027. Missing that date can push your aid offer past the point you must commit.
```

```reader "If you're a New York resident"
Five Cornell colleges charge New York residents contract tuition: CALS, Human Ecology, ILR, Brooks and Dyson. That is $49,816 for 2026-27 against $73,946 at Arts and Sciences, Engineering, Hotel and AAP. A required first year with a hall double and the Unlimited meal plan bills $72,188 against $96,318. File with HESC as well (code 0174 for contract colleges, 0175 for endowed) by March 15, 2027. For HEOP at a contract college, email stateprograms@cornell.edu. Applying to an endowed college for a subject a contract college also offers costs $24,130 a year more before aid.
```

```reader "If you're aiming at Engineering"
Engineering will not review your application if any of its four core courses is missing: two semesters of calculus, calculus-based physics with lab, and general chemistry with lab. They can be in progress at application time. A C counts; a C- does not. Engineering takes up to 33 credits per full-time year and 66 in all. Chemical Engineering says to apply as a sophomore if possible, because some of its sophomore courses are not offered at most two-year colleges. Waiting for the associate's degree can close that major.
```

```reader "If you're older or working full time"
If you have been out of school two or more years, a personal or employer recommendation can stand in for the instructor's Academic Evaluation. Check course dates: courses seven or more years old generally do not transfer. Community college graduates Cornell profiled in 2024 described working 40-plus hours a week. If you cannot move to Ithaca, the June 2026 SUNY Broome pathway leads to Cornell's online, part-time Bachelor of Professional Studies, a separate degree that requires you to be at least four years past high school.
```

## Your odds, in numbers

Cornell's [enrollment FAQ](https://faq.enrollment.cornell.edu/kb/article/325-does-cornell-prefer-transfer-applicants-from-certain-colleges-or-types-of-colleges/) says it does not prefer any institution or type of institution. It puts the community college share at about a quarter of transfers. Cornell's only published count is older and lower. In the [fall 2021 transfer profile](https://irp.cornell.edu/wp-content/uploads/2021/08/Profile2021-Fall-Transfers.pdf), 96 of 674 expected fall transfers came from two-year or community colleges, which is 14.2% by our division. Of those 96, 34 came from New York schools and 62 from other states.

A May 2024 [Cornell Chronicle feature](https://www.news.cornell.edu/stories/2024/05/community-college-transfers-flourish-cornell) gives a third figure: about 100 community college students admitted each year on average. That is an admit count, not an enrollment count.

| Measure | Figure | What it counts |
|---|---|---|
| Cornell's stated share | About a quarter of transfers | Enrollment FAQ, undated |
| Official count, fall 2021 | 96 of 674 fall transfers (14.2%) | July 2021 deposit data |
| Admits per year | About 100 on average | Chronicle, May 2024 |
| All transfer applicants, fall 2025 | 7,381 applied, 864 admitted (11.7%) | Common Data Set 2025-26 |
| Typical admitted GPA | "Generally" 3.3 or better | Enrollment FAQ |

The fall 2025 row comes from the [2025-26 Common Data Set](https://irp.cornell.edu/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/Cornell-Common-Data-Set-2025-26.pdf); the 11.7% is our division of 864 by 7,381. Cornell publishes no admit rate for community college applicants alone.

What admissions weighs most, per the same FAQ: an essay showing interest in a specific program, your grades at your current school, and professor recommendations. Your high school record and activities are read too.

Forum talk runs harsher than Cornell's own numbers. In a [2014 College Confidential thread](https://talk.collegeconfidential.com/t/what-are-my-chances-for-transferring-to-cornell/1642313), a community college student with a 3.5 got split replies: one poster said a near-flawless GPA was needed, another that the statutory (contract) colleges take community college transfers. A [CollegeVine answer](https://www.collegevine.com/faq/182748/advice-for-transferring-from-community-college-to-cornell) advises about 3.5 to near 4.0. Treat both as opinion. Cornell's figure is 3.3, and the college you pick sets its own.

## Step 1: check that you can apply now

You need a high school diploma or equivalent plus [at least 12 semester hours of college credit](https://admissions.cornell.edu/apply/transfer-applicants) earned after high school, or full-time enrollment elsewhere. Exam credit such as AP does not count toward the 12. Cornell takes students who have finished [two to four semesters](https://faq.enrollment.cornell.edu/kb/article/330-when-should-i-apply-to-cornell-as-a-transfer/) elsewhere; students in their first college semester cannot apply yet. So the earliest application comes in the spring of your first community college year, for sophomore entry.

Cornell admits transfers for the fall only. Anyone who already holds a bachelor's degree is not eligible.

## Step 2: choose the college, because that sets everything

You apply to one of Cornell's colleges, and usually one major, not to the university. That college reads your file, sets the courses you need and later evaluates your credit. In fall 2021, [472 of 674 transfers](https://irp.cornell.edu/wp-content/uploads/2021/08/Profile2021-Fall-Transfers.pdf) (70.0% by our division) entered the four contract colleges of that year, and CALS alone took 243.

| College | GPA signal | Courses that matter for a community college student |
|---|---|---|
| CALS | 3.0 minimum; 3.5 for Biological Sciences | Major-specific foundations, often English composition and biology or chemistry with lab |
| Human Ecology | 3.5 or higher is competitive | 23 credits for sophomores: lab science (8), writing (6), statistics (3), precalculus or calculus (3), intro psychology (3) |
| Engineering | No minimum stated | The calculus, physics and chemistry block; missing any course means no review |
| University-wide | "Generally" 3.3 or better | Each college's own list |

The [CALS transfer page](https://cals.cornell.edu/education/admissions/undergraduate-admissions/transfer-applicants) sets the 3.0 floor and lists closed majors: Global and Public Health Sciences takes no transfers, Global Development takes sophomores only, and Applied Economics and Management applies through Dyson. [Human Ecology](https://www.human.cornell.edu/admissions/transfer) wants letter-graded coursework and no pass/fail. The GPA guide by college is at [/transfer/cornell-transfer-gpa-requirements-by-college/](/transfer/cornell-transfer-gpa-requirements-by-college/).

## Step 3: take courses that will transfer

The university rule is short. A US school must be [regionally accredited](https://registrar.cornell.edu/students/credit-evaluation-services). A course needs to be about 80% similar to the Cornell course in scope, content and learning outcomes, and the Cornell department that owns that course decides from your syllabus. Two outside courses can combine into one Cornell course. And equivalency cannot be denied because a course was taken at a community college or delivered online.

| Rule | What it means for your courses |
|---|---|
| Grade | C or better; a major may require up to a B; Engineering accepts C, not C- |
| Grading basis | CALS and Human Ecology take letter grades only, no pass/fail |
| Match | About 80% similar; keep every syllabus |
| Age | Courses seven or more years old generally do not transfer |
| Never transfers | Orientation, study skills, career development, math below precalculus, non-credit remedial courses, MOOCs |
| Cap | Up to 60 credits in most colleges; Engineering 66 (33 per full-time year); Architecture 64 |
| Quarter system | Quarter credits count at two-thirds |
| Your Cornell GPA | Community college grades do not enter it; the transcript shows only the school's name and total credits |

Credit is evaluated after admission, case by case. CALS [cannot pre-approve your schedule](https://cals.cornell.edu/education/admissions/undergraduate-admissions/transfer-applicants) or evaluate credit before an offer, and Engineering offers no pre-evaluation either. Your best guide before then is the target college's course list and the 80% test. Applicants take credit questions to Undergraduate Admissions, [+1 (607) 255-5241](https://admissions.cornell.edu/apply/transfer-applicants); once enrolled, you ask your college registrar.

## Step 4: decide between sophomore and junior entry

An associate's degree usually means about 60 credits. That fits the 60-credit cap most colleges use, so a full associate's degree loses nothing to the cap. Cornell's warning against junior transfer in [its FAQ](https://faq.enrollment.cornell.edu/kb/article/323-can-i-transfer-to-cornell-in-my-junior-year-from-another-college/) is aimed at students finishing a junior year at a four-year school, where the cap throws away credits.

The standing rules still bind you. In CALS, two semesters mean sophomore standing, and four semesters or an associate's degree mean junior standing. A junior-eligible student cannot choose sophomore entry.

Engineering is the exception to "finish the degree first." Its [requirements page](https://www.duffield.cornell.edu/admissions/transfer-applicants/requirements) says Chemical Engineering applicants should apply as sophomores if possible, because some sophomore chemical engineering courses are not offered at most two-year colleges.

Example: a student at a SUNY community college finishes an associate's degree with 60 semester credits and applies to CALS. CALS places the student as a junior. If every credit is accepted, all 60 count. With 66 credits, the six beyond the cap are lost. The student cannot ask to come in as a sophomore instead.

Example: a student finishes one community college year with calculus I and II, physics with lab and chemistry with lab, and wants Chemical Engineering. Applying that spring for sophomore entry follows the department's advice. Waiting a year risks missing sophomore courses the community college does not teach.

## Step 5: use a pathway if one fits, but keep backups

There is no general guaranteed transfer from a community college to Cornell. The newest agreement uses the words "priority consideration," not guarantee. The full list, with each one's terms, is at [/transfer/cornell-guaranteed-transfer-pathways/](/transfer/cornell-guaranteed-transfer-pathways/).

| Pathway | Into | What it offers | Announced |
|---|---|---|---|
| TC3 Sustainable Farming and Food Systems A.A.S. | CALS Agricultural Sciences B.S. | "Priority consideration" with a 3.0 GPA and B or better in all transfer courses | August 2025 |
| SUNY Broome business administration A.S. | Online, part-time Bachelor of Professional Studies (School of Continuing Education) | Transfer of eligible credits; 45 to 60 transferable credits; at least four years past high school | June 2026 |
| SUNY Broome science A.S. | CALS Food Science and Food Operations and Management | 2+2 agreements | 2017 |
| Pathways to Success: Monroe CC, Morrisville (2006); BMCC, Suffolk CC, Nassau CC (2008) | Cornell | Partnership for high-achieving, low-income community college students | 2006 and 2008 |

The [TC3 pathway](https://cals.cornell.edu/news/2025/08/cornell-cals-and-tc3-establish-transfer-pathway-for-agriculture-students) runs one year at Tompkins Cortland Community College (two semesters plus a summer), then four semesters at Cornell. It publishes no separate deadlines, so the central March 15 date applies.

The [SUNY Broome online pathway](https://news.cornell.edu/stories/2026/06/cornell-sce-suny-broome-create-transfer-pathway-new-online-bachelors-degree) opens applications in January 2027 for a first cohort in August 2027. It leads to a different, mostly asynchronous online degree, not to a residential Cornell college.

The [2017 SUNY Broome agreements](https://cals.cornell.edu/news/2017/02/suny-broome-announce-transfer-agreement-cornell) and the [Pathways to Success partnership](https://news.cornell.edu/node/274405) are older. More than 55 students from Monroe and Morrisville had enrolled under Pathways to Success by July 2008, funded by the Jack Kent Cooke Foundation. Ask your transfer counselor and CALS admissions (cals_admissions@cornell.edu, 607-255-2036) whether an older agreement still applies before you plan around it.

## Step 6: apply by the two deadlines

```figure

title: Fall 2027 transfer dates
rows:
  - {label: "FAFSA opens", date: "October 1, 2026"}
  - {label: "Application, $85 fee or waiver, portfolio; CSS Profile, FAFSA and HESC", date: "March 15, 2027"}
  - {label: "College Report, Academic Evaluation, Mid-Term Report, transcripts", date: "April 15, 2027"}
  - {label: "Rolling decisions", date: "Late April through June 2027"}
```

The [admissions page](https://admissions.cornell.edu/apply/transfer-applicants) splits the file in two. By March 15: the Transfer Common App with Cornell's Writing Supplement, the $85 fee or a fee waiver, and any portfolio. By April 15: the College Report, the Academic Evaluation, the Mid-Term Report, every college transcript and your final high school transcript.

- **College Report:** a standing form an official at your community college fills out. You request it through Cornell's Applicant Portal.
- **Academic Evaluation:** from an instructor of a full-credit college course. Out of school two or more years? A personal or employer recommendation can replace it.
- **Mid-Term Report:** you fill this out yourself, listing spring courses with official or estimated grades.
- **Essay:** 250 to 650 words on how continuing at a new school helps your goals, plus the college's own supplement.
- **Tests:** SAT and ACT are neither required nor expected.

Decisions roll from late April through June. Hearing nothing in April is not a denial. The full deadline guide is at [/transfer/cornell-transfer-deadlines-decision-dates/](/transfer/cornell-transfer-deadlines-decision-dates/).

## What it costs, and why the price is not the answer

The 2024 Chronicle feature quotes a counselor calling the belief that Cornell is unaffordable a common misconception among community college students. The aid rules back that up. Cornell [meets 100% of demonstrated need](https://admissions.cornell.edu/apply/transfer-applicants) with grants, work-study and reasonable loans, and domestic admission is need-blind. There is no merit aid.

For 2026-27, families with incomes up to [$125,000 and typical assets](https://news.cornell.edu/stories/2026/03/board-trustees-approves-2026-27-budget-parameters) get grants covering tuition. Up to $75,000, grants, scholarships and work-study cover the full cost of attendance. In fall 2021, [220 transfers](https://irp.cornell.edu/wp-content/uploads/2021/08/Profile2021-Fall-Transfers.pdf) (32.7%) received Cornell need-based grants averaging $44,712.

The billed totals come from Cornell's [cost of attendance page](https://finaid.cornell.edu/cost-attend): tuition, $1,024 in fees, a $13,744 double and $7,604 in meals. New transfers [must live on campus for one academic year](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/campus-housing/new-transfer-undergraduates), so a sophomore entrant pays those lines. Students who enter as juniors are exempt and can rent instead. Prices for 2027-28 are set in spring 2027.

**The aid steps.** File the CSS Profile (code 2098) and the FAFSA (code 002711) by [March 15, 2027](https://finaid.cornell.edu/apply-aid/prospective-students-or-first-time-aid-applicants). New York residents also file with HESC: 0174 for contract colleges, 0175 for endowed. The CSS fee is waived automatically for adjusted gross income up to $100,000. A Cornell Aid Estimate comes soon after admission; answer document requests fast so it arrives before you must commit. Undocumented applicants, with or without DACA, are treated as domestic; those who graduated from a US high school are eligible for Cornell aid but not federal aid.

**Health insurance is extra.** The [Student Health Plan](https://studenthealthbenefits.cornell.edu/shp-costs-billing-important-dates/shp-costs-dates-2026-27) sits outside the cost of attendance. A new fall 2026 student was billed $1,705 for fall and $2,046 for spring unless they waived it with comparable coverage.

**EOP and HEOP.** These [opportunity programs](https://oadi.cornell.edu/node/50) use the same forms. Applicants to contract colleges email stateprograms@cornell.edu; applicants to Arts and Sciences, Engineering, AAP or Hotel file the HEOP Transfer Certification form. Fall 2027 applicants submit 2025 financial documents.

## What 2024 graduates said, and the help that exists

The Chronicle profiled four 2024 graduates who came from LaGuardia Community College, Monroe Community College (two of the four) and SUNY Niagara. The challenges the feature describes: working 40-plus hours a week, feeling behind, imposter feelings, and assuming Cornell cost too much.

The same feature names the supports:

- A transfer student success navigator in CALS, with similar roles in ILR and Human Ecology.
- PAVER, formerly HHMI-CURT.
- The CC STEP group.
- SUNY and CUNY pathways.

Ask about the navigator in your college once you are admitted. The other routes in, including the Transfer Option, are on the [pillar guide](/transfer/transferring-to-cornell-guide/).

## Common mistakes

- **Treating a pathway as a guarantee.** TC3 offers priority consideration, not admission. A denial with no backup applications costs a full year.
- **Taking a key course pass/fail or finishing below a C.** It earns no credit in CALS or Human Ecology, and a C- fails in Engineering. You retake the course at Cornell.
- **Finishing the associate's degree when you want sophomore entry to CALS.** Junior standing is fixed, and you cannot choose otherwise.
- **Waiting two years for Chemical Engineering.** Some sophomore courses are not taught at most two-year colleges, which can leave you short of the major's requirements.
- **Skipping aid because the price looks impossible.** Under $125,000 with typical assets, grants cover the $49,816 or $73,946 tuition. Missing March 15 can delay the offer past your commitment date.
- **Throwing away syllabi.** The department judges the 80% match from them after admission.

## FAQ

**Does Cornell look down on community college grades?** Cornell's FAQ says it has no preference by institution or type. A 2014 forum reply claimed community college grades run about a point high; nothing official supports it.

**Will my online classes transfer?** Yes, on the same terms. The [registrar's policy](https://registrar.cornell.edu/students/credit-evaluation-services) says equivalency cannot be denied because a course was delivered online.

**Do SUNY and CUNY credits transfer?** Under the same rules as any accredited school: about an 80% match and a C or better, judged course by course after admission. Only TC3's farming degree has a current named pathway into a residential Cornell college.

**Can I apply after one semester at community college?** No. You need two to four semesters done, so apply in the spring of your first year for sophomore entry.

**Can I start in January?** No. Cornell admits external transfers for the fall semester only.

**Do I need the SAT?** No. SAT and ACT scores are neither required nor expected for transfers.

**Will my community college GPA follow me?** No. Your Cornell GPA starts fresh, and the transcript shows only your old school's name and total transfer credits.
