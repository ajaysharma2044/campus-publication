```glance
- GPA: a 3.0 overall minimum, 3.5 for Biological Sciences; Cornell-wide, admitted transfers are generally 3.3 or better.
- Standing: two semesters done means sophomore entry; four semesters or an associate's degree means junior entry; more than four semesters at a four-year school means you cannot apply.
- Majors: you apply to one major; Global and Public Health Sciences takes no transfers, Global Development takes sophomores only, and AEM goes through Dyson.
- Credit: up to 60 credits including AP (15 at most), letter grade C or better, no pass/fail.
- Dates for fall 2027: application and $85 fee by March 15, 2027; other materials by April 15; decisions late April through June.
- NY residents pay $49,816 contract tuition in 2026-27, against $73,946 at endowed colleges.
```

To transfer into Cornell's College of Agriculture and Life Sciences, you apply through the Common App for Transfer to one CALS major, for fall only, with at least two full-time semesters done and a [3.0 GPA or higher (3.5 for Biological Sciences)](https://cals.cornell.edu/education/admissions/undergraduate-admissions/transfer-applicants). For fall 2027 the application is due [March 15, 2027 and the rest of the file April 15](https://admissions.cornell.edu/apply/transfer-applicants). Take your major's foundation courses for letter grades first: Cornell says [coursework and the essay matter most](https://faq.enrollment.cornell.edu/kb/article/325-does-cornell-prefer-transfer-applicants-from-certain-colleges-or-types-of-colleges/).

```reader "If you're a sophomore transfer from a four-year college"
You apply in the spring of your first year, with two semesters done or in progress, and CALS places you as a sophomore. All your credits up to 60 can count, so nothing is lost on the cap. Global Development is open to you and closed to juniors. Your required courses are this year's courses: English composition, and biology or chemistry with lab for most majors. Take them for a letter grade, since [CALS takes no pass/fail](https://cals.cornell.edu/education/admissions/undergraduate-admissions/transfer-applicants). The costly mistake is sending the March 15 pieces and forgetting the April 15 ones: the College Report, the Academic Evaluation, the Mid-Term Report and your transcripts.
```

```reader "If you're applying as a junior"
Four semesters or an associate's degree puts you in junior standing, and CALS will not let you choose sophomore entry instead. More than four semesters at a four-year school makes you ineligible for CALS. Cornell's own FAQ [discourages junior transfer from four-year schools](https://faq.enrollment.cornell.edu/kb/article/323-can-i-transfer-to-cornell-in-my-junior-year-from-another-college/) because of the roughly 60-credit cap. Juniors need more of the major done: Animal Science, for example, [expects junior transfers to have met CALS chemistry and math](https://cals.cornell.edu/animal-science/degrees-programs/undergraduate-studies/transfer-curriculum). The costly mistake is arriving with 90 credits: 30 do not count, and college takes ten semesters in all.
```

```reader "If you're at a community college"
Cornell says it has [no preference by school type](https://faq.enrollment.cornell.edu/kb/article/325-does-cornell-prefer-transfer-applicants-from-certain-colleges-or-types-of-colleges/) and that about a quarter of its transfers come from community colleges. A course [cannot be refused equivalency](https://registrar.cornell.edu/students/credit-evaluation-services) just because it was taken at a community college or online. You do not need the degree to apply: two full-time semesters are enough for sophomore entry. Finish an associate's and you enter CALS as a junior, with no option to come in as a sophomore. If you are at TC3 in Sustainable Farming and Food Systems, the 2025 pathway gives "priority consideration" for Agricultural Sciences, not a guarantee. The costly mistake is treating that pathway as admission and applying nowhere else.
```

```reader "If you're a New York resident"
CALS is a contract college, so New York residents pay [$49,816 in tuition for 2026-27](https://news.cornell.edu/stories/2026/03/board-trustees-approves-2026-27-budget-parameters), against $73,946 at endowed colleges such as Arts and Sciences. Your billed required year with a hall double and the Unlimited plan comes to [$72,188, against $96,318](https://finaid.cornell.edu/cost-attend). File HESC with [code 0174](https://finaid.cornell.edu/apply-aid/prospective-students-or-first-time-aid-applicants) besides the CSS Profile and FAFSA, all by March 15, 2027. The costly mistake is planning to switch into Applied Economics and Management after you arrive: CALS transfers cannot change to or add AEM, so apply through Dyson if that is your major.
```

```reader "If you're a Transfer Option holder"
You use the [Transfer Option application](https://admissions.cornell.edu/transfer-option-applicants) with your old portal login, pay no fee, and send the CALS extras: a resume, your course schedule, AP scores and two essays (a SlideRoom portfolio for Landscape Architecture). The deadline was March 15 for fall 2026, and Cornell opens the next application in winter 2027. The offer is an invitation to apply, not admission, and each college sets its own conditions. Meet the CALS floors anyway: a 3.0, or 3.5 for Biological Sciences, plus your major's foundation courses. The costly mistake is skipping a backup plan for your second year.
```

## Who can apply, and at what standing

CALS takes external transfers [for the fall only](https://cals.cornell.edu/education/admissions/undergraduate-admissions/transfer-applicants). You need at least two full-time semesters, or three quarters, of transferable coursework after high school, completed or in progress. That means a first-year student can apply in the spring of that first year.

How many semesters you have done sets your standing, and you do not get to pick it.

| Your record when you enroll | CALS standing | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Two full-time semesters (or three quarters) | Sophomore | The usual entry point |
| Four semesters, or an associate's degree | Junior | Cannot choose sophomore entry instead |
| More than four semesters at a four-year school | Not eligible | CALS will not review the application |
| A bachelor's degree already | Not eligible | [Cornell does not admit second-bachelor's students](https://admissions.cornell.edu/apply/transfer-applicants) |

The Cornell-wide floor is [12 semester hours of college credit after high school](https://admissions.cornell.edu/apply/transfer-applicants) or full-time enrollment elsewhere. CALS's two-semester rule is stricter, so it is the one that applies to you.

## The GPA you need

CALS sets a **3.0 overall minimum** and **3.5 for Biological Sciences**, per its [transfer page](https://cals.cornell.edu/education/admissions/undergraduate-admissions/transfer-applicants). That is a floor, not a target. Across all colleges, Cornell says admitted transfers [generally have a 3.3 or better](https://faq.enrollment.cornell.edu/kb/article/325-does-cornell-prefer-transfer-applicants-from-certain-colleges-or-types-of-colleges/), and it calls the process selective and highly competitive.

Review is holistic. The same FAQ names the factors that matter most: the essay showing interest in the program, your college coursework, and professor recommendations. Your high school record and activities are read too.

Advice: if you sit near 3.0, the courses for your major and a specific essay carry more weight than another half-point of electives.

## Majors you can and cannot apply to

You apply directly to one major, and [you cannot change it before you arrive](https://cals.cornell.edu/education/admissions/undergraduate-admissions/transfer-applicants). Three majors have their own rules.

| Major | Open to external transfers? | What it means for you |
|---|---|---|
| Global and Public Health Sciences | No | Pick another major or another college |
| Global Development | Sophomores only | Junior-standing applicants cannot apply |
| Applied Economics and Management (AEM) | Only through the Dyson School | CALS transfers cannot change to or add AEM later |
| Biological Sciences | Yes, with a 3.5 minimum | Strict prerequisites, listed below |
| Agricultural Sciences | Yes | The TC3 pathway leads here |
| All other CALS majors | Yes, 3.0 minimum | Each has foundation courses |

The AEM rule catches people. If AEM is the goal, Dyson's [own transfer rules](/transfer/cornell-dyson-transfer-requirements/) apply, and Dyson is also a contract college, so the New York tuition break still holds.

## Courses to take before you apply

Requirements vary by major. Most CALS majors [commonly want English composition or a writing-intensive course, and biology or chemistry with lab](https://faq.enrollment.cornell.edu/kb/article/101-what-courses-are-required-to-apply-for-transfer-admission-into-cals/). Each major lists foundation courses you should have finished or in progress when you apply.

Biological Sciences is the most specific. Its [admission page](https://cals.cornell.edu/biological-sciences/admission-biological-sciences-major) asks for:

```figure
kind: steps
title: Biological Sciences prerequisites for transfers
rows:
  - label: One academic year of introductory biology with labs
  - label: General Chemistry I and II with labs
  - label: Calculus I, plus Calculus II or Statistics
  - label: AP or IB biology does not count toward the biology year
```

Junior applicants need more of the major in hand. Animal Science, for one, [expects junior transfers to have met CALS chemistry and math](https://cals.cornell.edu/animal-science/degrees-programs/undergraduate-studies/transfer-curriculum).

CALS [cannot pre-approve your schedule](https://cals.cornell.edu/education/admissions/undergraduate-admissions/transfer-applicants) or give an official credit evaluation before admission. Match your courses to the major's foundation list yourself, and keep every syllabus.

## How CALS counts your credits

Credit is evaluated after admission, case by case. The [CALS rules](https://cals.cornell.edu/education/admissions/undergraduate-admissions/transfer-applicants) are:

| Rule | CALS standard |
|---|---|
| School | Regionally accredited; online courses included |
| Grade | Letter grade of C or better; no pass/fail |
| Proof | Official transcript |
| AP and other test credit | Up to 15 credits |
| Test plus transfer credit | Up to 60 credits in all |
| Quarter credits | Multiplied by two-thirds |

The [registrar's policy](https://registrar.cornell.edu/students/credit-evaluation-services) adds the test for equivalency: a course must be about 80% similar in scope, content and outcomes to the Cornell course, and the department that owns that course decides from the syllabus. Courses seven or more years old are generally not eligible. Math below precalculus, study skills and remedial courses never transfer. You must also earn at least 60 credits at Cornell.

Example: a student arrives with 90 semester credits, 15 a semester. CALS accepts at most 60, so 30 are lost. With 60 still needed at Cornell, that is four more semesters and ten in all, two more than a standard four-year degree. At 2026-27 endowed rates the extra year is $74,970 in tuition and fees ($73,946 plus $1,024).

Example: a sophomore applicant has 32 college credits and 20 AP credits. Only 15 AP credits can count, so at most 47 credits come in, well under the 60 cap.

## Deadlines and what you submit

CALS uses the central Cornell dates. For fall 2027, per [Undergraduate Admissions](https://admissions.cornell.edu/apply/transfer-applicants):

  - {label: "Rolling decisions", date: "Late April through June 2027"}

The $85 fee is waived automatically when it is a burden and you plan to apply for aid. The College Report comes from an official at your current school; the Academic Evaluation comes from an instructor; the Mid-Term Report is yours to fill in. Aid forms follow the [same March 15, 2027 date](https://finaid.cornell.edu/apply-aid/prospective-students-or-first-time-aid-applicants), and FAFSA opens October 1, 2026. CALS says transfers use the [same aid process as first-years](https://cals.cornell.edu/education/admissions/undergraduate-admissions/transfer-applicants).

## Community college and the TC3 pathway

In fall 2021, 96 of 674 incoming Cornell transfers came from two-year schools, per the [IRP profile](https://irp.cornell.edu/wp-content/uploads/2021/08/Profile2021-Fall-Transfers.pdf), and Cornell admits [about 100 community college transfers a year](https://www.news.cornell.edu/stories/2024/05/community-college-transfers-flourish-cornell) on average.

The one current CALS agreement is with Tompkins Cortland Community College (TC3). Announced [August 7, 2025](https://cals.cornell.edu/news/2025/08/cornell-cals-and-tc3-establish-transfer-pathway-for-agriculture-students), it gives graduates of TC3's Sustainable Farming and Food Systems A.A.S. "priority consideration" for the CALS Agricultural Sciences B.S. The terms:

- A 3.0 GPA and a B or better in every transfer course.
- One year at TC3 (two semesters plus a summer), then four semesters at Cornell.
- No guarantee, and no separate deadline: you apply by March 15 like everyone else.

The [Signature Pathway Program page](https://cals.cornell.edu/education/admissions/undergraduate-admissions/signature-pathway-program) names TC3 as its only partner and promises "strong admissions consideration." The full list sits on our [pathways page](/transfer/cornell-guaranteed-transfer-pathways/).

## Transfer Option holders applying to CALS

If Cornell offered you the Transfer Option as a first-year applicant, you apply after one full-time year elsewhere through the [Transfer Option application](https://admissions.cornell.edu/transfer-option-applicants), not the Common App. CALS asks for a resume, your course schedule, AP scores and two essays; Landscape Architecture adds a portfolio through SlideRoom. Everyone sends all college transcripts, the final high school transcript, the College Report and the Mid-Term Report. Decisions roll from mid-April, most in late April or early May.

Is it guaranteed? Cornell sends questions about academic requirements to the college that made the offer, so confirm your conditions with CALS admissions. Richard Ballard, who spent his first year at Ithaca College on the Transfer Option and is now a CALS junior, told [The Cornell Daily Sun in 2025](https://www.cornellsun.com/article/2025/08/from-both-sides-of-the-hill-an-ic-transfers-perspective) that it was described to him inconsistently, as guaranteed and then not. More on the conditions is in our [Transfer Option guide](/transfer/cornell-transfer-option-guide/).

## What CALS costs a transfer in 2026-27

CALS is one of Cornell's contract colleges, so residency changes the bill. New transfers [must live on campus for one academic year](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/campus-housing/new-transfer-undergraduates) unless they enter as juniors or lived on campus two years elsewhere.

  - {label: "Billed year, NY resident (hall double, Unlimited meals)", value: "$72,188"}
  - {label: "Billed year, non-NY resident (same)", value: "$96,318"}

Tuition comes from the [trustees' 2026-27 budget](https://news.cornell.edu/stories/2026/03/board-trustees-approves-2026-27-budget-parameters); the billed totals include $1,024 in fees and a $13,744 double plus $7,604 in food, per [Cornell's cost of attendance](https://finaid.cornell.edu/cost-attend). Families earning up to $125,000 with typical assets get tuition covered by grants, and up to $75,000 the full cost. New York applicants seeking HEOP at CALS [email stateprograms@cornell.edu](https://oadi.cornell.edu/node/50) rather than filing the certification form. Cornell [confirms 2027-28 rates before aid offers go out in early April 2027](https://finaid.cornell.edu/apply-aid/prospective-students-or-first-time-aid-applicants).

## After you are admitted

In 2024, CALS [emailed admitted transfers in early to mid-June](https://scl.cornell.edu/get-involved/tatkon-center-new-students/new-students-cornell/transfer/course-enrollment/college-school-links) about credit and enrollment. Send syllabi when asked, and keep them until your degree audit is right. Once admitted, take credit questions to CALS student services at cals-studentservices@cornell.edu. Before admission, questions go to CALS admissions at cals_admissions@cornell.edu or [607-255-2036](https://cals.cornell.edu/education/admissions/undergraduate-admissions/transfer-applicants).

Transfers are [nearly one-third of CALS students](https://cals.cornell.edu/education/admissions/undergraduate-admissions/transfer-applicants), so you will not be rare. CALS also has a [transfer student success navigator](https://www.news.cornell.edu/stories/2024/05/community-college-transfers-flourish-cornell), which the Chronicle named in 2024 alongside PAVER and the CC STEP group as supports for community college transfers.

## Common mistakes

- **Applying to CALS after a fifth semester at a four-year school.** You are ineligible, and the $85 fee and an essay go nowhere.
- **Finishing an associate's degree and hoping for sophomore entry.** Junior standing is fixed, and Global Development closes to you.
- **Choosing CALS to reach AEM later.** CALS transfers cannot change to or add AEM; apply to Dyson directly.
- **Taking a prerequisite pass/fail or earning a C-.** CALS will not count it, and Biological Sciences expects the full biology, chemistry and math set.
- **Arriving with 75 to 90 credits.** Fifteen to 30 credits are lost, one or two extra semesters, about $74,970 a year in endowed tuition and fees.
- **Treating TC3 or the Transfer Option as admission.** Both are consideration, not a promise; apply elsewhere too.
- **Counting on AP scores.** Only 15 AP credits can count, and AP or IB biology does not meet the Biological Sciences biology year.

## FAQ

**What is the CALS transfer acceptance rate?** Cornell does not publish one by college. Cornell-wide, 864 of 7,381 transfer applicants were admitted for fall 2025, 11.7%; see our [acceptance rate page](/transfer/cornell-transfer-acceptance-rate/).

**How many transfers does CALS take?** In fall 2021 CALS expected 243 of 674 incoming transfers, 36.1%, the most of any college, per the [IRP profile](https://irp.cornell.edu/wp-content/uploads/2021/08/Profile2021-Fall-Transfers.pdf).

**Can I apply to two CALS majors?** No. You apply to one major and [cannot change it before you arrive](https://cals.cornell.edu/education/admissions/undergraduate-admissions/transfer-applicants).

**Do online courses count?** Yes, if the school is regionally accredited and you earned a letter grade of C or better; Cornell's [registrar](https://registrar.cornell.edu/students/credit-evaluation-services) will not deny equivalency because a course was online.

**Do my old grades count in my Cornell GPA?** No. Your Cornell transcript shows the name of your previous school and the total transfer credits, but [not the courses or grades](https://registrar.cornell.edu/students/credit-evaluation-services), and transfer GPA is not part of the Cornell GPA.

**Is CALS cheaper than Arts and Sciences?** For New York residents, yes: $49,816 against $73,946 in tuition for 2026-27. For everyone else, tuition is the same $73,946.
