The nearest pharmacy is on campus: the [Cornell Health pharmacy](https://health.cornell.edu/services/pharmacy), on Level 4 just inside the Ho Plaza entrance at 110 Ho Plaza. Off campus, the closest chains and Wegmans are a short bus or car ride away. Nothing we found keeps a pharmacy counter open past 9 PM, even on weekdays, so "open late" in Ithaca mostly means planning ahead, or using the 24/7 vending machine at Cornell Health for basic over-the-counter items.

## Which one is open late

- **Latest pharmacy counter on weekdays:** Wegmans, until 9 PM. The [store itself](https://www.wegmans.com/stores/ithaca-ny) is open 6 AM to midnight every day, which helps for cold medicine, bandages and snacks after the pharmacy closes, but you cannot pick up a prescription once the counter shuts.
- **Late Saturday:** Walgreens and Wegmans pharmacies both close at 6 PM. Cornell Health closes at 4:30 PM.
- **Sunday:** Cornell Health is closed. Walgreens is open until 5 PM and Wegmans until 4 PM.
- **Any hour, no prescription:** the [vending machine](https://health.cornell.edu/services/pharmacy/vending) just inside the Ho Plaza entrance is open 24/7. It sells non-prescription medicines and supplies such as pain relievers, allergy medication, bandages and emergency contraception, and gives out free naloxone spray and fentanyl and xylazine test strips. Products may change.

If you feel sick at night and need a prescription, [Cornell Health's hours page](https://health.cornell.edu/get-care/hours) points to a 24/7 phone line at 607-255-5155 for after-hours care options.

## The Cornell Health pharmacy

It is open to the public, not only students: Cornell's page says [students, faculty, staff and visitors](https://health.cornell.edu/services/pharmacy) can use it. You do not need a Cornell Health clinician to fill a prescription from another doctor.

- **Insurance:** it accepts the Cornell Student Health Plan, Aetna, Caremark (most plans), Express Scripts, Optum and several others. Upload your pharmacy card in myCornellHealth or bring a card or photo. If your plan is not accepted, you can pay in full and submit the receipt for reimbursement.
- **Student Health Plan copays:** for a 30-day supply, [$12 generic, $40 preferred brand, $60 non-preferred brand](https://health.cornell.edu/services/pharmacy).
- **Payment:** students can have charges billed to the Bursar; cards, Apple Pay and personal checks also work.
- **Parking:** if you drive, there are 10 paid patient spaces under the building, at $3 an hour plus a $0.35 fee.
- **Mail-order medications:** students can have specialty drugs, including refrigerated ones, shipped to 110 Ho Plaza, Level 4. Staff notify you through myCornellHealth and you pick up at Window 3 with ID.
- **Out of stock:** the pharmacy can usually order a medication to arrive the next day.

## Getting there without a car

The Cornell Health pharmacy is a walk from most of central campus. For Wegmans, Walgreens and CVS you need a bus or a ride. We could not open TCAT's schedules while researching this page, so we are not listing route numbers or frequencies. Put the pharmacy address into a transit app or the TCAT trip planner and check the current schedule, because routes change each semester. Our [getting around guide](/getting-around/) covers transit in general. If you are ill, a rideshare or a friend with a car is worth the cost over a bus transfer.

## Transferring a prescription from home

1. **Ask your home pharmacy to transfer it, or ask Cornell Health to pull it.** The simplest route is to give the new pharmacy your medication name, prescriber and old pharmacy's phone number.
2. **Check that you have refills left.** If you are out, a transfer will not help and you need a new prescription from your prescriber.
3. **Controlled substances and ADHD medication are harder.** Cornell's page says that to move a prescription in, you schedule an appointment with a Cornell Health clinician and bring your prescription and previous records, and that [ADHD medications require thorough diagnosis documentation](https://health.cornell.edu/services/pharmacy). Start weeks before you run out.
4. **Out-of-state prescriptions:** Cornell Health lists a [separate fax line, 607-254-5042](https://health.cornell.edu/services/pharmacy), for out-of-state prescriptions. The pharmacy's own fax is 888-749-4967.

Moving a prescription out of Cornell Health works the other way. You need refills left, and you request the transfer through myCornellHealth, by phone or at the pharmacy. [All remaining refills move to the new pharmacy and the Cornell Health prescription becomes void](https://health.cornell.edu/services/pharmacy). If you are out of refills, call 607-255-5155 for a renewal; renewals are not accepted through myCornellHealth. The page also says New York's transfer law has temporarily changed without giving details, so confirm the current rule with the pharmacy.

## If you are...

- **A student on campus with a cold at 10 PM:** use the 24/7 vending machine, or Wegmans for anything else after the pharmacy closes.
- **A student with a monthly prescription:** transfer it to Cornell Health before you arrive, and plan pickups for weekdays before 5 PM.
- **A parent visiting:** Wegmans is the easiest to find with a car and has the longest weekday hours.
- **Someone needing medication on a Sunday:** Walgreens until 5 PM, or Wegmans until 4 PM.

## FAQ

**Is there a 24-hour pharmacy in Ithaca?** None that we found. The nearest thing is Cornell Health's 24/7 vending machine for non-prescription items.

**Can non-students use the Cornell Health pharmacy?** Yes, [it is open to the public](https://health.cornell.edu/services/pharmacy).

**What if I run out of a medication over break?** Hours around holidays are not on the pages we read, so call 607-255-6976 first. See also [daily life in Ithaca](/daily-life/).
