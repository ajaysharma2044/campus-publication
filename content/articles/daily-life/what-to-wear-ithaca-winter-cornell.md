Wear a down coat, mittens, a hat that covers your ears and insulated boots with thick, treaded rubber soles. Cornell's International Services office calls [down the warmest coat fabric](https://international.globallearning.cornell.edu/life-cornell/student-resources) and says winter lows can dip below 0°F. Add layers underneath that you can take off indoors.

```glance
- A down coat is warmest, wool is next, and leather alone is not warm enough.
- Mittens are warmer than gloves; cover your ears and hands.
- Boots need insulation and thick treaded rubber soles; waterproof them if they are leather.
- Cornell's Access Fund can pay up to $100 for boots or $150 for a coat, hat and gloves.
```

## What does an Ithaca winter feel like?

Cold, windy and snowy from December through March. The average January temperature is 22.8°F, and the average January snowfall is 16.6 inches ([Northeast Regional Climate Center](https://www.nrcc.cornell.edu/wxstation/ithaca/normal.html)). A year's normal snowfall is 62.9 inches; our [Ithaca winter survival guide](/cornell/daily-life/ithaca-winter-survival-guide-cornell/) has the month-by-month table. The first inch of snow normally arrives around Nov. 25, according to [The Cornell Daily Sun](https://cornellsun.com/2021/11/17/delayed-first-snowfall-in-ithaca-reflects-changing-climate-patterns/), which reported the date moved from Nov. 20 with the newest 30-year averages.

Averages hide the worst weeks. Winter 2025-26 ran 3.1°F below normal, with about 19 days that never rose above freezing from Jan. 23 to Feb. 11 and a low of -7°F on Jan. 30 ([The Cornell Daily Sun](https://cornellsun.com/article/2026/03/5z6poblqwoea)). Wind makes it worse: at 0°F with a 15 mph wind, the wind chill is -19°F, and exposed skin can freeze in 30 minutes ([National Weather Service](https://www.weather.gov/safety/cold-wind-chill-chart)).

## What should I wear on my body?

Dress in layers, with a water-resistant jacket on top, according to [Cornell Health](https://health.cornell.edu/about/news/cold-weather-safety). Cornell International Services says to wear many layers of loose clothing, because the layers keep pockets of warmer air between them, and to put long underwear under loose-fitting pants ([Cornell International Services](https://international.globallearning.cornell.edu/life-cornell/student-resources)). A layered outfit lets you shed a sweater in a warm lecture hall and add it back for the walk across the Arts Quad.

**Packing checklist**

- A down coat, or a wool coat if you have no down one
- Layers to wear under it, so you can adjust indoors
- Long underwear to wear under loose-fitting pants
- A hat that covers your ears
- Mittens, which are warmer than gloves
- Insulated boots with thick treaded rubber soles
- Silicone waterproofing spray for all-leather boots

## What boots should I buy?

Choose insulated hiking boots with thick treaded rubber soles, or waterproof all-leather boots treated with silicone spray, according to Cornell International Services. Treads matter on campus walks, because Cornell's snow removal page says grounds crews clear roads, lots, steps and sidewalks, but response times vary with the snowfall. Cornell's page urges people to walk like a penguin on slippery ground ([Cornell Facilities](https://fcs.cornell.edu/services/repair-maintenance/snow-removal)).

Salt helps less as it gets colder. A pound of salt melts 46 pounds of ice at 30°F but barely 5 pounds at 10°F, according to [Cornellians magazine](https://alumni.cornell.edu/cornellians/cold-comforts-of-a-big-red-winter), so expect slick patches in the coldest weeks. If you find a hazard, report it to Cornell Customer Service at 607-255-5322.

## How do I spot frostbite?

Watch for prickling, numbness, a color change or waxy-looking skin, and do not rub the area or apply direct heat ([Cornell Health](https://health.cornell.edu/about/news/cold-weather-safety)). Cover your hands and ears whenever you go outside. Cornell Health's 24/7 advice line is 607-255-5155.

## What if I ride a bike in winter?

On a bike, wear a helmet and brightly colored or reflective clothing so drivers can see you, and use legal headlights and taillights if you ride at dusk or at night ([Cornell Transportation Services](https://fcs.cornell.edu/departments/transportation-delivery-services/alternative-transportation-options/cycling-campus)). The [winter biking guide](/cornell/getting-around/biking-in-ithaca-winter-cornell/) covers brakes, locks and the bus backup. Most first-years walk and ride TCAT, which is covered in the [first-year getting around guide](/cornell/getting-around/getting-around-cornell-first-year/).

## Where can I buy winter clothes in Ithaca and how much will it cost?

Start at the discount and secondhand stores near South Meadow Square. In fall 2024, The Cornell Daily Sun priced coats there from $25 at Plato's Closet to about $89.99 at T.J. Maxx ([The Cornell Daily Sun](https://www.cornellsun.com/article/2024/10/bundle-up-cheaply-best-coat-bargains-in-ithaca)).

| Item and store | Price |
|---|---|
| Used name-brand coat, Plato's Closet | $25 to $40 |
| Sherpa parka or puffer jacket on sale, Old Navy | $34.99 |
| Coat, Walmart | About $40 on average |
| Name-brand coat, T.J. Maxx | $59.99 to about $89.99 |
| Winter boots | $150 or more (2023) |

Sources: The Cornell Daily Sun, Oct. 31, 2024 (coats); [Cornell's alumni website](https://alumni.cornell.edu/connect/downloads/then-and-now/), citing The New York Times (boots).

Plato's Closet is in South Meadow Square. T.J. Maxx is across the street, Old Navy is a few doors down from it, and Walmart is an eight-minute walk from Old Navy. TCAT Route 15 serves all four (The Cornell Daily Sun).

Cornell's Graduate School lists more stores: Dick's Sporting Goods, Kohl's, REI Co-op, Ross and Target for new clothes, and EverGreen Ithaca, Goodwill, the Ithaca ReUse Center and MegaCenter, the Salvation Army and Thrifty Shopper for used ones ([Cornell Graduate School](https://gradschool.cornell.edu/announcements/grad-tips-winter-clothing-resources)).

## What to do next

1. Check the packing list against what you own.
2. Buy boots and a coat first; they cost the most.
3. If money is tight, apply to the Access Fund before the first snowfall.
4. Save 607-255-5155 in your phone.
5. Read our [Ithaca winter survival guide](/cornell/daily-life/ithaca-winter-survival-guide-cornell/) for the season's rules, dates and services.
