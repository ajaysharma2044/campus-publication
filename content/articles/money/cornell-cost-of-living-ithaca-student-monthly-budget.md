A Cornell student who rents a room in a shared Collegetown house should plan on about $1,500 to $2,030 a month during the school year before utilities and phone, and about $1,600 to $2,240 with them. A room in a higher-end apartment costs more: about $2,150 to $3,030 before utilities. That is our sum, not a Cornell number: rent of [$800 to $1,300 per person in Collegetown houses](https://www.cornellsun.com/article/2025/09/maia-on-the-mic-the-collegetown-housing-hustle), plus groceries, books, personal spending, an optional bus pass, and sourced estimates for electricity and heat, internet and a phone plan. Cornell's own budget is higher on food and lower on rent, and the third-party "cost of living" sites that rank for this query are city-wide calculators, not student budgets. Below is each line, where it comes from, and what to check before you sign a lease.

## Cornell's official budget, line by line

Cornell's aid office publishes an estimated cost of attendance. Cornell has not yet posted figures for 2026-27, so the numbers below come from [the 2025-26 table](https://finaid.cornell.edu/cost-attend), the latest it publishes. They are yearly and apply to every undergraduate college. Treat them as a guide to how Cornell thinks about the budget, not as a bill.

```figure
  - {label: "Transportation", value: "Varies"}
```

The [2025-26 table](https://finaid.cornell.edu/cost-attend) lists housing at $13,744, food at $7,604, books and course materials at $1,234 and personal expenses at $2,182, with transportation shown as "Varies". Together the four priced lines come to $24,764 a year. The same page says the estimate [leaves out premium on-campus or off-campus housing options](https://finaid.cornell.edu/cost-attend), so a pricey lease sits above the budget, not inside it.

**Which month count to use.** The $13,744 housing line equals two semesters of the [$6,872 residence hall double for 2026-27](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/housing-details/rates-amenities-licenses/housing-rates), so it reads as an academic-year figure. Over nine months of classes it is about $1,527 a month; spread over 12 months it is about $1,145. We use nine months for every line here, so the lines add up on the same basis. The 12-month figure matters for one comparison: Cornell's [off-campus page](https://finaid.cornell.edu/special-circumstances/living-campus) says to look for housing that costs less for 12 months than the off-campus housing and dining budget. That page also says your financial need is calculated the same way whether you live on or off campus, and that aid cannot cover a security deposit, first and last month's rent, or rent in months you are not a registered student.

## What each line really costs in Ithaca

| Line | Monthly figure | Where it comes from |
|---|---|---|
| Rent, one room in a shared house | $800 to $1,300 | A Daily Sun column's 2025 range for Collegetown houses |
| Rent, higher-end apartment | about $1,450 to $2,300 | The same column's range, 3-4 bedroom up to studio; RentCafe averages below |
| Groceries, cooking nearly everything | about $330 | Our estimate: $2,960 a year over nine months |
| Books and course materials | about $137 | Cornell's $1,234 a year over nine months |
| Personal spending | about $242 | Cornell's $2,182 a year over nine months |
| Bus pass (optional) | $0 to about $22 | Cornell's $200 a year over nine months |
| **Subtotal, room in a house** | **about $1,500 to $2,030** | Sum of the lines above |
| Electricity and heat, your share | about $70 to $160 | Whole-unit estimate split two to four ways, winter month |
| Internet, your share | about $10 to $20 | $40 a month split two to four ways |
| Phone | $25 | One prepaid unlimited plan |
| **Total with utilities and phone, room in a house** | **about $1,600 to $2,240** | Subtotal plus the three lines above |
| **Total with utilities and phone, higher-end apartment** | **about $2,260 to $3,240** | Apartment rent, same other lines |

Here is why each line looks the way it does.

**Rent.** A Cornell Daily Sun columnist (ILR, Class of 2027) wrote in September 2025 that [Collegetown houses run about $800 to $1,300 per person a month](https://www.cornellsun.com/article/2025/09/maia-on-the-mic-the-collegetown-housing-hustle), that the lowest two- or three-bedroom apartment seen was about $1,300, and that house rents are roughly on par with on-campus housing. The same column puts higher-end apartments at about $1,450 for a 3-4 bedroom up to $2,300 for a studio. That is why the low end of our apartment total is $2,260 with utilities, not $1,600. This is one opinion column, so check it against listings. RentCafe's listing data, [updated October 30, 2026](https://www.rentcafe.com/average-rent-market-trends/us/ny/ithaca/collegetown/), shows Collegetown averages of $2,006 for a studio, $2,342 for a one-bedroom and $3,414 for a two-bedroom, with all rentals averaging $2,815, down 2.59 percent from $2,890 a year earlier. Split a $3,414 two-bedroom two ways and each person pays $1,707, which is our arithmetic and lands inside the column's apartment range. Check whether a listing is priced per person or per unit: a [2022 Sun column](https://cornellsun.com/2022/08/04/nguyen-the-tale-of-cornells-broken-housing-market/) by Niko Nguyen '22 says listed prices often reflect cost per person rather than per unit.

For comparison, a double in a residence hall costs [$6,872 per semester for 2026-27](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/housing-details/rates-amenities-licenses/housing-rates), and that rate covers gas, electricity, garbage pickup and internet capped at 200 GB a month. Off campus, those extras are yours to arrange, which is why the table adds them.

**Electricity and heat.** This line uses two published estimates. The state's Department of Public Service expects a household using 600 kWh a month to pay [about $74 a month for supply this winter, plus about $241 a month for natural gas at 140 therms](https://dps.ny.gov/winter) from November through March. Together that is about $315, but it omits delivery charges. [EnergySage](https://www.energysage.com/local-data/electricity-cost/ny/tompkins-county/ithaca/) reports an Ithaca average of about $280 a month on about 1,116 kWh, from residents shopping for solar, who may use more than a typical household. Split $280 to $315 two to four ways and your share is about $70 to $160 in a winter month. Our [Ithaca winter utility guide](/money/ithaca-utilities-cost-winter-heating-bill-apartment-students/) has the full math. Summer bills are lower, and a lease that includes heat removes most of this line, so ask the landlord which utilities are included and for last winter's bills for that unit.

**Internet.** Spectrum lists [Internet 500 at $40 a month for one year](https://www.spectrum.com/internet) for new customers, with standard rates after that. Availability depends on your address. Split two to four ways, that is $10 to $20 each.

**Phone.** Cornell's budget has no phone line. A prepaid example is Visible's [base plan at $25 a month with taxes and fees included](https://www.visible.com/plans/base). Your own plan may cost more or less.

**Food.** Cornell budgets [$7,604 a year](https://finaid.cornell.edu/cost-attend), which is the cost of a meal plan, not of cooking. Cooking nearly every meal runs about $2,960 a year by our estimate, built on the [USDA Low-Cost Food Plan, September 2026](https://fns-prod.azureedge.us/sites/default/files/resource-files/CostofFoodSep2026LowModLib.pdf): $63.80 a week for a woman aged 19 to 50 and $73.30 for a man, raised by the 20 percent USDA says to add for a one-person household, averaged, and run over 36 weeks. The math is in [our meal plan or cook yourself guide](/money/cornell-off-campus-meal-plan-or-cook-yourself-cost/). That is about $330 a month over nine months, and it leaves out restaurants and coffee. The USDA figures are national averages, not Ithaca prices.

**Books and personal spending.** These two lines are Cornell's own estimates, [$1,234 and $2,182 a year](https://finaid.cornell.edu/cost-attend). Both vary by major and habits, and personal spending is the one most students overshoot or undershoot.

**Transportation.** Cornell lists it as ["Varies"](https://finaid.cornell.edu/cost-attend). On TCAT, first-year students ride free with their Cornell ID, and after the first year students get [free, unlimited rides after 6 p.m. on weekdays and all day on Saturday and Sunday](https://sustainable.cornell.edu/focus-areas/transportation/bus-commuting). Daytime weekday rides need a pass. Cornell's [passes page](https://fcs.cornell.edu/departments/transportation-delivery-services/sustainable-transportation-options/bus-services-privileges-passes) says OmniRide passes cost $200 a year, pro-rated monthly, and that the current rate shows at purchase on myparking.cornell.edu. Over nine months, $200 is about $22 a month. Passes expire August 31. The Sun [reported in November 2024](https://cornellsun.com/2024/11/03/non-first-year-cornell-students-no-longer-able-to-access-tcat-for-free/) that students who had been riding free at all hours lost that access. If you live in Collegetown and your classes are on Central Campus, you may not need a pass.

## Why the aggregator numbers do not match

The sites that rank for this query disagree with each other, and with Cornell. [CostLiving's Ithaca page](https://costliving.net/ithaca/) cites its "CostLiving Engine, September 2026" and gives a tight budget of $2,190 a month, a moderate budget of $3,650 and a comfortable one of $5,658. Its tight tier assumes shared housing at $900 to $1,100 a month and minimal dining out. Its moderate tier assumes a one-bedroom apartment or small house, and the site cites BLS and City of Ithaca data as its inputs. It also says TCAT is free for students, which is true for first-years and for evenings and weekends but not for every ride, per Cornell's pages above. Use city-wide calculators as a rough check on your own sum, not as your budget.

## What the lines leave out

- **Furnishing.** The Sun column mentions unexpected costs for [kitchen appliances and cleaning supplies](https://www.cornellsun.com/article/2025/09/maia-on-the-mic-the-collegetown-housing-hustle) when you move into an apartment, so set aside money for those.
- **Deposit and first month.** [Aid cannot cover a deposit or first and last month's rent](https://finaid.cornell.edu/special-circumstances/living-campus), so you need cash up front.
- **Summer.** A 12-month lease charges rent in months you are away. See [how to sublet an Ithaca apartment](/housing/cornell-summer-sublet-how-to-sublet-apartment-ithaca/).
- **Early leases.** Nguyen wrote that students often sign [well before they move in](https://cornellsun.com/2022/08/04/nguyen-the-tale-of-cornells-broken-housing-market/), and that he signed his junior-year lease in October of his sophomore year. That was 2022, so check the current timing before you plan your cash.

## If you are...

- **Cooking, sharing a house, mostly walking:** the low end, about $1,600 a month with utilities and phone.
- **In a newer Collegetown apartment:** price it per person from the listing. A $1,707 share, as in the two-bedroom example above, puts you near $2,420 with utilities and phone.
- **A parent budgeting for a year:** use Cornell's yearly lines for the planning conversation, then replace the housing line with the actual lease. Divide a nine-month lease by nine and a 12-month lease by 12. Our [Collegetown vs Fall Creek vs downtown guide](/housing/collegetown-vs-fall-creek-vs-downtown-ithaca-where-to-live/) compares where rents differ.
- **Looking to cut costs:** a part-time job helps, and our guide to [off-campus part-time jobs](/careers/ithaca-part-time-jobs-for-cornell-students/) covers what is available.

## FAQ

**Is living off campus cheaper than a dorm?** Not automatically. The Sun column says Collegetown house rents are [roughly on par with on-campus housing](https://www.cornellsun.com/article/2025/09/maia-on-the-mic-the-collegetown-housing-hustle). The dorm rate includes utilities and internet and the house usually does not, so compare the totals, not the rents.

**Does Cornell give less aid if I live off campus?** Cornell says your [financial need is calculated exactly the same way](https://finaid.cornell.edu/special-circumstances/living-campus) as if you lived on campus.

**What is the 2026-27 official budget?** Not yet announced on Cornell's [cost page](https://finaid.cornell.edu/cost-attend), which still lists 2025-26 as its latest figures. The figures change each year, so check that page before you plan.
