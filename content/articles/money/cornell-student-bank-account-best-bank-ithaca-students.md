There is no single best bank for Cornell students, and Cornell [does not recommend one](https://international.globallearning.cornell.edu/getting-started/your-first-week-0). For most students in Ithaca, the two easiest starting points are Alternatives Federal Credit Union and Tompkins Bank & Trust's student checking, because their fees are published and low. You do not need a Social Security number to open an account: Cornell's international office says [you don't need an SSN to open a bank account](https://international.globallearning.cornell.edu/employment-and-taxes/apply-ssn), though how each bank handles it differs. Below is what each bank publishes, what we could not confirm, and a first-week checklist.

## Do you need a local account?

It is optional. Cornell's Bursar says a local account lets you write checks to off-campus businesses that may not accept out-of-town checks, and that [bank representatives are on campus during orientation](https://bursar.cornell.edu/students-parents/student-resources/local-banking-services). The Bursar also lists banks that have branches outside Ithaca, so you can keep the account after you leave.

The one thing you do need is a U.S. checking or savings account if you want refunds by direct deposit. The Bursar says you [have 24 hours after a refund is generated to set up direct deposit](https://bursar.cornell.edu/students-parents/deposit-refunds), otherwise a check is issued. Direct deposit should arrive in 2 to 3 business days, and a check takes 7 to 10 business days after printing. Set it up before your first refund, not after.

## Where each fits

- **You want the cheapest, simplest option:** Alternatives. The fee is avoidable by choosing eStatements. Its page lists [state driver's license, learner's permit, non-driver ID or U.S. passport as ID, plus a paycheck stub, utility bill or signed lease as proof of address](https://www.alternatives.org/accounts/open-an-account/). Membership requires living in a listed county; Tompkins is one. The Ithaca branch is at [125 N. Fulton Street](https://www.alternatives.org/accounts/checking/).
- **You are 25 or younger and a full or part-time student:** Tompkins student checking. [Tompkins charges no fee for using non-Tompkins ATMs](https://www.tompkinsbank.com/student-checking-101), which helps if you travel, but the ATM's owner may still charge its own fee.
- **You want a credit union with a bigger ATM network:** Beginnings. Its membership page says you can join if you [live, work, worship, attend school or own a business in its service area](https://www.beginnings.com/about-us/about-beginnings-credit-union/who-can-become-a-member) and keep $5 in a share account. Call 607-257-8500 for the student account's terms.
- **You will leave the area and want a national bank:** the Bursar lists Bank of America, M&T, Key and Citizens, but Cornell's page says Bank of America's nearest location is in Syracuse. Confirm that an Ithaca branch and a student account are available before you rely on one.
- **You are an international student with no SSN:** use Alternatives, CFCU, Ithaca Community Bank or M&T from the table, and bring your passport and lease. In general, international students [need authorized employment to be eligible to apply for an SSN](https://international.globallearning.cornell.edu/employment-and-taxes/apply-ssn), with exceptions such as J-2 dependents with a USCIS work card. See [how international students find on-campus jobs](/money/cornell-international-student-jobs-on-campus-f-1-how-to-find/).

## First-week checklist

```figure
kind: steps
title: Opening an account in week one
rows:
  - label: Get a lease or other proof of local address
  - label: Pick two banks and read their fee pages
  - label: Open the account in person with ID
  - label: Choose eStatements and a debit card
  - label: Add the account to Cornell direct deposit
```

1. Gather ID (passport or driver's license) and proof of address such as your lease.
2. Check the fee page and the SSN rule for your top two choices.
3. Open the account, in person if you can, since Bank of America requires it ([Cornell's page](https://international.globallearning.cornell.edu/getting-started/your-first-week-0)).
4. Choose eStatements. Both Alternatives and Tompkins charge for paper.
5. Add the account for refunds: log in to Nelnet Business Services with your NetID, [choose Direct Deposit and enter your routing and account numbers](https://bursar.cornell.edu/students-parents/deposit-refunds).

## Common mistakes

Our advice, not a bank rule:

- Waiting for your first refund before setting up direct deposit. The window is 24 hours.
- Assuming "no SSN needed to open" means every service works. Ask whether online banking and a debit card are included.
- Ignoring the age cap on Tompkins student accounts if you are a grad student.
- Picking by branch near campus alone. Compare ATM networks and what happens after you graduate.

## FAQ

**Can I open an account before I arrive?** We did not find an answer on the pages we read. Alternatives asks for [proof of address (a paycheck stub, utility bill or signed lease)](https://www.alternatives.org/accounts/open-an-account/), which many students only have after signing a lease.

**Is a credit union better than a bank?** Cornell does not rank them. Compare published fees and ATM access instead.

**Does Cornell have a partner bank?** The Bursar page describes bank representatives on campus at orientation but states no partnership.
