A Cornell grad student renting off campus in Ithaca can live on roughly $2,200 to $3,300 a month before utilities, depending mostly on rent: a room in a shared house, a Cornell-owned studio, or a one-bedroom in Fall Creek. That compares with a minimum stipend that works out to [$4,076 a month before tax](https://gradschool.cornell.edu/financial-support/stipend-rates/) for 2026-27 ($48,912 over 12 months, or $36,684 over nine). The $4,076 is the same monthly figure for a 12-month or a nine-month appointment, but a nine-month appointment pays for only nine months, so summer rent has to be planned separately. The budget below is built from Cornell's own cost estimates and current listings, so you can swap in your own rent and see where you land.

## The monthly budget

Cornell's financial aid office publishes an [Ithaca graduate and professional cost of attendance for 2026-27](https://finaid.cornell.edu/apply-aid/graduate-students): $19,962 a year for housing, $5,768 for food, $2,464 for books and course materials, $3,754 for transportation and $4,196 for personal expenses. Health insurance is not in that list. Those are aid budgets, not what students actually spend, so we divided each by 12 and then replaced the housing line with real rents. We also added about $74 a month for electric in the Fall Creek column, the [state's typical supply charge at 600 kWh](https://dps.ny.gov/winter). That figure is supply only and varies by household.

Read the table with three caveats. The stipend is before tax, and we have not estimated your tax. Utilities are listed as included in the shared-room and studio columns, while the Fall Creek column carries only an electric supply estimate and still leaves out garbage tags. And a nine-month stipend with no summer funding leaves three months without pay while rent runs 12; the aid office says aid [cannot cover rent in months you are not a registered student](https://finaid.cornell.edu/special-circumstances/living-campus), so plan those months separately.

## Where the rent numbers come from

- **Shared room, $850.** A Collegetown five-bedroom on Stewart Avenue advertised [a room at $850 a month](https://offcampushousing.ithaca.edu/city/ithaca-college-ny/listing/209-stewart-avenue-235095), with heat, water, electricity and WiFi included, a lease running August 1, 2026 to July 31, 2027, and a deposit of one month's rent. The listing also charges a $20 county solid waste fee and an $80 garbage fee split among five tenants at signing, so "$850 all-in" is accurate for rent only. Another house on North Aurora Street listed [$775 to $875 per room plus utilities](https://offcampus.housing.cornell.edu/city/ithaca-college-ny/listing/1006-n-aurora-st-ithaca-ny-14850-usa-237075), with a listed 19-minute commute to the Oval (the listing says less than half a mile to campus).
- **Cornell studio, $1,355.** Cornell's [housing rates page](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/housing-details/rates-amenities-licenses/housing-rates) lists Hasbrouck Apartments at $1,355 a month for a studio, $1,415 to $1,490 for a one-bedroom, and $620 to $910 for a single in a two-bedroom townhouse, all on 12-month contracts. The page says housing rates include gas, electricity, garbage pickup and internet up to 200 GB a month. Confirm eligibility for your program before counting on it.
- **Fall Creek one-bedroom, $1,600.** A [709 North Tioga Street listing](https://offcampus.housing.cornell.edu/city/ithaca-college-ny/listing/cosy-quiet-comfortable-fall-creek-gem.-238258) showed two one-bedrooms at $1,600 and $1,700, with the landlord paying internet and water and the tenant paying electric and garbage tags.
- **Maplewood.** [Greystar describes it](https://www.greystar.com/maplewood-ithaca-ny/p_15697) as unique to graduate, faculty and professional students, with electricity, trash, internet and cable included and a $130 amenity fee per leaseholder at move-in. It publishes no rents; you have to call.

The Stewart Avenue lease ran for the 2026-27 year. The $1,700 North Tioga unit was listed as available June 1, 2026, and the $1,600 unit shows no availability date. Any of these may be rented by now, so treat them as price points, not vacancies.

## Cheap neighborhoods, by what you trade away

Cornell's [neighborhood guide](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/campus-housing/housing-search-process/ithaca-neighborhoods) groups Downtown, Fall Creek and the Commons together: lower rents, frequent bus service, a 20 to 30 minute uphill walk to campus, and the page says graduate students prefer them for the lower rents. In Fall Creek, the listing above put a one-bedroom at $1,600 a month. The guide puts Collegetown up to a 15-minute walk from campus (lower Collegetown is uphill), but its popularity raises rent; the Stewart Avenue room was $850. Cornell-owned Hasbrouck Apartments, a different option from the private market, list a studio at $1,355.

Lansing and the Northeast are described as less expensive and somewhat remote, so you need a bus or a car. West Hill rents tend to be lower, but winter heating bills can be very high, and the page suggests asking NYSEG for a property's average yearly bills. No listing for Lansing/Northeast or West Hill is cited in this article, so we give no price for them.

Older student advice agrees. In a [Grad Café thread](https://forum.thegradcafe.com/topic/153-ithaca-ny/?page=8) from 2018, the poster Assotto said downtown and Fall Creek are where most grad students live, that studios started around $1,100 and that roommates could bring costs to about $600. Another poster, Manuscriptess, called Collegetown mostly undergrads. Those rents are eight years old; the pattern, not the prices, is what has held.

For a side-by-side of the three areas, see [Collegetown vs Fall Creek vs downtown Ithaca](/housing/collegetown-vs-fall-creek-vs-downtown-ithaca-where-to-live/).

## Utilities are the line that moves

If your rent does not include heat, winter is the cost to model. New York's Department of Public Service estimates [$241 a month for natural gas heating](https://dps.ny.gov/winter) from November through March for 2026-27, for a customer using 140 therms, and about $74 a month for electric supply at 600 kWh. It says actual amounts vary widely by region, and the electric figure is supply only. Our [Ithaca winter utility guide](/money/ithaca-utilities-cost-winter-heating-bill-apartment-students/) covers what to ask a landlord.

## Transport and health insurance

Cornell says registered [PhD students and graduate assistants receive free TCAT OmniRide passes](https://fcs.cornell.edu/departments/transportation-delivery-services/sustainable-transportation-options/bus-services-privileges-passes). Other degree-seeking students must be registered, enrolled in a degree program, and in school for a year or more to ride free after 6 p.m. on weekdays and all day on Saturdays and Sundays. If you qualify for the pass, the $313 transportation line is mostly room to spare, which is a reason a bus-served neighborhood beats a cheaper one that forces a car. See [Do you need a car at Cornell?](/money/should-i-bring-a-car-cornell-ithaca-cost/).

The 2026-27 student health plan costs [$4,092 for 12 months](https://studenthealthbenefits.cornell.edu/shp-costs-billing-important-dates/shp-costs-dates-2026-27), billed as $2,046 each semester for returning fully funded students; new fall 2026 students pay $1,705 in fall and $2,046 in spring. Whether your funding package covers it varies, so ask your department before you budget either way.

## If you are...

- **On a 12-month stipend:** the Fall Creek one-bedroom fits, with about $712 pre-tax left after the electric estimate. A roommate drops you toward the shared-room column.
- **On a nine-month appointment:** pick the shared room or a 12-month Cornell contract, and budget for summer separately.
- **Without a car:** prioritize Downtown, Fall Creek and the Commons for frequent bus service.

## Common mistakes

Our advice:

- Comparing rents without comparing what is included. An $850 room with utilities included can beat a $775 room plus utilities, once you add the listing's fees.
- Forgetting the deposit. The Stewart Avenue listing asks one month's rent, and aid does not cover [security deposits or first and last month's rent](https://finaid.cornell.edu/special-circumstances/living-campus).
- Budgeting nine months of income against twelve months of rent.

## FAQ

**Is the Cornell stipend enough to live on in Ithaca?** On the arithmetic above, yes for every option shown, but the margin before tax ranges from $712 to $1,536 a month, and summer months depend on your funding.

**Can I get more aid for a high budget?** The [Graduate School's cost of attendance page](https://gradschool.cornell.edu/financial-support/tuition-rates/cost-of-attendance) says additional expenses can raise your cost of attendance case by case with documentation. See [financial aid appeals](/money/cornell-financial-aid-appeal-success/).

**How do I cut the budget further?** Food is the most flexible line; see [meal plan or cook yourself](/money/cornell-off-campus-meal-plan-or-cook-yourself-cost/), and [student discounts in Ithaca](/money/how-to-save-money-at-cornell-student-discounts-ithaca/).

**Can I earn extra income?** See off-campus part-time jobs, and check your appointment terms first.
