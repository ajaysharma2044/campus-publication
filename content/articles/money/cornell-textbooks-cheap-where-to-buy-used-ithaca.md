If you are a Cornell undergraduate, you are already enrolled in the Cornell Academic Materials Program (CAMP), which charges [$295 per semester](https://www.cornellstore.com/about-textbooks) for your required course materials. You only buy used or cheap books elsewhere if you opt out before the deadline, and the opt-out covers every undergraduate course that semester. Graduate students are not in CAMP, so for them cheap means comparing prices and using the library.
## Which situation are you in?

| You are | Your cheapest route |
|---|---|
| Undergrad, heavy-reading or many-book semester | Stay in CAMP; the flat fee is known up front |
| Undergrad, few required books or friends' hand-me-downs | Compare, then opt out before the deadline |
| Undergrad past the opt-out deadline | Stay in; use library reserves for single books |
| Graduate or professional student | Buy yourself; check Instant Access for your course |

## How CAMP works

CAMP is for [undergraduate students taking undergraduate courses at Cornell](https://academicmaterials.cornell.edu/). The fee is [set in advance](https://academicmaterials.cornell.edu/), appears on the [July Bursar statement for fall and the January statement for spring](https://academicmaterials.cornell.edu/frequently-asked-questions), and buys the required materials only; optional ones are not included.

- The program is digital first. Ebooks open in Canvas under the "Course Materials" tab, and print is used [only when no digital option exists](https://academicmaterials.cornell.edu/frequently-asked-questions), picked up at The Cornell Store.
- You must be fully enrolled in a course to receive its materials, so [waitlisted students do not receive materials until they are fully enrolled, and students studying abroad are not enrolled in CAMP that semester](https://academicmaterials.cornell.edu/frequently-asked-questions).
- If you stay in, print materials are yours to keep. If you opt out or drop the course, return them to the Store in like-new condition within 14 days or [pay the retail price](https://academicmaterials.cornell.edu/frequently-asked-questions).
- Cornell's own first-year numbers: students saved an [estimated $2.88 million combined, about $145 per student per semester](https://scl.cornell.edu/news-events/news/students-see-savings-cornell-academic-materials-program). The program FAQ cites an average of $140 per semester. These are averages, not a promise for your schedule.

## How to opt out

1. Use the link in the CAMP email, or open a course's Canvas "Course Materials" tab and choose "Opt-Out" in the top right corner, [per the program FAQ](https://academicmaterials.cornell.edu/frequently-asked-questions).
2. Look for a confirmation email within 24 hours; if none arrives, write to textbooks@cornell.edu, as [the FAQ directs](https://academicmaterials.cornell.edu/frequently-asked-questions).
3. Expect the fee to be reversed on your bursar account in [5 to 7 business days](https://academicmaterials.cornell.edu/frequently-asked-questions). Return any print items first.

The fall 2026 opt-out deadline was [September 11, 2026](https://academicmaterials.cornell.edu/frequently-asked-questions), so it has passed. For spring, the [$295 charge lands on the January statement](https://academicmaterials.cornell.edu/how-it-works), the welcome email with the opt-out link [typically arrives in early January](https://academicmaterials.cornell.edu/frequently-asked-questions), and each semester's deadlines go on the program's [Important Dates page](https://academicmaterials.cornell.edu/important-dates/). Late requests are [reviewed individually and may be denied](https://academicmaterials.cornell.edu/frequently-asked-questions) without a valid academic reason. [Re-enrolling after the deadline carries a $295 charge](https://academicmaterials.cornell.edu/frequently-asked-questions); you request the opt-in by email to textbooks@cornell.edu, and the charge goes to your bursar account once it is confirmed.

Financial aid does not require you to stay in. [You do not have to stay in CAMP to keep your aid](https://academicmaterials.cornell.edu/frequently-asked-questions); you can opt out by the deadline. A bursar balance may remain after reversal, depending on your other charges and aid.

## Is opting out worth it?

Do the arithmetic per semester: add up the used or rental price of every required book, then compare it with $295. The program pitches itself against a [$620 per semester national average](https://www.cornellstore.com/about-textbooks), but that is the average, not your schedule.

In a [2022 Cornell Daily Sun story](https://cornellsun.com/2022/07/24/what-you-need-to-know-about-cornells-new-academic-materials-program/), Amerdeep Passananti '25 said he had spent less than $250 on textbooks each semester and estimated most peers spend about $100 to $200. Xi Wang '24, a Cornell Store student employee, expected students to compare the cost of the books they need with the fee. Brian Young, the Cornell Store's academic materials manager, suggested students check whether their subjects would benefit. That story was written when the fee was $225, so the gap has moved.

Our advice: list your required books from the syllabus or Canvas before the deadline, price them used, and opt out only if the total is clearly under the fee. Remember that opting out is all courses or none, and that the Store [no longer houses in-stock textbooks](https://www.cornellstore.com/about-textbooks); opted-out students can [special-order print textbooks from the textbook help desk](https://www.cornellstore.com/about-textbooks) or buy digital titles.

## Where to find cheap used books if you opt out

Most options are outside the Cornell Store:

- **Buffalo Street Books.** A 2013 Ithaca College article described this downtown co-op as not charging the [7% New York State sales tax and delivering to Cornell classes at no charge](https://ithacaweek-ic.com/?p=16149). Ask the shop whether both still apply.
- **Peer sales.** The same 2013 piece quotes the co-founder of the Ithaca Book Trade site, who said a textbook typically [returns only 10 to 25 percent of its original value](https://ithacaweek-ic.com/?p=16149) at the end of the semester. That site served only Ithaca College students at the time, so it is not a Cornell option; treat the figure as background, not current prices.
- **Online retailers.** The Store's page tells graduate students they can [compare prices with other online retailers](https://www.cornellstore.com/about-textbooks). That works for undergraduates who have opted out, too. Match the exact edition and ISBN on the syllabus.

## Graduate and professional students

[CAMP does not cover graduate students](https://academicmaterials.cornell.edu/frequently-asked-questions). The separate Instant Access Program provides digital textbooks and publisher software for [participating graduate and professional courses](https://www.cornellstore.com/course-materials-affordability-efforts) inside Canvas, and the FAQ describes it as select titles at reduced rates on an opt-in basis. [Graduate and professional courses are outside CAMP](https://academicmaterials.cornell.edu/frequently-asked-questions).

## Free option: library reserves

Cornell Library course reserves hold items at the circulation desk for [short-term loan](https://olinuris.library.cornell.edu/services/course-reserves), and instructors request them. Questions go to okureserve@cornell.edu. Undergraduates who struggle to get a required text can also [ask the library to put it on reserve](https://www.library.cornell.edu/reserves/textbook-request-form) through a form (Cornell login required), as long as the text is not already on reserve; funding is limited. When the library launched student requests in 2018, [loans were typically 2 hours](https://mann.library.cornell.edu/textbook-reserve-requests-now-open-to-students). Reserves suit a single expensive book you only need for problem sets, not a book you use daily.

## Common mistakes

- Assuming you can opt out of one expensive course. It is all or nothing.
- Missing the deadline, then asking for an exception without an academic reason.
- Buying a book before checking that CAMP already includes it.
- Keeping print items after opting out. Return them within 14 days.
- Relying on older articles about Store rentals and used books. Check [the Store's current page](https://www.cornellstore.com/about-textbooks).

## FAQ

**Do I get to keep the books?** Print items are yours if you stay in and remain enrolled. Many digital textbooks have [extended access of up to 5 years](https://www.cornellstore.com/about-textbooks).

**Who do I contact?** textbooks@cornell.edu.
