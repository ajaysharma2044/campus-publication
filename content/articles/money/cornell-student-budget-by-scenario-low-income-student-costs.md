Plan on about $380 a month in real spending money for books and personal costs, plus travel, whatever your aid situation. What changes is the size of the bill from Cornell. The aid office's cost table shows [$96,318 in billed costs and $3,416 in non-billed costs, $99,734 in total](https://finaid.cornell.edu/cost-attend), before travel and health insurance, but that table is labeled 2025-26 and the page's 2026-27 table is empty. We treat the $73,946 tuition and $13,744 housing as 2026-27 because [the trustees' announcement](https://news.cornell.edu/stories/2026/03/board-trustees-approves-2026-27-budget-parameters) and the [housing rates page](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/housing-details/rates-amenities-licenses/housing-rates) confirm them. Families with income up to $75,000 and typical assets are told that [grants, scholarships and work-study cover the full cost of attendance](https://news.cornell.edu/stories/2026/03/board-trustees-approves-2026-27-budget-parameters). A grad student on a standard assistantship should plan about $3,070 a month in non-tuition costs. Below are the four scenarios, with the math shown.

Your monthly number at a glance (our arithmetic, before travel and before the $341 health plan if you do not waive it):

- **Full aid:** about $380 a month in cash costs, with a $0 expected contribution.
- **Partial aid:** up to about $2,866 a month at the ceiling, probably less.
- **Full pay:** about $11,082 a month over nine months.
- **Grad student:** about $3,070 a month over twelve months.

How we built these: monthly figures divide yearly costs by nine for undergraduates (the academic year) and by twelve for grad students. That is our simplification, and bills actually arrive per semester. The aid page's cost table is labeled 2025-26 and its "Undergraduate Estimated Cost of Attendance - 2026-27" table has column labels but no values, so we use its figures as our best estimate for 2026-27. We treat $73,946 tuition and $13,744 housing as 2026-27 because [the trustees' announcement](https://news.cornell.edu/stories/2026/03/board-trustees-approves-2026-27-budget-parameters) and the [housing rates page](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/housing-details/rates-amenities-licenses/housing-rates) confirm them, and the food line matches the [Unlimited meal plan](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/dining/meal-plans-rates/undergraduate-meal-plans). Fees, books and personal expenses are unconfirmed for 2026-27. All of this assumes on-campus housing and dining. Contract-college tuition differs.

Fees of $1,024 are a [$424 student activity fee and a $600 Cornell Health fee](https://finaid.cornell.edu/cost-attend). Books are $1,234 and personal expenses $2,182, which is the $3,416 non-billed subtotal. Travel home is not priced: the page says it varies by region. Health insurance is excluded, and the [Cornell Student Health Plan costs $4,092 for 12 months, about $341 a month, in 2026-27](https://studenthealthbenefits.cornell.edu/shp-costs-billing-important-dates/shp-costs-dates-2026-27).

The Daily Sun's 2025 story on the 2025-26 numbers reports that [books, travel and professional clothing weigh on students](https://www.cornellsun.com/article/2025/09/financial-gymnastics-cornell-s-cost-of-attendance-surpasses-100k-for-some-students-in-2025-2026), in the words of Michelle Van-Ess Grant, senior associate dean of students in the Centers for Student Equity, Empowerment, and Belonging. It also lists items left out of the estimate: resident parking ($752.86 a year), OMNI Ride passes ($200) and optional gym memberships ($150 to $168), all 2025-26 figures.

## Scenario 1: full aid (family income up to $75,000)

Cornell's affordability page says families with income up to $75,000 and typical assets get [$0 expected student and parent contributions, no loans, and a $5,000 work-study award](https://finaid.cornell.edu/affordability). The trustees' announcement says these families [get grants, scholarships and work-study that cover the full cost of attendance](https://news.cornell.edu/stories/2026/03/board-trustees-approves-2026-27-budget-parameters).

| Per month, over 9 months | Amount |
|---|---|
| Bill from Cornell | $0 expected contribution |
| Books and personal expenses | about $380 ($3,416 a year) |
| Travel home | your own estimate |
| Work-study, if worked evenly | about $556 ($5,000 a year) |

The affordability page does not say whether aid covers books, personal expenses or travel, so ask the aid office how your package treats the $3,416 and your flights or bus fares before you arrive. Plan your cash around the $380, not the zero. If you do not waive the health plan, add $341 a month. The aid page lists Cornell Student Health Insurance among optional costs that [student and parent loans can finance](https://finaid.cornell.edu/affordability).

Cheap fixes from a 2024 Sun column by student columnist Carlin Reyen: she estimated [$450 a year for required books](https://www.cornellsun.com/article/2024/04/a-cheapskates-guide-to-navigating-cornell) but says she has not bought books since her first semester, using syllabi on Canvas, library copies and BorrowDirect. She names Anabel's Grocery in Anabel Taylor Hall as low-priced and accepting SNAP.

## Scenario 2: partial aid (family income $75,001 to $125,000)

The trustees say families earning up to $125,000 with typical assets [get grant and scholarship aid that covers tuition](https://news.cornell.edu/stories/2026/03/board-trustees-approves-2026-27-budget-parameters). The maximum loan offer is [$2,000 for $75,001 to $125,000 and $4,000 for $125,001 to $175,000](https://finaid.cornell.edu/affordability). Cornell gives no dollar figure for what families above $75,000 are expected to contribute, so use its financial aid calculators for your own number.

The ceiling for this band, if tuition is the only thing covered, is the rest of the billed costs: $1,024 in fees, $13,744 housing and $7,604 food, or $22,372 a year. That is about $11,186 a semester, or $2,486 a month over nine. Add the $380 non-billed spending for about $2,866. Your real contribution can be lower; do not treat the ceiling as a quote. Loans, up to the $2,000 maximum, and work-study reduce the monthly cash pressure: $2,000 is about $222 a month.

Even with aid, the numbers can strain a family. The Sun quoted Student Assembly finance vice president Hayden Watkins '28 saying his family had to do [some financial gymnastics, even with aid](https://www.cornellsun.com/article/2025/09/financial-gymnastics-cornell-s-cost-of-attendance-surpasses-100k-for-some-students-in-2025-2026).

## Scenario 3: full pay

You pay the full [$96,318 billed](https://finaid.cornell.edu/cost-attend): about $48,159 a semester, or $10,702 a month over nine. Add $380 non-billed for about $11,082 a month, then travel and, if you do not waive it, $341 for health insurance.

Non-aided students saw tuition rise [$1,806 to $2,680 for 2026-27](https://news.cornell.edu/stories/2026/03/board-trustees-approves-2026-27-budget-parameters). Cheaper levers are limited: a [co-op double runs $3,487 to $4,981 a semester](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/housing-details/rates-amenities-licenses/housing-rates) against $6,872 for a standard double. See [our meal plan or cooking comparison](/money/cornell-off-campus-meal-plan-or-cook-yourself-cost/) and the [Collegetown, Fall Creek and downtown guide](/housing/collegetown-vs-fall-creek-vs-downtown-ithaca-where-to-live/).

## Scenario 4: graduate student in Ithaca

Tuition varies by program, so the aid office lists only fees ($701) and non-billed estimates for Ithaca grad and professional students in 2026-27: [housing $19,962, food $5,768, books $2,464, transportation $3,754, personal $4,196, a $36,144 subtotal](https://finaid.cornell.edu/graduate-students). That is about $3,012 a month, or about $3,070 with the fees spread over 12 months. No health insurance line appears.

The Graduate School's stipend minimum, effective August 21, 2026, is [$36,684 for nine months for assistantships and University Fellowships, $48,912 for 12 months for assistantships and $44,463 for fellowships](https://gradschool.cornell.edu/financial-support/stipend-rates/). The nine-month stipend works out to $4,076 a month while it is paid, but spread over 12 months it is $3,057, about $13 a month below the $3,070 estimate, so a nine-month appointment leaves no margin and needs a summer plan. The 12-month assistantship is $4,076 a month, which leaves a margin of roughly $1,000 a month above the estimate before any deductions. A fellowship's 12-month rate is $3,705 a month, about $635 above it. Health insurance at $341 a month would take about a third of the assistantship margin and over half of the fellowship margin.

Registered PhD students and graduate assistants get [free TCAT OmniRide passes](https://fcs.cornell.edu/departments/transportation-delivery-services/sustainable-transportation-options/bus-services-privileges-passes), so the $3,754 transportation estimate is a general figure, not a bus-fare line for them.

## Where budgets break

1. **Skipping the health plan decision.** The fall waiver deadline was [July 31, 2026](https://studenthealthbenefits.cornell.edu/node/179). Spring new matriculated students have an earlier waiver deadline, [January 21, 2027](https://studenthealthbenefits.cornell.edu/node/179). Waivers must be approved every semester, and spring reverification is due January 31, 2027. After a deadline, a refund requires a termination of coverage request.
2. **Treating travel as free.** The aid table leaves it unpriced.
3. **Forgetting the bus.** Students enrolled a year or more get free OmniRide rides after 6 p.m. on weekdays and all day on weekends; the standard pass is [$200 a year](https://fcs.cornell.edu/departments/transportation-delivery-services/sustainable-transportation-options/bus-services-privileges-passes), with the current rate shown at purchase.
4. **Reading the monthly figure as a bill.** Cornell bills per semester.

## FAQ

**Does financial aid cover books and personal expenses?** The affordability page does not say. Ask the aid office at 607-255-5145 or finaid@cornell.edu, the [contact it gives for off-campus budgets](https://finaid.cornell.edu/special-circumstances/living-campus).

**What if I live off campus?** The aid office advises looking for housing that [costs less for 12 months than its off-campus housing and dining budget](https://finaid.cornell.edu/special-circumstances/living-campus), and says aid cannot cover a security deposit or rent in months you are not registered. We could not find the budget figure published, so ask for it.

**Do these numbers change?** Yearly. Tuition, housing, meal plans and stipends are reset each year.
