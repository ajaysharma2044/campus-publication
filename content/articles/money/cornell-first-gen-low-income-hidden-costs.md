The costs that catch first-gen and low-income students at Cornell are the ones nobody bills you for: a winter coat, a week of housing when the dorms close, the trip home, the student health plan, and a few hundred dollars of books and personal spending. Cornell's [Access Fund](https://scl.cornell.edu/sub/belonging-support-services/centers-student-equity-empowerment-and-belonging/first-generation-low-income-student-support/access-fund) covers some of them (winter clothing, a replacement laptop or phone, interview attire) and the rest have separate fixes. Below is each gap, what it costs when Cornell publishes a number, and where to get help. The Access Fund is open through April 15, 2027, and winter break housing is due December 1, so two of these have deadlines close by.

The pattern is not unique to Cornell. A FirstGen Forward summary of uAspire's report says [indirect expenses are more than half the cost of college](https://www.firstgenforward.org/reports/beyond-the-college-bill-the-hidden-hurdles-of-indirect-expenses) and that colleges often do not tell students about them. At Cornell, Michelle Van-Ess Grant, senior associate dean of students, [told the Cornell Daily Sun](https://www.cornellsun.com/article/2025/09/financial-gymnastics-cornell-s-cost-of-attendance-surpasses-100k-for-some-students-in-2025-2026) that books, travel, professional clothing and basic needs can weigh heavily on first-gen and low-income students.

## What Cornell's own estimate leaves out

Cornell's [cost page](https://finaid.cornell.edu/cost-attend) lists $1,234 for books and course materials and $2,182 for personal expenses as the annual amounts not billed by Cornell when we checked in November 2026. Transportation is listed as "Varies", with a footnote that [costs to and from campus vary by region](https://finaid.cornell.edu/cost-attend). The Student Health Plan is not in the estimate, and costs not charged to all students, such as optional fees and the plan, [are not eligible for need-based grant or scholarship aid](https://finaid.cornell.edu/cost-attend). The page says you may request student loan funding for them instead.

The Sun reported the 2025-26 plan at [$3,685 for new students and $4,020 for returning students](https://www.cornellsun.com/article/2025/09/financial-gymnastics-cornell-s-cost-of-attendance-surpasses-100k-for-some-students-in-2025-2026), with a waiver due July 31. Check the current year's price and waiver rules before you decide.

### Winter clothing

The Access Fund's Basic Needs category provides a [cold weather coat, hat and gloves](https://scl.cornell.edu/sub/belonging-support-services/centers-student-equity-empowerment-and-belonging/first-generation-low-income-student-support/access-fund) through the Cornell Store. Decisions go to your Cornell email, and the items are provided through the Cornell Store. Each Access Fund category [needs its own application](https://scl.cornell.edu/sub/belonging-support-services/centers-student-equity-empowerment-and-belonging/first-generation-low-income-student-support/access-fund), so apply for this one before the first cold month, not after.

### Laptop, phone, interview clothes

The Maximize Cornell category lists [temporary laptop replacement, temporary cell phone replacement, career readiness attire, graduation expenses and, for first-semester students only, gym memberships](https://scl.cornell.edu/sub/belonging-support-services/centers-student-equity-empowerment-and-belonging/first-generation-low-income-student-support/access-fund). The page gives no dollar cap for Basic Needs or Maximize Cornell, and says benefits depend on the individual application. The Power of Experience category (internships, research, conferences, study abroad and similar) pays [up to $1,500](https://scl.cornell.edu/sub/belonging-support-services/centers-student-equity-empowerment-and-belonging/first-generation-low-income-student-support/access-fund) and also asks for a resume, a budget and an alternative plan.

### Breaks: housing and food

Residence halls do not simply stay open. Winter break housing costs [$125 for either one-week option or $500 for the full break](https://scl.cornell.edu/sub/sub/residential-life/housing/campus-housing/winter-break-housing), with the application open October 1 and due December 1. You must be returning to on-campus housing for spring. Students not living in a designated open building can apply only for the full break at $500, and only for limited flex spaces.

Some residents stay free: [HILC/Low Rise 8 and Loving House residents, and Veterans House residents](https://scl.cornell.edu/sub/sub/residential-life/housing/campus-housing/winter-break-housing) because it is in their academic-year license. The housing page itself says its fee text is hard to read, so confirm amounts in the Housing and Dining Portal. We found no fee waiver on the page; ask the FGLI office if the fee is a problem.

Food is the second half. Fall meal plans [end with the semester](https://scl.cornell.edu/sub/sub/residential-life/housing/campus-housing/winter-break-housing). Cornell Dining is [closed December 25, 2026 to January 1, 2027](https://scl.cornell.edu/sub/sub/residential-life/dining/meal-plans-rates/winter-break-dining), fall BRBs work at open cafés through December 24 and again January 4 to 19, and City Bucks lets you pay for off-campus meals or groceries with your ID. The FGLI office is the contact for [urgent food needs](https://scl.cornell.edu/sub/belonging-support-services/centers-student-equity-empowerment-and-belonging/first-generation-low-income-student-support/swipe-out-hunger), and [Swipe Out Hunger](https://scl.cornell.edu/sub/belonging-support-services/centers-student-equity-empowerment-and-belonging/first-generation-low-income-student-support/swipe-out-hunger) offers temporary support for food-insecure students (fall 2026 runs September 21 to December 8). A 2018 Cornell Chronicle feature notes that [low-income students may not be able to go home over breaks or may worry about food when dining hours shrink](https://news.cornell.edu/node/305416).

### Toiletries

[Big Red Basics](https://scl.cornell.edu/sub/belonging-support-services/centers-student-equity-empowerment-and-belonging/first-generation-low-income-student-support/big-red-basics) is a free cabinet of hygiene and wellness items in 206 Willard Straight Hall, open Monday to Friday, 9 a.m. to 7 p.m. No request or appointment is needed.

### Travel home

We did not find a Cornell fund for flights or bus tickets home, and the Access Fund page does not list one. Cornell publishes no travel figure; the cost page says only that it [varies by region](https://finaid.cornell.edu/cost-attend). International students feel this most. A 2021 Sun article reported that [aid packages do not adjust for the cost of international flights](https://cornellsun.com/2021/12/07/first-generation-and-low-income-international-students-call-for-increased-support-resources/), and that some students go years between visits home. It also reported on-campus work for international students is limited, with work-study [capped at $1,300 per semester at the time](https://cornellsun.com/2021/12/07/first-generation-and-low-income-international-students-call-for-increased-support-resources/). Those rules may have changed since 2021, so ask Financial Aid.

### Course and move-in costs

We did not find an official Cornell page that lists lab fees or move-in costs, so we will not give a number. Our advice: before you enroll, ask each instructor or department what a course adds beyond the textbook, and ask the FGLI office whether a gap fits the Access Fund.

## Do this in order

```figure

title: Hidden-cost checklist

  - label: Decide on winter break housing by December 1
  - label: Check the health plan price and waiver deadline
  - label: Ask the FGLI office about food, travel and any gap not listed
```

1. **Apply for the Access Fund early.** Applications are open [September 21, 2026 through April 15, 2027](https://scl.cornell.edu/sub/belonging-support-services/centers-student-equity-empowerment-and-belonging/first-generation-low-income-student-support/access-fund) and reviewed on a rolling basis on the 15th of each month. Undergraduates attach their 2026-27 Financial Aid Summary. It is [not an emergency fund](https://scl.cornell.edu/sub/belonging-support-services/centers-student-equity-empowerment-and-belonging/first-generation-low-income-student-support/access-fund).
2. **Decide on winter break housing before December 1.**
3. **Price the health plan before the waiver date.**
4. **Email the FGLI office for anything else.** The office is in Barnes Hall, Room 200, at [607-254-1288 or FGLIEmpowerment@cornell.edu](https://scl.cornell.edu/sub/first-generation-low-income-support).

## Common mistakes

Our advice, drawn from the rules above:

- Treating the Access Fund as one application. Each category needs its own.
- Waiting for an emergency. The fund says it is not one; the FGLI office is the contact for urgent needs.
- Budgeting only from the billed bill. The estimate leaves out transportation and the health plan.
- Assuming the dorms stay open. Break housing is an application with a fee unless you live in a house that waives it.

## FAQ

**Does the Access Fund cover rent or groceries?** The page lists three categories (Basic Needs, Power of Experience, Maximize Cornell) and does not list rent or groceries. For other needs, [email the FGLI office](https://scl.cornell.edu/sub/belonging-support-services/centers-student-equity-empowerment-and-belonging/first-generation-low-income-student-support/access-fund).

**Who can apply?** [Full-time undergraduate, graduate and professional students on the Ithaca campus](https://scl.cornell.edu/sub/belonging-support-services/centers-student-equity-empowerment-and-belonging/first-generation-low-income-student-support/access-fund) who are low or limited income and show high financial need.

**What do students say about the support?** In a 2021 Sun article on a student-made resource guide, Rafael Bitanga '23 is quoted saying [the resources still feel limited in times of need](https://www.cornellsun.com/article/2021/03/first-generation-students-create-resources-guide-to-help-each-other-navigate-campus). The guide planned sections on employment, scholarships, housing, textbooks, clothing, food and study abroad.
