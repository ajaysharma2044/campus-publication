Most Cornell students do not need a car, and the numbers explain why. A resident permit is about $753 a year and sold in limited numbers, first-year students ride TCAT buses free, and Ithaca Carshare covers the occasional errand for $30 a year plus usage. A car makes sense mainly if you live off campus far from a bus line, commute, or have a regular trip home that no bus serves. **All permit and TCAT prices below come from Cornell pages labeled 2025-26. Rates for 2026-27 were not posted when we checked, so confirm them on Cornell's page before you buy.**

## What a car costs on campus

Cornell's [student parking page](https://fcs.cornell.edu/departments/transportation-delivery-services/parking/parking-students) lists these annual rates (map labeled 2025-26):

```figure
kind: cost_table
title: Cornell student parking and alternatives, annual, 2025-26
rows:
  - label: Resident permit
    value: "$752.98"
  - label: Commuter B permit
    value: "$359.77"
  - label: Motorcycle permit
    value: "$120.01"
  - label: TCAT OmniRide pass
    value: "$200"
  - label: Ithaca Carshare, Just In Case
    value: "$30 plus usage"
```

- **Resident permit:** $752.98 a year. The rate table on the [student parking page](https://fcs.cornell.edu/departments/transportation-delivery-services/parking/parking-students) shows daily proration from September 1, but the Requirements section on the same page gives August 16 for annual permits. Confirm the start date when you buy. The permit is for university housing residents and sold as limited availability. The North Campus ND permit is described as "extremely limited" and is limited to North Campus residents of university-owned housing.
- **Commuter B permit:** $359.77 a year, for commuters only, also limited availability.
- **Everything else:** the [parking page](https://fcs.cornell.edu/departments/transportation-delivery-services/parking-campus) says permits are first come, first served and space available, and every student with a vehicle must register the plate with Transportation Services, even without a permit. Rules are enforced whenever the university is open. Daily Decision lets you pay per day through the ParkMobile app.

Those prices are only the permit. Insurance, gas, maintenance and registration are not on any Cornell page, so price them yourself before deciding. A College Confidential poster, blprof, [said the North Campus pass was $750 for the year](https://talk.collegeconfidential.com/t/should-i-bring-a-car/1811430) and that you cannot drive to class. Another, jfx246, added that the buildings used for classes do not have their own lots. Ranza123 called parking fairly expensive with very little on or off campus.

## The alternatives, priced

**TCAT.** First-year students [automatically get OmniRide privileges](https://fcs.cornell.edu/departments/transportation-delivery-services/sustainable-transportation-options/bus-services-privileges-passes) for unlimited rides. Fall entrants lose them on August 31 and spring entrants on December 31. After that, registered degree students ride free after 6 pm on weekdays and all day on weekends, or buy an annual OmniRide pass for $200 a year. PhD students and graduate assistants receive free OmniRide passes, per the same [bus passes page](https://fcs.cornell.edu/departments/transportation-delivery-services/sustainable-transportation-options/bus-services-privileges-passes). The Cornell Daily Sun reported in [November 2024](https://cornellsun.com/2024/11/03/non-first-year-cornell-students-no-longer-able-to-access-tcat-for-free/) that students without a pass had lost earlier all-hours free access; it quoted Amy Hinesley, class of 2025, saying she bought a bus pass after her swipe stopped working.

**Ithaca Carshare.** Cornell [helps subsidize annual memberships](https://www.ithacacarshare.org/students-commuters/) for undergraduate and graduate students, and the [Cornell sustainability page](https://sustainable.cornell.edu/campus-initiatives/transportation/ride-car-sharing) lists four pickup locations on campus and four more in Collegetown and nearby neighborhoods. Two plans:

- **Just In Case:** $30 a year. $9 an hour plus 42 cents a mile for cars; meant for under 6 hours a month.
- **It's My Car:** $310 a year. $6 an hour plus 42 cents a mile; meant for over 6 hours a month.

Both add a one-time $20 application fee per driver, plus 8% sales tax and 12% special passenger car rental tax. The [home page](https://www.ithacacarshare.org/) says gas or EV charging, maintenance and insurance are included. A worked example with our arithmetic: a three-hour, 30-mile grocery run on Just In Case is $27 in hourly charges plus $12.60 in mileage, about $39.60 before taxes.

The catch is [eligibility](https://www.ithacacarshare.org/eligibility/): 18 or older with a full, unrestricted license held at least two years. Drivers under 21 need a completely clean record, and ages 18 to 20 also need a signed consent form and an insurance declarations page showing at least $300,000 in liability coverage per accident. Applicants under 21 must also be covered by their own insurance policy. One College Confidential poster, KloudKoder, found that most freshmen will not have the two years of licensing Carshare requires.

## Annual cost side by side

This is our own arithmetic from the published 2025-26 rates above, not a Cornell figure. It covers only the sourced items; insurance and gas for an owned car are not included, so price those yourself.

| Scenario | Sourced items | Annual total from those items |
|---|---|---|
| Own a car, resident permit | Permit $752.98 | $752.98, plus insurance, gas, maintenance (price these yourself) |
| Own a car, commuter | Commuter B permit $359.77 | $359.77, plus insurance, gas, maintenance (price these yourself) |
| Carshare, Just In Case, 10 grocery-run trips | $30 membership + 10 x $39.60 | $426.00, before the one-time $20 fee and taxes |
| Carshare, Just In Case, 4 trips | $30 membership + 4 x $39.60 | $188.40, before the one-time $20 fee and taxes |
| TCAT OmniRide pass | $200 | $200 (free for PhD students and graduate assistants) |

The trip figure is the three-hour, 30-mile example from the Carshare section. Carshare already includes gas, maintenance and insurance, so the rows are closer to a full cost than the car rows. The owned car will cost more than its permit once you add the items Cornell does not list.

## Trips home

Buses are the usual answer for New York City. Two College Confidential posters, 3rdsontocollege and blprof, said buses to NYC are easy and direct. The Sun reported in [September 2026](https://www.cornellsun.com/article/2026/09/8kl4l6fodjwk) that OurBus will run four to six buses daily between Cornell and Manhattan, with stops at the Ithaca Commons, West Campus and North Campus. OurBus co-founder Axel Hellman said demand rose partly because Ithaca Tompkins Airport lost its direct Newark flight in 2025. The article gave no fares, so check the carrier. For a drive to somewhere no bus goes, price a one-way Carshare rental with the rates above, or bring the car only for the trip home and leave it.

## If you are...

- **A first-year living on campus:** do not bring a car. TCAT is free and the resident permits are scarce.
- **A PhD student or graduate assistant:** your OmniRide pass is free, so the bus is the baseline to beat.
- **An upperclass student in Collegetown:** probably skip it. See [where to live](/housing/collegetown-vs-fall-creek-vs-downtown-ithaca-where-to-live/) for how walkable each area is. Join Carshare if you qualify.
- **A student living farther out or commuting:** the $359.77 commuter permit or a car can pay off, but check lot access first.
- **Someone who cooks at home:** a Carshare membership plus the grocery run is cheaper than a car; compare with [meal plan versus cooking](/money/cornell-off-campus-meal-plan-or-cook-yourself-cost/).
- **A student with a job off campus:** read [off-campus jobs in Ithaca](/careers/ithaca-part-time-jobs-for-cornell-students/) for where they are, then check the bus route.

## Common mistakes

Our advice:

- Buying the car permit before checking whether you are eligible. Resident permits are limited and recalled if you do not meet the housing rules.
- Skipping plate registration because you have no permit. Cornell requires it anyway.
- Assuming Carshare is open to you. Count your license years first, and if you are under 21, check that you have your own insurance policy.
- Pricing only the permit and forgetting insurance and gas.

## FAQ

**Is there a car-sharing service on campus?** Yes, Ithaca Carshare, which is the only car-sharing service Cornell's sustainability page names.

**Can first-years bring cars?** The student parking page lists no first-year restriction, but permits are limited and tied to housing.

**What about the other cheap options?** For groceries and errands, see the [getting-around guides](/getting-around/).
