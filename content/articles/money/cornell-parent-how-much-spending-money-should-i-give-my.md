Plan on roughly $250 to $600 a month in cash for a Cornell undergraduate who lives on campus on the meal plan, and decide where in that range from three things: who pays for books, how much your student eats and travels off the plan, and whether they work. Cornell's aid office gives 2025-26 estimates of [$2,182 for personal expenses and $1,234 for books and course materials a year](https://finaid.cornell.edu/cost-attend), a $3,416 total that is not billed by the university. The page's 2026-27 section lists no figures for these lines. Spread over nine academic months, our assumption and not on Cornell's page, that is about $380 a month (our arithmetic). Housing, food on the plan, fees and tuition are billed separately, so they do not come out of this number.

## What Cornell's budget already covers

The aid office's cost table splits costs into those Cornell bills and those it does not. The $1,234 for books, $2,182 for personal expenses and $3,416 subtotal are [Cornell's 2025-26 estimates](https://finaid.cornell.edu/cost-attend); the page's 2026-27 section lists no figures for these lines, so treat them as the 2025-26 estimate rather than a locked 2026-27 price.

The page says [transportation to and from campus varies by region](https://finaid.cornell.edu/cost-attend) and gives no number. It also lists the personal expenses line without saying what it covers, so do not read it as a promise that $182 a month buys everything. Our split of the same table, over nine months: about $137 a month for books and about $242 a month for personal expenses.

Health insurance is not in that table either. If your student does not waive the Cornell plan, it is a separate bill; see our [monthly budget by aid situation](/money/cornell-student-budget-by-scenario-low-income-student-costs/) for the 2026-27 plan price.

## A range by situation (our advice)

These tiers are our judgment built on the $380 baseline, not a Cornell figure.

| Your student | Suggested cash per month | Why |
|---|---|---|
| On the meal plan, parents pay books and laundry, student has a campus job | $250 to $300 | Covers the personal-expenses line only, about $242 a month on Cornell's 2025-26 estimate |
| On the meal plan, student buys own books | $350 to $450 | Matches the $380 baseline plus some buffer |
| Off the plan or cooking, goes out with friends | $450 to $600 | Groceries, takeout and Collegetown outings are not in the personal line |
| Full aid, no work-study yet | Ask the aid office first | Whether aid covers books, travel and personal costs depends on the package |

For comparison, a Mercer Advisors FAQ (updated July 2026, no author named) calls [a weekly cap of $100 to $150 reasonable for a cost-conscious student](https://www.merceradvisors.com/faq/how-much-spending-money-should-i-give-my-college-student-each-month/), which is roughly $430 to $650 a month at 4.33 weeks. It is national advice, written for no campus in particular, and it counts all discretionary spending, so it lands at the top of our range.

## What other parents say

A 2015 College Confidential thread asked this exact question for a Cornell student. It is old, so read it as culture rather than numbers. One poster, oldfort, said allowances range [from unlimited, to $1,000 a month, down to $20 to $50 a month](https://talk.collegeconfidential.com/t/spending-money/1707056), and gives their own kids a few hundred dollars a month. Other posters in the thread gave no monthly amount: some pay for books, clothing, travel or a laundry account directly, and some students cover leisure from work-study or summer earnings. Nobody gave a standard Cornell figure, which is the gap this page fills.

## Pay directly, or give cash?

Our advice: split it. Pay the predictable, lumpy costs yourself (books, travel home, the health plan) and send a smaller monthly amount for the rest. A student who gets $300 and knows books are handled budgets better than one who gets $380 and discovers a $200 course pack in week one. See our guide to [cheap Cornell textbooks](/money/cornell-textbooks-cheap-where-to-buy-used-ithaca/) before you commit to a books number.

## Ways to lower the number

- Check whether the [meal plan fits how your student eats](/money/cornell-meal-plan-worth-it/). Swipes unused are money spent.
- Point them to [student discounts in Ithaca](/money/how-to-save-money-at-cornell-student-discounts-ithaca/).
- If they want to earn cash, off-campus jobs pay for the outings the personal line does not.

## Common mistakes

1. **Counting housing and food twice.** Those are billed by Cornell, so they are not spending money.
2. **Forgetting travel.** The aid table leaves it unpriced, and flights or bus fares can dwarf a month of spending.
3. **Setting the amount once and never revisiting it.** Review it each semester against what your student actually spent.
4. **Skipping the aid office call.** If your student has aid, ask what it assumes for books and personal costs before setting a number.

## FAQ

**Is $380 a month enough?** For books plus personal costs on Cornell's 2025-26 estimate, yes. For a social student who is off the meal plan, probably not.

**What about international students?** Cornell's global learning office gives a single undergraduate living expense figure of [$27,507 for the 2026-27 academic year](https://international.globallearning.cornell.edu/life-cornell/tuition-and-living-expenses), about $2,292 a month. It does not break that figure into housing, food, books or personal costs, so it cannot tell you what to send for spending alone.

**Should the amount rise each year?** Cornell's estimates are reset yearly. Check the aid office's cost page each summer.

**Does this change for grad students?** Yes, the budget is much larger and is a living wage question rather than an allowance; see the grad scenario in our [monthly budget guide](/money/cornell-student-budget-by-scenario-low-income-student-costs/).
