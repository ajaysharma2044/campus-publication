If you are on an F-1 or J-1 visa, you can work on campus for Cornell for up to 20 hours a week while classes are in session. You find the job yourself in Workday, complete the Form I-9 with the hiring department, and apply for a Social Security number once the job has started. F-1 students need no special work authorization. J-1 students need an authorization letter first. Here is the path in order, with the limits that can cost you your status.

## What the rules let you do

Cornell's Office of Global Learning says F-1 and J-1 students are [limited to on-campus work done for Cornell and paid by the university](https://international.globallearning.cornell.edu/node/182). F-1 students can take any campus job, including one outside their field. J-1 students need an on-campus employment authorization letter before they start, and authorization can only be given [12 months at a time](https://international.globallearning.cornell.edu/node/182).

Foreign national students are not eligible for Federal Work Study, but the [on-campus work page](https://international.globallearning.cornell.edu/node/182) says many campus jobs are open to anyone.

## Hours limits

| When | What you can work |
|---|---|
| Classes in session, registered | Up to 20 hours a week |
| Summer and winter breaks, not registered | Full-time or part-time |
| End of a semester | Full-time only after your last class day and last exam |
| Other non-class periods | Full-time, but it must end before classes resume |

All of this comes from [Cornell's on-campus work page](https://international.globallearning.cornell.edu/node/182), which calls exactly 20 hours the weekly maximum and describes going over it, even once, as a serious violation of your immigration status. If you hold more than one campus job, confirm with International Services how the 20-hour maximum applies to your combined hours. If you think you may have gone over, contact an International Services advisor right away. Your semester dates are on the [academic calendar](https://registrar.cornell.edu/academic-calendar).

Two extra notes. The [Student Employment FAQ](https://studentemployment.cornell.edu/frequently-asked-questions) says you also cannot work during your scheduled class times. And Cornell Tech's student affairs office [encourages Masters students to limit work to 10 hours a week](https://studentaffairs.tech.cornell.edu/student-life-2/international-students/employment-and-taxes) (a Cornell Tech recommendation, not a visa rule).

## Step by step

1. **Check in with International Services.** The Student Employment FAQ says international students [should visit International Services before seeking on-campus employment](https://studentemployment.cornell.edu/frequently-asked-questions). If you are J-1 with a Cornell DS-2019, request the authorization letter through iStart, per the [on-campus work page](https://international.globallearning.cornell.edu/node/182). If another sponsor such as Fulbright issued your DS-2019, the letter comes from that sponsor.
2. **Prepare a resume and cover letter.** These are the first step on Cornell's [Find a Job page](https://studentemployment.cornell.edu/find-a-job).
3. **Search and apply in Workday.** Part-time, hourly student jobs are [posted on the student job board in Workday](https://studentemployment.cornell.edu/find-a-job). You run your own search and apply to postings yourself. Cornell HR also has a [Workday help page for student workers](https://hr.cornell.edu/browse-self-service/workday-help-student-workers) with a how-to video on applying. The Find a Job page warns about phishing aimed at student job seekers, so verify odd messages against Cornell's IT Phish Bowl.
4. **Complete the I-9 with your hiring department.** Cornell's [I-9 page](https://studentemployment.cornell.edu/resources/i-9-form) says that after you are hired you get a Workday notification to complete Form I-9. Complete Section 1 by your first day of work, but not before you accept the offer, then present original identity and employment authorization documents to an authorized Cornell representative within three business days of your start date. Photocopies and digital images are not accepted. Cornell HR has a [guide for students completing the I-9 in Workday](https://hr.cornell.edu/browse-self-service/workday-help-student-workers). Cornell says your department helps you with this, so you [do not need International Services](https://international.globallearning.cornell.edu/node/182) for it. J-1 students need it updated every year because authorization runs 12 months.
5. **Apply for your SSN.** See the next section.
6. **Enter your time in Workday.** Cornell HR has a [job aid and video](https://hr.cornell.edu/browse-self-service/workday-help-student-workers) for submitting time worked. Student employees are [paid bi-weekly on Thursdays](https://studentemployment.cornell.edu/frequently-asked-questions), by direct deposit or, without it, a paper check.

## Getting a Social Security number

The order trips people up: you apply for the SSN after the job exists. Cornell says you can apply once you have been in the U.S. at least 10 days and your job has started. After you submit the online application, you have 45 days to bring original documents to a Social Security office, per the [SSN page](https://international.globallearning.cornell.edu/employment-and-taxes/apply-ssn).

- **F-1 with a campus job:** bring your passport, I-94 record, I-20, an SSN eligibility letter from International Services, and a pay stub or hiring-department letter.
- **J-1:** the eligibility letter from International Services is required.
- **Timing:** the eligibility letter is valid for 30 days, so request it when you will apply within about two weeks.
- **Where:** the Ithaca office is at 127 West State Street, second floor of the Greenstate Building.

Ask your hiring department and International Services whether you can start working before the SSN arrives. If you already have an SSN from earlier U.S. work, you do not need a new one.

## Taxes

All of this is [Cornell's guidance](https://international.globallearning.cornell.edu/employment-and-taxes/us-income-tax-basics) for international students, and it changes by year:

- Wages are U.S.-source income. Federal and New York returns for the 2026 tax year are due April 15, 2027.
- Even with no U.S. income, you must send the IRS Form 8843. In that case you generally do not file a tax return. Cornell provides Sprintax, free, to work out which forms you need. The Cornell Tech page [advises against TurboTax](https://studentaffairs.tech.cornell.edu/student-life-2/international-students/employment-and-taxes) for this.
- Many nonresident students do not pay Social Security and Medicare taxes in their first five years in the U.S. If that is you and they are withheld, you can ask your employer for a refund or claim it when you file.
- If your country has a tax treaty with the U.S., a Foreign National Questionnaire is how Cornell applies treaty benefits to campus pay.

## Common mistakes

Our advice, drawn from the rules above:

- Starting a second campus job without asking International Services how the 20-hour maximum applies to your combined hours.
- Leaving the J-1 letter until after an offer. You cannot start without it.
- Waiting on the SSN eligibility letter too long. It expires after 30 days.
- Treating a paid internship off campus as an "on-campus" job. That is [Curricular Practical Training](https://international.globallearning.cornell.edu/node/185), which needs approval and a new I-20 before you work.
- Booking full-time hours the week of finals. Full-time work starts only after your last exam.

## FAQ

**Can I work off campus on an F-1?** Not as a student job. Off-campus work needs separate authorization such as CPT, which requires [a U.S.-based employer, a job related to your major, and an application through myStatus](https://international.globallearning.cornell.edu/node/185).

**What if I go over 20 hours?** Cornell treats it as a serious status violation. Contact International Services immediately.

**Does the authorization expire?** Yes. It lasts only while you keep F-1 or J-1 status and ends if you finish your final semester, take a leave of absence, or your I-20 or DS-2019 expires, per the [on-campus work page](https://international.globallearning.cornell.edu/node/182).

**Where do I ask about Student Employment?** Cornell's Student Employment office lists sesemp@cornell.edu and 607-255-5145 on its [Find a Job page](https://studentemployment.cornell.edu/find-a-job).
