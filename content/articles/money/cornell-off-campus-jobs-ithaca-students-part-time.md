If you have Federal Work-Study, the best off-campus job is a Community Work-Study placement, where [work-study covers 75 to 100 percent of the wage](https://einhorn.cornell.edu/?p=407) at an Ithaca or Tompkins County nonprofit, school or municipality. If you do not, you are looking at hotels, banquet and kitchen work, Collegetown shops and private tutoring, where you apply on your own. The state labor department says New York's [minimum wage outside New York City, Long Island and Westchester will stay the same on January 1, 2027, at $16.00 an hour](https://dol.ny.gov/minimum-wage-0), which sets the floor for most of it. The harder part is fitting a shift around classes and a bus schedule, which is what this page is about.

## Which route fits you

The central place to look is the [Cornell Student Employment page](https://www.studentemployment.cornell.edu/students). Start there for campus postings, then use the routes below for work in town.

| If you... | Start here | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Have Federal Work-Study | Community Work-Study | The program is [open to undergraduates eligible for FWS](https://einhorn.cornell.edu/?p=407); the employer pays only the unsubsidized share |
| Have work-study and like kids | America Reads or America Counts tutoring | These pre-K through 9th grade tutoring positions are [funded at 100 percent](https://einhorn.cornell.edu/?p=407) |
| Have no work-study and want regular hours | Hotels and conference center on the Chamber board | Employers there [list pay](https://business.tompkinschamber.org/jobs) |
| Have no work-study and want a short walk from class | Collegetown businesses | Apply in person |
| Are on an F-1 visa | International Services first | Off-campus work needs [specific authorization](https://international.globallearning.cornell.edu/employment-and-taxes/f-1-cpt) |

Off-campus jobs come through their own channels. Student and Campus Life's [student employment page](https://scl.cornell.edu/growth-opportunities/student-employment/students) points to Workday for campus postings and describes Community Work-Study as the way work-study students reach off-campus employers.

## Community Work-Study

This is the one structured off-campus program, and it is the only place where the employer's cost is reduced. The Einhorn Center page says FWS [covers at least 75 percent of wages](https://einhorn.cornell.edu/?p=407) for community-service positions, and off-campus wages can be subsidized 75 to 100 percent depending on the employer and position.

Postings listed on the page in November 2026 included a clothing closet assistant at Catholic Charities of Tompkins County, program lead, lifeguard and swim instructor roles at the YMCA of Ithaca and Tompkins County, communications and docent roles at the History Center of Tompkins County, a ReUse and circular economy associate at Finger Lakes ReUse, and a production associate at The Cherry Arts. Treat these as examples; postings change.

What you need, per the [same page](https://einhorn.cornell.edu/?p=407): an I-9 on file, FWS eligibility confirmed in Workday, full-time registration and no university holds. An Employee Earnings Expectation is not FWS and does not subsidize wages.

Rules that affect your week:

- Cornell "strongly suggest[s]" no more than [15 to 20 hours a week](https://einhorn.cornell.edu/?p=407) in the academic year.
- Hours cannot be recorded during your scheduled class or lab time, so a placement has to fit around your timetable.
- Timesheets are due in Workday by noon every other Wednesday.
- There is no holiday or vacation pay, and supervisor approval is required to work during a break.
- Two weeks' notice when you leave.

The program page says its postings are the place to start, and questions go to cwsp@cornell.edu. Getting to the job is on you (see below).

One student-experience note: a Cornell Daily Sun video feature from 2020 [featured two students, Peter Terrazas '23 and Lizette Conde '23](https://www.cornellsun.com/article/2020/02/watch-sciencenter-brings-student-workers-into-the-ithaca-community), who held off-campus federal work-study jobs at the Sciencenter, a hands-on science museum in Ithaca. It shows the kind of placement that exists beyond the usual nonprofit office.

Other listings on the board give pay without hours (a Fairfield Inn breakfast attendant at $17, a Canopy housekeeper at $17 to $18), and the Ithaca Downtown Conference Center has part-time banquet server, bartender, dish machine operator and cook postings. Several of those were posted in 2025, so confirm with the employer that the job is still open. Our advice: ask any hotel or banquet employer which evening, weekend and holiday shifts you would be expected to cover. That suits students with daytime classes and clashes with evening study groups.

If a job is tipped, the state labor department lists a [$10.70 cash wage plus a $5.30 tip credit](https://dol.ny.gov/minimum-wage-0) for tipped food service workers in 2027, for a total of $16.00. Only hospitality employers may take the credit.

## Collegetown and the Commons

Our advice for Collegetown restaurants and shops: walk in and ask for the manager during a slow hour. Two Sun pieces show what to weigh:

- A November 4, 2026 opinion column by Adriana Vink, an Opinion Columnist who is a student in ILR and identified as the Student Workers' Representative, looks back at the Collegetown Starbucks, which is a former location, not a current employer. The column says many of its workers were students who were [too often denied time off](https://www.cornellsun.com/article/2026/11/vink-get-starbucks-actually-off-campus) during regularly scheduled Cornell breaks, and cites a 2022 Sun article for that claim. It is an opinion piece about a closed store, not a rule, but the lesson holds for any employer: ask about break scheduling before you accept.
- The flip side: an April 2020 Sun piece reports that Collegetown Bagels co-owner Gregar Brous's group [cut 400 jobs across Collegetown Bagels, Rulloff's, Ithaca Bakery and Agava in three weeks](https://www.cornellsun.com/article/2020/04/ithaca-in-numbers-layoffs-surge-as-covid-19-and-student-departures-devastates-hospitality-sales). The Sun cites the student exodus plus COVID-19 restrictions as the cause of the broader hospitality downturn, not of these layoffs specifically. That was an extreme case, but it shows how much these businesses depend on the academic calendar. Expect hours to thin out over winter and summer breaks.

## Tutoring

With work-study, tutoring through America Reads or America Counts is the cheapest hire for the employer because the program covers 100 percent of wages. Without work-study, tutoring means finding private clients and setting your own rate; ask other tutors what they charge before you set one.

## Getting there without a car

Cornell's [bus page](https://fcs.cornell.edu/departments/transportation-delivery-services/sustainable-transportation-options/bus-services-privileges-passes) says first-year degree students get unlimited TCAT rides automatically, ending August 31 for fall entrants and December 31 for spring entrants. After that:

- Students enrolled in a degree program for a year or more ride [free on all TCAT buses after 6 p.m. on weekdays and all day Saturday and Sunday](https://fcs.cornell.edu/departments/transportation-delivery-services/sustainable-transportation-options/bus-services-privileges-passes), as long as they stay registered.
- Weekday daytime rides need a discounted OmniRide pass, bought at MyParking.Cornell.Edu, with a base rate of $200 a year prorated monthly. PhD students and graduate assistants get free passes.
- If your CU ID is not accepted at the farebox, you may need to pay the $1.50 cash fare.

Our advice: evening and weekend shifts are the cheapest to reach, because the free hours cover them. A weekday lunch or afternoon shift is where the pass pays for itself. Before you accept any job, check the route and the last bus home for the time your shift ends.

## Fitting a job around classes

1. Count your hours first. Cornell's 15 to 20 hours a week suggestion is for Community Work-Study, and a hotel or restaurant will not enforce it for you.
2. Put your fixed class and lab times in front of the employer at the interview. Community Work-Study does not allow hours to be logged during them.
3. Ask what happens during exam periods and breaks, and whether you will be scheduled for them.
4. Ask whether the shift ends before the last bus you can ride free.
5. If it is a night shift, check that it does not stack against an 8:00 a.m. class.

## If you are on an F-1 visa

Cornell International Services says F-1 students are generally limited to on-campus work without authorization. Its [CPT page](https://international.globallearning.cornell.edu/employment-and-taxes/f-1-cpt) says the work must be a curricular requirement for every student in the program, with a U.S. employer and directly related to your major, and that while classes are in session CPT is limited to 20 hours a week. You must have the authorization and a new I-20 before you start, and August 2026 SEVP guidance applies.

The same page sets three timing rules: you must have been enrolled full-time for at least one academic year (two full semesters) before you apply, though some graduate programs are exceptions; apply at least three business weeks before you want to start; and CPT approval cannot be backdated, so the start date must be in the future. A restaurant or retail job would not fit the description above, so talk to International Services before accepting any offer.

## Common mistakes

- Taking a nonprofit job that is not a Community Work-Study placement and expecting work-study to pay for it.
- Skipping the I-9 and Workday eligibility check.
- Accepting a weekday afternoon shift and discovering the bus is no longer free.
- Not asking about break and exam scheduling.

## FAQ

**Can I use Federal Work-Study off campus?** Yes, through [Community Work-Study](https://einhorn.cornell.edu/?p=407), at nonprofits, schools and municipalities. The page says universities do not count as a community for this purpose.

**Do I need work-study to work off campus?** No, only for Community Work-Study. Private employers hire without it, subject to the visa rules above.

**When do campus jobs hire?** Student and Campus Life says [most units hire in spring or summer for fall openings](https://scl.cornell.edu/growth-opportunities/student-employment/students), and some are always hiring.

**Can I work remotely for a local employer?** Community Work-Study allows it with a Remote Work Arrangement form, and the work must be done in the U.S.
