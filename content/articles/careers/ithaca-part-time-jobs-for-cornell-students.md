The most reliable off-campus route for a Cornell undergraduate with Federal Work-Study is the [Community Work-Study Program](https://einhorn.cornell.edu/?p=407), which places students at Ithaca and Tompkins County nonprofits, schools and municipalities. Without work-study, your best bets are downtown hotels, restaurants and event venues that post with the [Tompkins County Chamber](https://business.tompkinschamber.org/jobs), plus walking into Collegetown and Commons businesses, which Cornell itself [suggests as an option](https://as.cornell.edu/news/campus-work-can-provide-fulfilling-experiences). Most of the rest of this page is about picking among those, and getting to the job on a bus.

## Your main options

| Route | Best for | Pay | Where to look |
|---|---|---|---|
| Community Work-Study | Work-study students who want nonprofit, school or public-sector experience | Set by the employer; FWS covers at least 75% of wages | [Einhorn Center page](https://einhorn.cornell.edu/?p=407) |
| America Reads / America Counts tutoring | Tutoring pre-K through 9th grade | FWS covers 100% of wages | [Same page](https://einhorn.cornell.edu/?p=407) |
| Downtown hotels, venues, restaurants | Evening and weekend shifts | Listed examples run from $16 an hour up (see below) | [Chamber job board](https://business.tompkinschamber.org/jobs) |
| Collegetown and the Commons | Shifts near campus | Not published; ask the employer | Ask in person |

## What the pay looks like

The [New York State Department of Labor](https://dol.ny.gov/minimum-wage-0) lists the minimum wage for the rest of the state (outside New York City, Long Island and Westchester, so including Tompkins County) at $16.00 an hour, and says it stays the same on January 1, 2027. Chamber listings we saw in November 2026 that state pay: a Hotel Ithaca night auditor from $16 an hour, a Hotel Ithaca shuttle van driver at $16, a Fairfield Inn breakfast attendant at $17, a Canopy by Hilton housekeeper at $17 to $18, a PM line cook at Canopy at $19 to $21, and a Canopy valet supervisor at $21 to $22. Most listings do not say whether they suit a student schedule.

## Community Work-Study

The Einhorn Center says the program places about 250 students in local positions each academic year, in arts, K-12 education, environmental programs, health, legal services, older adults, public works, women's organizations and youth programs. Its [current postings](https://einhorn.cornell.edu/?p=407) included a clothing closet assistant at Catholic Charities, lifeguard and swim instructor roles at the YMCA, docent and communications roles at the History Center of Tompkins County, and a production associate at The Cherry Arts. Postings change, so treat those as examples of the kind of work, not an open list.

What you need before applying, per the same page: an I-9 on file, Federal Work-Study eligibility confirmed in Workday, full-time registration and no university holds. An Employee Earnings Expectation from financial aid does not subsidize wages.

Things worth knowing before you accept:

- The program suggests 15 to 20 hours a week during the academic year.
- Timesheets go in through Workday every two weeks, due by noon on Wednesday.
- Work-study does not pay for vacation or holidays. You do accrue New York paid sick leave at 1 hour per 30 hours worked, up to 56 hours a year.
- Hours over 40 in a week are paid at 1.5 times your rate and billed to the employer, which matters if you also hold a campus job.
- Give two weeks' notice when you leave.

Questions go to cwsp@cornell.edu.

Cornell's student employment office also lists [the program among its options](https://scl.cornell.edu/growth-opportunities/student-employment/students), and says every campus position in Workday notes whether it is work-study eligible, with a pay range, dates and weekly hours.

## Tutoring

Tutoring through America Reads and America Counts is the cheapest hire for an employer, because [work-study covers all of the wages](https://einhorn.cornell.edu/?p=407) for pre-K through 9th grade students. That makes it the first thing to try if you have FWS and like working with kids. If you do not have work-study, tutoring means private clients, and we did not find an official Cornell page listing rates, so ask other tutors before you set a price.

## Downtown hotels, venues and restaurants

The Tompkins County Chamber's [job board](https://business.tompkinschamber.org/jobs) lists openings from member employers. You can filter by location (City of Ithaca, Downtown Ithaca BID, Tompkins County) and by category, including Food Industry and Restaurants and Hotel, Gaming, Leisure, and Travel.

As of November 2026, the board showed these roles that name part-time hours:

- Valet Driver at Canopy by Hilton Downtown Ithaca: full-time and part-time, hourly pay plus tips (amount not listed), posted 10/08/2026.
- PM Line Cook at Canopy by Hilton Downtown Ithaca: part-time or full-time, $19 to $21 an hour, posted 08/26/2026.
- Night Auditor at The Hotel Ithaca: listed as part-time or full-time, from $16 an hour, posted 10/08/2026.

Related, but with hours not stated: Valet Supervisor at Canopy ($21 to $22 an hour, posted 10/04/2026), and a shuttle van driver at The Hotel Ithaca ($16, posted 04/22/2026) and a night auditor at a Fairfield Inn by Marriott ($18, posted 08/26/2026). Check the hours with the employer before applying. Overnight shifts also clash with early classes.

Honest limit: we could not find a reliable public list of which Collegetown restaurants are hiring now. For those, the usual route is to ask a manager during a slow hour, and to check the door signs.

## Getting there without a car

Most of these jobs are reachable by TCAT. Cornell's transportation page says students in their first year in a degree program [get unlimited TCAT rides automatically](https://fcs.cornell.edu/departments/transportation-delivery-services/sustainable-transportation-options/bus-services-privileges-passes), with those privileges ending August 31 for fall entrants and December 31 for spring entrants. After that:

- Registered students in a degree program can ride free after 6 p.m. on weekdays and all day on weekends, according to the same page.
- Daytime weekday rides need an OmniRide pass. The page says a discounted pass is available after your first year, and that the general annual rate is $200 a year, prorated monthly, with the current price shown when you buy at MyParking.Cornell.Edu. PhD students and graduate assistants get free passes.
- The same page says that if your CU ID card is not accepted, you may need to pay the $1.50 cash fare. A [November 2024 Cornell Daily Sun report](https://cornellsun.com/2024/11/03/non-first-year-cornell-students-no-longer-able-to-access-tcat-for-free/) says drivers should override the farebox if your ID is rejected during free hours. That conflicts with the official page, so carry cash until you know how your route works.

Our advice: a daytime shift on a weekday is where a pass pays for itself. Evening and weekend shifts are already free. Check the TCAT route and the last bus time for your shift's end before you accept it.

## If you are...

- **On Federal Work-Study:** start with Community Work-Study.
- **Not on work-study:** check the Chamber board, then ask in person around the Commons and Collegetown.
- **An international student on F-1 or J-1:** off-campus work needs specific authorization. Cornell's [CPT page](https://international.globallearning.cornell.edu/employment-and-taxes/f-1-cpt) says F-1 CPT is limited to work directly related to your major and required for your degree, with no more than 20 hours a week in fall and spring, and that August 2026 SEVP guidance narrowed CPT eligibility. A typical retail or restaurant job generally would not qualify. Talk to International Services before accepting any offer.
- **Looking for a first job with flexible hours:** Cornell Dining and other on-campus employers are an easier start. Student and Campus Life says [most units hire in spring or summer for fall](https://scl.cornell.edu/growth-opportunities/student-employment/students), and some are always hiring.

## What to do this week

1. Log in to Workday and confirm your Federal Work-Study eligibility.
2. Email cwsp@cornell.edu with questions about Community Work-Study placements.
3. Browse the [Chamber board](https://business.tompkinschamber.org/jobs) and filter to Downtown Ithaca or your category.
4. Look up the TCAT route and the last bus time for any shift before you accept it.

## Common mistakes

- Applying to a nonprofit without checking that it is a Community Work-Study partner. Work-study only pays when the placement is approved.
- Counting on a daytime free bus ride after your first year.
- Skipping the I-9. You must complete it before starting work.

## FAQ

**Can I use work-study off campus?** Yes, through the [Community Work-Study Program](https://einhorn.cornell.edu/?p=407), at nonprofits, schools and municipalities.

**Does the university count as a community employer?** No. The program page says universities do not count as a community for this purpose.

**Can I work remotely for a local employer?** The program page says remote positions need a Remote Work Arrangement form and must be performed in the U.S.

**Where do on-campus jobs live?** In Workday. Per [Student and Campus Life](https://scl.cornell.edu/growth-opportunities/student-employment/students), log in to Workday with your NetID and password, then search Find Jobs - student.
