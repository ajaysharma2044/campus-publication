To get into a full Cornell class, check the "wait list if class is full" box in Student Center, and keep a backup course in your schedule, because a waitlist is not a guarantee. Only [a limited number of classes have online wait lists](https://registrar.cornell.edu/students/enrollment/a-guide-to-enrollment), and the number Student Center shows you is not your real place in line. If there is no waitlist, or it is full, the next step is the department that offers the course. Here is the order to work in.

This guide is about course waitlists in Student Center, not the undergraduate admissions waitlist (as of November 2026).

## Step 1: Find out whether the class has a waitlist

1. In Student Center, run a Class Search and click the section.
2. Under Class Availability, read the Wait List Capacity. [A capacity of 0 means no waitlist; any larger number means one is available](https://registrar.cornell.edu/students/enrollment/common-enrollment-questions).
3. If there is no waitlist, [contact the offering department to ask whether a departmental waitlist exists](https://registrar.cornell.edu/students/enrollment/common-enrollment-errors). The registrar also says you can check back for openings or ask whether the department will add seats or sections.

## Step 2: Join the waitlist

When you enroll, [check the Wait List box on the "Select classes to add" Enrollment Preferences screen](https://registrar.cornell.edu/students/enrollment/common-enrollment-questions). The error message "Class XXXX is full. You have been placed on the wait list in position number 3" is the registrar's own example of success. To leave a waitlist, [do a drop transaction](https://registrar.cornell.edu/students/enrollment/common-enrollment-questions) in Student Center.

Your shopping cart does not hold a seat: [using it does not save you a spot or guarantee you can take the class](https://registrar.cornell.edu/students/enrollment/common-enrollment-questions). Dates for enrollment appointments and add/drop deadlines come from the academic calendar and the Enrollment Dates box in Student Center; they change each term. For how those dates fit together, see our [pre-enroll and add/drop strategy](/academics/cornell-course-enrollment-strategy-pre-enroll-tips/) and the [add/drop deadline and W guide](/academics/cornell-add-drop-deadline-w-on-transcript/).

## Step 3: Do not trust the position number

The Bowers registrar page says [Student Center waitlist numbers do not reflect actual priority](https://bowers.cornell.edu/offices/registrar). For Bowers courses, permission numbers (PINs) go out by priority: majors of the department first, then other Bowers majors, then everyone else, and within each group seniors go first. PINs [start at the end of the first week of add/drop, go out every other day as space allows, and end with the add period](https://bowers.cornell.edu/offices/registrar). A PIN arrives by email, cannot be transferred, and is typically valid for only 24 hours, so watch your inbox. Bowers asks students not to submit a ticket asking for waitlist or PIN status.

Other departments set their own rules. Physics says waitlists [open just before your pre-enroll window and close at 4pm ET on the day the add period closes](https://www.physics.cornell.edu/enrollment-guide). For PHYS 1110, 1112, 1116, 2210, 2213, 2214, 2217 and 2218, [the guarantee of a spot applies to Physics and Engineering students who join the waitlist during their pre-enroll period](https://www.physics.cornell.edu/enrollment-guide). Read the department's page for your course before assuming the general rule applies.

## Step 4: Watch the lecture-plus-section trap

Many courses need a lecture and a discussion or lab. These are the mistakes that cost students a seat they already had:

- **Swapping into a full section.** The registrar calls swapping only into open sections [best practice](https://registrar.cornell.edu/students/enrollment/student-center-help/swapping-sections-within-a-course), and warns against checking the waitlist box during a swap: if you are added to the waitlist, you are dropped from all sections of that class. Bowers says the same outcome applies when the target discussion is full: [you are automatically dropped from the course](https://bowers.cornell.edu/offices/registrar). Physics states plainly that [you cannot swap into a full class or section](https://www.physics.cornell.edu/enrollment-guide).
- **Dropping instead of swapping.** Physics says that if you drop and re-enroll yourself, [you are not added back automatically](https://www.physics.cornell.edu/enrollment-guide). The Engineering advising page gives the same warning for BIOMG 1350: [use swap, because dropping may mean the department cannot re-enroll you](https://www.duffield.cornell.edu/advising/course-enrollment-challenges/).
- **Enrolling in two sections that conflict**, hoping the department fixes it later. Physics says not to.

## Step 5: Keep a backup and ask the right person

The registrar's advice is to [have back-up courses planned in case you need to change your schedule](https://registrar.cornell.edu/students/enrollment/a-guide-to-enrollment). Bowers goes further: if you cannot get your first choice, [always enroll in a backup course](https://bowers.cornell.edu/offices/registrar). A backup must not conflict with the course you want, and you need room under your credit maximum.

Who to contact depends on the problem:

| Situation | Contact |
|---|---|
| Class requires consent | [The instructor or department named on the padlock](https://registrar.cornell.edu/students/enrollment/common-enrollment-questions) |
| Class and waitlist both full | [The offering college or department](https://registrar.cornell.edu/students/enrollment/common-enrollment-errors) |
| Add/drop deadline has passed | [Your college or school registrar](https://registrar.cornell.edu/students/enrollment/common-enrollment-questions) |
| Error message not on the registrar's list | [coursenroll@cornell.edu](https://registrar.cornell.edu/students/enrollment/common-enrollment-errors) |
| Some Engineering courses (MAE) | [Sibley School Qualtrics form, which does not guarantee placement](https://www.duffield.cornell.edu/advising/course-enrollment-challenges/) |

Application-based courses usually skip Student Center waitlists; [the professor emails a PIN to accepted students](https://bowers.cornell.edu/offices/registrar).

## If you are...

- **In Computer Science or Information Science:** Bowers says CS 1000 to 3000 level courses [are open, first-come, first-served](https://bowers.cornell.edu/offices/registrar), with CS 3700 and 3780 majors-only for pre-enrollment, and 4000 level courses are majors-only for pre-enrollment with waitlists for everyone else. The Engineering advising page says there are [no waitlists for CS 1000 to 3000 level courses](https://www.duffield.cornell.edu/advising/course-enrollment-challenges/), so you monitor seats instead. The two pages are consistent in practice: watch Student Center. A 2023 Sun article quoted Tommy Tsai '24, a computer science major, saying that getting into important classes means [checking Student Center every day during add/drop](https://www.cornellsun.com/article/2023/01/computer-and-information-science-students-struggle-with-course-enrollment-adding-stress-instead-of-classes). Our advice: for a course with a lecture and a lab or discussion, check that a seat is open in both, and keep a backup enrolled (see Step 5).
- **Taking physics for your major:** join the waitlist during pre-enroll. If your current Physics enrollment blocks a required course, Physics may move you into a full section; [email physicsenrollment@cornell.edu with your NetID, Cornell ID, the conflicting class, the required class and your advisor's contact](https://www.physics.cornell.edu/enrollment-guide). Requests for a specific time or section are not accepted.
- **A first-year looking for a writing seminar:** [enroll only in a seminar that has open spots](https://www.duffield.cornell.edu/advising/course-enrollment-challenges/). The same page says juniors and seniors may prefer ENGL 2880 or 2860, which can substitute for FWS credit.
- **Hitting a restriction, not a full class:** "Available seats are reserved" means [the class is limited to a certain population, such as A&S juniors](https://registrar.cornell.edu/students/enrollment/common-enrollment-errors). A waitlist will not fix that; ask the department.

## What students report

The Sun's 2023 piece quoted Emma Warden '25, an information science major, who [described a line of fifteen students trying to talk to the professor after class](https://www.cornellsun.com/article/2023/01/computer-and-information-science-students-struggle-with-course-enrollment-adding-stress-instead-of-classes). It quoted only students, so treat it as experience, not policy. It also reported enrollment pressure in computer and information science, with degrees nearly doubling between 2017 and 2022.

## Common mistakes

Our advice, drawn from the rules above:

- Reading a low waitlist number as safe. Bowers says it does not show real priority.
- Dropping a section to "make room" instead of using swap.
- Ticking the waitlist box during a swap.
- Skipping the backup course, then finding both seats gone.
- Ignoring a PIN email. Bowers says its PINs are typically good for 24 hours.
- Sharing a permission number. [They are issued to individual students and cannot be shared](https://registrar.cornell.edu/students/enrollment/common-enrollment-errors).

## FAQ

**Does being on the waitlist guarantee a seat?** No. The registrar's list of errors says to seek another course and check back for openings; some departments guarantee spots only under stated conditions, like Physics above.

**Can I log in from two devices to refresh faster?** Don't. The registrar says logging into Student Center from [multiple windows, browsers or devices can leave requests stuck in "pending"](https://registrar.cornell.edu/students/enrollment/a-guide-to-enrollment).

**What if I can't enroll at my appointment time at all?** [Check for holds first](https://registrar.cornell.edu/students/enrollment/a-guide-to-enrollment), then contact your college or school registrar.
