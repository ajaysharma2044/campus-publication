There is no reliable, current list of the easiest classes at Cornell, and the reason is official: the registrar says [median grade information is not calculated or made publicly available](https://registrar.cornell.edu/about/use-student-data). What exists instead is old student word of mouth, mostly intro-level courses in psychology, sociology, statistics and the earth sciences. This page collects what students have actually named, shows how old each claim is, and gives you a way to pick courses that does not depend on a stale forum post.

For more recent student voices, the Class Roster has a ["Show classes with syllabi only" option](https://classes.cornell.edu/), and the Sun's [2022 piece on iconic fall classes](https://cornellsun.com/2022/12/04/wrapping-up-a-semester-in-cornells-most-iconic-fall-classes) and [2018 "best course" Sunspots post](https://www.cornellsun.com/article/2018/10/sunspots-whats-the-best-course-youve-taken-at-cornell) are good places to look for courses people loved. Neither is a list of easy classes.

## Why you cannot look it up

Cornell used to publish this data. According to the Cornell Daily Sun, medians went on the registrar's website in 1998 and on transcripts in 2008, and the Faculty Senate [removed them from the website in 2011](https://cornellsun.com/2024/02/19/cornell-discontinues-median-grade-visibility-on-transcripts-15-years-after-inception/). The Cornell Chronicle reported that the Senate voted on May 11, 2011, after concluding that online grade information [induced students to select leniently graded courses](https://news.cornell.edu/node/270308). The registrar says median grade information is [not calculated or made publicly available](https://registrar.cornell.edu/about/use-student-data).

Transcripts followed. The Sun reports that on Dec. 15, 2023 the Faculty Senate approved a resolution ending median grades on transcripts, so [courses taken in Fall 2023 and later](https://cornellsun.com/2024/02/19/cornell-discontinues-median-grade-visibility-on-transcripts-15-years-after-inception/) do not show them. The Dean of Faculty's archive lists the measure as [Resolution 193, "Discontinue Posting Median Grades on Student Transcripts"](https://deanoffaculty.cornell.edu/faculty-governance/archives/resolutions/resolution-to-discontinue-posting-median-grades-on-student-transcripts).

Students noticed. Jonah Bernard, a sophomore in the College of Engineering, argued in a [January 2024 Guest Room column](https://www.cornellsun.com/article/2024/01/guest-room-a-students-plea-for-grade-transparency) that without medians students rely on rumors from friends, which favors the well-connected. That is the gap this page can only partly fill.

## How to choose without a ranked list

Our advice, drawn from the facts above:

1. **Start from the requirement, not the reputation.** The Class Roster lets you filter by [distribution requirements, class level, credits and grade option](https://classes.cornell.edu/), so build the list of courses that actually count for you first.
2. **Look for intro-level courses outside your major.** The pattern in the threads is consistent: introductory courses in other departments.
3. **Ask people who took it recently.** Upperclassmen in your college, and your adviser, know the current instructor. A 2008 post does not.
4. **Check the syllabus.** The Roster can show only classes with syllabi, which tells you the exam and paper load before you enroll.
5. **Balance the semester.** One lighter course beside three demanding ones is a sensible plan. Four light ones is how people end up with a thin transcript.

## The case against a schedule of only easy courses

Christian Baran, a sophomore in Arts and Sciences, wrote in a [November 2019 Sun column](https://cornellsun.com/2019/11/03/baran-college-shouldnt-be-a-breeze/) that students shouldn't load up on unrelated difficult courses, but also shouldn't hunt for the easiest classes until graduation. His example of a class worth taking out of genuine interest was oceanography. The Faculty Senate's own worry, in 2011 and again in 2023, was that visible grade data pushed students toward lenient courses. Easy is fine for one slot. A whole schedule of it is a choice you may want to make deliberately.

## FAQ

**Does Cornell publish median grades?** No. The registrar says median grade information [is not calculated or made publicly available](https://registrar.cornell.edu/about/use-student-data).

**Why did Cornell stop?** The Faculty Senate's stated concern was that students used the data to choose [leniently graded courses](https://news.cornell.edu/node/270308).

**Is there an easy stats class?** Posters in 2012 and 2018 pointed to ILR statistics, one calling it the easiest math course and the other saying it is likely relatively easy. Both are old, so confirm the current course and instructor.

**Can I take a class pass/fail?** The Class Roster has a [grade option filter](https://classes.cornell.edu/), so you can see which sections offer one. Check your college's rules for what counts toward requirements.
