The course Cornell pre-meds most often call the weed-out is CHEM 2070, General Chemistry I, taken in the first year, not organic chemistry. Cornell [stopped posting course median grades in 2011](https://news.cornell.edu/node/270308), so no median for either course is posted. Below are the course sequence Cornell's pre-health office lists, the prerequisites that force the order, and what the Cornell Daily Sun and its columnists have said about how hard the first course is.

## The sequence Cornell lists

Cornell's [Health Professions Advising page](https://prehealthadvising.cornell.edu/academic-preparation) gives the courses that generally meet medical school prerequisites. It warns that they are "not listed in the order you should take them," so sequencing is yours to plan with your college advisor.

| Subject | What Cornell lists |
|---|---|
| General chemistry | CHEM 2070 + 2071 (lab), then CHEM 2080 + 2081 (lab); or honors CHEM 2150 |
| Organic chemistry | CHEM 3570, 3580 and the lab CHEM 2510; or honors CHEM 3590, 3600 and CHEM 2510 |
| Biology | BIOMG 1350, BIOG 1440 or 1445, and BIOG 1500 (lab) |
| Biochemistry | BIOMG 3310 + 3320, or BIOMG 3300, 3350 or NS 3200 |
| Physics | PHYS 2207 + 2208; or PHYS 1112 + 1110 (lab), then 2208 |

The same page lists a calculus course, a statistics course and English (most medical schools accept First-Year Writing Seminars). Requirements differ by medical school, so check each program you plan to apply to.

## Which courses are the weeders

Cornell does not publish a list, so this is student opinion, not a ranking.

### CHEM 2070: the one students name

The [roster](https://classes.cornell.edu/browse/roster/FA26/class/CHEM/2070) lists it at 3 credits, letter grade only, with no prerequisites and CHEM 2071 as a corequisite. That open door is part of the problem: it is the first serious STEM course for a lot of pre-meds at once. In a [Cornell Daily Sun interview](https://www.cornellsun.com/article/2025/04/backlash-cocktails-and-humor-a-sit-down-with-chem-2070-professor-kyle-lancaster), the Sun reports about 800 students take General Chemistry I in the fall, and professor Kyle Lancaster says most need time to adjust. He rejects the idea that the course is designed to make people fail, says only two percent of students fail, and says he tells students a B+ or a C- will not ruin their lives. That two percent is his figure, not a published statistic.

Students see it differently. A first-year Arts & Sciences student, Asfi Tias, argued in a [September 2024 Sun column](https://cornellsun.com/2024/09/16/tias-its-time-to-weed-out-weeder-courses/) that the course assumes AP Chemistry knowledge and has a competitive curve, which she says hits first-generation and low-income students hardest. Another first-year student, Gabriel Levin, wrote in an [October 2022 Sun column](https://cornellsun.com/2022/10/12/levin-the-problem-of-weed-out-classes/) that weed-out classes are tied to Cornell's competitive culture and that grading by standard deviation alone is unfair. Both are opinion columns, so treat them as reports of how the course feels, not measurements of it.

### CHEM 2080 and organic chemistry

The weed-out label at Cornell attaches to CHEM 2070. The rosters show how tightly they chain: [CHEM 2080](https://classes.cornell.edu/browse/roster/FA26/class/CHEM/2080) requires CHEM 2070 or 2090, and [CHEM 3570](https://classes.cornell.edu/browse/roster/FA26/class/CHEM/3570), Organic Chemistry for the Life Sciences, requires CHEM 2080 and 2081 (or permission of the instructor). A weak grade in 2070 therefore follows you into every course behind it. If a friend tells you what the organic curve is, ask which semester and which instructor.

## Why you cannot look up a median

Cornell's Faculty Senate [voted in May 2011](https://news.cornell.edu/node/270308) to stop posting medians on the registrar's website, where they had appeared since 1998; the registrar still records them offline for deans, department chairs and researchers. Medians had also appeared on transcripts, and the Faculty Senate ended that too, for [courses taken in fall 2023 and later](https://cornellsun.com/2024/02/19/cornell-discontinues-median-grade-visibility-on-transcripts-15-years-after-inception/), per the Sun.

## How to sequence them

This is our advice, built from the prerequisites above.

1. **Do not stack the heavy courses.** CHEM 2070 plus a bio lab and a writing seminar is a normal first semester. Chemistry plus a second lab-heavy science is a choice, not a requirement.
2. **Take general chemistry before organic, and finish the full year first.** The prerequisite chain makes this unavoidable for CHEM 3570.
3. **Use the support course early.** [CHEM 1007](https://classes.cornell.edu/browse/roster/FA26/class/CHEM/1007) is a 1-credit S/U course that reviews CHEM 2070 lectures and gives problem-solving practice in the discussion sections. Lancaster also points to his office hours and TA hours.
4. **Be careful with AP credit.** The Chemistry department says a 5 on the AP exam [earns credit for CHEM 2070 and 2071](https://chemistry.cornell.edu/advising-guide), but it is kept only if you enroll in CHEM 2150, and it warns that not all medical programs accept AP chemistry credit. The pre-health office says many medical schools do not accept AP credit for prerequisites and recommends taking them at Cornell.
5. **Honors is an option for the well prepared.** CHEM 2150 requires AP calculus or concurrent college calculus and is [intended for students with strong chemistry preparation](https://chemistry.cornell.edu/advising-guide). Going from 2150 to CHEM 3590 and 3600 lets you finish general and organic chemistry in three semesters.
6. **Skip summer and winter courses elsewhere** unless an advisor signs off; the pre-health page generally discourages it.

## If you are...

- **A first-year with strong AP Chemistry:** ask the chemistry department about CHEM 2150 before enrolling, because AP credit and the other sequences interact.
- **A first-year without AP Chemistry:** start CHEM 2070 with CHEM 1007 alongside it, and plan a lighter semester around it.
- **First-gen or low-income:** the Tias column argues the course favors students who arrived with tutors and AP access, so use TA hours in week one rather than after the first exam.

## FAQ

**Is organic chemistry the weed-out course at Cornell?** The pre-med reputation in the Sun attaches to CHEM 2070, not organic.

**Can I see the median grade for CHEM 2070?** No. Cornell [stopped publishing course medians in 2011](https://news.cornell.edu/node/270308).

**Do I have to take 2070 and 2071 together?** The roster lists CHEM 2071 as a corequisite of 2070.

**Which advisor decides my sequence?** The pre-health page says to work with your college or major advisor, and the Chemistry department [strongly encourages](https://chemistry.cornell.edu/advising-guide) pre-medical students to consult Health Professions Advising before choosing a general chemistry path.
