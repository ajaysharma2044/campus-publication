Cornell publishes no ranking of which First-Year Writing Seminar (FWS) is easiest, and the seminars are built to a common workload, so the practical way to choose is by interest and by what fits your schedule. Seats go [first come, first served](https://knight.as.cornell.edu/fws-guidelines) with no waiting lists, so the winning move is a ranked list of several seminars that fit your open time slots, ready before your enrollment window opens. For the spring 2027 term, pre-enrollment [runs November 18 to November 20, 2026](https://knight.as.cornell.edu/fws-ballot).

## How FWS enrollment works

- **The requirement.** The Knight Institute says that [in general, Cornell students take two semesters of First-Year Writing Seminars](https://knight.as.cornell.edu/fws-guidelines). Architecture and hotel students take one, and Agriculture and Life Sciences students may take two or choose other courses.
- **One at a time.** [No student can be registered for more than one FWS at a time](https://knight.as.cornell.edu/fws-guidelines).
- **Capped seats.** Enrollments are [strictly capped, with no over-enrollments](https://knight.as.cornell.edu/fws-guidelines), and you can enroll only in a seminar that has open spots.
- **No waitlist, no shopping.** The same page says there are [no waiting lists](https://knight.as.cornell.edu/fws-guidelines). Per the [enrollment page](https://knight.as.cornell.edu/fws-ballot), you cannot sit in on a seminar unless you are on its roster, and instructors and your college registrar cannot add you, so Knight asks you not to contact them.
- **Dates for spring 2027.** Pre-enrollment for first-year students, sophomores and transfers [runs November 18 (7:30 a.m.) to November 20 (11:59 p.m.), 2026](https://knight.as.cornell.edu/fws-ballot). Add/drop [runs January 22 to February 8, 2027](https://knight.as.cornell.edu/fws-ballot). Dates change each term.

Seats can reappear. Knight's enrollment page says spots open during add/drop when enrolled students drop, but [capacity and choice will be limited by then](https://knight.as.cornell.edu/fws-ballot), and it advises checking the open-seminar list often because it changes. A fall 2023 notice explained how to find open seminars in Student Center: [Search for Classes, Additional Search Criteria, then "FWS Session"](https://knight.as.cornell.edu/news/fws-enrollment). Menus change, so treat that as a pointer.

If you want the general mechanics of pre-enrollment, see our guide to [Cornell course enrollment strategy](/academics/cornell-course-enrollment-strategy-pre-enroll-tips/), and for full sections elsewhere, [how to get into a full Cornell class](/academics/how-to-get-into-a-full-class-at-cornell-waitlist/). The FWS no-waitlist rule is the exception to that general advice.

## Is there an easiest FWS?

We found no official or student source that ranks seminars by difficulty. What Cornell does publish is a shared standard. The Knight Institute's guidelines say each seminar should include [at least four and at most six formal essays on new topics, about 20 pages of polished prose, and reading well under 75 pages per week](https://knight.as.cornell.edu/fws-guidelines). At least three essays should be developed under the instructor's guidance, and students have two individual conferences with the instructor. These are guidelines for the program, so a given section can feel different, but the topic on the cover is not what sets the workload.

The Cornell Daily Sun's September 2026 piece on FWS asked three instructors for advice. [Song Han, Isabelle Duval and Trifosa Simamora each told students to follow their interests](https://www.cornellsun.com/article/2026/09/zuqdrbhzj2av). The article quotes student Zoe Lamb '29 saying to go for something interesting, [not something you think might be easy](https://www.cornellsun.com/article/2026/09/zuqdrbhzj2av). She also said what was above par at her high school may be below par at Cornell, a reason not to assume the "easy" pick will be easy.

One caveat on student voices: we looked for r/Cornell and College Confidential threads that rate individual seminars and did not find any we could cite, so this page does not report forum opinion. Ask upperclassmen in your own college about specific instructors.

## How to judge a seminar before you rank it

1. **Read the description on the class roster, not just the title.** The Sun's examples show how different topics are: a bird-focused seminar, a course on Caribbean history through dress and textiles, and one on robots and science fiction, all [from different departments](https://www.cornellsun.com/article/2026/09/zuqdrbhzj2av).
2. **Look at the meeting time.** Your time slots decide what you can get. Knight's advice is a [prioritized list of several FWSs that fit open slots](https://knight.as.cornell.edu/fws-guidelines).
3. **Check the credit limit.** Do not create time conflicts or [exceed your college's credit limit](https://knight.as.cornell.edu/fws-guidelines).
4. **Rank at least five.** With no waitlist, the first few may be full by the time you enroll.
5. **Be ready the moment your window opens.** Knight says to enroll [immediately when your enrollment window opens](https://knight.as.cornell.edu/fws-ballot).

Our advice: choose a topic you would read about anyway, then use time slot as the tiebreaker. An A&S ambassador, Leah Condon '29, wrote that her preferred seminars filled, so she took a topic she described as [seemingly random](https://as.cornell.edu/news/ask-ambassador-how-my-first-year-writing-seminar-enriched-my-four-years). She credits one-on-one work with her professor for research and campus opportunities she says she otherwise would not have found.

## If you are worried about your writing

Two official options exist.

- **The FWS Writing Consultation.** It is optional. The Knight Institute's page recommends it for students who [have never studied in the US or at an English-medium institution, or who feel a general lack of confidence about writing](https://knight.as.cornell.edu/news/fws-writing-consultation). In the version we read, you enroll in the consultation on Canvas, post a short essay on an assigned prompt, and book a 10-minute consultation. Dates and details are set each term.
- **WRIT 1370 and 1380.** The fall 2015 roster describes WRIT 1370 as [designed for students who need more focused attention to master the expectations of academic writing](https://classes.cornell.edu/browse/roster/FA15/class/WRIT/1370). That listing showed weekly one-on-one conferences, a cap of 12 per section, 3 credits graded satisfactory/unsatisfactory, and a note that instructor consent is needed to add a section, so you do not rank it in the ballot. That listing is from 2015, so check the current roster before relying on any detail. This is support for writing, not an easier grade.

## Common mistakes

- Picking by rumor of "easy" instead of by interest and fit.
- Contacting the instructor or registrar to get in. Knight says they [cannot lift the cap](https://knight.as.cornell.edu/fws-guidelines).
- Ranking only one or two seminars.
- Waiting for add/drop to choose.

## FAQ

**Can I take two FWS in one semester?** No. [One at a time](https://knight.as.cornell.edu/fws-guidelines).

**Can AP credit replace an FWS?** Knight's guidelines say students who score 5 on the Advanced Placement English exam [receive 3 credits](https://knight.as.cornell.edu/fws-guidelines). Whether that credit replaces a seminar depends on your college, so ask your advising office.

**I am a junior, senior or transfer. Do I need an FWS?** Knight advises those students that [ENGL 2880 or ENGL 2860 can substitute for FWS credit](https://knight.as.cornell.edu/fws-ballot). Confirm with your college.

**Who do I ask about enrollment problems?** Knight points you to your college's advising or student services office or the University Registrar, not the instructor.
