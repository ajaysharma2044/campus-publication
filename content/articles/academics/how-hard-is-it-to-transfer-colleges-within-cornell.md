Internal transfer at Cornell is possible but never guaranteed, and how hard it is depends on the college you want. Every college sets its own GPA floor and prerequisite courses, but Cornell's central page applies [one rule to all of them](https://internaltransfer.cornell.edu/apply): you must remain in your home college for two semesters before you can enroll in a new one. Meeting the requirements does not guarantee admission, and [decisions are final with no appeal](https://internaltransfer.cornell.edu/apply). Cornell does not publish internal transfer acceptance rates, so the table below compares the rules each college sets: GPA, timing, courses and deadlines.

## The rules that apply everywhere

- **One application per term.** [You can only apply to one college or school per term](https://internaltransfer.cornell.edu/apply).
- **Two dates.** [Fall entry: application opens March 1, closes May 1, decisions in mid-June. Spring entry: opens October 1, closes December 1, decisions in mid-January](https://internaltransfer.cornell.edu/apply). Dyson is [fall-only](https://business.cornell.edu/admissions/undergraduate/bs-aem/application-requirements/).
- **Grades decide.** Decisions come [after final grades are available](https://internaltransfer.cornell.edu/apply), so a bad semester while you wait can sink an application.
- **Reapplying is discouraged.** Cornell says it is possible, but you should [contact the college first](https://internaltransfer.cornell.edu/apply).

## Rules by destination college

GPA is the minimum cumulative Cornell GPA each college sets. Engineering sets no minimum but [weighs your cumulative GPA](https://www.duffield.cornell.edu/advising/incoming-internal-transfers/). Requirements change by year, so confirm with the college before you apply.

| [Arts & Sciences](https://as.cornell.edu/admissions/transfer) | 3.0 cumulative, and 3.0 in 12 credits the semester you apply | After your second semester; latest is the end of your first junior semester | Second-semester sophomores and first-semester juniors need a course plan and proof of formal admission to an A&S major | Must be able to graduate on time. Seniors, current second-semester juniors and anyone who already transferred internally are not eligible |
| [AAP](https://www.aap.cornell.edu/admissions/undergraduate/internal-transfer) | 2.7 | End of second semester; a first-semester application is denied | No course list. Portfolio required for B.Arch. or B.F.A. | Fall deadline May 1, spring December 1. Recommendation letter required |

Dyson is the only page that names a competitive GPA (3.3 or higher) in addition to the floor. Older numbers floating around are stale: the Sun's 2023 article put the Arts & Sciences minimum at 2.7, while the A&S page now says 3.0.

## How hard, by situation

Every page says meeting the minimums does not guarantee admission, so the floor is only the start. These are the places the rules bite, from the pages above:

- **Dyson and Nolan** have the tightest eligibility: first- and second-year students only, a 3.0 minimum, preference for first-years, and a [discouragement of cross-applying between the two](https://business.cornell.edu/admissions/undergraduate/bs-aem/application-requirements/).
- **Arts & Sciences** is the most flexible on timing (you can apply through the first semester of junior year) but demands a [course plan that finishes on time](https://as.cornell.edu/admissions/transfer) once you are a late sophomore or junior.
- **Engineering** is hard in a different way: there is no GPA number, but the page says [it typically takes one to three semesters to meet the criteria for a major](https://www.duffield.cornell.edu/advising/incoming-internal-transfers/), and you cannot transfer in undeclared.
- **CALS** has the widest range of floors, from 2.0 for Environmental Engineering to 3.0 for Global Development and Information Science.

## What students report

The Sun's 2023 article quotes Jonathan Burdick, vice provost for Enrollment, saying admission to the new college isn't guaranteed. It also profiles two students who switched in fall 2022. Isabel Hou '24 moved from the Nolan School to ILR and said she was certain she made the right decision; the article says she felt awkward taking first-year core courses alongside first-years. Kayciel Ramos '24 moved from Dyson to CALS for information science and said she was more regretful that she did not switch sooner; the article says she had trouble getting into required courses. Both are single examples, not a ranking.

If a required course is full, see [how waitlists work](/academics/how-to-get-into-a-full-class-at-cornell-waitlist/) and the [enrollment strategy guide](/academics/cornell-course-enrollment-strategy-pre-enroll-tips/).

## How to apply, step by step

1. **Pick one college** and read its page in the table.
2. **Take the prerequisites now.** Most colleges want them completed or in progress, and some require letter grades.
3. **Talk to the college.** CALS has you [meet the advising contact for your intended major](https://cals.cornell.edu/education/admissions/undergraduate-admissions/internal-transfer); ILR asks you to [email its admissions office](https://www.ilr.cornell.edu/programs/undergraduate-admissions/apply-ilr/internal-transfer-guidelines); AAP recommends [a meeting with its admissions team](https://www.aap.cornell.edu/admissions/undergraduate/internal-transfer). Arts & Sciences says its advising office [cannot meet with prospective applicants](https://as.cornell.edu/admissions/transfer), so use its information sessions.
4. **Write the statement.** The Engineering, Dyson, Nolan, Brooks, ILR and CALS pages cite 650 words (CALS recommends about 650), and [Human Ecology allows up to 600](https://human.cornell.edu/admissions/transfer/internal).
5. **Apply by the deadline** through [the internal transfer site](https://internaltransfer.cornell.edu/apply), then protect your grades until decisions post.

## Common mistakes

Our advice, drawn from the pages above:

- Applying after one semester. AAP, Human Ecology and Engineering all say a one-semester application is denied.
- Counting on AP credit for Dyson's calculus, microeconomics or statistics requirement. The page says it is not accepted.
- Waiting until junior year. Several colleges rarely or never consider juniors.
- Applying to two colleges at once. Cornell allows one per term.

## FAQ

**Can I appeal a denial?** No. [Internal Transfer decisions are final](https://internaltransfer.cornell.edu/apply).

**Can I go back if I change my mind?** CALS says admitted students whose plans change can request to return to their previous college by the deadline given with the decision. Cornell's central page adds that you are expected to transfer unless you decline by the date on your offer letter.

**Is transferring into Engineering easier than business?** Cornell publishes no acceptance rates for either. Engineering sets no GPA minimum but weighs your cumulative GPA and how closely your courses match its curriculum; its bar is completing the course criteria for your target major.

**Which college is the hardest to get into?** Cornell does not rank them and publishes no internal transfer acceptance rates.
