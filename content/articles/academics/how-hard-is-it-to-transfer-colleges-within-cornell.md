Internal transfer at Cornell is possible but never guaranteed, and how hard it is depends on the college you want. Every college sets its own GPA floor and prerequisite courses, but Cornell's central page applies [one rule to all of them](https://internaltransfer.cornell.edu/apply): you must stay in your home college for two semesters before you can enroll in a new one. Cornell's own line on every application is that meeting the requirements does not guarantee admission, and [decisions are final with no appeal](https://internaltransfer.cornell.edu/apply). Cornell does not publish acceptance rates for internal transfer; [the Cornell Daily Sun's 2023 article](https://cornellsun.com/article/2023/01/self-discovery-sparks-internal-transfers-across-cornells-eight-undergraduate-colleges) says plainly that it does not report how many students transfer internally. So the table below compares what is published: GPA, timing, courses and deadlines.

## The rules that apply everywhere

- **One application per term.** [You can only apply to one college or school per term](https://internaltransfer.cornell.edu/apply).
- **Two dates.** [Fall entry: application opens March 1, closes May 1, decisions in mid-June. Spring entry: opens October 1, closes December 1, decisions in mid-January](https://internaltransfer.cornell.edu/apply). Some colleges are fall-only (see below).
- **Grades decide.** Decisions come [after final grades are available](https://internaltransfer.cornell.edu/apply), so a bad semester while you wait can sink an application.
- **Reapplying is discouraged.** Cornell says it is possible, but you should [contact the college first](https://internaltransfer.cornell.edu/apply).

Two details tell you where the bar sits. Dyson and Nolan each name a 3.0 minimum and then say what the competitive range looks like or that preference goes to current first-years. And the Sun's 2023 article reported Arts & Sciences at 2.7 then; the A&S page now says 3.0, so ignore older GPA figures on forums and old news.

## How hard, by situation

The published GPA floors are not the whole story, because every page says meeting them does not guarantee admission. These are the places the rules bite, from the pages above:

- **Dyson and Nolan** have the tightest eligibility: first- and second-year students only, 3.0 or better, prerequisites that must be earned at Cornell, and a [warning against cross-applying between the two](https://business.cornell.edu/admissions/undergraduate/bs-aem/application-requirements/).
- **Arts & Sciences** is the most flexible on timing (you can apply through the first semester of junior year) but demands a [course plan that finishes on time](https://as.cornell.edu/admissions/transfer) once you are a late sophomore or junior.
- **Engineering** is hard in a different way: there is no GPA number, but the [criteria for your major can take one to three semesters](https://www.duffield.cornell.edu/advising/incoming-internal-transfers/) to meet, and you cannot transfer in undeclared.
- **CALS** has the widest range of floors, from 2.0 for Environmental Engineering to 3.0 for Global Development and Information Science.

## What students report

The Sun's 2023 article quotes Jonathan Burdick, vice provost for Enrollment, saying admission to the new college isn't guaranteed. It also profiles two students who switched in fall 2022: Isabel Hou '24 moved from the Nolan School to ILR and said she was certain it was the right decision, though she felt awkward taking first-year core courses alongside first-years. Kayciel Ramos '24 moved from Dyson to CALS for information science and said she regretted not switching sooner; the article says Ramos had trouble getting into required courses at Dyson. The one College Confidential thread the search turned up is from 2007 and says nothing useful about difficulty, so do not rely on forum posts that old.

If a required course is full, see [how waitlists work](/academics/how-to-get-into-a-full-class-at-cornell-waitlist/) and the [enrollment strategy guide](/academics/cornell-course-enrollment-strategy-pre-enroll-tips/).

## How to apply, step by step

1. **Pick one college** and read its page in the table. Check you can [graduate on time](https://as.cornell.edu/admissions/transfer) from there if that college requires it.
2. **Take the prerequisites now.** Most colleges want them completed or in progress, with letter grades.
3. **Talk to the college.** CALS requires you to [meet the advising contact for your intended major](https://cals.cornell.edu/education/admissions/undergraduate-admissions/internal-transfer); ILR asks you to [email its admissions office](https://www.ilr.cornell.edu/programs/undergraduate-admissions/apply-ilr/internal-transfer-guidelines); AAP recommends [a meeting with its admissions team](https://www.aap.cornell.edu/admissions/undergraduate/internal-transfer). Arts & Sciences says its advising office [cannot meet with prospective applicants](https://as.cornell.edu/admissions/transfer), only through its information sessions.
4. **Write the statement.** Word limits are 650 for [CALS, ILR, Engineering, Brooks, Dyson and Nolan](https://www.duffield.cornell.edu/advising/incoming-internal-transfers/) pages (check each) and [600 for Human Ecology](https://human.cornell.edu/admissions/transfer/internal).
5. **Apply by the deadline** through [the internal transfer site](https://internaltransfer.cornell.edu/apply), then protect your grades until decisions post.

## Common mistakes

Our advice, drawn from the pages above:

- Applying after one semester. AAP, Human Ecology and Engineering all say a one-semester application is denied.
- Counting on AP credit for Dyson's calculus, microeconomics or statistics requirement. The page says they must be taken at Cornell.
- Waiting until junior year. Several colleges stop considering juniors.
- Applying to two colleges at once. Cornell allows one per term.

## FAQ

**Can I appeal a denial?** No. [Internal Transfer decisions are final](https://internaltransfer.cornell.edu/apply).

**Can I go back if I change my mind?** CALS says admitted students whose plans change can request to return to the previous college by the deadline given with the decision. Cornell's central page adds that you are expected to transfer unless you decline by the date on your offer letter.

**Is transferring into Engineering easier than business?** Engineering publishes no GPA minimum, so the page does not say. Its published bar is completing the course criteria for your target major.

**Which college is the hardest to get into?** No official source ranks them, and Cornell publishes no acceptance rates.
