Take a class S/U when it is a free elective or a distribution requirement outside your major and you want to explore without risking your GPA. Do not take it for anything that counts toward your major, or for prerequisites you will need for a professional school. Cornell sets the basic meaning, and each college sets its own limits, so check yours before you switch. Here is how it works and how to decide.

## How S/U grading works

At the ILR School, [S means a grade of C- or better and U means D+ or lower](https://www.ilr.cornell.edu/current-students/academic-policies/academic-grades-and-notations). ILR also says a U counts as an F for academic status but [does not affect your grade point average](https://www.ilr.cornell.edu/current-students/academic-policies/academic-grades-and-notations). AAP draws the same line: [an S is a C- or higher, a U is any grade below that and is a failing grade equal to an F, and S/U courses are not factored into your GPA](https://aap.cornell.edu/student-services/academic-policies). CALS says an S/U class [will not contribute to your GPA and that a passing grade is C- or higher](https://cals.cornell.edu/undergraduate-students/cals-student-services/academic-resources/enrollment); the Nolan School of Hotel Administration [says the same](https://business.cornell.edu/students/by-program/bs-hotel-administration/resources-for-students/).

The option is only there if the course offers it, and [the Class Roster shows which grading options a course carries](https://cals.cornell.edu/undergraduate-students/cals-student-services/academic-resources/enrollment). Some courses are letter-only, and some are S/U-only.

## The rules by college

| College | S/U rule |
|---|---|
| Arts & Sciences | You [may elect S/U if the course does not also count toward major requirements or serve as a prerequisite for admission to the major](https://as.cornell.edu/registrar/course-enrollment). S/U courses [can fulfill college distribution requirements](https://as.cornell.edu/registrar/course-enrollment). |
| ILR | The option is available only in [ILR electives and out-of-college electives](https://www.ilr.cornell.edu/current-students/academic-policies/academic-grades-and-notations). No more than 8 S/U credits in a semester, and at least 105 letter-grade credits toward the degree. |
| Human Ecology | S/U [cannot fulfill distribution or major requirements](https://human.cornell.edu/academics/policies/requirements) unless a course is S/U-only. A maximum of 12 S/U credits can apply toward graduation. These rules apply to students who matriculated in fall 2024 or earlier. |
| CALS | S/U courses [can be used to fulfill college distribution requirements](https://cals.cornell.edu/undergraduate-students/student-services/academic-resources/petitions-forms-and-policies/policies), but [most majors require major requirements to be taken for a letter grade](https://cals.cornell.edu/undergraduate-students/cals-student-services/academic-resources/enrollment). CALS also caps the number of classes you can take S/U, so ask your advisor before planning several. |
| AAP | The S/U option [may be used for free elective credit](https://aap.cornell.edu/student-services/academic-policies). Classes that fulfill specific graduation requirements must be taken for a letter grade, and each program has its own grading requirements. |
| Nolan School (Hotel Administration) | Student-option S/U classes [count only toward free elective requirements](https://business.cornell.edu/students/by-program/bs-hotel-administration/resources-for-students/). Up to 4 credits S/U in a semester, and no more than 8 non-graded credits in a semester once S/U-only courses are counted too. |

Do not rely on a friend in another college.

## Deadlines

ILR says you cannot change your grading option after [the 57th calendar day of University instruction, with no exceptions and no appeals](https://www.ilr.cornell.edu/current-students/academic-policies/academic-grades-and-notations). Arts & Sciences points to its academic calendar for deadlines. After the deadline, A&S students may petition only to [change from S/U to letter graded, and only to count the course toward a major or minor that requires a letter grade](https://as.cornell.edu/registrar/petitions-policies). CALS lets you [switch a course's grading option in Student Center while your enrollment window is open, up to a deadline on the academic calendar](https://cals.cornell.edu/undergraduate-students/cals-student-services/academic-resources/enrollment). Add, drop and grade-option dates are set each semester, so check the current calendar.

## Decision by situation

These are our recommendations, based on the rules above.

- **A free elective:** a good use of S/U in every college above. The risk is a grade below C-, and the reward is trying a hard class without a GPA hit.
- **A distribution course outside your major:** yes in Arts & Sciences and CALS. No in Human Ecology, and not in AAP or the Nolan School, where S/U counts only toward free electives.
- **Anything counting toward your major or minor:** do not. Arts & Sciences and Human Ecology bar it, AAP requires a letter grade for anything that fulfills a graduation requirement, and CALS says most majors require letter grades. In Arts & Sciences the fix after the deadline is a petition back to a letter grade.
- **Heading to grad school:** Cornell's Graduate School said, about spring 2020, that it would [respect students' and institutions' decisions about S/U grading](https://cornellsun.com/2020/05/12/students-left-in-the-dark-over-grades-as-su-deadline-approaches/). That was a pandemic statement, not a standing policy. In a pandemic-free semester, ask the programs you want.
- **Pre-med or other pre-health:** keep prerequisites as letter grades. Cornell's pre-health office says [prerequisite requirements vary by profession and by school, so verify them with your intended programs](https://prehealthadvising.cornell.edu/academic-preparation), and it [recommends letter grades even for the optional courses on its competencies list](https://prehealthadvising.cornell.edu/pre-professional-competencies/). Ask each school before switching any prerequisite.
- **A course where you are not sure of your grade:** decide before the deadline, not after. In spring 2020, [several students told the Cornell Daily Sun](https://cornellsun.com/2020/05/12/students-left-in-the-dark-over-grades-as-su-deadline-approaches/) they could not tell whether to switch without knowing where their grades stood. Ask your instructor where you stand.

## Common mistakes

- Switching a course that your major quietly requires. Check your degree tracker first.
- Assuming a U is harmless. ILR counts it as an F for academic status and AAP calls it a failing grade equal to an F, even though neither puts it in your GPA.
- Missing the grade-option deadline, which at ILR has no exceptions.
- Taking a prerequisite for a later course S/U without checking that the next department accepts it.

## FAQ

**Does an S or U affect my GPA?** No. AAP, CALS and the Nolan School keep S/U courses out of the GPA, and ILR says a U ["will not affect grade point average"](https://www.ilr.cornell.edu/current-students/academic-policies/academic-grades-and-notations).

**Can I take a distribution course S/U?** In Arts & Sciences and CALS, yes. In Human Ecology, no unless the course is S/U-only. In AAP and the Nolan School, S/U counts only toward free electives.

**What if I want to go back to a letter grade?** Before the deadline, change it in your enrollment. After it, Arts & Sciences allows a petition only for courses that count toward a major or minor.
