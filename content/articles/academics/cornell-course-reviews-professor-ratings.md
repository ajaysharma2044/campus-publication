Use [CU Reviews](https://www.cureviews.org/) to judge the course and RateMyProfessors to judge the instructor, then read the newest written comments instead of the star average. Cornell no longer publishes official median grades, so no site gives you a reliable grade picture. Most students end up combining several sources, and that is the right instinct. Here is what each one is good for and where it misleads.

## The sources side by side

| Source | Best for | Weak spot |
|---|---|---|
| CU Reviews | Course structure, workload, how a class runs | Reviews are opinions, and old ones describe old versions of a course |
| RateMyProfessors | A quick read on a specific instructor | Self-selected reviewers, thin data, no check that the reviewer took the class |
| Median grades | Grade expectations | Not published by Cornell anymore; informal copies may be stale |
| r/Cornell and friends | Context, warnings, advice on pairing courses | Threads age badly, and one comment is one comment |

## CU Reviews

CU Reviews is the Cornell-specific one. [The Cornell Daily Sun reported in 2018](https://cornellsun.com/2018/05/09/website-designed-by-students-provides-more-comprehensive-course-information/) that a Cornell Design and Tech Initiative team built it to give detailed information about classes, not only the professors who teach them, and that its rating scales at launch were overall quality and difficulty. The [project's GitHub page](https://github.com/cornell-dti/cureviews) describes it as a "rate my classes" site for Cornell. The [Chrome Web Store listing for ProfessorPeek](https://chromewebstore.google.com/detail/professorpeek/jilfmfcpampggogoeppklpbkkejnoglo) says the CU Reviews class ratings come from cureviews.org, which Cornell Digital Tech & Innovation runs.

The Sun's 2018 piece also said submitted comments are reviewed before they post, to filter spam. Cornell DTI's [product listing](https://cornelldti.org/products) describes the site as it runs now: browse classes and view their metrics, read other students' experiences, and submit your own review.

## RateMyProfessors

It is the one most students know, and it is the most criticized. A [2019 Sun report](https://www.cornellsun.com/article/2019/11/longtime-teacher-evaluation-website-ratemyprofessors-receives-mixed-ratings-from-cornellians) found mixed views at Cornell:

- Student Becky Borrazzo '22 uses it to narrow choices, especially for electives, and reads the written comments rather than relying on the average.
- Prof. Jane-Marie Law (Asian studies) said online reviews skew toward unhappy students, and Prof. Simone Pinet (romance studies) said the research on gender and racial bias in evaluations makes hesitation about reading them reasonable.
- Prof. Peter Katzenstein (government), who had 139 ratings and a 4.3 out of 5 at the time, called the site a valuable, mostly accurate resource that fills a gap left by the lack of public official evaluations.
- The article said no Cornell professor had more than 250 ratings, so small samples are normal. That figure is from 2019.

Two later columns make complementary points. [Ayman Abou-Alfa wrote in 2024](https://www.cornellsun.com/article/2024/12/abou-alfa-we-can-do-better-than-rate-my-professor) that reviewers are self-selecting and tend toward the extremes, and that reviews focus on personality or personal outcomes rather than teaching methods. [Roei Dery argued in 2021](https://www.cornellsun.com/article/2021/02/dery-instructor-evaluation-or-retaliation) that the lowest ratings often come from students who got poor grades, so a strict grader can look like a bad teacher. On the other side, [Pallavi Kenkare wrote in 2019](https://www.cornellsun.com/article/2019/09/kenkare-in-defense-of-rate-my-professor) that the site is useful for choosing courses but is not a way to judge a professor's competence.

## Median grades

Cornell used to publish these, and many students still look for them. The [Sun reported in February 2024](https://cornellsun.com/2024/02/19/cornell-discontinues-median-grade-visibility-on-transcripts-15-years-after-inception/) that the Faculty Senate approved Resolution 193 on December 15, 2023, ending median grades on transcripts for courses from Fall 2023 on. The Faculty Senate had already removed them from the registrar's website in 2011. The same article says students have shared median data informally, including on Reddit and in a shared spreadsheet. Treat anything like that as a rough hint.

## How to read them without being misled

1. **Start with the course, then the instructor.** Look up the course on CU Reviews, then check the instructor on RateMyProfessors if a section choice depends on it.
2. **Read comments, not averages.** Richard Ballard's [November 2025 Sun column](https://cornellsun.com/article/2025/11/grading-the-graders-empathy-for-professors-during-the-course-selection-process) says the most useful reviews are the most recent, since course content, syllabi and instructors change over time, and cites decade-old Reddit threads as examples of outdated information.
3. **Look for patterns, not outliers.** Because reviewers skew to the extremes, ignore the angriest and the most glowing and look for what several comments repeat.
4. **Separate "hard" from "bad."** If a review complains about grading, ask whether it describes the teaching or only the grade.
5. **Check the count and the dates.** A 4.8 from four reviews means little. Our advice: if there are fewer than about ten reviews, or none from the last two years, lean on other sources.
6. **Cross-check once.** If CU Reviews and a Reddit search say the same thing about a workload, believe it.

## A browser shortcut

ProfessorPeek is a Chrome extension that, per its [store listing](https://chromewebstore.google.com/detail/professorpeek/jilfmfcpampggogoeppklpbkkejnoglo), shows CU Reviews class scores and RateMyProfessors professor scores on Cornell's Course Roster. The listing shows version 1.9.5, last updated July 4, 2025, and says the developer will not collect or use user data. It also shows only five ratings, so it is a convenience, not an endorsed tool.

## If you are...

- **Picking an elective:** Read CU Reviews for workload and format, then glance at the instructor rating.
- **Choosing between two sections:** Compare the instructors' recent written comments, not their stars.
- **Chasing a GPA:** Remember that grade stories are the least reliable thing in any review, and that medians are gone.
- **Stuck on a full class:** Our guides on [pre-enrollment and add/drop](/academics/cornell-course-enrollment-strategy-pre-enroll-tips/) and [the waitlist](/academics/how-to-get-into-a-full-class-at-cornell-waitlist/) cover what to do next.

## FAQ

**Are official course evaluations public?** Access varies by college. The [Nolan School's course evaluation system](https://apps.business.cornell.edu/courseeval/) posts the numeric results of past evaluations behind a Cornell NetID login. [CALS](https://cals.cornell.edu/faculty-staff/advising-resources/course-evaluations) gives instructors access to their results after final grades are submitted. Ask your college's advising office what it shares with students.

**Should I write a review?** Ballard's column suggests that students who benefit from reviews should write their own, thoughtfully and with respect for the professor.

**Is Reddit useful?** For context and warnings, yes. Check the thread's date before you trust it.
