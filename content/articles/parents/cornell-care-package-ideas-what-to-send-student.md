Send the box to the student's own service center, not a generic Cornell address, and put their NetID in the name line. Keep it to 50 pounds or less: that is the [Central Campus center's stated limit](https://web.archive.org/web/20251209224202/https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/service-centers-and-mail/central-campus-service-center) and matches Cornell's move-in guidance, but check your student's own center page for its limit. Fill the box with shelf-stable food and things that fit a small dorm room, and leave out anything Cornell bans in residence halls: toasters, coffee pots, candles and extension cords. Below is what to put in, what to leave out, and how the box reaches the student.

## How to address and ship it

```figure
kind: steps
title: Getting a care package to a Cornell student
rows:
  - label: Ask the student which service center serves their residence
  - label: Address it to their name and NetID at that center's address
  - label: Keep the box at 50 pounds or less, and check their center's limit
  - label: Write PERISHABLE on food that needs it
  - label: Tell the student to watch for the pickup email
  - label: They collect it within seven days
```

1. **Find the right service center.** Each unit has its own shipping address, listed on the [service centers page](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/student-service-centers). The centers are North Campus, Central Campus, South Campus, the Graduate & Professional Student Service Center (which serves Hasbrouck) and the West Campus house offices. The simplest route is to have your student send you the exact address from their housing portal.
2. **Use the NetID.** Cornell says the NetID is [the key information used to process mail](https://web.archive.org/web/20251209224202/https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/service-centers-and-mail/central-campus-service-center), and leaving it off can slow the box or send it back. Here is the Central Campus format, as Cornell shows it. This is the Central Campus address only; the other centers have their own addresses on their pages.

   > Your Name, NetID
   > 401 Willard Straight Hall
   > Cornell University
   > Ithaca, NY 14853
3. **Stay at 50 pounds or under.** The Central Campus center [accepts packages of 50 pounds or less](https://web.archive.org/web/20251209224202/https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/service-centers-and-mail/central-campus-service-center). Cornell's packing advice for move-in is to [ship in as few boxes as possible, each under 50 pounds](https://scl.cornell.edu/sub/sub/residential-life/housing/housing-details/moving-campus/what-pack-what-not-bring). That is the limit we can source; look at your student's own center page for theirs.
4. **Tell the student it is coming.** They get an email from Student Mail Services when the package is processed, and the housing portal shows waiting parcels under [My Packages](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/service-centers-and-mail/best-practices-mail-and-packages). If they have not had the email, it has not been processed, and Cornell tells students not to go and look before the notice.
5. **Know the clock.** Packages are [held for seven days after processing and returned to the sender on the eighth day](https://web.archive.org/web/20251209224202/https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/service-centers-and-mail/central-campus-service-center). A student heading home for a break should not get a box the week they leave.

## Perishable food and delivery apps

Cornell's guidance is to mark food that needs it [PERISHABLE in large, bold letters near the address](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/service-centers-and-mail/best-practices-mail-and-packages). The service centers have no refrigeration, processing can take a day or more, and perishables must be picked up the day the student is notified or they are discarded. Packages sent directly from a food vendor are usually processed the same day.

Our advice: unless the student can collect it the day the email arrives, send shelf-stable food and skip the perishables.

Two things do not work. Service centers are [not drop-off points for local deliveries](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/service-centers-and-mail/best-practices-mail-and-packages) such as DoorDash, Grubhub, Instacart, Walmart or Target. And Amazon's own locker outside Robert Purcell, called Dodgeball, takes only [eligible Amazon orders under 35 pounds and 19 x 12 x 14 inches, with no perishables](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/community-centers/robert-purcell-community-center/amazon-lockers). It is separate from the service centers.

## What not to put in the box

Cornell's list of [prohibited items in residence halls](https://scl.cornell.edu/sub/sub/residential-life/housing/housing-details/moving-campus/what-pack-what-not-bring) rules out most gifts that sound useful. Check it before you buy.

| Skip this | Why |
|---|---|
| Toaster, coffee pot, rice cooker, air fryer | Small kitchen appliances are prohibited |
| Candles, wax warmers, incense | Prohibited |
| Extension cords, multiplug adapters | Prohibited; a UL-approved outlet strip with a breaker switch is the approved alternative |
| Peel-and-stick wallpaper, artificial plants, wall tapestries, curtains | Prohibited |
| Halogen or "spider" lamps, bulbs over 150 watts | Prohibited; LED lights are allowed |
| Medication | Cornell says not to ship it with other items; see below |

A freestanding microwave is also prohibited outside Townhouse apartment kitchens. Fans are fine: [air conditioners of any kind are prohibited](https://scl.cornell.edu/sub/sub/residential-life/housing/housing-details/moving-campus/what-pack-what-not-bring), and fans are not on the prohibited list.

**Medication** goes a different way. Cornell recommends sending prescriptions, including refrigerated ones, to the [Cornell Health Pharmacy](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/service-centers-and-mail/best-practices-mail-and-packages) (ATTN: Tracey DeNardo, 110 Ho Plaza, Ithaca, NY 14853), and the student is notified to pick them up.

## What to send

These are our suggestions, not Cornell rules.

- **Shelf-stable snacks and tea.** Granola bars, nuts, instant oatmeal, ramen cups and tea bags need no appliance. Cornell does not allow the toaster or the coffee pot, so anything that needs an appliance is out. If you are thinking of an electric kettle, check Housing's [prohibited items list](https://scl.cornell.edu/sub/sub/residential-life/housing/housing-details/moving-campus/what-pack-what-not-bring) or ask the student first.
- **Winter basics.** Cornell Undergraduate Admissions says [average daily highs in mid-January and February hover around 31 to 33 degrees Fahrenheit, and monthly snowfall from December through March ranges from 10 to 16 inches](https://faq.enrollment.cornell.edu/kb/article/317-how-is-the-weather-in-ithaca). The Cornell Daily Sun reported in [January 2023](https://www.cornellsun.com/article/2023/01/freshmens-first-winter-a-culture-shock-for-students-from-warm-climates) that first-years from warm places were swapping hoodies for heavy jackets and snow boots. Wool socks, a hat, gloves, hand warmers and a warm blanket are small, cheap and light. For students who bike, see our guide to [biking in Ithaca in winter](/getting-around/biking-in-ithaca-winter-cornell/).
- **Laundry and bathroom supplies.** Detergent pods and dryer sheets save a first-year a trip, and they pack well.
- **LED string lights.** They are allowed, and they make a small room feel less bare.
- **Study-week items.** Earplugs, a phone charger, a reusable water bottle and a box of snacks for finals.
- **Something handwritten.** A card costs nothing and weighs nothing.
- **Gift cards.** Light, easy to ship, and the student picks what they need. Which cards work depends on what the student uses.

## If you are...

- **A parent sending the first box in August:** Cornell warns that packages sent too early may be [returned to sender](https://scl.cornell.edu/sub/sub/residential-life/housing/housing-details/moving-campus/what-pack-what-not-bring). Check the student's move-in dates with their service center before shipping.
- **Sending from far away:** buy the non-perishables and have them shipped straight to the service center rather than through the student.
- **Planning a birthday surprise:** coordinate the day with the student so they are on campus and check email.

## FAQ

**Can I send food?** Yes, with the PERISHABLE label for anything that needs it. Shelf-stable food is safer, because the center has no refrigeration.

**Can I send medication?** Not in the care package. Use the Cornell Health Pharmacy address above.

**What if the box is lost or delayed?** Per Cornell's [mail and packages guidance](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/service-centers-and-mail/best-practices-mail-and-packages), a parcel delivered to the wrong center is returned to the carrier, and the correction may take up to two business days. Packages sent to a previous campus address stay at the center that received them, and the student must collect them there. Questions go to scl-student-srv@cornell.edu.

**Can a friend pick it up?** Cornell has a Parcel Pickup Authorization Form in the Housing and Dining Portal that [lets someone else pick up the parcels](https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/student-service-centers).

**Does the student live off campus?** Then the residence-hall service centers do not apply, and you should ask the student for the best address. Our guides for [parents](/parents/) and [daily life in Ithaca](/daily-life/) cover other questions you may have.
