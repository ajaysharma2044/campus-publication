If you transfer to Cornell as a first- or second-year student, you will almost certainly live on campus for at least a year, you are not promised a room the way first-years are, and the clock on your housing application starts the day you get your NetID. Those three facts are what most transfer-housing confusion comes down to, and each has a specific rule behind it.

## Do transfers have to live on campus?

Cornell's residential policy requires two years of on-campus residence, with a meal plan, for full-time students on the Ithaca campus. For transfers the rule is scaled to what you have already done: if you have not met a residency requirement elsewhere, you live on campus for one academic year. If you arrive in the spring, you live on campus for that spring and the following academic year. If you already lived on campus for two years at your previous school, or you enter as a junior or senior, the requirement does not apply to you. Exemptions beyond those are requested through the housing application during its open periods, one academic year at a time, and have to be renewed.

This is a different rule from the first-year one, which is why a generic "Cornell requires two years on campus" line misleads transfers in both directions: sophomores who think they are exempt are not, and juniors who think they must live on campus need not.

## Is housing guaranteed?

Not for transfers. Cornell's admitted-student FAQ guarantees on-campus housing to all first-year students. The equivalent transfer page does not make that promise; it says transfer rooms are grouped together "whenever possible" and lists where transfers may be placed. Students who apply and are not immediately assigned go on a waitlist that receives weekly updates. In 2020 the administration told the Student Assembly it hoped to house every transfer who applied once the North Campus expansion opened, but declined to commit to it. Treat a transfer assignment as likely, not certain, and apply on time.

## Where transfers actually live

Four areas appear on the official transfer page: the West Campus House System, the South Campus residence halls, the North Campus residence halls, and Program Houses. In limited cases, a transfer who qualifies as a first-year is placed in first-year housing. West Campus has historically been about 15 percent transfer students; 112 Edgemoor, a 20-person hall between West and South Campus, is reserved for transfer and upper-level students. ## What it costs

The 2026-27 rates are billed per person, per semester, with the same charge in fall and spring: a double is $6,872 per semester ($13,744 for the year), a single $7,783 ($15,566), a triple or one-room quad $6,322 ($12,644), a super single $8,298 ($16,595). The rate includes gas, electricity, garbage pick-up and internet up to 200 GB a month. It does not include the meal plan the residency requirement obliges you to buy. The same rates page shows a second, lower set of figures for cooperative residences; do not budget a residence-hall room from those.

## Deadlines that matter

Apply for housing within ten days of receiving your NetID; there is no application fee. For fall 2026, assignment confirmations began in mid-June. Under the cancellation policy effective August 2026, new first-year and transfer students cannot cancel an assignment except in specified circumstances; spring-term cancellation requests were due December 1, 2026, and later requests carry a $500 fee. Those dates are cycle-specific and will move for 2027 entry.
