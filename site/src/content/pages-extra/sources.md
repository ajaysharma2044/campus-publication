Every hard fact on EduExplained links to the page it came from. This page explains which sources we use, how we rank them, and what the numbered markers in a guide mean.

## What counts as a source

- **Official pages first.** The university's own offices (housing, dining, the bursar, the registrar, financial aid), the City of Ithaca, Tompkins County, TCAT, airlines and bus companies, and landlords' own listings. A price, date, fee, deadline or rule is cited to one of these whenever one exists.
- **Student experience, attributed.** Student newspapers, public forums and public reviews tell us what the rules feel like in practice. We paraphrase and link; we never quote someone as if we had interviewed them, and we never invent a student.
- **Our own compilations.** Data pages combine official numbers into one table. The method section on each data page says exactly which pages were read and when.

## The numbered markers

A small gold number after a sentence points to the entry in that guide's Sources list. The entry links to the page we read and shows the date we read it; the arrow beside it takes you back to the sentence. If an official page changes after that date, the guide is out of date until its next check, and the Updated date on the byline tells you when that was.

## What we do not use

Pages that do not name their own source, anonymous listings with no address, social media posts that cannot be linked, and anything behind a login. If we cannot source a fact, it is not on the page.

## Reporting a broken or outdated source

Use the [corrections page](/corrections/). A source that has moved or changed is a correction like any other, and the guide is re-checked when one is reported. The [editorial standards](/editorial-standards/) describe the full process, and the [about page](/about/) says who is responsible for it.
