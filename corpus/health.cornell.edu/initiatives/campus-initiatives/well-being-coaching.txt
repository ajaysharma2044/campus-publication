What is Well-being Coaching?
Well-being Coaching is a
free service
designed to support you in setting meaningful goals, building motivation, and creating greater balance and purpose in your life. It is strengths-based, collaborative, and focused on helping you get unstuck, tap into your potential, and take action in ways that feel realistic and empowering.
Coaching is
available to all students studying on Cornell's Ithaca campus
(undergraduate, graduate, and professional).
When you partner with a trained Well-being Coach, they will help you explore what’s important to you, what’s getting in the way of you achieving your goals, and how you want to move forward.

How can coaching help?
Well-being coaches can support you in moving toward goals you already have or in creating new goals during your meetings. Coaches can help you better understand your strengths and values, increase motivation for change, and overcome challenges along the way. Coaches can also help you identify other resources that may help you on your well-being journey.
What students say:
More 90% of students who have participated in coaching reported making positive changes in themselves and making progress toward their goals!
How is coaching different from counseling?
Coaching is ideal
if you are generally functioning well but want support with work-life harmony, motivation, decision-making, navigating transitions, self-awareness, etc.
What coaching is:
It is a student-driven, goal-focused approach that helps you move forward and focus on the future.
What coaching is
not
:
Coaches are not clinical or mental health providers and do not diagnose or treat mental health conditions such as anxiety or depression.
When to seek counseling instead of coaching:
If you’re experiencing significant distress, struggling with mental health issues that are interfering with your daily life, or would like to work through past experiences, counseling offered through
Counseling and Psychological Services (CAPS)
would be a better fit.
Privacy vs. confidentiality:
Coaching is private, so what you discuss with your coach will not be shared with others, except in certain specific circumstances (for example, concerning potential harm to yourself or others).
Coaching is
not
confidential. Well-being Coaches are required to report knowledge of alleged incidents of sexual harassment involving students (including sexual assault, dating violence, domestic violence, and stalking). CAPS therapists (among others) are
confidential resources
, and are not designated reporters.
Who are the coaches?
Well-being coaches are professional staff at Cornell who have received additional training in Motivational Interviewing (MI). Using this student-centered, evidence-based conversation style, coaches help you explore your goals, clarify and strengthen your motivation, and build confidence to help you make wanted changes. Regardless of any other professional training, in their coaching roles Well-being Coaches focus on providing support and guidance rather than providing health or mental health advice. Learn more about our coaches below.
How do I sign up for coaching?
If you’re interested in signing up for coaching, please complete the initial
Well-being Coaching Interest Form
. Our Program Director for Well-being Coaching, Sheila Singh, will follow up with you with more details (usually within two business days).
If your needs are a good fit for coaching, Sheila will send you a link so you can book a meeting with a coach whose availability matches your schedule.
If your needs would be best served by another resource, she will suggest appropriate alternatives to coaching.
What should I expect from the first meeting with a coach?
A supportive space:
In the first meeting, your coach will listen with care and compassion with the objective of getting to know you as well as your strengths and challenges.
Exploration of needs and goals:
You'll likely explore areas of your life that you want to improve such as time management, sleep, motivation, relationships, or overall well-being. Your coach will partner with you to develop your goals and work toward creating realistic, achievable plans. Don’t worry if you don’t have clear goals figured out yet; part of the process can be discovering them together. And, if you meet with a coach and it turns out your needs are better supported by a different service, your coach will help connect you to the most appropriate resources.
Tools to help you grow:
Your coach may use tools and resources (like the VIA Strengths Survey to better understand your
strengths
, a Well-being Vision worksheet, and
SMART goals
) to help deepen your self-understanding and help you move forward in life. If you are interested, you are welcome to take the
VIA Strengths Survey
on your own or before meeting with a coach.
How long do meetings last?
Initial meetings are 60 minutes long. Subsequent meetings are also generally about an hour but can be shorter based on your needs and schedule.
How many meetings can I have with a coach?
Students generally meet with a coach 1-3 times to get unstuck and start making progress, but the exact number of meetings is flexible and tailored to each student’s needs.
How will I know when I should stop coaching?
Many students choose to pause or stop coaching when they feel they’ve made meaningful progress, gained clarity, or developed tools to move forward on their own. You might notice you’re feeling more confident, are more consistent in your habits, or better able to self-reflect. You and your coach will regularly check in about your progress and revisit your goals. When it feels like the right time to wrap up, your coach will support you in making a thoughtful transition.
Where can I find other types of support?
We suggest starting with these resources:
Counseling and Psychological Services (CAPS)
Student Support and Advocacy Services (SSAS)
24/7 hotlines & text lines
Confidential resources
Campus-wide options for support (e.g., Advising, Career Development, etc.)
