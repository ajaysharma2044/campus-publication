About ADHD
Attention Deficit Hyperactivity Disorder (ADHD) is a natural variation in brain structure affecting executive functioning, including planning, decision making, organization, time management, and attention.
People with ADHD may experience inattentiveness (inability to focus), hyperactivity (being extremely active), and/or impulsivity (not being able to control behavior). To be diagnosed as ADHD, symptoms must be out of the normal range for a person’s age and development.

ADHD evaluation (provided off-campus only)
If you’re interested in being tested for ADHD, Cornell Health can provide you with information and help you connect with an off-campus provider for evaluation (which may include neuropsychological testing for students with some co-occurring conditions).
Our CAPS staff can provide you with a list of recommended off-campus providers and offer guidance about insurance coverage. Note that the cost of ADHD evaluation, and the coverage provided by insurance plans, can vary widely.

ADHD medication services at Cornell Health
To be eligible for
ADHD medication management services
at Cornell Health – including transferring management of a current ADHD prescription to Cornell Health –
you must provide documentation of your diagnosis
that meets the

If your documentation is insufficient to support the requested services

If you are currently taking ADHD medication
, please note that it may take two to three months from the time you initiate care to complete the transfer of your medication management to Cornell Health. Be sure to plan ahead with your current health care provider so you will have a sufficient supply of your medication(s) while you arrange to continue your care here.

IMPORTANT: Please note that the prescribing of ADHD medication is up to the discretion of the treating clinician, regardless of diagnosis.

ADHD DOCUMENTATION REQUIREMENTS
To be eligible for ADHD medication services at Cornell Health – including transferring management of a current ADHD prescription to Cornell Health – you must provide the following documentation.
FULL Neuropsychological Evaluation Report
Necessary components:
Evaluation and formal report completed by a Licensed Clinical Psychologist (PsyD or PhD)
Thorough clinical diagnostic interview including: history of presentation and onset of symptoms, current presentation,
medical history, family mental health history
Comprehensive battery of diagnostic tests including measures of: intelligence, learning and memory, executive
functioning and attention, social and emotional functioning (additional measures may be requested based on clinical
picture)
Narrative interpretation of testing scores/results
Diagnostic formulation and summary of reasoning
DSM diagnoses
– OR –
Alternative Documentation (Comprehensive Records)
Students who have a history of treatment, with a well-established diagnosis, who are looking to transfer their ADHD
medication management to Cornell Health have the option to submit the following alternative documentation in lieu of
neuropsychological testing.
Necessary components (please note that summary letters or progress notes alone are not sufficient):
History of onset of diagnosis
Copies of validated assessment measures used to determine the diagnosis, such as limited psychological testing
documenting symptoms in two or more settings (basic self-report screening measures may not be sufficient
Clinical interview or additional measures used to assess possible cooccurring diagnoses including learning disorders,
mood disorders, anxiety disorders, and substance use disorders
Diagnostic Statement (including DSM diagnosis of ADHD)
A record of prescribed medications
Description of current functional limitations
