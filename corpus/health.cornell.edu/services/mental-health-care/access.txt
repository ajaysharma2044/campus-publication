How to connect with mental health support
Scheduling an access appointment is the first step to connecting with many services offered by
Counseling & Psychological Services (CAPS)
, as well as other support options.
During this
free 20-minute Zoom appointment
, a Cornell Health counselor will ask you questions, listen to your concerns, and match you with appropriate care, which might include:
Individual counseling
with a CAPS counselor at Cornell Health or at one of our
Embedded Therapist
locations
Group counseling
(students may also schedule a group orientation through myCornellHealth)
Mental health medication management
(managed by members of our Medical Team)
Other options for support, including on-campus and off-campus services and resources
Note:
You do
not
need to schedule an access appointment to participate in
Let's Talk drop-in consultation
.
Learn more about what to expect from your access appointment.

How to connect directly with a specialist:
Schedule an ADHD Triage Appointment
to request ADHD medication management services at Cornell Health or explore off-campus ADHD evaluation.
Connect with the CAPS Sexual Violence Response Counselor
for confidential support related to a recent sexual assault or unwanted sexual contact.
Schedule an AOD Support Session
if you would like to meet with a counselor about an alcohol or other drug (AOD) concern.
Connect with a CAPS Referrals Manager
for help finding long-term counseling or specialty care options off-campus, in Ithaca or in New York State.
What to expect from your appointment
The purpose of an access appointment is to understand your concerns and connect you with appropriate support. Access appointments are
not
counseling sessions, but can be the first step to being connected with individual counseling.
Here’s what to expect.
Log in 5 minutes before you appointment begins to complete your pre-appointment questionnaire:
Log in to
myCornellHealth
and click "Appointments" to access your questionnaire.
The questionnaire is designed to gather some initial information about you to guide your conversation with the access counselor, and will ask you to indicate which types of concerns you’re experiencing (including stress, mood concerns, loneliness, grief / loss, relationship issues, identity concerns, career concerns, international student concerns, etc.).
During your appointment:
Your access counselor will
join you via secure Zoom platform accessed through myCornellHealth. The access counselor will ask you questions to get to know you, your background, and your reasons for seeking help. They will refer to the concerns you indicated in your pre-appointment questionnaire, and may also ask you additional questions about your sleep habits, nutrition, substance use, history with mental health care, history of self-harm, and suicidality (among other topics and concerns).
At the end of the conversation:
The counselor will provide you with care recommendations to meet your needs, and help you get connected with services and support.
