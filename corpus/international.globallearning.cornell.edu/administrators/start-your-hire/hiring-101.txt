Hiring International Scholars 101
Your department may be running a faculty search or appointing a visiting international researcher—or maybe you need to arrange for an international speaker or intern to visit campus. If you're new to the administrative procedures required to arrange work authorization for foreign nationals, this 101 overview is for you.
Federal immigration regulations can be confusing. International Services advisors are Cornell's trained immigration experts.
We're here to advise you
and your international employees on employment authorization, visa requirements, and all immigration-related questions.
Below, we'll introduce you to visa sponsorship at Cornell—including first steps, roles, and resources—and answer some frequently asked questions, so you can get your new international colleague off to a great start.

During Your Search
Here are some key considerations if you are running a search with potential international candidates.
Interview Questions
Cornell HR has a guide to lawful and
unlawful inquiries
, including questions that may be asked about citizenship. Please reach out to your HR representative or central HR if you need additional support. International Services can’t advise on interview questions or vetting job applicants.
Job Offers
Before your department makes an offer, you must determine whether the international scholar is currently in the United States—and if so, under what visa status. Candidates' current status may affect the visa types they can be sponsored for and how soon they can begin working at Cornell.
All job offers must state that employment is contingent on the candidate having and maintaining U.S. work authorization.
Immigration Consultations
A
mandatory consultation
is required for all international citizens accepting permanent teaching positions. Contact us as soon as your department's offer has been accepted to set up the meeting.
If your department is making or considering an offer to someone with a complex immigration background, please
contact us
as early as possible in the search process. For example, we recommend early advice for candidates who are citizens of
travel ban countries
, almost out of
H-1B time
, or potentially subject to the
J-1 two-year rule
.

Glossary: Lingo to Know
As you navigate the visa sponsorship process at Cornell, you'll encounter new vocabulary, acronyms, and a veritable alphabet of visa types and federal form names. Here are a few common terms to know.
Alert
An alert is high-priority guidance from International Services.
Our alerts keep you informed about evolving U.S. immigration and travel policies and provide time-sensitive guidance for Cornell's international community. We encourage you to
check the alerts page
regularly and
subscribe to International-Partners-L
, our email list for Cornell staff who want to receive immigration updates sent to international scholars and students.
I-94
When foreign nationals are admitted to the United States, their entry generates an
I-94 arrival/departure record.
It
indicates the visa status they’ve been admitted in (for example, J-1, H-1B, or O-1) and how long they can stay ("admit until date" or AUD).
People can typically only remain in the U.S. until the end date on the I-94, even if their visa stamp and/or I-797 approval notice are valid for longer.
Advise your incoming scholars to check their passport stamp and I-94 carefully when they receive them. If they spot any errors, they should
work with U.S. Customs and Border Protection (CBP)
to get them corrected. The CBP-issued I-94 is only updated by international travel. Scholars should always refer to their most recent I-94. If they’ve applied for a change or extension of status since they last entered the U.S., this will be a USCIS-issued I-94 at the bottom of their I-797.
Immigrant and Nonimmigrant
An immigrant intends to live permanently in the United States.
A nonimmigrant intends to leave the U.S.
at the end of the work or exchange program. The majority of U.S. visa statuses have nonimmigrant intent and are issued to individuals with permanent homes outside the country. H-1B status allows for “dual intent” (immigrant or nonimmigrant), and therefore is usually the most appropriate visa status for tenure-track faculty and long-term employees.
MyStatus and SEVIS
MyStatus is Cornell's
international case management platform.
It is integrated with SEVIS for federal compliance.
The Student and Exchange Visitor Information System
(SEVIS) is a Department of Homeland Security database
used to track international students and visitors with J, F, and M visas.
Starting a new position?
If you're responsible for submitting visa requests,
reach out to us
for access to myStatus and other resources.
Scholar
International scholars are foreign nationals on visas sponsored by Cornell. They hold temporary
academic appointments
(paid or unpaid) to pursue scholarly activities at Cornell like research or teaching. Most are employees—faculty and staff with a variety of academic job titles—and some are short-term visiting researchers, interns, and others. International Services supports 1,300+ international scholars on Cornell-sponsored visas. The most common scholar visa types are J-1 and H-1B.
Visa versus Visa Status
A visa is the entry stamp in an international passport
that allows a foreign national to enter the U.S. It certifies the individual is eligible to apply for admission at a U.S. port of entry in a particular classification, such as J-1 or H-1B. At U.S. ports of entry, CBP officers examine entry visas and supporting documents and decide if travelers are eligible to enter the United States and how long they can stay.
Your incoming scholars are
admitted to the country with a visa status
—a nonimmigrant status that matches the visa in their passport. This is also called their immigration status.

How far ahead of the employee's start date should I submit my visa request?
Submit your request
as early as possible before the planned start date. After
International Services processes your request, additional time is needed for U.S. Citizenship and Immigration Services (USCIS) to process the request and/or the scholar to obtain a U.S. visa stamp. Consequently, we can't guarantee a specific start date.
J-1 requests can be submitted at any point, although the I-901 SEVIS fee (typically paid by the prospective J-1 exchange visitor) is generally only valid for 12 months.
H-1B and O-1 petitions can be filed with USCIS up to six months before the requested start date.
What's happening with my pending myStatus request?
When you submit a request in myStatus, you receive an email from International Services stating an estimated processing time. If your request has been pending for longer than the stated processing time, you can
contact us
for an update.
Many requests have separate parts for the department and employee to complete. If your request is pending past our stated processing time, be sure to confirm that your scholar has completed the employee section.
When a case is pending with the federal government, we'll notify you when there are any updates, such as notice of receipt or a request for evidence. USCIS and Department of Labor processing times often run several months to more than a year, so there may be long periods without updates.
How do I upgrade a petition to premium processing?
Submit a USCIS fee payment request in myStatus for the premium processing fee. We aim to upgrade the petition within five business days of receiving the request. Premium processing guarantees a USCIS processing time of ...
15 business days for H-1B, O-1, TN, E-3, EB-1B I-140, and EB-2 PERM I-140 petitions
45 business days for EB-2 NIW I-140 petitions
Premium processing can accelerate the timeline significantly, but keep in mind that it does not change International Services’ processing times or expedite steps (if any) with the U.S. Department of Labor. There may still be delays if we receive a request for evidence or if there are mailing delays.
