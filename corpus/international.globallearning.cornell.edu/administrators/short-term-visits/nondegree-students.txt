Beyond the
J-1 student intern program
, Cornell offers several pathways for visiting international students to study or conduct research for a limited time without earning a Cornell degree.
The duration is typically under 12 months: a summer, semester, or part of an academic year.
For Undergraduates
Office of Global Learning Incoming Exchange
The
Office of Global Learning manages two different pathways
for international undergraduates studying during the academic year.
Summer Research Academy
internships are another way for students from participating international exchange partners to experience Cornell.
Options from Colleges and Schools
Some Cornell colleges and schools offer special programs for visiting undergraduates in related disciplines or from partner universities. The
CALS Incoming Exchange Program
, open to international undergraduates from CALS exchange partners, is one example. These opportunities change frequently, so we advise you to check with your own college or school to determine if there's a current program to suit your prospective visitor.
For Graduate Students
The Graduate School welcomes a limited number of visiting graduate students to the Ithaca campus each year to conduct research with faculty hosts or take specific in-person courses. Nondegree graduate students have a maximum enrollment limit. They may not enroll in degree-granting programs at Cornell without formally applying and being approved for admission. Learn more about
graduate nondegree admissions
.
For All International Students
International Research Internship Program
The International Research Internship Program (IRIP) offers qualified international undergraduate and graduate students the opportunity to conduct research on campus under the direction of a Cornell faculty member. Unlike
J-1 Student Interns
, IRIP interns are on F-1 visas and must register for research credits during their time at Cornell.
No other course enrollment is allowed.
IRIP acceptance requires prior approval from the faculty mentor, department chair, and dean of your college or school. Students who are accepted into IRIP must be registered by the School of Continuing Education (SCE) and pay an administrative fee. Please
reach out to SCE
for a detailed explanation of IRIP procedures and information about how to request approval for an international intern.
