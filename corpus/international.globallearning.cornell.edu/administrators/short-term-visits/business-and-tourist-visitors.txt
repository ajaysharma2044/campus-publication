Business and Tourist Visitor Types
B-1/B-2
Speakers or short-term independent researchers who will be at Cornell for six months or less may be eligible for B visitor status. The B-1/B-2 visitor or volunteer program best serves individuals who are planning short visits to one or several U.S. universities. It is not intended for use by a scholar accepting any type of formal academic appointment. Scholars planning short visits to one or more U.S. university campuses may also use the B visa category.
B-1 visa status is for business visitors, while B-2 is for tourists. A Customs and Border Protection officer at the U.S. port of entry will make the final determination. If the business purpose of the visit is not clear, a B-2 visa is issued by default.
Individuals coming to Cornell to do research might have trouble obtaining a B-1/B-2 visa stamp at a U.S. consulate and/or entering the U.S. using a B-1/B-2 visa stamp or visa waiver at a U.S. port of entry.
B visitors and visa waiver visitors cannot receive any salary or compensation. Reimbursement for expenses (e.g., travel, food, lodging) is allowed, but be careful about recording exactly what is being reimbursed. The visitor can accept an honorarium, but only if the visit to Cornell does not exceed nine days, and the visitor has accepted honoraria from no more than five institutions or organizations within the previous six months.
B visitors and visa waiver visitors might not meet
university requirements for volunteers
. Generally, if a person is not allowed to work in the United States due to a visa restriction, then they are also prohibited from working as a university volunteer.
B visitors and visa waiver visitors are not permitted to study while in the United States. They may not register for classes at Cornell.
WB/WT
Citizens of certain countries are eligible to enter the United States without obtaining a visa as either business visitors (WB waiver business) or tourists (WT waiver tourist). The eligibility criteria for admission are identical to those seeking admission based on a B-1/B-2 visa.
Application is made through ESTA
(Electronic System for Travel Authorization) and must occur at least 72 hours in advance.
WB/WT visitors should present their Cornell letter of invitation when entering the United States. An officer will issue an electronic entry record (I-94) with the notation WB or WT. If the business purpose of the visit is not clear, WT is issued by default.
WB/WT visitors are limited to three months with no extensions or change of status possible. If the visit will be longer than three months, the visitor should obtain the B-1/B-2 visa stamp or consider another visa classification.
WB/WT visitors are not permitted to study while in the United States.
They may not register for classes at Cornell.
How to Apply for B-1/B-2
Here are the steps your prospective visitor must follow when applying for
B-1/B-2 visitor status.
Show Letter
Apply for B-1
Honoraria
1
Take invitation letter to U.S. consulate.
The prospective visitor must present a letter of invitation from Cornell to a U.S. consulate. The letter from a department representative or faculty member must state the purpose of the visit and make it clear that Cornell will pay no salary or compensation for services. The letter can mention reimbursement or an honorarium, if the visit will meet honoraria guidelines.
The U.S. consulate makes the final decision about granting any visa.

Steps to Follow
The visitor must present the invitation letter to an admitting official at a U.S. point of entry and request entrance in the B-1 classification.
The officer will note B-1 on the visitor's electronic entry record (I-94) if the business purpose is clear. If not, B-2 is issued by default. Either classification is sufficient for reimbursement or honoraria payments.
Canadians who wish to be admitted in B status must also present a letter of invitation when entering the United States and receive a properly annotated I-94 entry document.
