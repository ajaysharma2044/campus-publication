Guiding Principles for Sorority and Fraternity Recognition
Guiding Principles for Sorority and Fraternity Recognition at Cornell
Acknowledged by the Board of Trustees, May 2026
In recognition of the importance of sororities and fraternities, the University sets forth the following principles for its sorority and fraternity student organizations (SFSOs), which will provide guidance for ongoing compliance, accountability and structure, and expectations for students who are involved and lead sororities and fraternities.
Sorority and Fraternity Life (SFL) at Cornell is currently comprised of almost one third of the undergraduate student body and over 50 chapters. These organizations have been a vibrant part of the Cornell student and alumni experience for over 150 years, serving as partners in creating meaningful student experiences that may complement the university's educational mission. Cornell recognizes that SFSOs can be a valuable community for developing student leadership skills, a greater sense of belonging, personal growth, and civic engagement.
The framework for recognition builds upon the 1997 Trustee Recognition Policy, updated most recently in 2015. Cornell is committed to supporting the long-term viability of Sorority and Fraternity Life through adaptive policies and practices that evolve with changing student needs and societal expectations.
To preserve and elevate the sorority and fraternity experience on campus, Cornell is dedicated to supporting a diverse and inclusive sorority and fraternity community that welcomes students from all backgrounds while maintaining high standards of conduct.
Sororities and Fraternities are and will remain independent legal entities. They shall be entitled to selective membership within the limits of the University's non-discrimination policies and can remain single sex in their membership as permitted by law. Cornell respects the autonomous traditions and cultures of sorority and fraternity life while ensuring alignment with institutional values and legal requirements.
Sorority and Fraternity student organizations are viewed as extensions of Cornell's educational environment, with emphasis on academic success, personal development, and preparation for post-graduation life. The university prioritizes working with organizations that demonstrate commitment to these educational values and contribute to students' intellectual and personal growth.
Cornell values SFSOs that contribute positively to the broader Cornell and local communities through service, philanthropy, and responsible citizenship. While each chapter has its own values and programming requirements, all SFSOs will reinforce the core values for which the university stands.
The university will develop common rules and expectations across all SFSOs through a collaborative approach that allows for growth and improvement while maintaining clear expectations and consequences. Compliance with those rules and regulations annually will allow for ongoing recognition of sororities and fraternities. Recognition will be the process by which benefits and privileges are extended. Key elements of recognition include:
Sororities and fraternities will comply with annual registration, insurance, risk management, fire safety, and other safety requirements in order to maintain recognition.
Organizations will provide information annually about their general operations, advisors, and active alumni in order to maintain recognition.
The university expects chapters to demonstrate robust alumni engagement such as chapter advisors, mentors, and positive role models to undergraduate students.
In addition to the benefits that all recognized student organizations receive, including the use of Cornell’s name and use of university facilities and event spaces, recognized sororities and fraternities receive:
Eligibility to house second year students as part of the university residency requirement
Ability to promote philanthropic, service, and recruitment events through university channels.
Access to sorority and fraternity life staff advisement and participation in SFL programming and student governing councils.
Promotion on the SFL website as recognized.
Ability to engage in capital campaigns for Cornell donor recognition credit.
Ability to request membership data from the university (i.e., Alumni contact information, Student GPA data, etc.)
Lifetime membership and connection to the broader sorority and fraternity network.
These guiding principles strive to affirm, preserve, and elevate fraternity and sorority life, allowing Cornell to be nimble, efficient, and creative in support of the sorority and fraternity experience for the future.

New York State Law,

).

and

:
Hazing is any act that, as an explicit or implicit condition of recruitment, admission, or initiation into, affiliation with, or new or continued membership status within a group, team, organization,
living group, or academic group or cohort, does one or more of the following:
Causes, encourages, or compels another person to engage in any activity that could reasonably be perceived as likely to create a risk of mental, physical, or emotional distress or harm; examples include but are not limited to
:
a. Undertake acts of servitude or menial tasks;
b. Undergo undue financial expenditures;
c. Engage in acts relevant to those of the group (for example practice or training activities), but in a manner that a reasonable person would consider excessive or dangerous;
d. Abuse, humiliate, degrade, or taunt another person or persons.
Involves any of the following
:
a. Consumption of alcohol or drugs;
b. Consumption of unpalatable substances, or palatable substances to excess;
c. Damage to or theft of property, or any other illegal act;
d. Violation of any University policy.
Subjects any other person (including an existing member or cohort of existing members of the group) to any of the above activities
.

to hazing does not need to regard or identify the act as hazing. The fact that an individual does not
object to and/or appears willing to participate in the activity, does not signify the conduct is not
hazing.

Recognition Policy Agreement for Sorority and Fraternity Student Organizations (SFSOs)
Cornell University (the “University”) recognizes Sorority and Fraternity Organizations (SFSOs) and provides them with certain benefits and privileges pursuant to the Student Organization Recognition Policy (the “Recognition Policy”). Accordingly, the undersigned officer on behalf of the SFSO, represents, acknowledges and agrees as follows:
Compliance with Recognition Policy.
The SFSO president, vice president and advisor, have carefully reviewed the Recognition Policy and the SFSO understands and agrees to comply with all the conditions of recognition and requirements to enjoy the benefits of recognition as set forth in the policy, including all supplemental rules, policies and regulations established by the University with respect to SFSOs. The SFSO further understands and agrees that recognition may be withdrawn at any time for non-compliance with the Recognition Policy and that the University may suspend or terminate recognition for the relevant academic year if it, in its sole discretion, determines a student organization has not complied with the necessary requirements to maintain recognition, regardless of whether a disciplinary referral is made to the Office of Student Conduct and Community Standards for misconduct covered under the Student Code of Conduct.
Status and Constitution.
The SFSO represents that it is affiliated with an inter/national sorority or fraternity. The SFSO further represents that it is a separate legal entity from Cornell University, either a not-for-profit New York State corporation in good standing with the New York State Department of State or an unincorporated association existing under the laws of New York State pursuant to the SFSO's constitution, a copy of the current version of which, and all future amendments, the SFSO agrees to keep on file with Sorority and Fraternity Life, at all times.
Independent of University.
The SFSO understands and agrees that the University is a New York State not-for-profit education corporation and the SFSO is not part of that corporation, but rather exists and operates independently of the University. The SFSO is not an agent, servant or employee of the University, but rather is independent and manages its own affairs. The SFSO understands and agrees it is not required to enter into this Agreement and the only result of the SFSO failing to acknowledge and agree to comply the Recognition Policy will be the loss of recognition and the benefits described in that policy.
Taxes.
The SFSO understands and agrees that it shall not use the University's taxpayer identification number or the University's tax-exempt status in connection with purchases or sales by the SFSO, gifts to the SFSO, interest or other income of the SFSO, or any other activity of the SFSO. Gifts can be made to the University and designated for use by the SFSO provided that such gifts are provided to further the educational, research, and community service missions of the University. As such the University reserves the right to decide if the use of such gifts are consistent with the University's non-profit educational status. SFSOs may work with the Office of Sorority and Fraternity Life and/or the Student & Campus Life Business Services Center to set up gift accounts.
Third Party Dealings.
The SFSO understands and agrees that it shall not hold itself out as being part of, controlled by, or acting on behalf of the University. The SFSO shall take affirmative steps in all of its recruitment, business, and other dealings with third parties (including, for example, prospective members and businesses) to explain its relationship with the University.
Liability, Insurance and Defense.
The SFSO understands and agrees that the University, the State of New York, and the employees and agents of either will not be liable for any of the SFSO 's contracts, torts, or other acts or omissions, or those by the SFSO 's directors, officers, members, staff or activity participants. The SFSO understands and agrees that neither it nor its directors, officers, members, staff, or activity participants are protected by the University's or the State of New York's insurance policies or self-insurance plans and that the University and the State will not provide any legal defense for the SFSO or any such person in the event of any claim against any of them. The SFSO further agrees that, consistent with and as detailed in the SFSO Insurance Policy, it will maintain commercial general liability insurance in the minimum amount of $1,000,000.00 (one million dollars) of primary liability coverage (each occurrence), coverage of at least $2,000,000.00 aggregate (total). Such liability insurance shall include Cornell University, its officers, employees and agents, as additional insured and shall be written with an insurance carrier acceptable to the University. A certificate of such insurance shall be forwarded to the University as evidence of such coverage, and the University must receive notice of any change, cancellation, or renewal of the policy. The insurance shall be considered primary.
Waiver.
The SFSO understands and agrees that a failure of the University to enforce any of the provisions of the Recognition Policy or this Recognition Policy Agreement shall not be construed as a waiver of that provision or any later breach thereof.
Sorority & Fraternity Life Expansion Policy
SFL Expansion Policy
I. Purpose
The purpose of this policy is to ensure that the expansion or reestablishment of a social sorority or fraternity at Cornell University is intentional, sustainable, and aligned with the values of the University and the Sorority and Fraternity Life (SFL) community.
Cornell University recognizes social sororities and fraternities that demonstrate the ability to positively contribute to student development, campus life, and community wellbeing. Expansion will only be approved when there is sufficient evidence that a proposed organization has the structure, support, and resources necessary for long-term success. Additionally, Cornell SFL will actively support responsible growth in all governing councils that balance community needs with expansion goals.
II. Scope
This Policy applies to all inter/national sororities and fraternities seeking to establish or reestablish a recognized chapter at Cornell University.
III. Definitions
For the purposes of this Policy:
“SFL” refers to the Sorority and Fraternity Life department within the Division of Student and Campus Life.
“Governing Councils” refers to the Interfraternity Council (IFC), Multicultural Greek and Fraternal Council (MGFC), and Panhellenic Council (PHC).
“Expansion” refers to the establishment of a new or returning sorority or fraternity chapter at Cornell University.
“Inter/National Organization” refers to the organization overseeing a sorority or fraternity at a regional or national level.
IV. Policy Statement
A. University Recognition Requirement
No social sorority or fraternity may officially operate at Cornell University without formal recognition by the University.
All recognized organizations must maintain active affiliation with one of the Governing Councils.
B. Authority and Oversight
The University, through SFL and in consultation with the Governing Councils, retains sole discretion to:
a. Assess community readiness for expansion.
b. Determine whether expansion will occur in a given academic year;
c. Limit the number of organizations approved for expansion;
Meeting minimum requirements outlined in this Policy does not guarantee approval to return or establish a chapter on campus.
C. Pre-Approval Requirement
Organizations must obtain approval from SFL prior to engaging in any formal expansion process with a Governing Council.
Governing Councils may impose additional requirements consistent with their governing documents.
D. Returning Organizations
Organizations previously withdrawn from University recognition must complete the full term of any imposed suspension; and
Obtain formal approval from SFL prior to reestablishment.
Organizations are eligible to request consideration for return no earlier than one (1) year prior to the conclusion of the suspension period, unless otherwise specified in a return agreement.
All return approvals must be in alignment with prior conduct outcomes and any established return agreements.
E. Compliance with University Policies
All organizations must comply with applicable University policies, including but not limited to:
Student Code of Conduct
Universal Student Organization Recognition Policy
SFL Supplemental Recognition Policy
SFL Social Event Policy
SFL Deferred Recruitment Policy
F. Recruitment and New Member Education Restriction
Cornell University operates under a deferred recruitment model. All incoming students, including spring admit freshmen must wait at least one semester before beginning a recruitment or intake process.
Chapters may not extend their new member education process longer than four (4) weeks unless granted prior approval.
Procedures for Expansion
A. Expansion Eligibility and Application
Organizations must submit an initial expression of interest to SFL.
SFL, in consultation with the relevant Governing Council, will determine whether expansion is open.
The University may limit the number of concurrent expansion processes.
Panhellenic Extension requires a vote of the PHC and shall follow the current National Panhellenic Conference Manual of Information.
B. Inter/National Organization Support
IFC and PHC organizations must provide full-time inter/national staff presence in Ithaca, New York, for a minimum of one (1) year during the establishment period.
MGFC organizations must submit a four (4) semester regional support plan demonstrating sustained engagement and the ability to recruit at least two (2) membership classes within four (4) semesters.
All organizations must provide written documentation confirming inter/national support, including designated personnel and resources.
C. Facilities
Chapters with access to housing facilities, whether University-owned or privately owned, must remain in good standing for one (1) full year prior to occupancy.
Any use of chapter facilities prior to occupancy requires prior written approval from SFL.
D. Application Requirements
Complete the online Intent to Expand form.
Complete an expansion packet to include, at minimum, the following:
a. Organizational Information:
i. Organization name and history
ii. Evidence of alumni/ae support
iii. Identification and contact information for chapter advisors
iv. Description of inter/national presence and strength
v. Description of regional support available
vi. Summary of recent expansion efforts
vii. Identification of neighboring chapters
b. Governance and Values
i. Constitution and bylaws aligned with University values
ii. Inter/national anti-hazing policy
iii. Nondiscrimination or inclusion policy
iv. Statement of organizational mission, values, and programming
c. Operational Planning
i. Membership goals for founding class
ii. Recruitment plan
iii. New member education or intake program, not to exceed four (4) weeks, including:
Purpose and goals
Detailed schedule of activities
Alignment with organizational and University values
Defined roles and responsibilities
Financial obligations
Hazing prevention education
Plan for transparency with prospective members
d. Risk Management and Compliance
i. Risk and social event management policies
ii. Proof of insurance meeting University requirements
f. Institutional Alignment
i. Acknowledgment of deferred recruitment policy;
ii. Plan for officer training and sustainable chapter operations.
g. Inter/National Support Letter
i. Formal letter from the inter/national organization outlining:
Staffing commitments
Resources allocated
Identification of individuals responsible for expansion
E. Additional Requirements for Returning Organizations
Organizations seeking reestablishment following loss of recognition must additionally submit:
a. Prior Cornell Presence
i. History of prior recognition
ii. Explanation of departure
iii. Lessons learned and commitments for future success
b. Chapter Success Plan addressing:
i. Prior conduct issues
ii. Return status management plan (if applicable)
iii. Risk management improvements
iv. Short- and long-term operational strategy
c. Staffing and Support Plan demonstrating:
i. Minimum one (1) year of full-time inter/national presence in Ithaca for IFC and PHC organizations, or four (4) semester regional support plan for MGFC organizations.
d. Recruitment Plan and Timeline
i. Plan and timeline should reflect semester returning to campus. Spring semester should include an end of fall semester plan for visibility.
F. Review and Approval Process
SFL will review all applications for completeness and alignment with University standards.
Completed applications will be shared with the appropriate Governing Council.
The Governing Council will evaluate and determine whether to recommend expansion.
Final approval is contingent upon coordination between SFL and the Governing Council.
Upon approval, SFL and the Governing Council will establish an official expansion timeline.
IV. Terminology
The Cornell Sorority and Fraternity community does not use the term “colonization.” All organizations must use language that reflects inclusive and respectful practices when referring to the establishment of new chapters.
VII. Effective Date and Review
This Policy shall be effective upon approval and is subject to periodic review by the Sorority and Fraternity Life in consultation with the governing councils.

The University, the State of New York, and the employees and agents of either will not be liable for any of an SFSO 's contracts, torts, or other acts or omissions, or those by an SFSO 's directors, officers, members, staff or activity participants. These requirements, including representations regarding liability, defense, and maintenance of insurance, are incorporated herein by reference and apply as a condition of University recognition and must be maintained at all times.

Certificates of Insurance Collection Process
Sorority and Fraternity Life (SFL) is responsible for the collection of a Certificate of Insurance (COI) from each SFSO that is recognized by Cornell University pursuant to the Recognition Policy. The SFL Administrative Assistant is responsible for the following process for the office, with oversight from the SFL Director or designee:
The SFL Administrative Assistant will
maintain an Excel document
with each recognized SFSO on it and list the date the current policy expires.
The Administrative Assistant will
send a notice
to each chapter president, chapter advisor, house corporation president (if applicable), and the national organization representative 30 days prior to the expiration date of the current policy, notifying the chapter of the need to provide a new COI documenting insurance compliance and that failure to do so by the policy expiration date will result in an immediate suspension of recognition.
The Administrative Assistant will also copy the SFL Director and the SFL advisor for the relevant Council. Included in this notice will be the current language of the Insurance Policy as well as a link to the Insurance Policy (see sample email below).
If this COI documenting insurance compliance is not received at least seven (7) days prior to the expiration of the chapter’s policy, the Administrative Assistant will send a second and final reminder to the chapter president, chapter advisor, house corporation president (if applicable), and the national organization representative.
If the COI documenting insurance compliance is not received within five (5) days following the expiration date, the Director or designee will send a notice to the chapter president, chapter advisor, house corporation president (if applicable), and the national organization representative notifying them the chapter’s recognition has been suspended until, at a minimum, a current COI documenting insurance compliance is received.
During a suspension of recognition, all chapter activities and events must immediately cease. The Director of Risk Management from the Office of Risk Management and Insurance will be copied on any notice of suspension.
When SFL receives a COI, the Administrative Assistant will take the following steps:
Check to make sure the COI documents compliance with all insurance requirements in the Recognition Policy (in terms of amounts, primary status of coverage, listing of additional insured, and issuance from a reputable insurance company).
If the COI does not document such compliance, reply to the chapter within 24 hours of receipt (president, advisor, house corporation president [if applicable], national representative, SFL Director, and Council advisor) and tell them what they need to change to comply with the Recognition Policy.
If the COI documents compliance with insurance requirements, promptly scan and put in the folder on the SFL Shared Drive.
Promptly update the Excel document.
Each compliant COI will be retained in the SFL Shared Drive for a total of five years from the date it is effective and shall be available to the Office of Risk Management and Insurance as requested.
Social Event Registration:
SFL staff responsible for the social event registration process will have access to the Excel document regarding insurance certificates for their reference when approving social events. Only chapters with up-to-date COI’s on file will be permitted to register social events. Any social event or chapter activity that takes place while a suspension of recognition is in place will result in immediate termination of recognition for the academic year consistent with the Recognition Policy and a disciplinary referral to the Office of Student Conduct and Community Standards.

Sorority and Fraternity Life Social Event Policy
Purpose
To promote a safe environment for all Cornell students participating in sorority and fraternity sponsored or endorsed events.
To assist chapter officers, chapter members, alumni advisors and volunteers with reducing the risk associated with the consumption of alcohol by members and guests through the promotion of safe and responsible behavior.
To affirm the sorority and fraternity community’s tradition of and commitment to responsible self- governance.
Educational Requirements
The sorority or fraternity President, Social Chair(s), and Risk Management Chair (or applicable positions) each must complete Event Management Training at the beginning of each semester. Training will take place prior to the activation of the event notification form. As leaders, these officers are responsible for conveying relevant information regarding event management to their chapter.
Event monitors must complete the required event monitor training annually prior to serving as an event monitor. The form is available via
Campus Groups
.
Each semester, chapters will host a Sorority & Fraternity Life (SFL) approved independent bystander intervention training, like Cayuga’s Watchers, and 50% of each chapter must attend. Trainings must be approved by the assigned chapter coach before executed.
Requirements to Host Events
Each sorority and fraternity must first register their organization in
Campus Groups
at the beginning of each semester.
The chapter President must sign and submit the Recognition Policy Acknowledgement Form at the beginning of each semester. The form is available via
Campus Groups
.
The chapter must have adequate insurance coverage in place ($1,000,000 of primary liability coverage for each occurrence; general aggregate coverage of at least $2,000,000; and Cornell shall be listed as additional named insured). An approved certificate of insurance must be on file with SFL.
A valid copy of the Ithaca Fire Department Operating Permit, Maximum Occupancy Permit for Assembly Space, and Certificate of Compliance (as applicable) must be on file with SFL. For university-owned properties, a New York State Fire Permit is also required.
The sorority or fraternity must be recognized and in good standing at the time the event is scheduled to take place.
No chapter will be eligible to host an event until Event Management Training is conducted. Events are not permitted after the final day of instruction each semester, with discretion given to events for Slope Day.
If your organization’s National/International risk management/social event policy sets stricter and/or additional requirements, it is expected that you follow that policy/expectation. Each sorority and fraternity is, and remains, an independent legal entity responsible for its own actions and for meeting its own legal duties and obligations. Therefore, all sororities and fraternities are responsible for knowledge of and compliance with both this policy and the policies of their National/International.
Event Notification Process
All social events with outside guests (with or without alcohol) must be submitted via
Campus Groups
. A detailed schedule will be provided with notification deadlines at Event Management Training each semester.
Deadlines to Notify:
For social events without alcohol, BYOB events, and third-party vender events (hosted at a bar, hotel, restaurant, etc.) the deadline is
Tuesdays at noon ET
.
For events hosted at a chapter facility using a third-party vendor to sell/distribute alcohol, the deadline is
15 business days prior to the event
.
Events held on campus but not in a fraternity or sorority house must follow the University’s Academic and Event Scheduling
guidelines

Social Event Policy
The following is required of any activity or event sponsored or endorsed by the chapter/organization, including those that occur on or off organizational/chapter premises:
The organization, members, and guests must comply with all federal, state, provincial, and local laws. No person under the legal drinking age may possess, consume, provide, sell or be provided alcoholic beverages.
Organizations, members, and guests must follow the federal law regarding illegal drugs and controlled substances. No person may possess, use, provide, distribute, sell, and/or manufacture illegal drugs or other controlled substances while on organizational/chapter premises or at any activity or event sponsored or endorsed by the organization. In addition, cannabis remains an illegal drug under federal law and university policy 4.8 prohibits the possession, use or distribution of cannabis on university- owned property or as part of any university sponsored event or activity.
Alcoholic beverages must either be: (1) provided and sold on a per-drink basis by a licensed and insured third-party vendor (e.g., restaurant, bar, caterer, etc.); or (2) brought by individual members and guests through a bring your own beverage (“BYOB”) system.
The presence of alcohol products above 15% alcohol by volume (“ABV”) is prohibited on any organizational premises, and at any event and cannot be served by third-party vendors to event attendees.
Common sources of alcohol, including bulk quantities, which are not being served by a licensed and insured third party vendor, are prohibited (i.e., amounts of alcohol greater than what a reasonable person should consume over the duration of an event).
Alcoholic beverages must not be purchased with organizational/chapter funds or funds pooled by members or guests (e.g., admission fees, cover fees, collecting funds through digital apps, etc.).
An organization must not co-host or cosponsor, or in any way participate in, an activity or event with another group or entity that purchases or provides alcohol, illegal drugs or controlled substances.
An organization must not co-host or cosponsor an event with a bar, event promoter, or alcohol distributor; however, an organization may rent a bar, restaurant, or other licensed and insured third-party vendor to host a chapter event.
Attendance by non-members at any event where alcohol is present must be by invitation only, and the organization must utilize a system to track attendance to capture Cornell students and a guest list system for non-Cornell students.
Attendance at events with alcohol must not exceed local fire or building code capacity of the organizational premises or host venue.
Any event or activity related to the new member joining process (e.g., recruitment, intake, rush, etc.) must be substance free. No alcohol or drugs may be present if the event or activity is related to new member activities, meetings, or initiation into an organization, including but not limited to “bid night,” “Big/Little” events or activities, “family” events or activities, and any ritual or ceremony.
Organizations, members, or guests must not permit, encourage, coerce, glorify or participate in any activities involving the rapid consumption of alcohol, such as drinking games.
Each chapter hosting or co-hosting an event must have event monitors who are substance free for the duration of the event. The ratio of 1 event monitor to 25 attendees which includes at least one chapter officer.
All events must meet all fire code regulations, including the construction of temporary structures of decorations.
All event themes must not be motivated by
bias
in whole or in part including but not limited to bias against age, ancestry or ethnicity, color, creed, disability, gender, gender identity or expression, height, immigration or citizenship status, marital status, national origin, race, religion, religious practice, sexual orientation, socioeconomic status, or weight.
No more than four chapters may sponsor or co-sponsor any one event with alcohol and event must be within fire code capacity.
All events with alcohol must conclude by 1 a.m.
Chapters are only permitted to co-host events with alcohol with sororities and fraternities who are recognized by the university as defined in the Recognition Policy for Fraternities and Sororities.
Licensed and insured third-party security is required for events utilizing third party vendors (can be provided by the host venue) or at social events that exceed 150 members and guests, regardless of location.
All events must comply with all campus health and safety requirements, including but not limited to those enacted in response to the COVID-19 pandemic.
Enforcement
Any chapter who hosts an event which was not submitted to SFL via the notification form or an event that is otherwise not in compliance with this policy will be referred immediately to the appropriate conduct process. Local and University partners such as, Cornell University Police, Cayuga Heights Police, Ithaca Police Department, Ithaca Fire Department, City of Ithaca, Cornell Environmental Health and Safety, Interfraternity Council, Panhellenic Council and Multicultural Greek and Fraternal Council, etc. are asked to refer all chapters who appear to be holding events in violation of this policy.
Should any violations, at events or other activities, pose health and safety risks (including but not limited to facilitating, allowing, or requiring underage alcohol consumption, insufficient event monitoring/controls, or failure to complete the event notification form for an on- or off-campus event where alcohol is present), the chapter may be immediately placed on interim suspension, with all chapter activities ceased pending the outcome of an organizational conduct proceeding.
Student leaders from the sorority and fraternity community and university roving security teams will check on- and off-campus properties to make sure organizations are in compliance with this policy. Chapter leadership and event monitors must allow entry.
Chapters understand that selling or giving alcohol to an individual less than 21 years of age (by a person other than parent or guardian) is a Class B misdemeanor, punishable by imprisonment for up to three months and/or a fine, in addition to a violation of the Social Event Policy. Individuals may be referred to the Office of Student Conduct and Community Standards for individual violations.

What Constitutes a Chapter Social Event?
Any social activity or social event sponsored or endorsed by the organization, including those that occur on or off chapter premises.
Please note:
For the remainder of the 2026-2027 academic year, all parties and social events in IFC Fraternity Houses are suspended, per the
Presidential Statement issued on October 13, 2026
.
Determining a “Social Event” of the Chapter
The following factors are considered by the university when determining whether an event is a social event of the organization. Be honest and go through the list. The more you answer yes, the more likely the event is a chapter social event.
EVENT PLANNING
Is the event being planned by one or more members/new members?
Are the officers aware of the event being planned?
Are any officers involved in event planning?
Do the officers have prior knowledge of the event?
Is the event actively or passively endorsed by a majority of the active chapter?
If a crisis occurred, would the chapter president take charge?
TERMINOLOGY
Are members attempting to rename the event in order to give the appearance that it isn’t associated with the chapter?
Is the event being coined as “unofficial” or “unregistered”?
Is the event occurring before or after an event (“pre-game” or “after party”)?
COMMUNICATION
Is the event listed on a chapter calendar (public or private)?
Is the event listed or advertised on the chapter website (public or private)?
Is the event listed or advertised on social media accounts (e.g. Instagram, Facebook, Twitter, GroupMe, Google Chats, etc.)?
Do online invitations refer to the organization (e.g. Facebook events)
Will the event be announced at a chapter meeting?
Will the event be marketed over the chapter texts/list serves?
Is there a theme for the event?
If guests were stopped on their way to the event, would they say they were going to a the “XYZ” event?
If guests posted on social media, would they say they were at the “XYZ” event?
FUNDING
Is the event financed by the chapter?
Is the event being financed by collection of funds from a group of members?
LOCATION
Is the event being hosted on chapter property?
Is the event being hosted in a house/apartment/chapter annex/live outs where multiple members live?
Is the event being hosted in a university provided meeting space?
Is the event being hosted in a location where you traditionally hold your events?
ATTENDANCE
Is the entire chapter invited?
Are new members invited to attend?
Are certain pledge classes invited to attend?
Is a majority of the chapter invited?
If a member/new member showed up, could he/she attend the event?
Will officers be in attendance?

Interfraternity Council Social Event Suspension Quick Guide
In effect through May 31, 2027
SUMMARY:
IFC fraternities may continue chapter operations. Parties and social events at fraternity houses are suspended for the remainder of the 2026–27 academic year. Normal chapter operations, including Chapter meetings, brotherhood events, new member activities, philanthropy/service events and family/alumni programming can continue, but must be substance-free. Every member is responsible for understanding and following the restrictions.
1. What you need to do now
Tell every member.
Make sure brothers, new members, and anyone organizing chapter activities understand the restrictions. Do not use another organization’s name, registration, or venue to circumvent the suspension.
Check before organizing an event.
If you are unsure whether a gathering is permitted, contact Sorority and Fraternity Life (SFL) by email at
sfl@cornell.edu
before making plans. Do not assume an event is allowed simply because it is informal or alcohol-free.
Understand
what a social event
is and therefore needs to be registered.
Cancel prohibited events.
Cancel any party or social event planned at your fraternity house that would normally require registration under Cornell’s
Social Event Policy
.
Know the consequences.
A prohibited in-house party or social event will result in an immediate emergency suspension, with the matter referred to the Office of Student Conduct and Community Standards (OSCCS). Individual students are also subject to referral for Code violations.
2. What is allowed vs. not allowed?

Chapter meetings and rituals
: Normal chapter operations may continue and must remain substance-free.
Parties or social events in fraternity houses
: These events will not be permitted for the remainder of the 2026-2027 academic year.
Brotherhood events and member-only meals
: Must be limited to chapter members and substance-free.
IFC events hosted at another organization’s house:
Hosting an event at another organization’s house to circumvent the suspension violates the mandate and will result in an emergency suspension. (Ghost-hosting not allowed)
New-member activities
: New-member activities are permitted and must be substance-free, as per policy.
Events hosted by another organization at an IFC house:
Do not lend your chapter house to another organization to host an event at your house to circumvent the suspension. This violates the mandate and will result in an emergency suspension.

social event policies.
We also strongly encourage students to approach such gatherings with the same attention to safety and care for one another that are expected across our community.
3. What happens if a chapter violates the suspension?
Immediate emergency suspension for an IFC fraternity holding a prohibited in-house party or social event.
Referral to OSCCS for review under Cornell’s Student Code of Conduct and applicable procedures.
Potential organizational on-going sanctions, up to and including suspension or dismissal.
Another fraternity, Greek, and/or student organization may also be referred to OSCCS if it knowingly helps an IFC organization circumvent these directives.
4. What comes next?
This suspension is an opportunity to pursue meaningful and lasting changes to Cornell’s social environment amidst the important conversations about campus safety and sexual assault. During this period, the University will lead the assessment of fraternity and broader campus social life and consider whether and how they can safely continue.
The university is in the process of planning additional University- or student-sponsored events that will provide additional social outlets. We also look forward to working with students to plan and execute these events, and to do the broader work of determining the safest model for campus social life.
5. If someone needs emergency help
Call 911 or Cornell emergency services immediately.
Do not delay seeking help because you are worried about disciplinary consequences. Cornell’s Good Samaritan Protocol remains in effect for medical emergencies, including alcohol or other substance intoxication, suspected overdose, serious injury, and other safety concerns.
