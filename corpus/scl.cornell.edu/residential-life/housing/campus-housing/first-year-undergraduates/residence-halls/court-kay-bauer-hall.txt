Court-Kay-Bauer Hall
Court-Kay-Bauer (CKB) Hall is a vibrant North Campus community that combines the energy of a large residential neighborhood with the close-knit feel of a smaller living environment. Comprised of three interconnected residence halls that opened in 2001, CKB is an excellent fit for students who want to meet new people, explore a wide variety of interests, and remain fully engaged in the Cornell experience.
Designed to foster both community and academic success, CKB offers expansive lounges, study spaces, kitchens on every residential floor, multipurpose rooms, and convenient access to tutoring and academic support. Residents regularly connect through traditions and programs such as First Years on Ice, the Ice Cream Crawl, Chinese New Year celebrations, weekly study breaks, and trips to the Corning Museum of Glass. These experiences help students build friendships, develop a sense of belonging, and create lasting memories throughout their Cornell journey.
Bauer Hall is home to Cornell’s
Sober Living Community
, a dedicated residential environment designed for students who choose to pursue a substance‑free lifestyle. This lifestyle may appeal to individuals who want to be surrounded with a community of like-minded peers and for students who seek a living space that aligns with their personal or spiritual beliefs. First year, transfer and upper-level students are welcome in this community.
More About Court-Kay-Bauer (CKB) Hall
Residents benefit from the dynamic social atmosphere of North Campus while building meaningful friendships and connections within their residential community.
The hall's unique name reflects both its architectural design and the generosity of Cornell alumni. Originally known as Court Hall, the name referenced the grassy quadrangle formed by the building's layout. As part of Cornell's North Campus Residential Initiative, two wings were later named in honor of dedicated alumni and philanthropists Robert J. and Virginia Bauer and L. William "Bill" Kay II '51, whose support helped advance the university's residential vision. Together, these names form the now-familiar Court-Kay-Bauer, or CKB, Hall.
Living in Court-Kay-Bauer Hall gives students the opportunity to engage deeply in campus life while enjoying a welcoming and supportive residential experience. Whether participating in community traditions, taking advantage of academic resources, connecting with peers through shared interests, or simply finding a place to call home, residents gain the relationships, experiences, and sense of community that help make Cornell feel both exciting and personal.

Sober Living Community
Bauer Hall is also home to Cornell’s Sober Living Community, a dedicated residential environment designed for students who choose to pursue a substance‑free lifestyle. This lifestyle may appeal to individuals who want to be surrounded with a community of like-minded peers and for students who seek a living space that aligns with their personal or spiritual beliefs. Occupying Bauer Hall, this community offers a supportive, inclusive, and wellness‑centered home for students committed to personal growth, academic success, and intentional living.

Faculty Involvement
Connie Yuan, Professor of Communication, is the Faculty-in-Residence for Court-Kay-Bauer Hall. She lives in an apartment in the building and regularly spends time with student residents of the Court-Kay-Bauer community.
Court-Kay-Bauer Hall also has a number of Faculty and Community Fellows that provide students with opportunities to explore and cultivate their academic, intellectual, and cultural interests. You will find them attending events in your building, eating with students in the dining room, and leading on and off campus trips to lectures, plays, films, concerts, and regional points of interest.
