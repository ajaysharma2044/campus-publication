Low Rise #7
Low Rise 7 offers students a welcoming residential experience that combines the close-knit feel of a smaller community with the energy and opportunities of North Campus. Residents enjoy access to shared lounges, study rooms, and kitchens that serve as gathering places for everything from movie nights and faculty-in-residence programs to informal conversations and study sessions.
The combination of comfortable community spaces, residential programming, and a supportive neighborhood atmosphere helps residents develop meaningful relationships, strengthen their sense of belonging, and make the most of their time at Cornell. Low Rise 7 provides a home where students can grow academically, socially, and personally while staying connected to the heart of campus life.
More About Low Rise 7
Opened in 1969, Low Rise 7 was part of a major expansion of Cornell's residential system during a period of rapidly growing enrollment. Located within a residential neighborhood shared with several of Cornell's Program Houses, Low Rise 7 is connected by brick pathways and features modern, yet comfortable living spaces designed to foster community.
As part of the North Campus community, Low Rise 7 is well suited for students who want to meet new people, explore a variety of interests, and remain connected to the broader Cornell experience. Living here gives students the opportunity to build friendships, engage with a diverse group of peers, and participate in a vibrant residential culture that values connection, exploration, and shared experiences.
"My favorite part of living in Low Rise 7 is the people I've met. I am so happy with the community I ended up in." — Ava F.

Faculty Involvement
Stephanie Thomas, Senior Lecturer in Economics, is the Faculty-in-Residence for the Low Rises. She lives in an apartment in Low Rise 6 and regularly spends time with residents of the Low Rise community.
The Low Rises also have a number of Faculty and Community Fellows that provide students with opportunities to explore and cultivate their academic, intellectual, and cultural interests. You will find them attending events in your building, eating with students in the dining room, and leading on and off campus trips to lectures, plays, films, concerts, and regional points of interest.
