Balch Hall
Balch Hall is one of Cornell's most iconic and historic residence halls, offering female-identified students a distinctive blend of tradition, community, and leadership development.  Opened in 1929 through the generosity of Allan C. Balch '89 and Janet Jacks Balch, the hall continues to serve generations of Cornell students while reflecting the enduring importance of women's education and community.
Living in Balch Hall gives students the opportunity to cultivate independence, build confidence as leaders, connect with peers across generations, and participate in a residential community that values engagement, support, and shared experiences. Rooted in Cornell history and enriched by the contributions of its residents, Balch Hall remains a vibrant place where students can explore their interests, strengthen their connections, and make the most of their Cornell journey.
More About Balch Hall
Located on North Campus, Balch places residents at the heart of undergraduate life while fostering meaningful connections across class years. Its ivy-covered stone walls, Gothic architecture, and picturesque courtyards create a distinctive living environment that combines historic character with modern enhancements.
Balch is also known for its tradition of student leadership and self-governance. Through the student-led Balch Hall Council, residents help shape their community, organize programs and events, and develop leadership skills while exploring topics such as wellness, academic success, identity, and career preparation.

Faculty Involvement
Emily Wilcox Gier, Associate Professor of Practice, Dietetics Program Leader and Internship Director in the Division of Nutritional Sciences, is the Faculty-in-Residence for Balch Hall. She and her husband, Paul, reside in Balch Hall and regularly spend time with student residents of the Balch community.
Balch Hall also has a number of Faculty and Community Fellows that provide students with opportunities to explore and cultivate their academic, intellectual, and cultural interests. You will find them attending events in your building, eating with students in the dining room, and leading on and off campus trips to lectures, plays, films, concerts, and regional points of interest.
