Clara Dickson Hall
Clara Dickson Hall is one of North Campus's largest residence halls, whose central location and energetic community make it an ideal place for students to build friendships, explore new interests, and fully engage in campus life.
Clara Dickson Hall offers a vibrant residential experience with social and educational programs designed to foster connection and belonging. From community dinners and wellness programs to excursions and recreational activities, residents have numerous opportunities to engage with peers, create lasting memories, and make the most of their Cornell experience.
More About Clara Dickson Hall
Opened in 1946, the hall was named for Clara Dickson White (1811-1882), the mother of Cornell co-founder and first president Andrew Dickson White. Clara Dickson was a strong advocate for coeducation, and Andrew Dickson White later credited her as one of the most influential voices in shaping his commitment to ensuring educational opportunities for women at Cornell. Originally built as a women's residence hall, the building's name honors her role in advancing the principle of equal academic access that became a defining part of Cornell's history and mission.

Faculty Involvement
Dickson and McLLU have a number of Faculty and Community Fellows that provide students with opportunities to explore and cultivate their academic, intellectual, and cultural interests. You will find them attending events in your building, eating with students in the dining room, and leading on and off campus trips to lectures, plays, films, concerts, and regional points of interest.
