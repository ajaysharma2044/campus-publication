Mary Donlon Hall
Mary H. Donlon Hall is a vibrant North Campus community known for its welcoming atmosphere, strong traditions, and opportunities for involvement. Home to students who enjoy meeting new people and engaging in campus life, Donlon offers an active residential environment where friendships are formed, leadership skills are developed, and community is built through shared experiences.
Its spacious lounges, library, and multipurpose room provide gathering spaces for studying, relaxing, and community events. An active Hall Council organizes tournaments, game nights, and other programs, while leadership opportunities, including service on the Judicial Board, help residents develop skills in governance, collaboration, and decision-making.
More About Mary Donlon Hall
Opened in 1961, the hall is named for Mary Honor Donlon Alger '20 (1893-1977), a pioneering Cornell alumna, trustee, lawyer, and judge. Donlon was the first woman appointed to the federal bench by President Dwight Eisenhower and the first woman in New York to serve as a federal judge. The residence hall honors her remarkable achievements and lasting contributions to Cornell and society.
Today, Donlon combines this legacy of leadership with a highly social, student-centered living experience. Living in Donlon gives students the opportunity to build lasting friendships, explore new interests, gain leadership experience, and contribute to a close-knit community within the energy of North Campus. Residents benefit from a vibrant residential culture grounded in connection, engagement, and shared community.

Faculty Involvement
Sabrina Karim, Associate Professor of Government, is the Faculty-in-Residence for Donlon Hall. She and her family live in an apartment in the building and regularly spends time with student residents of the Donlon community.
Donlon also has a number of Faculty and Community Fellows that provide students with opportunities to explore and cultivate their academic, intellectual, and cultural interests. You will find them attending events in your building, eating with students in the dining room, and leading on and off campus trips to lectures, plays, films, concerts, and regional points of interest.
