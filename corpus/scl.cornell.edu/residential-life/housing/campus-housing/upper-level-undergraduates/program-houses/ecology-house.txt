Ecology House
Hurlburt House, home to Ecology House (EcoHouse), brings together students who share an interest in sustainability, environmental stewardship, and social responsibility. Located on North Campus, this Program House fosters a close-knit community where residents explore environmental issues, build leadership skills, and turn their values into meaningful action. Whether new to sustainability or experienced advocates, students find opportunities to learn, contribute, and grow alongside like-minded peers.
Living in Hurlburt House allows students to connect their values with action, engage with environmentally minded peers, and contribute to a community rooted in education, stewardship, and social change. Through shared purpose and hands-on learning, residents gain the knowledge, relationships, and experiences that prepare them to become thoughtful leaders on campus and beyond.
More About Ecology House
Originally opened in 1953 as the Cornell Heights Residential Club, the building became student housing in 1966 and was renamed Hurlburt House in 1972 in honor of Alfred and Ellen Hurlburt for their support of Cornell's residential system. Established in 1971, Ecology House has become one of Cornell's longest-standing themed living communities.
As both a Program House and part of the broader North Campus community, Hurlburt House offers a living environment centered on shared interests while keeping residents connected to campus life. Students build friendships through sustainability initiatives, environmental advocacy, community service, outdoor recreation, creative activities, and discussions on environmental justice. Signature experiences, including the annual Adirondack retreat, student-led committees, recycling programs, and sustainability projects, help residents develop leadership, teamwork, and problem-solving skills while making a positive impact.
Community Features
Open to first-year, transfer, and upper-level students
96 residents
Located on North Campus off of Triphammer Road, near the Africana Studies Library
All double rooms, each with a private bathroom
TV room; large social lounge with pool table, and ping pong; piano; lobby with sitting and meeting areas; laundry; large kitchen with pantry and fridge storage; printer; two study lounges; woodshop; large backyard and patio for campfires, hammocks, and occasional backyard camping.
Bedrooms contain, for each occupant: an extra-long twin bed, a closet (or wardrobe) and a dresser, a desk and desk chair, a recycling bin, and a mini-bin for landfill waste. Roommates share floor carpeting, a floor lamp, a compost bin, a Terracycling bin, overhead lighting, a heating and air conditioning unit, and a bathroom with a full-size shower with curtain, toilet, sink and vanity, and medicine cabinet. Pictures of rooms and furniture (with dimensions) can be found on the Eco CampusGroups community pages above.
Sustainable community amenities: Textbook Lending Library, Bike Share program and workshop, Swap Shop, onsite greenhouse, growing apple tree orchard, Cora Balls and drying racks in the laundry room, and a Sustainable Cleaning Station with environmentally friendly cleaning products. Upper-level Ecohousers also receive a free bus pass to encourage sustainable and accessible travel around town (all Cornell first-years already receive a free bus pass for their first year).
Wi-fi internet access is provided throughout.
Ecology House rooms have manually controlled air conditioning and heating in each room and residents are expected to make environmentally-friendly temperature choices.

How to Apply
Rising junior and rising senior students may select to live in a program house as part of the fall Housing Selection process while rising sophomores can select during their General Room Selection process in the spring. New students can indicate their preference to live in a program house on their New Student Housing Application.
