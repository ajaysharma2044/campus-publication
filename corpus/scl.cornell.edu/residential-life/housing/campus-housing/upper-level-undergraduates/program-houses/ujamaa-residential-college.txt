Ujamaa Residential College
Low Rise 10, home to Ujamaa Residential College, is a vibrant community that, while open to students of all backgrounds, celebrates the rich and diverse heritage of Black people in the United States, Africa, the Caribbean, and around the world. The name Ujamaa, a Kiswahili word meaning "extended family" or "brotherhood," reflects the community's founding values of cooperation, mutual support, self-reliance, and collective growth.
Today, Ujamaa offers residents the opportunity to engage deeply with Black culture, history, leadership, and scholarship through a wide variety of academic, cultural, and social programs. Signature events include faculty-hosted dinners, Black History Month celebrations, the State of Black America Conference, the Festival of Black Gospel, and the student-led Unity Hour discussion series. These experiences encourage thoughtful conversations, cultural awareness, leadership development, and community building.
More About Ujamaa Residential College
Opened in 1970 as part of Cornell's North Campus residential expansion, the building quickly became an important gathering place for Black students. In 1972, student leaders helped establish Ujamaa as an official program house dedicated to exploring issues affecting communities of African descent and fostering a strong sense of community and belonging. While the building officially remains Low Rise 10, it has long been identified with Ujamaa and its enduring mission.
As both a Program House and part of the broader North Campus community, Ujamaa is an ideal fit for students who value shared identity, cultural exploration, community engagement, and meaningful dialogue while remaining connected to the energy and opportunities of undergraduate life at Cornell.
Students who live in Ujamaa gain more than a place to live. They become part of a supportive and welcoming network that promotes personal growth, academic success, and lifelong connections. Strong alumni involvement provides mentorship, guidance, and professional networking opportunities that often continue long after graduation. Whether students are seeking a deeper connection to Black culture and history, opportunities for advocacy and leadership, or a community rooted in shared purpose and mutual support, Ujamaa offers a residential experience centered on belonging, empowerment, and collective achievement.

How to Apply for Residency
Rising junior and rising senior students may select to live in a program house as part of the fall Housing election process while rising sophomores can select during their General Room Selection process in the spring. New students can indicate their preference to live in a program house on their New Student Housing Application.
*Please note in some years when housing space is limited and there are available spaces in the Ujamaa, some students who did not preference housing in Ujamaa may be placed there. While the Ujamaa community is intended to celebrate Black culture and create a space for Black students to find community with those who have similar experiences and interests, all students of all identities are welcome. Regardless of their housing preference, all students residing in Ujamaa will have the opportunity to connect with each other, build community, and grow. Finally, given the theme of Ujamaa, all residents are expected to be thoughtful of the intention of the space and engage with every student in a respectful and open-minded manner.
