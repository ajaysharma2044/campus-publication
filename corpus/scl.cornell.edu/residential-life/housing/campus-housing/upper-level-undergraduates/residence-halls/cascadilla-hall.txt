Cascadilla Hall
Located in the heart of Collegetown, just steps from the Schwartz Center for the Performing Arts, restaurants, cafés, shops, and gathering spaces, Cascadilla Hall offers convenient access to the intellectual, cultural, and culinary opportunities that surround campus. Residents enjoy South Campus's unique blend of independence and community, with opportunities to engage through programs, shared spaces, and traditions while shaping a Cornell experience that strengthens the connections and interests that matter most to them.
Cascadilla Hall is one of Cornell's most historic residential communities, offering students the opportunity to live in a building that predates the university itself. This community is particularly well suited for students who value a calm, self-directed residential experience that supports focus, personal recharge, and meaningful connections rather than constant activity and exploration.
More About Cascadilla Hall
Built in 1864, the building was ultimately transformed by Ezra Cornell into student housing to affirm the historical commitment to a residential campus. Welcoming students and faculty when Cornell University opened in fall 1868, Cascadilla Hall became the university's first residence hall when ownership officially transferred to Cornell in June 1869.
Today, Cascadilla Hall blends its historic legacy with the distinctive character of Cornell's South Campus community. South Campus is home to students who have found their place at Cornell and are seeking a more independent and quieter living environment where they can deepen existing friendships, pursue their academic and personal interests, and enjoy a stronger sense of balance.

Affiliated Faculty
South Campus Residence Halls have a number of Faculty and Community Fellows that provide students with opportunities to explore and cultivate their academic, intellectual, and cultural interests. You will find them attending events in your building, eating with students, and leading on and off campus trips to lectures, plays, films, concerts, and regional points of interest.
