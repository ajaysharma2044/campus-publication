YTI Receives Grant to Fund ADA Center, Support Mental Health Research
The Yang-Tan Institute on Employment and Disability (YTI) has been awarded a $1.12 million grant to operate the Northeast ADA Center, which the institute has hosted for over 30 years. One of 10 regional ADA Centers in the U.S., the
Northeast ADA Center
provides guidance and training on the Americans with Disabilities Act (ADA) in New York, New Jersey, Puerto Rico and the U.S. Virgin Islands. During this three-year grant cycle, researchers at the center will investigate ways to make workplace mental health programs more accessible.
Northeast ADA Center Director
Wendy Strobel Gower
will lead a team of researchers, including
Sarah von Schrader
, director of research and program evaluation;
Jennifer Brooks
, research associate; and
William Erickson
, senior research specialist, all from the Yang-Tan Institute. Other key staff members are employed at the Yang-Tan Institute or at the center’s affiliate organizations: Resources for Independent Living in New Jersey, Movimiento para el Alcance de Vida Independiente in Puerto Rico and the Disability Rights Center of the Virgin Islands.
“Helping people with rights and responsibilities under the Americans with Disabilities Act to understand and implement the ADA is a responsibility we take very seriously,” said Strobel Gower, who is also executive director of YTI. “While we don’t provide legal assistance, we provide critical information to help people understand and advocate for their rights or to develop policies and practices that help public and private organizations to comply with the nondiscrimination tenets of the law.”
The researchers will gather information using a literature review, data analysis and focus groups to:
Identify the workplace mental health programming needs of workers with disabilities.
Identify factors that help or hinder workers with disabilities from accessing workplace mental health programming, and discover whether workers with disabilities can access available workplace mental health initiatives and support as required by the ADA.
Understand what training or resources are needed to assist employers with making their mental health programming accessible to all employees.
The research is important partly because the ADA requires that employees with disabilities have equal access to the “benefits and privileges of employment” available to similarly situated employees.
“Research indicates that many Americans, with and without disabilities, are struggling with their mental health at work,” Strobel Gower said. “Employee Assistance Programs provide a critical bridge to support many workers. Unfortunately, many are not fully accessible to employees with disabilities. This research will help us understand what organizations are doing well to ensure access and where there is room for improvement. This will enable us to provide targeted training and information to support improvements in accessibility for all.”
In addition to the research project, the Northeast ADA Center will host its
primary website
, as well as several specialized secondary sites, including
Small Business at Work
and
DisabilityStatistics.org
. Together, these sites contain a wide range of ADA-focused content and provide easy access to disability-related statistics.
The center also operates a technical assistance service that provides one-on-one support to people with ADA questions, as well as ADA training for groups. Although some training responds to ad hoc requests, over the next three years, the center will focus its training on small businesses, state and local governments, and web accessibility professionals.
To build nationwide capacity for understanding the ADA, the center will host and administer the national
ADA Trainer Leadership Network
in partnership with the ADA National Network. The network uses a train-the-trainer model and has about 1,000 members. Its 50-module curriculum covers ADA implementation.
The center will also work with other regional centers to develop and host the ADA Coordinator Impact Network. This program will provide professional development and networking opportunities to ADA coordinators in state and local governments.
The funder, the
National Institute on Disability, Independent Living, and Rehabilitation Research
, is the U.S. federal government’s primary disability research organization. Its mission is to generate new knowledge and to promote its effective use to improve the abilities of individuals with disabilities to perform activities of their choice in the community, and to expand society’s capacity to provide full opportunities and accommodations for its citizens with disabilities.
