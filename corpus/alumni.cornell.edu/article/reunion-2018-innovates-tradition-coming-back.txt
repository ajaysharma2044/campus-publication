Reunion drew more than 7,500 alumni, family members, and friends back to the Ithaca campus June 7-10. And with more than 5,400 alumni in attendance, 2018 was the most highly attended Reunion in Cornell’s history.
“Cornellians are coming home in record-breaking numbers,” Paul Cashman ’73, president of the Cornell Association of Class Officers, said at Cornelliana Night, the raucous, song-filled culmination of the weekend.
The Class of 1978 broke the record for 40th Reunion attendance, with 326 classmates. Reunion class fundraising campaigns totaled $89.7 million from 6,210 members, and the Classes of 1948, 1973, and 1988 set new campaign records.
Five members of the Class of 1943 were present for their 75th Reunion, while two members from the Class of 1938 joined Cornelliana Night via live stream.
In addition to reuniting classes, four of Cornell’s diverse alumni associations held Reunions, registering nearly 500 alumni and family members for the Cornell Asian Alumni Association (CAAA), Cornell Black Alumni Association (CBAA), Cornell Latino Alumni Association (CLAA), and Cornell University Gay and Lesbian Alumni (CUGALA).
Traditions of friendship
The tradition of coming back at all stages of alumni life was seen clearly at the “Spirit of ’31–Passing It Forward” ceremony on Thursday evening. Watching the ceremony, Jane Vanneman ’68, on campus for her 50th Reunion, recalled the Big Red enthusiasm that William Vanneman ’31, her father-in-law and one of the originators of “Passing It Forward,” brought to Reunion.
“Bill always said that Reunion is not just about connecting with old friends, as important as that is,” she said, “but finding new friends.”
In the Big Red spirit of friendship, Shigeo Kondo ’43, president of the most senior class on campus for Reunion 2018, presented the Reunion banner to Ankur Bajaj ’13 and James Feld ’13, with his best wishes for their fifth Reunion and many more to come.
“We are thrilled to be here,” Bajaj said. “We hope to be half the alumni you are.”

While looking up to elder classes for inspiration, Bajaj and his classmates improvised on Reunion traditions: To continue the fun of the popular tent parties on the Arts Quad, they teamed up with the Classes of 1998, 2003, and 2008 to host a “Y2K”-themed dance party that started midnight Saturday and lasted until 3 a.m. Sunday.
“As an alumni leader, I want to learn to make an event meaningful,” Bajaj said. “And people want to stay out late!”
A knack for innovation
During Reunion 2018, many tried-and-true traditions took on new dimensions. Reunions of the diversity alumni associations, coordinated for the first time, added rich educational opportunities, lively discussion, and collaboration.
“The all-diversity Reunion has been exciting,” Erica Kagan ’05, president of CUGALA, said at a dinner for CAAA, CBAA, CLAA, and CUGALA. “The intersectionality has helped us. We’re sharing a headquarters with CAAA. We don’t always get to mingle, and that’s what Reunion is all about.”
At the Olin Lecture on Friday, Justice Debra A. James ’75, JD ’78 continued the themes of education, history, and social justice raised by an earlier screening and discussion of the documentary
Agents of Change
, sponsored by CBAA and the Class of 1973, which chronicles black students’ activism at Cornell that led to the Willard Straight Hall occupation in 1969.
“We are indebted to the legacies of alumni who paved the way,” James said in her talk, “‘Daily More Useful’: My Life in Public Service.”

At Cornell, innovation has long been a tradition, and many Reunion events and activities showcased advances in the sciences, technology, and humanities. The College of Agriculture and Life Sciences (CALS) presented exhibits and events around the history and future of the cider industry. The College of Human Ecology hosted a talk and exhibit focused on fashion and the female form. The College of Arts and Sciences collaborated with the Cornell University Library (CUL) in programming about witchcraft.
“Cornell’s witchcraft collection is one of the most important in the world,” Kathleen Long, professor of French and Francophone and comparative literature, said in her lecture, “Wicked Women: Witchcraft and the Debate over Women’s Rights in Early Modern Europe.” Presented in conjunction with CUL’s exhibit
The World Bewitch’d: Visions of Witchcraft f
rom the Cornell Collections
, the talk filled Hollis E. Cornell Auditorium with alumni eager to participate and learn.
“There is so much to do,” said Terrence German ’92, an architecture graduate returning for the first time since his graduation to be a part of the CBAA Reunion. “There is a whole weekend to be enjoyed.”
Lifetime connections
For German and other first-time Reunion attendees, the simple act of re-entering the Ithaca campus after decades away was overwhelming and inspiring.
“I love Cornell,” said Jessica Nowillo ’98, “but this is my first Reunion ever.”
To see old friends and to introduce family members to the university she loves, Nowillo organized a group of alumnae from several states to meet up at Reunion. On Saturday morning, she, her friends, and their kids explored the mannUfactory maker space at Mann Library. Her son, Sebastian Nogueira, 11, said he especially enjoyed seeing the Fuertes Observatory and the astronomy department and attending a lecture on sustainability.
“It feels wonderful to be here,” said Julie Gray ’98, a member of the group. “Being in college at Cornell was a very intense time. I didn’t get to appreciate the place.”

For Amanda Quain ’13, attending her fifth Reunion continued Cornell traditions she and her friends—members of the Big Red Bands—established when they were students, such as picnicking in the Cornell Botanic Gardens before playing a concert at Saturday’s “Fun in the Sun” activities.
Myron Gross ’58 came to Reunion celebrating a lifetime of Cornell connections. Walking through the campus, he mourned the passing of several classmates but looked forward to a dinner event with Barry Strauss, professor of history and classics. Gross, who traveled from New York City, also enjoyed his first stop on campus: “The Cornell Dairy Bar, for a malted.”
For Jim White ’39, the most senior alumnus on campus and a former professor, Reunion was an opportunity to connect with generations of Cornellians he taught in CALS, the School of Hotel Administration, and the College of Veterinary Medicine. “I really enjoy being able to talk to students,” White said.
The weekend also witnessed once-in-a-lifetime moments. At least three couples got engaged over the weekend. And on Saturday, the Reunion crowd shared in a triumphant moment for the incoming president of the Class of 1993, Mike McMahon ’93, and his wife, veterinarian Natanya Nieman, when Justify—a horse under Nieman’s care and associated with McMahon’s business—won the Belmont Stakes. Mike and Natanya had left Reunion with their two kids early Saturday morning to catch the race and, as fate would have it, join the winner’s circle.
“Justify won the Triple Crown tonight,” Paul Cashman announced from the stage at Cornelliana Night. “Another Big Red victory!”
