Local Elections Basics #4: How the County prepares for disruptions
Published on October 20, 2026
Election preparedness is a Countywide effort.
Before voting begins, the Board of Elections and partner agencies use exercises and operational planning to test roles, communications, and backup procedures. The Sheriff's Office coordinates with local police agencies and State partners, so help can be dispatched quickly if needed. The Department of Emergency Response supports coordination, and County IT employs a range of cybersecurity safeguards to protect its systems and information. Specific security measures are not publicly disclosed to preserve their effectiveness.
Election continuity depends on multiple safeguards rather than any single system. These layers include paper ballots, voting systems that do not connect to the internet, trained poll workers, sealed election materials, documented bipartisan custody, and coordinated emergency-response procedures. If a contingency procedure is needed, election materials remain secured and accounted for throughout the response.
Voting procedures are designed to continue through common disruptions. Poll sites have instructions for equipment issues and communications outages; paper ballots provide continuity; and officials plan for power, weather, and facility problems. If a polling place must change, the County will publish confirmed information through official channels and place notices at the affected location when possible.
The Board of Elections coordinates with County emergency-response and public-safety partners to prepare for Election Day and respond to any issue that could affect voting operations. This planning supports safe, orderly, and accessible polling places while protecting every voter’s right to cast a ballot free from intimidation or unnecessary disruption.

Submit any address update immediately and confirm that the change appears in the voter lookup.

and date, and check the official schedule again before leaving home.
Important dates
Oct. 19:
Address changes received by the Board of Elections by this date must be processed for the General Election.
Oct. 24:
Last day for the Board of Elections to receive a voter’s change of address so the updated address is reflected in the electronic poll book for the General Election.
Oct. 24-Nov. 1:
Early voting at Tompkins County Veterans Services, 111 N. Albany St., Ithaca, or the Crash Fire Rescue Building, 72 Brown Road, near the airport. Dedicated parking for voters is available at each of these sites.
Oct. 24-26, Oct. 28, and Oct. 30-Nov. 1:
Both early-voting sites are open 9 a.m. to 5 p.m.
Oct. 27 and Oct. 29:
Both early-voting sites are open 9 a.m. to 8 p.m.
Myth / Fact
Myth:
A power, internet, or equipment problem automatically stops voting or invalidates ballots.
Fact:
Election officials maintain backup and offline procedures, including paper-ballot processes, to continue voting and secure ballots while an issue is addressed.
Voters in special situations
College students:
Students may register using a qualifying campus or home residence, but may vote only once in an election. Check your registration and assigned poll site before voting.
