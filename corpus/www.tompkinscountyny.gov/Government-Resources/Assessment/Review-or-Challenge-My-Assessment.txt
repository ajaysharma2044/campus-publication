Assessment Department
Review or Challenge My Assessment
Grievance filing is currently closed. The next grievance period will begin May 1, 2027, through May 25, 2027
Any property owner who has a change in assessment will be notified by the first week of March via mail. Here are the steps you can take to challenge your assessment:
1. Call us
The best thing you can do to better understand your assessment is to give us a call - any time of year!
Our office is open Monday through Friday, 8:00 a.m. to 4:30 p.m., and we're always happy to answer your questions.
We often hear from property owners who are frustrated or upset about their assessments. But after our staff takes the time to explain the assessment process, what an assessment actually represents, and how it relates to property taxes, those same conversations often end with something much more valuable: understanding.
Sometimes, frustration comes simply from not knowing what our department does or how the assessment process works. We're here to help clear up the confusion, explain the details, and make sure you have the information you need.
Don't wait until you're upset or until it's time to grieve your assessment. Give us a call!
A little information can go a long way toward understanding your property assessment.
2. File an Informal Assessment Review (Mid-March through early-April)
Received an Assessment Change Notice?
Property owners who received an assessment change notice have the opportunity to schedule a phone appointment with a member of our valuation staff. These appointments provide an opportunity to review your property's inventory, ask questions, and better understand how we determined your assessed value.
Appointments last approximately 10 minutes and must be scheduled by calling
607-274-5516
.
Didn't Receive a Change Notice? No Worries!
You can still request a review of your assessment! Property owners who did not receive a change notice can complete an
Informal Assessment Review
form to request that a member of our valuation staff take another look at their property's assessed value.
The form is available only during the Informal Assessment Review period, which runs from mid-March through early April.
What Happens Next?
Any assessment changes resulting from the Informal Assessment Review process will be reflected on the Tentative Assessment Roll, which is filed on May 1. Property owners whose assessments change through this process will be notified by mail on or around May 1.
Still Have Concerns About Your Assessment?
If you're still dissatisfied after the informal review process, the next step is to file a formal grievance with the Board of Assessment Review. This gives you the opportunity to present your case for why your assessment should be changed.
3. File a Grievance - (May 1 through the 4th Tuesday in May)
Anyone Can File a Grievance!
Whether you received an assessment change notice, participated in an Informal Assessment Review, or didn't request a review at all,
any property owner can file a grievance
if they disagree with their assessed value.
The grievance period begins May 1 and runs through the fourth Tuesday in May, known as
Grievance Day
.
Who Reviews Your Grievance?
When you file a grievance, your application is reviewed by the
Board of Assessment Review (BAR)
, not by Assessment Department staff. The BAR is an independent board made up of community members with a variety of backgrounds and experience, including members of the real estate community, retired mortgage loan officers, and local developers.
The BAR reviews your application and considers the information you provide when determining whether a change to your assessment is warranted.
Want to Present Your Case in Person?
On Grievance Day, property owners have the opportunity to meet with the BAR at the Assessment Office to discuss their assessments.
Appointments:
Call 607-274-5517 to schedule a 15-minute appointment between 10:00 a.m. and 2:00 p.m.
Walk-ins:
Available from 4:00 p.m. to 8:00 p.m. only.
Important:
You must complete a grievance application before meeting with the BAR.
Can't make it to Grievance Day? You can still file a grievance!
You are not required to appear before the BAR in person. Simply submitting a completed grievance application ensures that your case is reviewed by the board.
When Will You Hear Back?
Don't expect an answer on Grievance Day! The BAR listens to property owners, asks questions, and reviews the information presented. The board then reconvenes at a later date to discuss the cases and make its decisions.
Any assessment changes resulting from the grievance process will be reflected on the
Final Assessment Roll, filed July 1
. Property owners who filed a grievance will be notified by mail on or around July 1 if their assessment has changed.
Assessment Cycle
Key date definitions
:
Valuation Date
: Real property price level for the assessment cycle is frozen as of this date. (July 1)
Taxable Status/Exemption Filing Date
: The ownership and physical condition of real property as of this date are assessed (valued) according to price fixed as of the valuation date. All applications for property exemptions must be filed with the assessor by this date. (March 1)
Tentative Assessment Roll
: The assessor completes, certifies and files a roll containing proposed assessed values for each property in the assessing unit. (May 1)
Grievance Day
: Board of Assessment Review meets to hear assessment complaints. Last day property owners may file a formal complaint seeking reduction in their tentative assessments. (4th Tuesday of May).
Final Assessment Roll
: The assessor signs and files a roll that contains the final assessments, including all changes. (July 1)
School Tax Lien Date
: The date school authorities attach to the school tax roll as an authorization for collection of taxes.
County/City/Town Tax Lien Date
: The date county/city/town authorities attach to the county/city/town tax roll as an authorization for collection of taxes. Tax collector publishes notice of tax collection. (Jan 1)
End of Interest-Free Period County/City/Town Tax
: County/city/town taxes may be paid without interest or penalty fees for one month after publication of a notice of collection. (Jan 1)
