## B2. ANALYSIS 1: Rent by unit size, Collegetown vs all Ithaca (aggregator averages, not a hand sample)

Question: How much more does Collegetown cost than the Ithaca average, by unit size?
Method: RentCafe publishes averages for Collegetown and for Ithaca, both "last updated October 30, 2026". Neither page states listing counts. Difference = Collegetown minus Ithaca; percent = diff / Ithaca.

| Size | Collegetown avg rent | Coll. avg sq ft | Ithaca avg rent | Ithaca avg sq ft | Diff $ | Diff % | $/sq ft Coll. | $/sq ft Ithaca |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Studio | 2,006 | 148 | 1,772 | 335 | 234 | 13.2% | 13.55 | 5.29 |
| 1 BR | 2,342 | 433 | 2,063 | 638 | 279 | 13.5% | 5.41 | 3.23 |
| 2 BR | 3,414 | 550 | 2,459 | 902 | 955 | 38.8% | 6.21 | 2.73 |
| 3 BR | n/a | n/a | 3,039 | 1,141 | - | - | - | 2.66 |
| All | 2,815 | 375 | 2,263 | 724 | 552 | 24.4% | 7.51 | 3.13 |

Steps: 2,006-1,772=234, 234/1,772=0.132. 2,342-2,063=279, 279/2,063=0.135. 3,414-2,459=955, 955/2,459=0.388. 2,815-2,263=552, 552/2,263=0.244. $/sq ft = rent / sq ft (e.g. 2,006/148=13.55).
Sources: https://www.rentcafe.com/average-rent-market-trends/us/ny/ithaca/collegetown/ and https://www.rentcafe.com/average-rent-market-trends/us/ny/ithaca/ ; applies to 2026-10-30; checked 2026-11-07.
Caveats: Collegetown "average sq ft" of 148 (studio) and 550 (2BR) looks implausible, so the sample is probably tiny or mismatched and weighted toward a few new buildings. Asking rents, not what students pay. Rent per unit, shared by roommates. Treat as a rough reading only. The Ithaca-wide page gave no neighborhood rents for Fall Creek, Downtown, South Hill.

## C2. Residential requirement and exemptions (official, corpus)

Page: https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/campus-housing/residential-policy (corpus; fetched date in corpus file not rechecked; page current as of corpus crawl).
- 2-year residential requirement for full-time Ithaca-based first- and second-year students, with required meal plan.
- Exemptions (single academic year; reapply yearly; request during open application period, not after a signed housing license): military veterans (not ROTC); age 21+ by start of the academic year; age 16 or younger living with custodial parent/guardian; transfers with two years of on-campus residence at previous school or entering as junior/senior; students living 6+ months with a custodial parent/guardian within 25 miles of campus.
- Recognized fraternities/sororities and university-owned co-ops may house sophomores if the student applies and, for Greek houses, is a member.
Implication for off-campus: juniors, seniors, transfer juniors, and exempt students are the lease market. (Interpretation, not a fact.)

## D2. ANALYSIS 3: Rough yearly housing cost per person, on campus vs off campus (estimate)

Question: What monthly rent per person equals Cornell's on-campus double?
Inputs: on-campus double $13,744 per academic year, includes utilities and internet (B4); RentCafe averages (B2). Assumptions (mine, labeled): off-campus lease is 12 months; roommates split rent evenly; utilities and renters insurance are NOT in the rents and are excluded because no official figure was found; no sublet income; no security deposit (refundable).

Step 1, break-even rent per person: 13,744 / 12 = $1,145 per month. If only 10 months of rent are counted (sublet the summer), 13,744 / 10 = $1,374.
Step 2, off-campus yearly cost per person at average asking rents:

| Scenario | Monthly unit rent | People | Per person / month | Per person x 12 | vs on-campus double 13,744 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Collegetown 1BR alone | 2,342 | 1 | 2,342 | 28,104 | +14,360 |
| Collegetown 2BR, 2 people | 3,414 | 2 | 1,707 | 20,484 | +6,740 |
| Ithaca 2BR, 2 people | 2,459 | 2 | 1,230 (1,229.50) | 14,754 | +1,010 |
| Ithaca 3BR, 3 people | 3,039 | 3 | 1,013 | 12,156 | -1,588 |
| Ithaca 1BR alone | 2,063 | 1 | 2,063 | 24,756 | +11,012 |
| Ithaca studio alone | 1,772 | 1 | 1,772 | 21,264 | +7,520 |

Arithmetic: 3,414/2=1,707 x12=20,484; 2,459/2=1,229.5 x12=14,754; 3,039/3=1,013 x12=12,156; 2,342x12=28,104; 2,063x12=24,756; 1,772x12=21,264. Differences = scenario minus 13,744.
Result (estimate): at October 2026 average asking rents, only a 3-person Ithaca-wide 3BR comes in under the on-campus double before utilities; a 2-person Collegetown 2BR is about 49% above it (6,740/13,744=0.490). Sample: two RentCafe pages dated 2026-10-30; listing counts unknown; Collegetown 3BR n/a.
Caveats: averages are asking rents of unknown mix; per-person shares in newer luxury buildings are often quoted per bedroom (Sun column, opinion); no utilities (NYSEG, internet), no renters insurance, no furniture; the on-campus rate includes utilities and internet; the 2025-26 aid budget used the same $13,744 figure for housing, so the budget page and the 2026-27 double rate agree on that number (possible coincidence or carry-over; not resolved). Co-op comparison: a semester double $3,487 to $4,981 is $6,974 to $9,962 per year if two semesters (my arithmetic: 3,487x2=6,974; 4,981x2=9,962); co-op price includes what is not stated on the page read.
Grad comparison: Hasbrouck 1BR unfurnished $1,415 x 12 = $16,980; furnished $1,490 x 12 = $17,880; studio $1,355 x 12 = $16,260 (official, 2026-27). Versus Ithaca average 1BR 2,063 x12 = 24,756: Hasbrouck is $7,776 less than the Ithaca average 1BR (24,756-16,980), average vs average, utilities treatment of Hasbrouck not stated on the part read.

## D3. ANALYSIS 4: TCAT pass break-even

Question: How many paid rides equal the $200 OmniRide pass?
Method: 200 / 1.50 = 133.3, so 134 cash rides. Using Sun's 2024 cash fare of $1.50, which may have changed (TCAT fares page blocked). Free windows (after 6 p.m. weekdays, weekends) mean only weekday daytime rides count. At 2 daytime weekday rides per day, 134 rides is 67 weekdays, about 13 weeks of 5 days. Estimate only. If the actual fare has risen, break-even falls. Sources: C3.

Analysis 5 (date arithmetic; the count-start is the open question). Question: if 120 days are counted back from the end of the current lease, what is the earliest a landlord could show or advertise a unit? Method: end date minus 120 days. Example: current lease ends July 31, 2027. Count back: July 31 to June 30 = 31 days; to May 31 = 61; to April 30 = 92; to April 2 = 120 (April 2 to July 31: 28 days left in April after the 2nd, plus 31 May, 30 June, 31 July = 120). Result: April 2, 2027, if the rule is read that way. Check against the sponsor's examples: his "June 1 lease ... October 1" does not equal 120 days before June 1 (that would be Feb 1), so his example is counted some other way (for example 120 days before a renewal decision date, or before the end of the lease term that began June 1 of the prior year). The count start is therefore NOT resolved by any source read. Do not publish a date until a person reads sec. 258-10 on eCode360.
Contrast with what is on the market: units for 2027-28 were already shown on a management company's site on 2026-11-08 (G6). Possible explanations, none verified: vacancy or new construction with no current tenant, written waivers, buildings outside the City (the rule is City of Ithaca code), or the notice count start being different from the reading above. Sample: 1 landlord site plus 5 unread listing snippets (B3). This is a lead, not a finding.
