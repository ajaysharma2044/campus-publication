## 1. The topic in one page
**What everyone needs to know.** Off-campus housing at Cornell is a junior, senior and graduate market: first-years and sophomores must live in Cornell or affiliated housing (co-ops, recognized Greek houses) unless exempt, and a sophomore who signs a lease without an approved exemption is still billed for the full year. About 95% of graduate and professional students live off campus. Collegetown, directly south of campus, is where undergrads want to be, and its popular units are leased 8 to 16 months ahead: every dated signing in the Sun record from 2017 to 2025 bar one fallback falls in that window, and on 2026-11-08 Ithaca Renting already showed 14 of 67 Collegetown rows for 2027-28 sold out. The rest of the market (downtown, Fall Creek, Lansing, sublets, grad housing) moves months later.

Rent is quoted per bed in most student buildings and per unit everywhere else. One landlord's 2027-28 Collegetown medians run from $800 per bed in a 6BR to $2,025 for a 1BR alone; The Ivy's 3BRs work out to $1,833 to $1,933 per person. Cornell's 2026-27 double costs $13,744 with utilities and internet, which equals $1,145 a month over a 12-month lease; only big shared units (6BR and up, rooms in shared suites, downtown 2BR and 3BR shares) come in under it. The same landlord's downtown per-bed prices run about 20% to 28% below Collegetown (Collegetown runs 25% to 39% above downtown).

The law is better for tenants than most students assume. New York caps a deposit (plus any rent advance) at one month's rent, bans application and cleaning fees, caps a credit check at $20 and late fees at the lesser of $50 or 5%, and forfeits a landlord's right to keep any deposit not returned with an itemized statement within 14 days. In the City of Ithaca a landlord must give current tenants 120 days' written notice before renewing, showing or re-letting a unit, unless the tenant initialed a bolded first-page waiver. Ithaca opted into Good Cause Eviction in July 2024: increases above the lower of 10% or 5% plus CPI are presumed unreasonable, though buildings with a 2009-or-later certificate of occupancy are exempt for 30 years. Those City rules, the Certificate of Compliance system and the Cornell safety tool stop at the city line, so Town of Ithaca, Lansing and Cayuga Heights addresses are different. Cornell students after year one ride TCAT free only after 6 p.m. on weekdays and on weekends; the OmniRide pass is $200 a year and a B commuter parking permit $359.77.

**The five biggest decisions**
1. Whether to stay in Cornell or affiliated housing or go off campus, and if the latter, whether you are eligible and when to file the exemption (by the April housing-application deadline; April 6 in 2026).
2. Who to live with and how many: per-bed rent falls steeply with unit size, but every co-tenant is jointly liable for the whole rent.
3. Where: Collegetown (walk, sign early, pay most) versus downtown, Fall Creek, East Hill or Lansing (cheaper, later market, bus or car, 20 to 30 minute uphill walks from the flats).
4. When to sign: in the Collegetown rush of the fall before, or later in the wider market; weigh the 120-day waiver if a landlord asks for it.
5. What is included: heat, electricity, internet, trash, furniture, parking and lease length (10 or 12 months) change the real cost more than a $100 difference in rent.

**The five most common mistakes**
1. Signing a lease as a sophomore before an exemption is approved, or signing with a group formed in the first weeks of school that later shrinks, leaving the rest to pay.
2. Comparing a per-bed price with a per-unit price, or a 12-month rent with Cornell's academic-year rate.
3. Skipping the Certificate of Compliance and safety-tool check (the 2018 117 Thurston tenants found an expired certificate only after signing) and not documenting the unit with photos and the move-in inspection.
4. Paying fees the law bans (application, non-refundable or cleaning fees, deposits above one month) or initialing the 120-day waiver without asking why.
5. Assuming a lease can be walked away from: housemates stay liable, Cornell aid will not intervene, and sublets in small houses depend on the lease; the legal route is a written sublet request, an assignment, or the landlord's duty to re-rent.

### 2.1 Cornell's residential requirement and who may sign a lease

The two-year requirement decides who is in the off-campus market at all. First- and second-year undergraduates should not sign a lease without an approved exemption; a signed lease does not release a student from the requirement, and a non-compliant student is billed for the full year anyway.

## 4. Timeline
The off-campus year runs on two clocks at once: the lease year (most Ithaca leases end July 31 and the next one starts on or about August 1) and the leasing year for the lease after that, which for Collegetown opens about 10 to 16 months before move-in. A student who arrives in August for a first lease is already in the market for the second one.

### 4.2 How the rush has moved over time

Cornell's assistant dean said in 2013 the signing period had moved from January to late October or November, and the August-September push appeared only in the previous four to six years. In 2019 Off-Campus Living's manager said the rush had moved from November-December into the first days of fall semester and called "you must sign the day you arrive" a myth. Every dated signing in the Sun sample from 2017 to 2025 falls 10 to 16 months before move-in (section 10.6), and neither the 2013 60-day rule nor the 2022 120-day rule shows up as a change in that window. In 2013 a columnist described a split market: 12-month units go first, 10-month August units appear in October.

### 4.3 Spring start

- Spring entrants' free TCAT access runs to Dec 31 of the same year they start (fall entrants: Aug 31 of the following year).
- Spring transfers live on campus for the incoming spring semester and the following academic year, so a spring transfer's first off-campus lease is about 18 months away; no source describes spring-start off-campus leasing.
- Mid-year off-campus supply is mostly sublets and semester leases: Ithaca Renting lists "Spring/Fall Semester 2027" rows; the Ithaca College portal lists "Spring '27" roommate-matching leases (Foundation Housing); Dryden South had a Fall 2026 sublet at $1,395.
Sources: https://fcs.cornell.edu/departments/transportation-delivery-services/sustainable-transportation-options/bus-services-privileges-passes ; https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/campus-housing/new-transfer-undergraduates ; https://ithacarenting.com/collegetown/ ; https://offcampushousing.ithaca.edu/city/ithaca-ny/listing/spring-27-and-full-year-leases-available-for-roommate-matching-foundation-housing-96176 ; https://www.apartments.com/dryden-south-sublets-available-fall-2026-ithaca-ny/yeje6qs/

### 4.4 Transfers

- A sophomore transfer lives on campus for one academic year and is not exempt; a transfer entering as a junior or senior, or with two years of on-campus residence elsewhere, is exempt and may sign a lease, but the exemption must be requested each year.
- Admission decisions come long after the Collegetown rush has picked over the best stock; in 2018 transfers learned on June 22 whether they had campus housing and some were placed far away (Schuyler House).
- A transfer looking off campus in spring or summer is shopping in the leftover market (downtown "available now" units, sublets, Lansing and Northeast complexes).
Sources: https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/campus-housing/new-transfer-undergraduates ; https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/campus-housing/residential-policy ; https://www.cornellsun.com/article/2018/12/isolated-vulnerable-and-nervous-transfer-students-express-frustration-over-not-being-able-to-live-on-campus ; https://ithacarenting.com/downtown/

### 4.5 International arrivals

- Visa appointments cannot be more than 120 days before program start; leases must often be signed sight unseen before arrival.
- No Cornell page offers arrival-week temporary housing for students hunting for a lease.
- An SSN is not needed to rent, per Cornell International Services; guarantor and credit-history practice in Ithaca was not found.
- New international degree students get free TCAT rides for the first year.
- Students who cannot travel home during breaks may ask housing@cornell.edu about on-campus housing; the break question matters to anyone whose lease ends in May or July.
Sources: https://international.globallearning.cornell.edu/life-cornell/faq-international-students ; https://fcs.cornell.edu/departments/transportation-delivery-services/sustainable-transportation-options/bus-services-privileges-passes ; https://global.cornell.edu/resources/immigration-advice-and-resources

### 4.6 Graduate and professional students

- Grad leasing runs later than undergrad leasing: the grad guide says start looking March to April for August, and a few places remain June to July; a 2014 grad forum thread said June is not too late because year-long leases start at the end of July.
- Hasbrouck takes rolling applications on 12-month contracts.
- New grad students get free TCAT in year one; PhD students and graduate assistants get free OmniRide passes every year.
- Graduate assistants on full assistantships can buy faculty and staff parking permits.

## 5. Places and players
### 5.1 Neighborhoods

Cornell's neighborhoods page is the only official description, and it reuses the same paragraph for several areas (Fall Creek = Downtown = Commons; East Hill = Belle Sherman; Lansing = Northeast). Treat its distances and walk times as rough ranges. Whether an address is inside the City of Ithaca matters more than the neighborhood name: the 120-day rule, the Certificate of Compliance system, the Cornell safety tool, City parking permits, City trash tags and the City noise code all stop at the city line.

**Collegetown.** Directly south of central campus around College Ave, Dryden Rd, Eddy St, Linden Ave and Stewart Ave. Cornell: 0.1 to 0.5 miles, up to a 15-minute walk, lower Collegetown is uphill to campus; densest undergrad housing, which raises rent, noise and congestion; a mix of luxury and average complexes, multi-unit houses and room rentals. Prices: RentCafe 1BR $2,342 and 2BR $3,414 per unit (large buildings, Oct 2026); Ithaca Renting per-bed medians from $800 (6BR) to $2,025 (1BR) for 2027-28; The Ivy 3BR $1,833 to $1,933 per person; older houses $785 to $1,300 per person. Suits undergrads who want to walk, and groups of four or more who can share a big unit. Students say: closest and liveliest, signs earliest, many houses on steep hills, condition often poor for the price, a few agencies own the nicer College Ave buildings. Central Collegetown (R-3 zoning) has no residential street parking permits, per an older Cornell item; the Dryden Road Garage sells a $232 monthly permit "recommended for Collegetown residents". Sources: https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/campus-housing/housing-search-process/ithaca-neighborhoods ; https://www.rentcafe.com/average-rent-market-trends/us/ny/ithaca/collegetown/ ; https://ithacarenting.com/collegetown/ ; https://www.cornellsun.com/article/2025/09/maia-on-the-mic-the-collegetown-housing-hustle ; https://news.cornell.edu/node/278473 ; https://www.cityofithacany.gov/718/Dryden-Road-Garage

**Belle Sherman and East Hill.** East of Collegetown (Cornell's text also calls Belle Sherman "south of campus"), near East Hill Plaza and Judd Falls Plaza. Cornell: Belle Sherman 1 to 2 miles, East Hill 0.75 to 2 miles, both "15-minute level walk", which only fits the near end at a 3 mph pace (section 10.2). Quieter. The grad guide says East Hill's newer complexes (such as Maplewood) have in-unit laundry. Parts of East Hill lie outside the City in the Town of Ithaca, where the Town's family definition limits unrelated occupants. Sources: https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/campus-housing/housing-search-process/ithaca-neighborhoods ; https://reedgroup.github.io/GraduateStudentResources/LifeAtCornell.html ; https://law.justia.com/cases/new-york/other-courts/2017/2017-ny-slip-op-30471-u.html

**Cayuga Heights and Cornell Heights.** North of campus. Cornell: 1 to 4 miles, up to a 30-minute level walk; Cornell Heights has many fraternity and sorority houses (music, parties); Cayuga Heights is a quiet faculty area with limited walkable shopping. Cayuga Heights is a separate village, outside the City's 120-day rule. The grad guide calls it quiet and not walkable. Sources: https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/campus-housing/housing-search-process/ithaca-neighborhoods ; https://reedgroup.github.io/GraduateStudentResources/LifeAtCornell.html ; https://ecode360.com/27390826

**Fall Creek.** Below campus to the west and north, around Cascadilla Ave, Lake St, N Tioga St, E Falls St and N Cayuga St. Cornell: 0.5 to 1 mile, 20 to 30 minute uphill walk, frequent bus, lower rents, popular with grad students, subdivided houses and apartments over shops. Prices: 1BR listings $1,375 to $1,950 asking (709 N Tioga $1,600 to $1,700; 121 Cascadilla 400 sq ft $1,375 to $1,590); Rentable 1BR $1,600. Cliffside Terrace (405 Lake St) reviewers praise a quiet building with fast repairs and complain of limited bus and parking. The grad guide: shared houses, 10 to 15 minute bus, groceries need a bus or car. Cornell's page calls Fall Creek "west of campus" and "base of East Hill" in the same entry. Sources: https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/campus-housing/housing-search-process/ithaca-neighborhoods ; https://offcampus.housing.cornell.edu/city/ithaca-college-ny/listing/cosy-quiet-comfortable-fall-creek-gem.-238258 ; https://www.rentable.co/ithaca-ny/neighborhoods/fall-creek ; https://www.veryapt.com/ApartmentReview-a16145-cliffside-terrace-apartments-ithaca ; https://reedgroup.github.io/GraduateStudentResources/LifeAtCornell.html

**Downtown and The Commons.** West of campus at the bottom of the hill. Cornell: 0.5 to 1.5 miles (Commons 1 mile), 20 to 30 minute uphill walk. RentCafe downtown average $2,747 per unit and 1BR $2,742 (large new buildings, Nov 2026); Ithaca Renting's downtown per-bed medians are $1,395 studio, $1,625 1BR, $992.50 2BR, about a fifth to a bit over a quarter below its Collegetown prices (Collegetown runs 25% to 39% above downtown). **West Hill.** Across the inlet, 4 miles, rural, not practical on foot; cheaper; Cornell warns "cottages" are likely uninsulated with very high heating bills and suggests asking NYSEG (800) 572-1111 for average annual heating costs. ## 6. How it actually works
Each process below gives the official steps, then what students learn the hard way. Sources for every fact are in sections 2 to 5; URLs are repeated here only where a step depends on one page.

### 6.1 Confirming you are allowed to live off campus

1. Check the requirement. First-years and sophomores (full-time, Ithaca-based) must live in Cornell or affiliated housing. Juniors, seniors, junior or senior transfers, and students who are 21+, married, veterans, with dependents, or local commuters can be exempt. (https://scl.cornell.edu/residential-life/housing/campus-housing/residential-policy)
2. If you need an exemption, request it in the Housing application during the open window (spring and fall). Exemptions cannot be requested after you have signed a housing license. Supporting documents may be required.
3. Reapply every year: an exemption lasts one academic year.
4. Do not sign a lease before the exemption is approved. A sophomore who signs a Collegetown lease and is not exempt is billed for the full year of campus housing as well.
5. If you hold a Cornell license and decide to move off campus, cancellation as a returning junior or senior costs $50 through Jan 31, $500 Feb 1 to Mar 31, $1,000 Apr 1 to Jun 30, $2,000 Jul 1 to Aug 15, then 50% of the fall room rate. Moving off campus is not an extenuating circumstance for a spring release.

The hard part: the Collegetown rush for a junior-year lease happens in the fall of sophomore year, before most sophomores think about exemptions; the junior-year lease is a decision made around October of sophomore year.

### 6.2 Forming a group

1. Decide group size first: per-bed price falls as the unit gets bigger (Ithaca Renting 2027-28 medians: $2,025 for a 1BR alone, $1,377.50 per person in a 2BR, $1,246.25 in a 4BR, $800 in a 6BR).
2. Use Cornell's housemate questionnaire: study hours, noise, guests, cleanliness, smoking, alcohol, who handles money (student or parent), what happens if someone takes leave, transfers or studies abroad.
3. Understand joint and several liability before signing: on most local leases, each housemate owes the full rent and damages if another stops paying.
4. Find extra housemates through the Off-Campus Living email list (NetID), the Cornell marketplace, or the Ithaca College portal's roommate-matching listings.

The hard part: groups formed in the first weeks of sophomore fall often shrink before move-in (Dery 2020 wrote that large groups are likely to lose people and that individuals and small groups face higher prices; Snabes 2018 and Sun 2021 describe the roommate-search pressure). Big houses (8 to 14 people) are rarer than they were as new apartment buildings replace them, and a resident of a 14-person house (found through Ithaca Renting in February after Greek housing plans fell through) described it in 2018 as "falling apart at the seams," with squirrels in the roof. A transfer student's advice: spend about a week getting to know roommates before committing. Students with Greek ties report finding houses through chapter networks with a single email.

### 6.3 Finding listings

1. Cornell Off-Campus Housing Marketplace (College Pads, NetID or proof of affiliation needed; City listings must have a current COC).
2. Landlord websites: Ithaca Renting publishes full per-bed price lists; Modern Living, Student Agencies and others publish little or nothing.
3. Ithacarents.com (Landlords Association members only), the Sun, Ithaca Journal, Ithaca Times; Facebook groups, Craigslist and Apartments.com are named by the grad guide.
4. Ask whether a listed price is per bed or per unit, which utilities are included, the lease length (10 vs 12 months) and the start and end dates.

The hard part: aggregator sites block automated reads, the Cornell portal has many "Call For Price" and stale listings (one test listing with a 555 phone number), and many sites list one unit at three prices. In 2022 a landlord reportedly gave a unit to whoever produced a cash deposit first, and listings vanished mid-decision.

### 6.4 Screening a landlord and a building

1. City of Ithaca addresses: check the Certificate of Compliance (call 607-274-6508 or email ibd@cityofithaca.org) and the Cornell/City safety tool (sprinklers, alarms, construction type). Neither covers the Town of Ithaca, Lansing or Cayuga Heights.
2. Ask the landlord Cornell's questions: years in business, number of properties, who repairs, emergency contact, how long the last tenants stayed.
3. Call references (past tenants): repairs, deposit return, notice, whether they would rent again.
4. Visit in person, look past fresh paint, check two exits and smoke detectors, ask about heat source and NYSEG heating history.
5. Ask what share of tenants are undergrads (grad students looking for quiet do this).
6. For an occupancy over four unrelated people in the City, or more than two unrelated in the Town of Ithaca, ask whether the occupancy is legal.

The hard part: the 2018 tenants at 117 Thurston Ave learned only after signing that the certificate had expired in 2015. The safety tool rates fire safety, not landlord behavior. No current, independent review source exists for the big Collegetown landlords; veryapt has a handful of grad reviews for a few buildings.

### 6.5 Reading and signing the lease

1. Get every promise in writing; any term is negotiable before signing; a signed lease binds even before money changes hands (state assistant AG at a 2012 Collegetown meeting).
2. Look for the 120-day waiver: a bolded, initialed statement on page one beginning "As per Chapter 258-10(A)...". The rule's sponsor (Council member and Sun columnist Patrick Mehler) wrote that a discussion about why you are waiving the days should be your first question when asking about renting somewhere. The Sun's printed waiver line reads "By initialing here ____, I fully understand and willingly waive my rights to 120 days' written notice" (the "As per Chapter 258-10(A)" wording comes from a code snippet; confirm in the law lane).
3. Check: rent per person or per unit; due date; late fee (cannot exceed the lesser of $50 or 5% of rent, only after 5 days); deposit (cannot exceed one month's rent, including any "last month"); utilities; parking and its fee; snow removal and trash duties; sublet clause; renewal and auto-renew terms; occupancy limit; furniture inventory; who to call for emergencies.
4. Fees you should not pay: application or processing fees, non-refundable deposits, cleaning fees. A background check fee is allowed up to $20 or actual cost, and must be waived if you bring a check from the past 30 days.
5. Expect to pay first month's rent plus a deposit at signing (209 Stewart; Cityview). Cornell aid does not cover deposits, first or last month's rent, or summer rent.
6. Free lease review before signing: Cornell Law Tenants Advocacy hotline 607-301-1560; Off-Campus Living one-on-one consultations.
7. Get a written receipt for every payment.

The hard part: students report reviewing three leases during prelims. Leases for Ithaca Renting units are 12 months; some Collegetown units are 10 months (Aug 1 start). An oral lease over one year is unenforceable.

### 6.6 Before move-in

1. Accept the landlord's move-in inspection offer and sign the condition record; take dated photos of every room.
2. Utilities you pay: set up NYSEG ("Turn On Service" at nyseg.com or 800.572.1111); arrange internet if not included; buy City trash tags ($6 each) if trash is not included.
3. Renters insurance: buy a policy (GradGuard is Cornell's preferred insurer; Ithaca quotes found at about $5 to $27 a month depending on source); check whether the lease requires proof.
4. Parking: Cornell B commuter permit $359.77 (sales start in early August, first come); City residential permit $45 if your block is in a permit zone; Dryden Road Garage $232 a month.
5. Tell the landlord the name of any occupant not on the lease within 30 days.

The hard part: new buildings open late. Fontana in 2016 and 301 College Ave in 2026 both opened with students waiting or living in unfinished space. Ask what the plan and compensation are if the certificate of occupancy is late.

### 6.7 Repairs and complaints

1. Report the problem in writing (email or text with date, address, name); for proof, send certified mail.
2. Keep photos, dates and a log of every contact; ask the landlord for a timeframe.
3. If heat is below the legal minimum (68F by day when it is under 55F outside; 62F at night; Sept 15 to May 31 in Ithaca), say so in writing.
4. Bed bugs: report in writing; do not self-treat; the landlord must exterminate and notify neighboring units within 72 hours.
5. If ignored, file with code enforcement: City Building Division (607-274-6508, "Report a Concern" online), or the Town of Ithaca (607-273-1783) or Town of Lansing (607-533-7054). Ask for inspection reports in writing.
6. Get free advice (607-301-1560) before withholding rent or repair-and-deduct; withholding invites a nonpayment case.
7. After 30 days in the unit and a written notice, a tenant can petition the court for an order directing repairs (since January 2024).
8. A complaint is protected: a landlord who refuses to renew or raises rent unreasonably within a year may face a retaliation defense (not in owner-occupied buildings with fewer than four units).

The hard part: in 2018 it took 53 days from the first complaint to a fixed heater at 117 Thurston; the students who left said the Building Department could then only send a written warning. Penalties were stiffened in 2019. A Solidarity Slate candidate told the Sun in 2021 that college students are often afraid to call their landlord about repairs for fear of getting evicted (source says evicted, not non-renewed).

### 6.8 Renewal, non-renewal and rent increases

1. In the City, the landlord must give you at least 120 days' written notice before renewing your lease, showing the unit or signing new tenants, unless you initialed a first-page waiver.
2. Under state law, notice of non-renewal or a rent increase of 5% or more must come 30, 60 or 90 days ahead, depending on how long you have lived there; late notice keeps you on existing terms until the period runs.
3. Under Good Cause (City of Ithaca, from July 2024), a covered tenant has a right to renew; an increase above the lower of 10% or 5% plus CPI is presumed unreasonable. Exempt: buildings with a certificate of occupancy from 2009 on (for 30 years), owner-occupied buildings of 10 or fewer units, landlords with one unit, units above 345% of fair market rent, condos and co-ops, Cornell dorms.
4. A landlord's 226-c notice must say whether the unit is covered by Good Cause and, if not, why.
5. Auto-renewal clauses need a separate written reminder 15 to 30 days before your opt-out deadline.

The hard part: most new Collegetown buildings are post-2009 and so exempt from Good Cause until about 2039 or later. Students in the rush often face renewal decisions within weeks of moving in (reported in 2012 and 2022).

### 6.9 Subletting (summer, study abroad, internship)

1. Read the sublet clause. In a building with four or more units you have a legal right to sublet with consent that cannot be unreasonably withheld; in a house of three or fewer units the lease controls.
2. Send the request by certified mail, return receipt: term, subtenant's name and addresses, reason, your address during the term, co-tenant and guarantor consent, and a copy of the sublease.
3. The landlord may ask for more information within 10 days and must answer within 30; silence is consent.
4. Use a written sublease; you stay liable to the landlord for the subtenant's rent and damage.
5. Advertise free on the Cornell marketplace or the Off-Campus Living list (sublets were free to list in 2019).
6. Assignment (handing over the whole lease) ends your liability but the landlord may refuse it without cause.

The hard part: no student account of sublet scams or summer sublet prices in Ithaca was found; Modern Living advertised sublets "from $900 per person" and Dryden South a Fall 2026 sublet at $1,395.

### 6.10 Breaking a lease early

1. Ask the landlord for an early-termination agreement in writing with the move-out date and amount owed.
2. Try a sublet or an assignment first.
3. If you leave anyway, the landlord must make documented, good-faith efforts to re-rent at the lower of market or lease rent; once re-rented, you owe nothing more; the duty cannot be waived.
4. Your housemates remain jointly liable for your share unless the landlord releases you.
5. Domestic violence victims can end a lease on 30 days' notice; service members on active duty can too.
6. Cornell Financial Aid will not intervene in a mid-year lease break.

### 6.11 Moving out and getting the deposit back

1. Give notice if your lease requires it (none needed at the end of a fixed term).
2. Ask for a move-out inspection between two weeks and one week before the end; the landlord must give 48 hours' notice and then list intended charges, so you can fix them.
3. Two weeks out: confirm move-out time, review the lease, change your address. One week out: schedule NYSEG and internet shutoff, clean, give a forwarding address in writing.
4. Take dated photos of every room on the way out.
5. The landlord must return the deposit or an itemized statement within 14 days; missing the deadline forfeits the right to keep any of it. Deductions only for unpaid rent, damage beyond normal wear, unpaid utilities owed to the landlord, and removal of belongings left behind.
6. If money is withheld unfairly: demand letter, then CDRC mediation (607-273-9347), then small claims at Ithaca City Court (up to $5,000) or a complaint to the Attorney General. A willful violation can bring up to twice the deposit.
7. Do not leave furniture at the curb: the City bars couches, mattresses and appliances outdoors and trash goes out at most 24 hours before pickup with a $6 tag per bag.

The hard part: Cornell's own move-out page tells tenants cleaning charges will be deducted, while the Cornell Law guide says a separate cleaning fee is banned and only excessive cleaning beyond routine turnover cleaning may be deducted (see section 8).

### 6.12 Summer storage

1. Book a pickup service early: Big Red Shipping and Storage (Cornell's vendor, free pickup at apartments too, no published price), Storage Scholars ($172 a month "2025 Avg." on its site, not stated as Ithaca-specific; book by early April), Storage Squad.
2. Or rent a unit: Ithaca self-storage 10x10 averages about $93 (SpareFoot) to $153 (SelfStorage.com) a month; 4-Season Storage on Dryden Rd from $115.
3. A Cornell license offers no storage, so students moving between a dorm and an off-campus lease need a plan for June and July.
4. The alternative many leases offer is to keep the apartment and sublet it.

### 6.13 Getting to campus

1. First-year degree students (including new grads and transfers) ride TCAT free all year with a Cornell ID.
2. Everyone else rides free after 6 p.m. weekdays and all weekend; daytime weekday rides cost $1.50 unless you buy the $200 OmniRide pass at myparking.cornell.edu (PhD students and graduate assistants get it free).
3. Driving: register your plate with Transportation Services even without a permit; buy a B commuter permit ($359.77) in August if eligible; watch City odd/even parking Nov 1 to Apr 1 and the 24-hour and 72-hour rules.
4. Walking: Collegetown up to 15 minutes; Fall Creek and downtown 20 to 30 minutes uphill (Cornell's estimates).

The hard part: no buses 1:30 to 4:00 a.m.; route numbers per neighborhood are not confirmed; the free-ride glitch that let upperclass students ride 24/7 ended in Nov 2024. Students in 2023 said the pass fee and confusing routes put them off the bus.

### 6.14 When things go badly wrong

1. Displacement: if the City orders a building vacated, landlords owe displaced tenants double the monthly rent prorated per day (2019 rule). Do not sign a release for a cash payment without advice: Asteri residents were offered $2,000 to leave within five days, which a city official and the ITU called predatory.
2. Eviction notice: a 14-day rent demand comes first; a court petition must be served 10 to 17 days before the hearing; you can usually ask for one 14-day adjournment; a judge can stay an eviction up to a year for hardship; removal needs 14 days' notice by law enforcement.
3. Call LawNY (607-273-3667) or the Tenants Advocacy hotline (607-301-1560) on day one.
4. International students: a landlord dispute does not affect visa status, per Cornell Graduate School staff.

## 10. Original analysis

### 10.4 When the OmniRide pass pays off

### 10.5 Counting back 120 days

Question: if 120 days are counted back from the end of the current lease, what is the earliest a landlord could show, advertise or re-sign a unit?
Method: lease end date minus 120 days.
Example: current lease ends July 31, 2027. From April 2 to July 31: 28 days remaining in April after the 2nd + 31 (May) + 30 (June) + 31 (July) = 120. Result: April 2, 2027.
Check against the sponsor's examples: Mehler wrote that for a June 1 lease the renewal decision is due about October 1, and for August leases about December. 120 days before June 1 would be about February 1; his column words the example as 120 days after a lease starting June 1 (about Sept 29), so his dates count forward from the lease start, not back from its end, and are approximate (August leases: "until December"). Source: https://cornellsun.com/2022/04/18/mehler-student-tenants-rights-shifting-in-your-favor/ (checked 2026-11-08).
Contrast: on 2026-11-08 Ithaca Renting was already marketing 2027-28 units (https://ithacarenting.com/collegetown/), nine months before an August 2027 start. Possible explanations, none verified: vacant or new units with no current tenant, initialed waivers, buildings outside the City, or a different count start.
Result: no publishable "earliest date". The count start is unresolved until a person reads section 258-10 on eCode360 (https://ecode360.com/27390826).
