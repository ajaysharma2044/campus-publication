## 1. The topic in one page

**What everyone needs to know.** Cornell takes external transfers for the fall only, through the Transfer Common App, and you apply to one of its colleges, not to the university. For fall 2027 entry the application, the $85 fee (or waiver) and any portfolio are due March 15, 2027, and everything else (College Report, Academic Evaluation, Mid-Term Report, transcripts) by April 15; US aid forms (CSS Profile and FAFSA) and international aid forms are also due March 15. Decisions roll from April through June. You need 12 semester hours of college credit after high school or full-time enrollment, and two to four semesters done; first-semester students and bachelor's holders cannot apply. In fall 2025, 7,381 people applied, 864 were admitted (11.7%) and 625 enrolled, about 14% of the new class; the first-year rate was 8.4%. Admitted transfers are "generally" 3.3 or better, but the colleges differ: CALS sets a 3.0 minimum (3.5 for Biological Sciences), ILR's admits typically have 3.4, Human Ecology calls 3.5 competitive, Engineering states no minimum but will not review an application missing its math, physics and chemistry courses. Most colleges accept at most 60 transfer credits (Engineering 66, Architecture 64) and require 60 credits and usually four semesters at Cornell, so credits beyond 60 are lost. New transfers must live on campus for one academic year unless they lived on campus two years elsewhere or enter as juniors or seniors; a hall double with the Unlimited plan, tuition and fees bills $96,318 at 2026-27 endowed prices ($72,188 for a New York resident in a contract college), before aid that meets 100% of demonstrated need. There is no general guaranteed transfer program; the Transfer Option is an invitation to apply after a year elsewhere, not admission; the newest community college pathway (TC3 to CALS, 2025) gives "priority consideration," not a guarantee.

**The five biggest decisions.**
1. **Which college and major to apply to.** It sets the prerequisites, the GPA signal, the credit cap and the residence rule. Some doors are shut: Global and Public Health Sciences takes no transfers, Global Development takes sophomores only, AAP takes no external B.Arch. transfers for fall 2027, and Engineering admits you to one major you must keep for a year (2.6).
2. **Sophomore or junior entry.** At 15 credits a semester, a student who arrives with 90 credits loses 30 and spends ten semesters in college; up to 60 credits nothing is lost (10.4). Cornell discourages junior-year transfer from four-year schools; CALS bars students with more than four semesters at a four-year school.
3. **Which courses to take before applying.** Engineering's block, Human Ecology's 23 sophomore credits, Brooks's 15, ILR's lists and Hotel's microeconomics and writing course decide whether you are reviewed at all (2.6).
4. **Where to live.** The required year means auto-allocated upper-level housing from $12,644 (triple) to $16,595 (super single) a year, or a co-op double for $6,974 to $9,962, or a Program House you choose by name (3.6, 10.3). Exempt juniors face a Collegetown market leased up to 16 months ahead, where a large-building studio averages $2,006 a month (3.8).
5. **How to pay.** Need-based aid only, no merit aid; families under $125,000 with typical assets get tuition covered by grants, under $75,000 the full cost (2.15). International applicants who skip the March 15 aid deadline can never get Cornell aid.

**The five most common mistakes.**
1. **Planning for spring entry.** It is fall only now; competitor pages and Cornell's own housing page still mention spring transfers (8.1 #1).
2. **Treating "guaranteed" as guaranteed.** The Transfer Option, TC3 pathway and old SUNY agreements promise consideration, not admission (2.5, 2.20).
3. **Assuming credits and AP scores carry over.** Each college evaluates case by case after admission; AP credit your old school accepted is not automatic; exam credit needs a written request by the last day of instruction of your first Cornell semester (2.8).
4. **Missing the second deadline or the aid deadline.** March 15 is only for the application, fee and portfolio; April 15 is for the rest; aid forms are March 15 (2.2).
5. **Counting on a guaranteed room or a late lease.** Apply for housing within ten days of getting your NetID; the license cannot be cancelled by a new transfer; exempt students who wait until admission in April to June find Collegetown already signed (2.10, 6.5).

## 2. Fact base

Cornell University in Ithaca, New York. Not Cornell College in Mount Vernon, Iowa, whose transfer, scholarship, cost and orientation pages crowd the search results for the same queries (https://www.cornellcollege.edu/admissions/transfer-students/transfer-of-academic-credit.shtml). The open external transfer cycle on 2026-11-11 is fall 2027 entry: the aid page names aid year 2027-28 and the EOP page says "Fall 2027 applicants"; the admissions page itself states only "March 15" with no year.

### 2.5 The Transfer Option

## 4. Timeline

The cycle below is fall 2027 entry, the one open on 2026-11-11. Dates that Cornell has published for 2027 are marked as such; dates carried from the fall 2026 cycle are marked "(2026 pattern)" and must be rechecked when the 2027 versions appear. Sources are in sections 2 and 3.

### 4.1 The year before you apply (first college year elsewhere)

- **Fall of the first college year (September to December 2026).** You cannot apply from your first college semester (FAQ 330). This is the term to take the prerequisites your target college names: Engineering's MATH 1910, MATH 1920, PHYS 1112 with 1110 and CHEM 2090 with 2091 must be finished, in progress or planned by application time; Human Ecology wants 8 credits of lab science, 6 of writing, 3 of statistics, 3 of precalculus or calculus and 3 of intro psychology for sophomore entry; Brooks wants 15 credits across writing, micro, statistics or calculus and sociology; ILR wants composition, intro economics, math and a science-and-technology course. Transfer Option students take broad liberal arts courses and need a 3.5 every semester with no grade below B- (A&S).
- **October 1, 2026.** FAFSA opens for 2027-28; the CSS Profile for fall 2027 should be filed "as soon as possible" after this date (US and international aid pages).
- **Fall 2026 at Cornell (for comparison).** Instruction began Aug 24; add deadline Sep 8; drop deadline Oct 19; last day of instruction Dec 7.
- **November.** Current Cornell students pre-enroll for spring in November, which is why spring 2023 transfers found classes already full (Sun 2023). Hotel internal-transfer office hours November 18, 2026.
- **December 1, 2026.** Internal-transfer (current Cornell students) deadlines for spring entry: Hotel and AAP. Also the last day to cancel a spring housing license without penalty for leave, study away or withdrawal.

### 4.2 Application season (January to April 2027)

- **January 2027.** Spring semester at Cornell begins Jan 25, 2027. The Transfer Option page label says "Application Available Winter 2027." Hotel internal-transfer decisions for spring arrive early January. SUNY Broome to SCE BPS applications open January 2027 (separate online program).
- **Spring term at your current college.** Collect the College Report (your registrar or dean completes it), ask one instructor of a full-credit course for the Academic Evaluation, and plan the Mid-Term Report, which you complete yourself with official or estimated grades.
- **March 15, 2027.** Hard deadline for the Transfer Common App, the $85 fee or waiver, and any portfolio or design supplement (AAP via SlideRoom, Landscape Architecture via SlideRoom, Fashion and DEA design supplements). Also the deadline for the CSS Profile and FAFSA for US applicants and for the CSS Profile for international applicants; an international applicant who misses it can never get Cornell undergraduate aid. ILR wants the Mid-Term Report by now if possible. Transfer Option applications were also due March 15 in 2026 (2026 pattern).
- **Late March to early April.** Spring break at Cornell Mar 27 to Apr 4, 2027 (relevant for campus visits).
- **Early April 2027.** Official aid offers "tentatively early April" for those already admitted; 2027-28 prices are confirmed before then.
- **April 15, 2027.** Deadline for all other required materials, including the Mid-Term Report and recommendations.
- **Mid-April onward.** Transfer Option decisions roll from mid-April, most in late April or early May (2026 pattern). Regular transfer decisions begin "in April" on a rolling basis (FAQ 427).

### 4.3 Decisions and commitment (late April to July 2027)

- **Late April through June.** Regular transfer decisions (admissions page). Hotel decides in May and June. The CDS gives a notification date of June 15 and a reply date of July 1. No enrollment deposit deadline was found on any page.
- **Right after admission.** Cornell Aid Estimate for aid applicants. Engineering admits send the course description form with the Enrollment Response Form. AAP admits have two weeks to send syllabi and coursework. A&S produces a tentative credit evaluation only after the deposit.
- **Within ten days of getting a NetID.** Apply for housing through the Housing and Dining Portal (no fee).
- **May 1, 2027 (2026 pattern).** Internal-transfer deadline for fall at Hotel, AAP and A&S (current Cornell students only).
- **Early May.** Family orientation information arrives.
- **Early to mid-June (2024 pattern).** Colleges email admitted transfers: AAP, Brooks, ILR, CALS early to mid-June; Engineering from early June; Human Ecology asks students to verify their major June 8 to 30.
- **Mid-June.** Housing confirmations or a place on the guaranteed waitlist with weekly updates; room swaps mid-June to mid-July. June 15 is the no-penalty cancellation date for a fall housing license when leaving for leave or deferral (2026 pattern).
- **Early July.** Fall tuition bill (2024 example: generated July 12, due August 7). Fall housing billed July or August; meal plans billed in July.
- **July.** Dyson transfers meet an advisor to review credit evaluations.
- **July 31 (2026 pattern).** Student Health Plan waiver deadline for fall new admits; after it, waivers lock.

### 4.4 Arrival and first semester (August to December 2027)

- **August 1.** Last day to change the move-in timeslot; SHP coverage for new fall students starts Aug 1.
- **Mid-August (2026 pattern: Aug 17 first-years, Aug 18 transfers).** Transfer move-in, family orientation, New Student Orientation (Aug 18 to 23 in 2026) with transfer small groups, college programs, Community at Cornell, Cornell's Caring Community, Orientation Leader meetings. Convocation was Aug 18, 2026 at Schoellkopf Field. Engineering ran a transfer resource fair, advising drop-in, optional MATH CASE (Aug 20, 2026, Olin 155) and a Transfer Welcome Lunch.
- **Before classes.** CASE exams in Chemistry, Computer Science, Mathematics and Physics; online language placement. Clear the Re-Entry Term Checklist or face an enrollment hold.
- **First week of classes (Aug 24 in 2026).** Most transfers enroll during add/drop because their evaluations finish late. Popular classes may be full.
- **First six weeks.** Tatkon Block Party and CU Downtown.
- **Add deadline (Sep 8 in 2026), drop and grading-basis deadline (Oct 19 in 2026).** Verify the schedule before each.
- **Fall break (Oct 10 to 13 in 2026), Thanksgiving break (Nov 25 to 29 in 2026).**
- **Last day of instruction (Dec 7 in 2026).** Deadline for external transfers to request AP, IB or other exam credit in writing from the college registrar, with scores already sent (code 2098).
- **December.** Spring housing billed. Students who must stay only one year on campus face the off-campus lease market, where desirable Collegetown leases can be signed up to 16 months before move-in (Sun, 2018), so the search for year two often starts in the first fall.

### 4.5 When prices and rules change

- Tuition and housing and dining rates are set by the Board of Trustees in late March (2026-27 parameters were announced March 23, 2026; 2025-26 on March 27, 2025). Increases were 3.76% for 2026-27, and 4.22% (endowed) and 4.24% (NY contract) for 2025-26.
- Student Health Plan rates are published per year (2025-26 $4,020; 2026-27 $4,092).
- College transfer guidelines are updated in late winter (ILR's fall 2026 guidelines were updated 2/10/26); Engineering's requirements page carries an admissions-cycle label (2026-27).
- Rule changes visible on current pages: the residential requirement covers transfers arriving fall 2022 on; Engineering's PE and water competency rule covers fall 2025 entrants on; AAP closed external B.Arch. transfers for fall 2027.

### 4.6 How it shifts

- **Spring start.** External transfer is fall only for fall 2027 entry. Spring entry existed until recently (72 spring 2023 transfers; 88 spring 2021; ILR listed an October 15 spring deadline in 2022) and older pages and competitors still show it. If spring entry ever returns, the housing page's rule would apply: live on campus for the incoming spring plus the next academic year, and the SHP would cost $2,046 for the six spring months. Spring arrivals in 2023 could not add classes until mid-January and found many full. Do not confuse with the First Year Spring Admit program, which is for first-years.
- **Internal transfer (already at Cornell).** A separate track with its own deadlines: December 1 for spring and May 1 for fall at Hotel and AAP; A&S used May 1, 2026. Students who entered Cornell as external transfers can move college only after two semesters at Cornell (A&S) and are not eligible for Hotel's internal process.
- **International applicant.** Same March 15 and April 15 dates. The CSS Profile is due March 15 too, with generally no fee waiver, and missing it ends any chance of Cornell aid. After admission, an F-1 or J-1 record must be transferred to Cornell (Global Learning pages exist; timing not read). 14% tax withholding applies to aid above tuition and fees unless a treaty applies.
- **Graduate student.** Not covered by this topic: graduate and professional transfers follow their own programs (the DVM, for example, needs at least five semesters at Cornell). A bachelor's holder cannot transfer into a second Cornell bachelor's.
- **Exempt junior or student with two years of residence elsewhere.** No on-campus requirement, so housing becomes a private lease decision made after an April to June admission, when much of Collegetown is already leased.

## 6. How it actually works

### 6.1 Deciding whether and when to apply

1. **Check that you are a transfer at all.** You need a high school diploma or equivalent and either 12 semester hours of college credit earned after high school (exam credit does not count) or full-time enrollment elsewhere. Dual-enrollment students still in high school apply as first-years. A bachelor's holder cannot apply. A first-semester college student cannot apply yet (FAQ 330), so the earliest realistic application is in the spring of the first college year, for sophomore entry.
2. **Pick the year.** Cornell takes students who have finished two to four semesters elsewhere. The arithmetic favors sophomore entry: most colleges cap transfer credit at 60 and require at least 60 credits and usually four semesters at Cornell. A student who arrives with 90 credits still needs four Cornell semesters and ends up with ten semesters of college (section 10.4). Admissions itself discourages junior-year transfers from four-year schools (FAQ 323). CALS bars students with more than four semesters at a four-year school, and Hotel bars current juniors.
3. **Pick the college, because that is what you apply to.** There is no university-wide transfer admission: you apply to one college and usually one major, and that college reads your file and later evaluates your credit. Several majors are closed or narrowed: CALS Global and Public Health Sciences takes no transfers; Global Development takes sophomores only; AEM applies through Dyson; AAP will not take external B.Arch. transfers for fall 2027. Engineering admits you to one major you must keep for a year.
4. **Check the prerequisites before the spring term starts.** Each college lists required courses (Engineering's math, physics and chemistry block; Human Ecology's 23 credits for sophomores; Brooks's 15; ILR's sophomore and junior lists; Hotel's foreign language, chemistry, precalculus, microeconomics and Knight-standard writing course; CALS's major-specific foundations). Engineering will not review an application missing any required core course. Courses can be in progress or planned at application time for Engineering and CALS.
5. **Judge your grades against the right number.** The university says admitted transfers are "generally" 3.3 or better. College signals differ: CALS minimum 3.0 (3.5 Biological Sciences), ILR typical 3.4 or higher, Human Ecology competitive at 3.5 with mostly As and Bs, Brooks more competitive at 3.5 with As and B+s in prep courses, AAP minimum 2.7, Engineering states no minimum. Pass/fail courses are a problem: CALS and Human Ecology want letter grades.
6. **Plan the money question in parallel.** US applicants file the CSS Profile and FAFSA by March 15; aid is need-based only. International applicants must file the CSS Profile by March 15 or lose Cornell aid for good, and admission is need-aware for them.

The parts students learn the hard way:
- The College Report is not a recommendation; it is a standing form your school's official fills out. The Mid-Term Report is filled out by you, not by a professor.
- The March 15 date covers only the application, fee and portfolio. Everything else can arrive by April 15, but the aid forms are due March 15.

- Writing seminars are the hardest courses to transfer, according to a 2016 forum poster; introductory biology and psychology were easier (voice, College Confidential).
- Do not contact faculty during admissions (Human Ecology tips page).

### 6.3 The Transfer Option route

1. **Get the offer.** Only some first-year applicants receive a Transfer Option instead of admission. It names a college. It is an invitation to apply, not admission, and cannot be deferred (A&S). Its central page does not say it is guaranteed.
2. **Do the year elsewhere.** A&S expects two full-time semesters or three quarters at a regionally accredited degree-granting school (summer does not count), in broad liberal arts and sciences, not business, engineering or pre-professional programs, with at least one writing-intensive course, a 3.5 GPA each semester and no grade below B-. A&S cannot pre-approve courses. Other colleges set their own conditions on pages no lane opened; a consultant says most CALS Transfer Option majors need 3.0 and Biological Sciences 3.5 (voice, Koppelman 2023).
3. **Apply through the Transfer Option application, not the regular one.** Use your earlier portal credentials. No fee. Due March 15 (2026 pattern). Send all college transcripts, the final high school transcript, the College Report, the Mid-Term Report and college extras (CALS: resume, course schedule, AP scores, two essays; Human Ecology: resume, AP scores, 600-word essay, design supplement; SC Johnson: resume; ILR: AP scores; A&S: none). File aid forms by March 15.
4. **Decision.** Rolling from mid-April, most by late April or early May; some wait for final grades.
5. **What students learn the hard way.** Students and consultants have described it as guaranteed and as not guaranteed; posters in 2020 cited a CALS FAQ saying applicants who miss requirements are reviewed with the regular pool; a 2025 IC-to-Cornell transfer feared the second application would be as hard as the first. TO admits often find their first friends in a Discord or group chat of other TO students over the summer.

### 6.4 After admission: credit evaluation

1. **Accept and deposit.** No deposit deadline was found; the CDS gives a July 1 reply date. A&S issues its tentative credit evaluation only after acceptance and deposit.
2. **Send what your college asks for.** Engineering: course description form with the Enrollment Response Form, detailed syllabi and a certified transcript. AAP: syllabi and sample coursework within two weeks of the offer (portfolio for studio courses). Hotel: final transcript to the Hotel registrar and syllabi for core credit. Course content hours, assessments and textbooks are what A&S compares.
3. **Final transcript.** Evaluations finish only after the final spring transcript, which is why transfers usually build schedules during add/drop.
4. **How a course is judged.** The Cornell department that owns the matching course decides from the syllabus whether it is about 80% similar; two outside courses can combine into one Cornell course. Minimum grade C (C- fails in Engineering); a major may demand up to B. Pass/fail counts only with a written statement of C or better (A&S), and not at all in CALS or Human Ecology. Courses seven or more years old are generally out. Community college and online courses cannot be refused for being community college or online.
5. **What never transfers.** Orientation, study skills, career development, math below precalculus, remedial and ESL courses, MOOCs, CLEP (A&S), military and work experience, credit a prior school gave for its own departmental exam, internships outside an approved program.
6. **Exam credit is a separate request.** AP, IB and A-Level credit accepted by your old school does not carry over automatically; Cornell's own tables apply (Engineering needs 5s on AP Calculus BC, Chemistry and Physics C Mechanics; ILR needs a 5 on AP Microeconomics where your first school took a 4; Hotel takes AP Micro 5 for HADM 1410 but not AP Statistics; Dyson gives no AP or IB credit for calculus, micro or statistics per a 2019 file; Brooks takes IB Higher Level only). Send scores (College Board code 2098) and make a written request to the college registrar by the last day of instruction of your first Cornell semester. Where exam and transfer credit overlap, only the transfer course counts.
7. **Caps.** 60 credits in most colleges, including exam credit (CALS: AP up to 15 within 60; ILR: AP up to 12 within 60; Hotel: AP, IB, GCE up to 15 within 60). Engineering: 33 per full-time year, 66 total. Architecture: 64. Summer and part-time courses taken elsewhere, high school courses and courses taken after the offer are treated under current-student rules in A&S and cannot fill requirements.
8. **What the transcript shows.** Only the name of the prior institution and the total transfer credits; the transfer GPA does not enter the Cornell GPA.
9. **Check the degree audit.** A spring 2023 transfer said her advisor told her biology credits should count but her DUST record did not yet show them (Sun 2023). Keep syllabi until the audit is right.
10. **Later outside courses.** Once enrolled, get prior approval before taking courses elsewhere (registrar); Engineering allows winter transfer credit only with fewer than 6 Cornell credits that term and summer with fewer than 12; A&S allows up to 12 summer credits and no simultaneous outside enrollment in fall or spring. The physics department wants credit requested before you enroll in the outside course.

### 6.5 Housing for a new transfer

1. **Find out whether the rule applies to you.** New transfers live on campus for one academic year. You are exempt if you already lived on campus for two years at your previous school or if you matriculate as a junior or senior. Students with first-year standing may land in first-year halls. Individual exemptions are requested through the housing application, one year at a time.
2. **Apply within ten days of receiving your NetID** on the Housing and Dining Portal. No fee and no deposit. State room type (single, double, triple or quad), roommate preferences, interest in a female-only community or gender-inclusive housing. Register with Student Disability Services first if you need accessible housing.
3. **Choose whether to try for a Program House.** These are the only places you choose by name; everything else is random auto-allocation from your preferences. Over 500 first-year and transfer students live in Program Houses.
4. **Wait for mid-June.** You get a room or a place on the guaranteed waitlist with weekly updates. Sign the license, then pick a move-in slot (changeable until August 1). Room swaps run mid-June to mid-July. In 2026-27 the system was full and not taking room change requests.
5. **Know the license is binding.** New transfers cannot cancel unless they leave Cornell or win an exemption. Leave, deferral or withdrawal cancellations are free until June 15 (fall) or December 1 (spring) in the 2026-27 license, then cost $500.
6. **Expect the meal plan.** Hall residents are auto-enrolled: Unlimited ($7,604) or House ($7,604, West Campus), or the Collegetown plan ($7,056) in the named South Campus halls, plus a $50 fee. Billed July and January.
7. **Expect the bill.** Fall housing appears on the July or August Bursar bill, spring in December. A double runs $13,744 for the year.
8. **What students learned the hard way, in earlier years.** In 2016 and 2013 transfers were placed in converted North Campus lounges, some five to a room, and told only on move-in day it was temporary; placement went by application date and some had applied after the July 1 deadline then in force. In 2018 transfers learned on June 22 whether they would be housed, some admitted in summer had ten days to apply, and Cornell advised off-campus options. No report of lounge placements since the 2022 North Campus expansion was found.
9. **Year two.** After the required year you are free to rent. Desirable Collegetown leases sign up to 16 months ahead, so the search for year two begins during the first fall.

### 6.6 Financial aid for a transfer

1. **US applicants.** File the CSS Profile (2098) and FAFSA (002711) after October 1 and by March 15; NY residents also file with HESC. Fee waiver for CSS is automatic up to AGI $100,000. Upload IDOC documents only when asked.
2. **Undocumented applicants** who graduated from a US high school are considered for Cornell aid but not federal aid.
3. **International applicants.** CSS Profile by March 15; generally no CSS fee waiver; admission is need-aware; missing the deadline ends eligibility for the whole undergraduate career.
4. **EOP and HEOP.** Same forms plus a HEOP Transfer Certification form (AAP, A&S, Engineering, SC Johnson with Nolan) or an email to stateprograms@cornell.edu (CALS, Human Ecology, ILR, Brooks, Dyson). Fall 2027 applicants use 2025 tax documents.
5. **Estimate, then offer.** A Cornell Aid Estimate arrives soon after admission; the official offer comes tentatively in early April for those already admitted. Respond to document requests fast so the estimate arrives before you must commit.
6. **What to expect.** No merit aid. Families up to $125,000 with typical assets get grants covering tuition; up to $75,000, grants and work-study cover the full cost of attendance (2026-27). In fall 2021, 32.7% of transfers received Cornell need-based grants averaging $44,712.
7. **What students learned the hard way.** In spring 2021 admitted first-years and transfers had to commit by May 3 before many aid offers arrived, some students waited on the phone more than two hours and one described her offer as mostly loans (Sun 2021; the vice provost for enrollment cited volume and the pandemic). The finaid URLs that searches still return for transfers (apply-aid/transfer-students, apply-aid/prospective-applicants) are dead.
8. **Health insurance is extra.** The Student Health Plan ($1,705 fall plus $2,046 spring for a fall 2026 entrant) is outside the cost of attendance. Waive it with comparable coverage by the waiver deadline (July 31 in 2026) or it is billed.

### 6.7 Orientation and the first weeks

1. **Move-in.** Transfers moved in the day after first-years in 2026 (Aug 18). NSO ran Aug 18 to 23.
2. **Required programs.** College programs, Community at Cornell, Orientation Leader meetings, Cornell's Caring Community. Optional events can be skipped by non-traditional students. Transfers get small groups and socials of their own.
3. **College-specific events.** Engineering's 2026 program: resource fair, family briefing, advising drop-in, optional MATH CASE, Transfer Welcome Lunch. Spring 2023 transfers said college-specific orientation socials helped.
4. **Enrollment.** Activate NetID and Two-Step Login, clear the Re-Entry Term Checklist, build a schedule in Class Roster and Scheduler, enroll at your appointment, then check again before the add and drop deadlines. Pre-enrolled courses that cannot be dropped show a greyed box; ask your registrar.
5. **Placement.** CASE exams before term; online language placement.
6. **First six weeks.** Tatkon Block Party and CU Downtown; academic advisor; Learning Strategies Center; Writing Centers; CAPS.

### 6.8 Community college route

1. **Know the base rates.** No preference by school type; about a quarter of transfers come from community colleges (FAQ), while 2021 counts give 14.2% and the Chronicle says about 100 are admitted from community colleges a year.
2. **Choose courses that map.** Equivalency cannot be denied because a course is from a community college or online, but it must be about 80% similar, letter-graded C or better, and from a regionally accredited institution. Chemical Engineering warns some sophomore courses are not available at most two-year colleges.
3. **Associate degree and standing.** In CALS an associate's degree or four semesters means junior standing, and junior-eligible students cannot choose sophomore entry.
4. **Use a pathway if one fits.** TC3's farming A.A.S. to CALS Agricultural Sciences (priority consideration, 3.0, B or better). SUNY Broome business A.S. to the online SCE BPS (from 2027). Older CALS agreements with SUNY Broome (2017) and SUNY Orange (2014) exist but their current status is unverified. Pathways to Success (2006 to 2008) is of unknown status.
5. **Apply like everyone else.** Same March 15 and April 15 dates, same materials.
6. **Supports after arrival.** A CALS transfer student success navigator (similar roles in ILR and Human Ecology), PAVER and the CC STEP group (Chronicle 2024).

### 6.9 Moving colleges after you arrive

External transfers in A&S can apply to another college only after two semesters at Cornell and must still meet A&S's residence rule; Hotel's internal process excludes students who came from other institutions. Internal deadlines are December 1 (spring) and May 1 (fall) at Hotel and AAP. A&S's 2026 internal process required a 3.0 cumulative GPA and 3.0 in 12 credits in the application semester. Applications go through internaltransfer.cornell.edu/apply. Source: https://as.cornell.edu/admissions/admissions-requirements-and-process ; https://business.cornell.edu/admissions/undergraduate/bs-hotel-administration/application-requirements/

### 7.2 Housing (9 sources, 2008 to 2025)

- **2008.** After Cornell's transfer program house ended, transfers were placed across North Campus, Collegetown and West Campus (Sun headline, https://www.cornellsun.com/article/2008/09/without-transfer-center-students-placed-on-north-in-c-town-and-on-west).
- **2009.** A Student Assembly Committee on Transfer Affairs survey (527 responses) found 88% of former transfer-house residents rated their first-year living positive against 36.7% after the house closed; 26.4% of West Campus transfers (41.9% of townhouse residents) said housing hindered their transition; 89.3% of former house residents called a transfer program essential against 51% of later arrivals. It recommended an optional transfer house and Schuyler House as a transfer center; a Campus Life vice president said the office had looked at the survey but had no answer to it yet (https://cornellsun.com/2009/04/24/study-finds-transfers-feel-marginalized-campus).
- **2013.** Transfers cramped in converted lounges (headline only, https://cornellsun.com/2013/08/29/transfers-cramped-in-converted-lounges-at-cornell).
- **2016.** About 30 transfers began fall 2016 in converted lounges in Jameson and High Rise 5 (7 of 10 lounges), some five to a lounge; 10 were still there at reporting time. They said they were told on move-in day it was temporary, the rooms were cramped with shared bathrooms, and it was hard to meet peers because North was all first-years. Cornell said lounge conversion had been used in 8 of the previous 9 years and placement went by application date (https://cornellsun.com/2016/10/03/transfer-students-forced-to-live-in-north-campus-lounges/). A Sun editorial that week urged Cornell to admit it lacked housing for transfers (https://cornellsun.com/2016/10/05/editorial-building-a-better-cornell).
- **2018.** Transfers learned only on June 22 whether they would be housed; some admitted in summer had ten days to apply; Cornell advised off-campus options and later arranged traditional rooms, some on West Campus or in Schuyler House. Eitan Wolf '21 in Schuyler described isolation, a walk of nearly a mile to central campus and a 7-meal plan that kept him from eating with friends on West; Lauren Jung '21 felt nervous living off campus; Student Assembly transfer representative Catherine Huang '21 called housing one of the biggest transfer issues (https://www.cornellsun.com/article/2018/12/isolated-vulnerable-and-nervous-transfer-students-express-frustration-over-not-being-able-to-live-on-campus).
- **2019.** The same representative suspected first-year housing problems drove some transfers to leave (https://www.cornellsun.com/article/2019/03/guest-room-transfer-in-transfer-out); Student Assembly R47 asked for guaranteed centralized transfer housing.
- **2025.** A Class of 2028 sophomore transfer called upper-level selection stressful and chaotic: a 7:30 p.m. slot on the last day left only Clara Dickson triples and Sheldon Court doubles; they looked off campus but did not want a lease (https://www.cornellsun.com/article/2025/11/new-changes-to-fall-housing-selection-leave-some-rising-juniors-seniors-disappointed). An IC-to-Cornell transfer wrote that the unlimited meal plan and residency requirement keep students on campus (https://www.cornellsun.com/article/2025/08/from-both-sides-of-the-hill-an-ic-transfers-perspective).
- **Positive.** A Human Ecology transfer recommended West Campus house dinners (https://hecec.human.cornell.edu/?p=8161).
- **Gap.** No first-hand account of a transfer's assigned housing after the 2022 residency rule was found.

### 7.3 Social life and belonging (10 sources)

- Fall 2020 transfers felt disconnected: Zoom fatigue, no dorm community like first-years, scattered across campus; a transfer GroupMe had 350-plus members; an ILR Transfer Option student from Penn State and a student from Maryland urged messaging classmates and stepping outside one's comfort zone; Will Bodenman '23 arrived "not knowing a soul" in the Sun's phrasing (https://cornellsun.com/2020/12/09/lack-of-connection-marks-transition-to-cornell-for-transfers/).
- Rachel Eisenhart, an ILR transfer of the Class of 2027, wrote that transfers repeat freshman small talk ("where are you from?"), feel like small fish again, find it awkward to explain they have not started at Cornell yet, sit in limbo between first-years and experienced students, and miss admitted-student celebrations. Her advice: reuse lessons from the first year, keep old friends while making new ones, visit Ithaca in the summer (Cascadilla Gorge, Collegetown coffee, a campus tour). She says you feel like a regular sophomore within weeks (https://www.cornellsun.com/article/2025/05/a-letter-to-incoming-transfer-students-what-no-one-tells-you).
- Alexia Kim, a Human Ecology sophomore and guaranteed transfer from Case Western, described culture shock, homesickness and a campus so big she still needed maps, calling it "Freshman Year 2.0," along with gratitude and perspective (https://cornellsun.com/2019/05/02/kim-what-it-means-to-be-a-transfer-at-cornell/).
- Lane Rosen, a Hotel School transfer after two years at the Culinary Institute of America, listed loneliness because first-years bond early, but found others who knew no one became friends, plus huge club choice and alumni networking (Point Foundation, 2018, snippet only, https://pointfoundation.org/10-things-learned-transferring-cornell-university/).
- Eve Iulo, who entered as a transfer, wrote about imposter feelings in a 100-plus student lecture after a hometown class of 73, and argued everyone feels it (https://www.cornellsun.com/article/2023/11/persona-non-grata-actually-you-do-belong).
- Four 2024 community-college graduates profiled by Cornell described feeling behind and imposter feelings, having worked 40-plus hours a week, and assuming Cornell was too expensive (a counselor called that a common misconception) (https://www.news.cornell.edu/stories/2024/05/community-college-transfers-flourish-cornell).
- Transfer Option admits found their first friends in a large Discord of other TO students over the summer; another student credited Big Red Welcome Fest (admissions Q&A, snippet, https://admissions.cornell.edu/node/6748). A 2026 TO admit asked r/Cornell for a group chat; a 24-year-old non-traditional transfer asked how welcoming Cornell is; commenters said Reddit is more negative than the real school and advised joining clubs (Reddit mirrors, https://reddit.sentinel-team.org/posts/1tqfc4f/snapshots/2026-05-28T22%3A58%3A35.021264Z).
- Jessica Silverman (UCLA to Human Ecology) recommended the Tatkon Center, CUeLinks and West Campus house dinners (https://hecec.human.cornell.edu/?p=8161).
- An admissions blog writer was surprised how welcoming people were after orientation (search summary of https://admissions.cornell.edu/community/blog/what-surprised-me-a-sophomore-transfer-student-and-college-student-general).

### 7.4 Academics and credits (4 sources plus adjustment pieces)

- Spring 2023 transfers worried about closed classes at add/drop (current students had pre-enrolled in November), graduation timing, a degree audit not yet showing their credits, rigor and balance. Abbey Yang '25 said her advisor told her biology credits should count but DUST did not yet show them; one student planned 18 credits; Cynthia Cui '25 found Cornell more rural and much colder than UC Santa Barbara; another student compared the spread-out campus layout to Buffalo; college-specific orientation socials helped (https://cornellsun.com/2023/01/26/spring-transfers-start-cornell-journey-mid-year-and-prepare-for-challenges-ahead/).
- A 2016 UVA-to-Cornell poster said Cornell did not ask for syllabi because it had taken students from the same school, warned writing seminars were harder to transfer, and found intro biology and psychology easier; hedged and secondhand (https://talk.collegeconfidential.com/t/cornell-transfer-courses/1840413).
- An article by two Her Campus writers says Cornell reviewed the author's syllabi and approved most of her classes after a Transfer Option year (the article gives no entry year; it points to fall 2019; https://www.hercampus.com/school/cornell/transfer-option-blessing-disguise/).
- Alexia Kim cited organic chemistry prelims and 12-page essays as the rigor jump (2019).
- Silverman described switching from UCLA's quarter system to semesters.

### 7.5 The Transfer Option (6 sources)

- College Confidential, decision night March 26 and 27, 2020: posters disagreed on whether the TO is guaranteed; some cited a CALS FAQ saying those who miss requirements are reviewed with regular transfer applicants; one cited a 3.0 rule without a source; one doubted spending a year elsewhere; waitlist-to-TO was reported as rare (https://talk.collegeconfidential.com/t/cornell-class-of-2024-transfer-option/2087946).
- Richard Ballard, who spent his first year at Ithaca College on the TO and is now a CALS junior, said the TO was described to him inconsistently as guaranteed and then not, and he feared the second application would be as hard (https://www.cornellsun.com/article/2025/08/from-both-sides-of-the-hill-an-ic-transfers-perspective).
- Koppelman Group (consultant) said most CALS TO majors need 3.0 and Biological Sciences 3.5, and that it has never seen one of its own students denied after completing all the requirements; it drew denial reasons from Reddit and flagged them as uncertain (https://www.koppelmangroup.com/blog/2023/7/3/using-the-cornell-transfer-option-read-this-first). Its 2022 post said anecdotal evidence suggests most get in with required courses and a 3.3 (https://www.koppelmangroup.com/blog/2022/7/11/what-is-the-cornell-transfer-option).
- A Reddit commenter said the TO application needs the full Common App again with slight changes (mirror, https://reddit.sentinel-team.org/posts/1qbvg0g/snapshots/2026-01-16T10%3A01%3A13.578438Z).
- William Wang (CALS sophomore, 2017) compared applying as a transfer to an audition with no guarantee (https://cornellsun.com/2017/10/23/wang-the-improbable-path-of-a-cornell-transfer).
- A 2011 Sun piece reported Cornell administrators disputing a New York Times article on "guaranteed" transfer (https://cornellsun.com/2011/05/04/cornell-administrators-dispute-new-york-times-transfer-article/; title only).

### 7.6 Money (4 sources)

- Spring 2021: admitted first-years and transfers had to commit by May 3 before aid offers arrived; some students waited on the phone more than two hours; one student described her offer as mostly loans; the vice provost for enrollment explained the delay as a higher volume of students and changing financial circumstances in the pandemic; late applicants were offered extensions (https://cornellsun.com/2021/05/14/admitted-students-await-financial-aid-offers-weeks-after-commitment-deadlines/).
- February 2017: an admissions and financial aid working group, which said it was weighing nine possible options, listed need-aware transfer admission among its recommendations and described them as contingencies; a transfer on aid called it a "terrible policy" (a former Student Assembly officer also opposed need-aware admission, in comments about international students); the outcome is not in the article (https://cornellsun.com/2017/02/26/cornell-considering-need-aware-admissions-for-transfers-additional-controversial-measures/). Current pages say domestic admission is need-blind.
- Community college students assumed Cornell was too expensive (Chronicle 2024).
- A College Confidential thread on aid for international transfers had no substantive answers (https://talk.collegeconfidential.com/t/does-cornell-university-provide-decent-financial-aid-packages-for-international-transfer-students/1919903).

### 7.7 Community college and odds (5 sources)

- A 2014 community college student with a 3.5 got split replies: "near-flawless GPA needed," "statutory colleges take CC transfers," and an unverified claim that community college grades are inflated by about a point (https://talk.collegeconfidential.com/t/what-are-my-chances-for-transferring-to-cornell/1642313).
- CollegeVine answers advise a GPA of about 3.5 to near 4.0, the hardest available courses, prerequisites, a program-specific essay, professor letters and backup schools; one claims rates vary by college from about 8% to 20% with no source (https://www.collegevine.com/faq/182748/advice-for-transferring-from-community-college-to-cornell ; https://www.collegevine.com/faq/183199/experience-transferring-from-nyc-community-college-to-cornell).
- A CollegeVine reply summarizing what "many transfer students have reported" came from someone who did not transfer; not a transfer account (https://www.collegevine.com/faq/27647/cornell-transfer-experiences).

### 7.8 Transferring out (1 source)

Cat Huang, ILR sophomore and Student Assembly transfer representative, wrote in 2019 that transferring out of Cornell is more common than assumed, that one friend decided within a semester to leave, that leavers were often involved and doing well, and that first-year housing may be a cause. She said the first-time-freshman six-year graduation rate was steady at 93 to 94% while the transfer rate was lower and more variable (her chart was not visible), and that transfers are about one-fifth of each graduating class. These are her own conversations, not data (https://www.cornellsun.com/article/2019/03/guest-room-transfer-in-transfer-out).

### 10.4 Credits lost and semesters needed under a 60-credit cap

**Question.** For an applicant with X credits, how many are lost and how long is college in total?

**Method.** A&S rules: at most 60 transfer credits; 120 credits to graduate; at least 60 credits and four semesters at Cornell (https://as.cornell.edu/registrar/transferring-credits ; https://as.cornell.edu/admissions/admissions-requirements-and-process). Assumption: 15 credits a semester in both places and every credit accepted up to the cap. Real evaluations are case by case.

| Credits earned before Cornell | Semesters there (at 15) | Accepted (min of earned, 60) | Lost | Cornell semesters (120 - accepted) / 15, minimum 4 | Total semesters |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 30 | 2 | 30 | 0 | 6 | 8 |
| 45 | 3 | 45 | 0 | 5 | 8 |
| 60 | 4 | 60 | 0 | 4 | 8 |
| 75 | 5 | 60 | 15 | 4 | 9 |
| 90 | 6 | 60 | 30 | 4 | 10 |
| 105 | 7 | 60 | 45 | 4 | 11 |

**Worked step (90 credits).** Accepted = min(90, 60) = 60. Lost = 90 - 60 = 30. Remaining = 120 - 60 = 60. Cornell semesters = 60 / 15 = 4, which is also the residence floor. Total = 6 + 4 = 10 semesters, two more than eight. Overloading cannot shorten it, because the four-semester residence rule still applies. The extra year at 2026-27 endowed tuition and fees costs 73,946 + 1,024 = 74,970 before housing and aid.

**Quarter credits.** Multiply by 2/3: 4 quarter credits = 2.67 semester credits; 5 = 3.33; 90 quarter credits = 60 semester credits, exactly the cap; 180 quarter = 120 semester. A&S counts only courses of 4 or more quarter credits toward requirements.

**How the caps differ by college.**

| College | Cap | Minimum at Cornell | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| A&S | 60 | 60 credits and four semesters | 120 to graduate, 100 must be A&S credits |
| CALS | 60, test plus transfer (AP up to 15) | not stated on the page | 2 semesters = sophomore, 4 or an associate's = junior |
| Engineering | 66 total, 33 per full-time year | 60 credits | 123 to 137 credits to graduate (registrar) |
| ILR | 60 including AP up to 12 | 60 credits and four full-time semesters | |
| Hotel | 60 including AP/IB/GCE up to 15 | not stated | |
| Brooks | 60 | four semesters on the Ithaca campus | prep courses 6 + 3 + 3 + 3 = 15 credits |
| Architecture | 64 | 90 credits | 154 credits over five years |

Engineering example: two full years elsewhere x 33 = 66, equal to the total cap; 1.5 years x 33 = 49.5 credits. A student with three years elsewhere is still capped at 66.

**Result.** Up to 60 credits nothing is lost; each 15 credits beyond 60 adds a semester of total college time. Sample: rules model, no student data. Date 2026-11-11.

**Caveats.** Courses that transfer only as electives can still leave requirements unmet, which the model ignores. Exam credit sits inside the cap in most colleges, so a student with AP credit hits 60 sooner.
