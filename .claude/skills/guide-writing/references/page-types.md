# Page types

The search brief's `intent` and the coverage map's `page_type` tell you which shape to use.

## how-to
The reader needs to do something. Opening: the outcome and the first step. Body: numbered steps in order, each with its deadline, cost and form, then "what goes wrong" with the fix for each. Close with where to get help (office, phone, hotline).

## decision
The reader must choose (sign now or wait, Ithaca or Syracuse airport, meal plan or cook). Opening: the recommendation split by situation ("If X, do A. If Y, do B."). Body: the factors that decide it, a comparison table, then a section per option with when it wins. Never end without a recommendation.

## cost
The reader wants a number. Opening: the number or range, what it includes, and the period. Body: a cost table built from fact rows, how the total is computed, what changes it, and how to pay less. Show the arithmetic with its inputs.

## checklist
The reader wants to not miss anything. Opening: the one or two items people miss most. Body: the list in time order (two weeks before, one week before, the day of), each item one line with its rule or deadline. Keep explanations short; link to the guide that explains each in depth.

## explainer
The reader wants to understand a rule or system (Good Cause eviction, academic standing). Opening: what it means for them in one or two sentences. Body: who it covers and who it doesn't, what it requires, what changes in practice, common myths with the correction, and what to do if it applies to them.

## roundup (pillar)
The whole topic. Opening: the shape of the topic in three or four sentences (who it applies to, the key decisions, the biggest cost). Body: a short table of contents, then one section per major use case that gives the essential answer and links to the guide for depth. A pillar is a map with real answers in it, not a summary of other pages. See `pillars.md`.
