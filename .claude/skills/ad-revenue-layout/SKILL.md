# Ad revenue and layout

EduExplained earns money mainly from display ads. Ad networks pay per thousand pageviews (RPM), and that rate depends on how readers behave and who is advertising against the topic. Everything in this skill serves one principle: **the page that is best for the reader is the page that earns the most over time.** Tricks that raise one visit's ad count lower trust, return visits, search rankings and ad network approval.

## What raises earnings
- **Deep reading.** Ads that are actually seen pay more, so pages people read past the first screen earn more. Clear layering and sections worth reading do this; padding does the opposite.
- **A second pageview.** Each additional page in a visit adds a full set of ad impressions. Internal links placed where readers naturally want the next guide, and a strong related-guides module, are the cheapest revenue gain available.
- **Tools and calculators.** They hold attention, get revisited and attract links.
- **High-value topics.** Advertisers pay more to reach people making money decisions. In education, financial aid, student loans, college costs, scholarships, 529 plans and paying for graduate school attract finance advertisers, which typically pay several times more per view than general lifestyle topics. Housing costs and test prep also carry commercial intent.
- **U.S. readers,** who command the highest ad rates. Our scope is already the United States.

## Ad networks, roughly (as of 2026)
Premium ad managers lowered their entry bars recently: reports put Raptive at about 25,000 monthly pageviews and Mediavine's entry tier at about 1,000 monthly sessions, with full programs requiring more. All of them require original, family-safe content and reject sites with thin or copied pages. Treat these figures as approximate and check each network's current terms before applying.

## Where ads may go
The layering agent marks ad break positions; the renderer places ads only there.
- After the opening layers (headline, takeaways, opening answer), never before the reader has the answer.
- Between complete sections, never inside a table, list, numbered steps, tool, chart or reader block.
- Spread through the body rather than stacked; the ad network's own spacing rules apply on top of ours.

## Never
- Pad a page to fit more ads. Length follows the coverage map.
- Split one answer across pages to force extra pageviews.
- Write clickbait headlines that the page doesn't deliver on; they raise exits and hurt search performance.
- Use interstitials or ads that shift the page while it loads; both hurt the reader and search rankings.
- Let an advertiser or affiliate relationship change what a page says. Any sponsored or affiliate content is labeled clearly and kept apart from editorial content.

## For the topic scout
When choosing between topics with similar demand and openings, prefer the one with commercial value to advertisers, but never choose a topic for its ad rate alone. A high-value topic we can't answer better than existing pages earns nothing.
