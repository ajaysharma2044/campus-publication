# Source rules

## Order of trust
1. **Statute and official code** (state law, city code). The legal text wins over any summary of it.
2. **The responsible institution's own page** (the registrar, the housing office, the transit authority).
3. **Official data** (federal and state datasets, the institution's published data such as a Common Data Set).
4. **Reputable reporting** (established newspapers, the campus paper's news reporting). Good for events, dates, history and context; weaker than 1 to 3 for current rules and prices.
5. **Established commercial sources** (a landlord's own price list, an operator's fare page) for that business's own prices and terms.
6. **Aggregators and listicles.** Use only to find primary sources; never as the final source of a value.

Forums, social media and Q&A posts are never sources of facts. They feed lane C as demand signals only.

## Dates
Record the date you read every page. Prefer the most recent official version. If a page carries no date and its content looks old (a past academic year, a closed office), record it with a note and look for a newer source.

## When a page won't load
Note it in gaps with the URL and the error. Use a search-result snippet only to locate the real page, never as the value itself. If the snippet is all that exists, record the value with confidence `secondary` and a note "snippet only, page not read."

## Copying
Record facts in your own short words. Quote official wording only for definitions, eligibility rules and legal language where exact words matter, and keep any quote under 15 words.
