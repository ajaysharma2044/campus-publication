# Publisher anatomy

How each publisher builds an explainer page, what makes it digestible, and what EduExplained takes from it. Observations marked **(read Nov. 2026)** come from pages read directly for this file. Those marked **(format knowledge)** describe each publisher's well-known page format; their sites blocked automated reading, so those details were not re-verified this month. Re-verify by viewing a live page in a browser before relying on a detail.

## Investopedia
- **Headline:** definition formula, e.g. "Stablecoins: Definition, How They Work, and Types" **(read Nov. 2026, via search results)**.
- **Trust strip:** author, "Updated" date, "Reviewed by" a credentialed expert with a full bio, "Fact checked by" a named fact checker, "Part of the Series" **(read Nov. 2026, via search results)**.
- **Key Takeaways** box of 3 to 5 bullets directly after the trust strip, then a table of contents **(read Nov. 2026, via search results)**.
- **Body (format knowledge):** first sentence defines the term; then "Understanding X," "How X Works," types or variations, a worked example with real numbers, pros and cons (often a two-column table), comparisons with related terms, FAQs, and "The Bottom Line."
- **Scale:** more than 14,000 term definitions, all cross-linked **(read Nov. 2026, via search results)**.
- **Take:** definition-first openings, the trust strip, Key Takeaways, worked examples, the glossary model (one short page per term, each linking to deep guides).
- **Leave:** "The Bottom Line" recap that repeats the takeaways; it would break our each-fact-once rule.

## Niche
- **Format knowledge:** each school is an entity page with a letter-grade report card (overall and by category), a band of key statistics (acceptance rate, net price, test ranges, graduation rate), rankings it appears in, and many student reviews and polls.
- **Take:** the entity page model for data pages: top stat band with year and source, then a clear verdict. Grades only with a published method.
- **Leave:** reviews and polls as content (we don't use forum or review posts), and grades whose method readers can't check.

## The New York Times
- **Format knowledge:** explainers and The Upshot lead with a chart or a one-sentence finding, write in a calm, authoritative voice, and use interactive calculators that let readers put in their own numbers (the rent-versus-buy calculator is the famous example). Short "what we know / what we don't" explainers handle fast-moving topics.
- **Take:** chart-led data findings, calculators for personal decisions, and separating what is known from what isn't in plain sentences.
- **Take for feature mode:** a short scene lede, then the nut graf by the second or third paragraph, as in our approved "Where Cornell Sleeps" pillar.
- **Leave:** narrative build-ups that run past the third paragraph before saying what the page is about.

## CNN
- **Read Nov. 2026:** syndicated explainers use the "Here's what to know" headline formula, a dateline, very short paragraphs (often one sentence), and an opening that states what changed and when.
- **Read Nov. 2026 (AP piece on the same topic):** sections segmented by situation, each opening "If you...": "If you were enrolled in the SAVE plan," "If you're working toward Public Service Loan Forgiveness."
- **Take:** situation-segmented sections; short paragraphs for scanning.
- **Leave:** quote-driven paragraphs that restate the point, and the "critics argued" balance sentence where a fact would do.

## Fox News (FOX Television Stations explainer)
- **Read Nov. 2026:** headline "What borrowers and parents need to know about new limits, repayment plans"; a dek-style summary; **The Brief** with 3 bullets; a dateline; H2 sections each opened by a bold label: **What we know**, **By the numbers**, **Why you should care**; caps presented as a 3-item list ("Master's degree students: $20,500 per year, $100,000 lifetime"); and a closing **The Source** line naming where the information came from.
- **Take:** The Brief (our Key takeaways), labeled scan sections, caps in lists, an explicit source line.
- **Leave:** opinionated phrasing inside factual sentences.

## NerdWallet-style finance guides (Credible observed)
- **Read Nov. 2026:** a takeaways box, an "options as of [date]" table near the top, a question heading ("Which repayment plans am I eligible for?"), then situation sections ("If you borrowed federal loans on or after July 1, 2026").
- **Take:** the dated options table near the top, question headings, situation sections. These guides are built for high-value finance traffic, which is where display-ad rates are highest.
- **Leave:** product promotion blocks ("Compare refinance rates") inside editorial content.
