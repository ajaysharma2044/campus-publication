# Before and after: real pages, rewritten

Every "before" below is a real passage from a published page (November 2026 audit of all 108 articles). Every "after" uses only facts the "before" already contained. Study the change, not just the result: each pair shows one rule from SKILL.md doing its job.

These are a stand-in until the approved gold-set pages exist in `.claude/skills/gold-set/examples/`. Once they do, the gold set wins wherever the two differ.

---

## 1. The overloaded opening (rule: the opening is the answer, 60 words, one link)

**Before** (landlord guide)
> In the City of Ithaca, a rental building with three or more units needs a Certificate of Compliance, inspected and renewed every three years, for a fee of $300 for 3 to 8 units. Every student lease then runs on two clocks: the City's 120 days' written notice before you re-let, and New York's rule that the deposit comes back with an itemized statement within 14 days or is lost. Ithaca also opted into Good Cause Eviction...

**After**
> If you rent to students in the City of Ithaca, three rules matter most. Any building with three or more units needs a Certificate of Compliance ($300 for 3 to 8 units). You must give tenants 120 days' written notice before re-renting. And you have 14 days to return a deposit with an itemized list, or you lose the right to keep any of it.

**Why it's better:** one idea per sentence, a clear frame ("three rules"), and Good Cause moves down to its own section where it can be explained properly.

## 2. The negative opening (rule: lead with what the reader can do)

**Before** (Arts & Sciences distribution courses)
> Nobody, including Cornell, publishes a trustworthy ranked list of the easiest Arts & Sciences distribution courses, and we will not invent one. What you can do is cut the work with the rules. You need a minimum of 8 courses to cover all 10 distribution categories, because faculty can list a course under up to two categories and up to two courses may count twice...

**After**
> You can cover all 10 Arts & Sciences distribution categories with as few as 8 courses, because a course can be listed under two categories and up to two courses can count twice. Check each course's listing before you count it. Cornell publishes no ranking of easy courses, so the rules are the most reliable way to lighten your load.

**Why it's better:** the reader gets a usable answer in the first sentence. The missing list is still mentioned, once, without "we will not invent one."

## 3. The hedge (rule: when sources disagree, say which to follow)

**Before** (landlord guide)
> A code excerpt puts the unrelated-occupant trigger at five, not six, so treat five as the safe line until the Building Division says otherwise.

**After**
> The City's website says six unrelated tenants trigger a certificate. The city code says five. Go by five, and if you're unsure, call the Building Division at 607-274-6508.

## 4. The "Not confirmed" label (rule: state a gap once, plainly, with a next step)

**Before** (bikeshare)
> Not confirmed: whether Bikeshare runs through the winter. Check the app before you count on it in January.

**After**
> Ithaca Bikeshare doesn't post a winter schedule, so check the app before you count on it in January.

## 5. Research narration (rule: the link is the attribution)

**Before** (cheapest off-campus housing)
> ...a 6BR or 10BR in Collegetown at $695 to $800 a month for 2027-28, or a downtown 3BR at a $795 median, per Ithaca Renting's Collegetown and downtown listings read on Nov 8, 2026.

**After**
> ...a bed in a Collegetown 6- or 10-bedroom costs $695 to $800 a month for 2027-28, and a share in a downtown 3-bedroom has a [median of $795](https://...).

**Why it's better:** the source is one click away and the date lives in the page's "Updated" line. "Read on Nov 8" is a note for the writer, not the reader.

**Also:** "a 20-minute trip is $6.50 before tax by our arithmetic" becomes "a 20-minute ride costs $6.50 before tax ($1.50 to start plus 20 minutes at $0.25)." Show the inputs; drop the narration.

## 6. The repeating reader block (rule: a reader block holds only what changes)

**Before** (bikeshare, "If you're an upper-year without a car")
> Your daytime TCAT ride costs $1.50, and buses are free after 6 p.m. on weekdays and all weekend. A 20-minute Bikeshare ride costs $6.50, more than four bus fares. Use Bikeshare when the bus is late or does not go where you need... If you ride most weekdays, the $60 monthly plan covers 30 minutes a day; ten 20-minute pay-as-you-go rides already cost $65. The mistake that costs most: ending rides wherever you stop. Each one outside a hub adds $1.50.

**After**
> Treat Bikeshare as your backup for late buses and routes TCAT doesn't run. If you ride most weekdays, the monthly plan pays for itself after about ten 20-minute rides.

**Why it's better:** the TCAT fares, free hours, ride price and hub fee were already stated elsewhere on the page (the bus fare 13 times in prose alone). The block keeps only the advice specific to this reader.

## 7. Time-bound advice (rule: anchor to a date)

**Before** (Thanksgiving bus)
> Book your Thanksgiving bus this week. Cornell's break runs Wednesday Nov 25 through Sunday Nov 29, 2026, OurBus lists the Wednesday before Thanksgiving as a peak day, and the cheapest seats on Ithaca routes ran about a third higher that week in a Nov 8 sample.

**After**
> Buy both legs of your Thanksgiving bus trip by mid-November. Cornell's break runs Wednesday, Nov. 25, through Sunday, Nov. 29, 2026, and the Wednesday before Thanksgiving is a peak day, when the cheapest seats on Ithaca routes cost about a third more.

## 8. Planner notes on the page (rule: SEO talk stays in the packet)

**Before** (bikeshare)
> The pages that rank for this question date from 2011 to 2023 and quote old prices.

**After**
> Prices have risen since 2023, so older pages quote fares that no longer apply.

**Why it's better:** the reader needs the warning, not our view of the search results.
